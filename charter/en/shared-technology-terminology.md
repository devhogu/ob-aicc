# Shared Business and Technology Terminology

Edition 1.6 · 4 October 2026

## 1. Purpose and scope

1.1. This reference forms part of the AICC charter corpus. It specifies the shared names, meanings, protected uses and permitted language variants of business, finance, delivery and technology terms used in the corpus and its portal.

1.2. Established industry terminology shall be used where it accurately identifies the concept. An AICC-specific name shall be used where it expresses a distinct concept, responsibility, Record or state.

1.3. This reference and Vocabulary and Style have the following complementary functions.

| Reference | Function |
| --- | --- |
| [Vocabulary and Style](documents/vocabulary.md) | Defines AICC concepts, Roles, authority, work items, states, controls and Records |
| Shared Business and Technology Terminology | Specifies shared names, abbreviations, aliases, protected terms, definitions and their application |

1.4. A term shall retain the meaning and operational application established in the governing documents. Its inclusion in this reference does not establish a service commitment, authorize a system or product, or change a control, measurement boundary or decision right.

1.5. Each language edition shall provide definitions and application in its own language. Corresponding entries shall describe equivalent concepts. Fixed names shall remain consistent across language versions.

## 2. Terminology conventions

2.1. Entries are grouped by subject and identify a universal term, meaning and application. The universal term is the shared entry name across language editions; its application specifies whether its exact form is required. The Application column governs usage: a fixed form is retained across languages; a permitted language variant follows the language of the document; a context-specific form follows the identified methodology, system or Record.

2.2. Fixed names, including AI and IT, shall retain their spelling, script and internal capitalization. Product names, including CloudWatch when used without a company name, shall be preserved. Sentence and heading capitalization may vary for ordinary words.

2.3. The surrounding text shall follow the grammar of its language. Examples include AI capabilities, an IT function, workflow execution, an FSM model, a DAG and CloudWatch metrics.

2.4. Recorded singular and plural forms of fixed names shall be preserved. Inflection shall be expressed through the surrounding text where the fixed form does not support the grammar of that language. Translatable adjectives shall follow the language and meaning of the sentence.

2.5. A full name may precede its abbreviation at first use: Finite State Machine (FSM), directed acyclic graph (DAG), Amazon Web Services (AWS). Subsequent references may use the abbreviation. An explanatory translation does not replace a fixed name.

2.6. A name shall be applied to the complete concept or entity it identifies. Generic words shall not be treated as product names solely because their spelling occurs within such a name. Service Management does not identify a particular vendor product unless that product is specified.

2.7. Exact identifiers, interface labels, schema keys, file paths and quoted titles shall retain their original form. Terminology changes shall preserve the identity of historical Records and the distinctions between defined AICC concepts.

2.8. A protected term is a term established by a professional practice, such as agile delivery, scaled agile delivery or IT service management, or a fixed name, whose meaning is not conveyed by the meaning of its constituent words. Fixed names include the company, organizational and product names and the fixed abbreviations recorded in this reference.

2.9. The meaning of a protected term is the meaning stated in this reference. Where the term is also defined in Vocabulary and Style, the stated meaning agrees with that definition, and Vocabulary and Style governs its functional interpretation.

2.10. The Application of each entry states whether the term is a protected term and, where it is, its protected use: the professional practice and the sense in which the term is protected, or its status as a fixed name. An entry that states that a term is not protected gives the established meaning of the term without restricting its use.

2.11. The same word used outside its protected use has its ordinary meaning. The context of each occurrence determines whether a word is used in its protected sense. Examples include a Feature in agile delivery and a feature of a product, a task as an agile work item and the task of an AI agent, and an incident in IT service management and an incident in ordinary narrative.

2.12. The language editions of the corpus render protected terms according to their own terminology edition.

## 3. Business and governance terms

3.1. These terms describe business justification, governance, responsibilities and performance.

| Universal term | Meaning | Application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| business case | The justification for an investment or change: the problem or opportunity, alternatives, expected benefits, costs, risks, and the basis for deciding whether to proceed | Fixed form. Not a protected term. Plural form: business cases. Business case identifies the reasoning and justification. The Initiative Brief is the AICC record that captures it; the record name and the concept have different purposes. A business case need not be a long document |
| business intelligence / customer intelligence | Analysis supporting business decisions / analysis of customers, their behavior, and experience | Permitted language variant. Protected terms in business analytics, for these analytical activities. Intelligence identifies analytical activity; the term alone does not establish the use of AI. |
| dashboard | A consolidated view of selected measures and status information for a defined audience or decision | Permitted language variant. Not a protected term. The defined Dashboard Record has the scope assigned in the governing documents. A generic dashboard does not acquire Record status through terminology alone. Operating Model 7.1; Solution Lifecycle Model 4.4 |
| embedded finance | The provision of financial services within a non-financial product or application, typically through a partnership with a licensed financial institution | Permitted language variant. Protected term in financial services, for that mode of provision. The portal uses it to describe industry practice in digital banking; its use does not describe a product or partnership of the Bank |
| ESG | Environmental, social, and governance. A grouping of environmental, social, and governance considerations | Fixed form. Protected term as a fixed name. Statement of Intent 3.2. ESG is the fixed abbreviation. Its use does not constitute certification or evidence of performance |
| FP&A | Financial planning and analysis. The finance activity or function that prepares budgets, forecasts, and analyses of financial performance for management decisions | Fixed form. Protected term as a fixed name. The portal uses FP&A to name a client function in the examples of the service catalog. The abbreviation names the activity or function; it does not by itself identify a Domain |
| HR | Human resources | Permitted language variant. Not a protected term. HR identifies the human resources function |
| impact assessment | A structured assessment, made before a use that may carry higher risk, of its effects on the persons concerned and on the rights at stake | Permitted language variant. Not a protected term. The portal explains it with reference to the law on AI and on personal data; the assessments required of the Bank are those that the applicable law and the AI Policy set. An impact assessment is distinct from the assignment of a Risk Tier |
| KPI | Key performance indicator. A measure selected as particularly important for assessing progress or performance against an objective | Fixed form. Protected term as a fixed name. A KPI is a Measure designated as key to an objective. Its objective, definition, source, responsible Role and review basis shall be specified. Other metrics remain Measures without KPI designation |
| KYC | Know your customer. The discipline of establishing and maintaining knowledge of a customer's identity and relevant risk characteristics | Fixed form. Protected term as a fixed name. Statement of Intent 11.3.1. KYC is the fixed abbreviation. The applicable customer identification requirements remain those established by the Bank |
| Lean Portfolio Management | The practice of managing a portfolio of investments by lean and agile principles: strategy and investment funding, agile portfolio operations, and lean governance, with funding allocated to strategic themes or value streams rather than to individual projects | Context-specific form. Protected term in scaled agile delivery, for that portfolio practice. The Portfolio Management Model applies it in AICC's own terms: Investment Envelopes fund Strategic Priorities, and decisions are taken in small steps on evidence (Vocabulary and Style, Portfolio management). The portal also writes it in lower case |
| model risk management | The discipline of identifying, measuring, and controlling the risk of loss or harm from the use of models, through an inventory of models, independent validation, documentation, monitoring, and a named owner for each model | Permitted language variant. Protected term in banking risk management, for that discipline. The Control Function of model risk performs Validation within its remit (Vocabulary and Style, Control Function and Validation; AI Policy 1.4 and 3.3). AI governance extends the discipline to AI models and Solutions |
| OKR | Objectives and key results: a method of relating objectives to measurable results | Context-specific form. Protected term as a fixed name. OKR identifies the method or a set of objectives and key results. PI Objective retains its distinct AICC definition and scoring |
| open banking | The sharing of customer account data and the initiation of payments with licensed third parties through secure interfaces, with the customer's consent | Permitted language variant. Protected term in financial services, for that arrangement. The portal uses it to describe industry practice; its use does not record a service or an interface of the Bank |
| RACI | Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed. A matrix distinguishing the performer, the owner answerable for the result, those consulted, and those informed | Fixed form. Protected term as a fixed name. Organization guide, section 4; Appointments Record template. RACI and its letters are retained. Responsible identifies the performer; Accountable identifies the person answerable for the result |
| regtech | Regulatory technology. Technology, including AI, applied to compliance tasks such as screening, monitoring, reporting, and the tracking of regulatory change | Permitted language variant. Protected term in financial services, as the established short name of regulatory technology. Its use does not change the remit of a Control Function |
| SOW | Statement of work: a description of the scope, deliverables, and related conditions of work | Context-specific form. Not a protected term. SOW identifies the statement of work. Its relationship to a Service Agreement depends on the scope and standing of the actual documents; the terms are not automatically equivalent |
| stakeholder | A person or body whose interests, work, or decisions are affected by an activity or its outcome, or who can affect it | Permitted language variant. Not a protected term. Plural form: stakeholders. For the purposes of a Service Agreement, the defined Stakeholder is a person outside AICC who receives notifications about the Engagement; that designation assigns no AICC Role and creates no commitment (Vocabulary and Style, Stakeholder; Business Model 3.1 and 5.4). Elsewhere the word has its general meaning, as when the AICC Lead is a stakeholder in every AI Incident (AI Policy 5.5) |

## 4. Delivery and workflow terms

4.1. These terms describe the organization, movement, delivery and operation of work and services.

| Universal term | Meaning | Application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| acceptance criteria | The observable conditions against which a delivered item is assessed to determine whether it meets its agreed requirements | Permitted language variant. Not a protected term. In AICC, acceptance criteria are written as Given a situation, when an action occurs, then an observable result follows, and the product owner, the AICC Lead, and the Business acceptor apply them at their respective levels (Vocabulary and Style, Acceptance criteria and Acceptance; Solution Lifecycle Model 7.3) |
| agile delivery | An approach to delivering work in short, regular cycles with frequent feedback, close collaboration with the customer, working results in preference to comprehensive documentation, and response to change in preference to following a fixed plan | Permitted language variant. Protected term in product and software delivery, for that approach. The adjective agile in its ordinary sense of quick or nimble has its ordinary meaning. Business Model 5.2 describes the Service Agreement as a form of agile working agreement that follows these principles. Agile delivery across several teams is scaled agile delivery |
| backlog | An ordered collection of work to be considered or carried out; its level and admission rules determine what it contains | Permitted language variant. Protected term in agile delivery, for an ordered collection of work; an accumulation of overdue work has the ordinary meaning. Backlog identifies the general concept. Portfolio Backlog, Program Backlog and Iteration Backlog identify the respective levels. Vocabulary and Style; Solution Lifecycle Model section 4 |
| Backlog Refinement | In agile delivery, the recurring activity in which upcoming backlog items are clarified, split, sized, or ordered so that they are ready to be selected for work | Context-specific form. Protected term in agile delivery. In AICC, it is an Event of the Iteration loop that keeps the next items ready under Solution Lifecycle Model 4.5; it is held within the weekly sessions and is optional in light mode (Vocabulary and Style, Event; Solution Lifecycle Model 6.3 and 6.6) |
| Capability | In scaled agile delivery, a higher-level behavior of a solution that delivers value to its users and is decomposed into Features. In AICC, a capability of a Solution, delivered by the Teams over one or more Program Increments, held in the Program Backlog, and grouped under its Initiative, as Vocabulary and Style defines it | Context-specific form. Protected term in scaled agile delivery and in its defined AICC use. Plural form: Capabilities. In Jira, a Capability maps to an Epic (Portfolio Management Model 8.3). The word capability in the sense of an ability of the Bank, AICC, a person, or a system has its ordinary meaning, as in AI capabilities or the capabilities recorded in a Solution Definition |
| change enablement / change management | In IT service management, the practice of ensuring that changes to services and their components are assessed, authorized, scheduled, and implemented so that successful changes are maximized and their risk is controlled | Context-specific form. Protected terms in IT service management. Change management of the Bank names the Bank's practice, which approves each deployment to production and each change to a released Solution; change enablement names the corresponding practice of a Service that AICC runs, which uses the change management of the Bank (Solution Lifecycle Model 8.3, 8.6 and 8.10; Operating Model C-30). Neither term denotes the management of organizational change |
| change failure rate | A software delivery measure: the share of deployments to production that cause a failure requiring remediation, such as a rollback, a fix, or the handling of an incident | Permitted language variant. Protected term in software delivery measurement. In AICC, the share of deployments and changes that are rolled back or cause an incident; its target is set in the Service Agreement (Solution Lifecycle Model 10.3 and 10.4) |
| change ticket | The record of a change in the change management system, through which the change is raised, assessed, approved, and traced | Permitted language variant. Protected term in IT service management. In AICC, the Solution Engineer raises the change in the change management of the Bank and enters the change ticket and the test result in the Feature (Solution Lifecycle Model 8.3; Operating Model C-30). See ticket |
| change request (CR) | A formal request to change a Solution, Service, or configuration item, raised, assessed, approved, and traced under change management | Context-specific form. Protected term in IT service management; abbreviation CR. A change request is recorded as a change ticket in the change management system of the Bank. The word request otherwise has its ordinary meaning |
| class of service | In Kanban practice, a category of work items that share a policy for their selection and handling, reflecting their urgency or cost of delay | Context-specific form. Protected term in Kanban practice. Plural form: classes of service. In AICC, the urgency of a Feature or a request: Urgent, High priority, or Normal, each carried by a Lane of the Program Kanban (Vocabulary and Style, Class of service and Lane; Solution Lifecycle Model 8.5). A swimlane may display a class of service |
| code review | Examination of source code to understand a proposed change and identify defects or weaknesses | Permitted language variant. Not a protected term. Code review identifies the examination of source code. It does not replace the charter's broader Check or the Control Functions' Validation. Statement of Intent 9.8 |
| cutover | The controlled transition at which an agreed new system or way of working takes over from the previous one | Permitted language variant. Protected term in IT service transition, for that controlled transition. AICC uses it specifically for moving working state from the Registry to Jira and Confluence; it does not move the evidence store. Operating Model 7.1–7.3; Collaboration tooling |
| Daily Stand-up | In agile delivery, a short daily meeting of a team to synchronize its work, share progress, and identify impediments | Context-specific form. Protected term in agile delivery. In AICC, the Event of the day loop at which the Team shares progress and clears blockers; it is optional in light mode (Vocabulary and Style, Event; Solution Lifecycle Model 6.1, 6.4 and 6.6) |
| defect / bug | A flaw in a product or its components that causes it to fail to meet a requirement or to behave as intended; bug is the established name of a defect in software | Permitted language variant. Defect is not a protected term. Bug is a protected term in software engineering, for a software defect and for a work item that records one; the word otherwise has its ordinary meaning. The Solution Lifecycle Model measures the defects found in a Solution in operation after its check or validation (10.3), and such defects lead the AICC Lead to reassess the Risk Tier (AI Policy 3.7). A confabulated output of a model is a property of the model rather than a defect that a fix removes |
| definition of ready / definition of done | In agile delivery, the agreed conditions that a work item meets before it is taken into work / before it is considered complete | Context-specific form. Protected terms in agile delivery. In AICC, both apply to Features: the definition of ready states the conditions that a Feature meets before it is approved for work, and the definition of done the conditions that it meets before it enters the Done column, including acceptance by the product owner (Vocabulary and Style, Definition of ready and Definition of done; Solution Lifecycle Model 4.5) |
| deployment | Placing and configuring a version in a target environment so it can run | Permitted language variant. Protected term in software delivery and IT service management, for placing a version in an environment. Initial deployment and the Release decision are distinct events in AICC. |
| deployment frequency | A software delivery measure: how often changes are deployed to production in a stated period | Permitted language variant. Protected term in software delivery measurement. In AICC, the deployments to production in an Iteration, read as a trend with no target (Solution Lifecycle Model 10.3) |
| emergency change | In IT service management, a change that must be implemented as soon as possible, for example to resolve an incident, and that is authorized through an expedited procedure | Context-specific form. Protected term in IT service management. In AICC, a change to a Solution in production that cannot wait for the normal process of the change management of the Bank; it follows the emergency procedure of the Bank, and the Executive Sponsor reviews it within five working days (Vocabulary and Style, Emergency change; Solution Lifecycle Model 8.6) |
| environment | In IT, a configured set of infrastructure, software, and data in which a system is built, tested, or operated, such as a test environment or production | Permitted language variant. Protected term in IT, for such a set. The defined Environment of use is the environment in which a Feature runs for its users; it is not production until the Solution is deployed to its first users, and production afterwards. The Lab is the isolated and logged environment of AICC for Experiments (Vocabulary and Style, Environment of use and Lab; Solution Lifecycle Model 7.1). Environment in its ordinary or ecological sense, as in ESG, has its ordinary meaning |
| Epic / issue / sub-task | Jira item names or types used to organize work at different levels | Context-specific form. Protected terms as the fixed names of Jira item types; Epic is also a protected term in agile delivery. Configured item labels shall retain their exact form. In AICC's Jira mapping, Capability maps to Epic, Feature to an issue type, and Work Item to sub-task. Use in another framework is explained in that context and does not change this mapping |
| Feature | In scaled agile delivery, a unit of solution functionality that meets a user need and is sized to be delivered within one Program Increment. In AICC, a deliverable of a Capability that closes within one Program Increment; run-rate work is a Feature directly under a Standing Initiative and is done within one Iteration, as Vocabulary and Style defines it | Context-specific form. Protected term in scaled agile delivery and in its defined AICC use. Plural form: Features. In Jira, a Feature is an issue type (Portfolio Management Model 8.3). The definitions of ready and done, throughput, and acceptance by the product owner are stated for Features. The word feature in the sense of a characteristic or function of a product has its ordinary meaning, as in a request for a new feature (Solution Lifecycle Model 8.5) |
| feature set | In agile delivery, a group of related Features that together deliver a Capability or a defined part of it | Context-specific form. Protected term in agile delivery. A feature set is not a separate level of the AICC work hierarchy; it groups Features within a Capability |
| flow distribution | A flow measure: the proportion of each type of work in a flow system over a stated period | Permitted language variant. Protected term in lean and scaled agile flow measurement. In AICC, the share of the Active Initiatives by Strategic Priority and by kind of work (Portfolio Management Model 4.6 and 9.2) |
| flow efficiency | A flow measure: the proportion of lead time during which work is actively progressed rather than waiting | Permitted language variant. Protected term in lean flow measurement. In AICC, the days that an item is Active and not Waiting, divided by its lead time (Solution Lifecycle Model 10.3; Portfolio Management Model 9.2) |
| flow load | A flow measure of the demand placed on a flow system | Permitted language variant. Protected term in lean and scaled agile flow measurement. In AICC, the Approved Initiatives waiting to be pulled, and the days that each has waited (Portfolio Management Model 9.2). In scaled agile practice, flow load commonly counts the work in progress; the AICC measure counts the queue that waits for a place under the limit on the Active Initiatives |
| incident / incident management | In IT service management, an unplanned interruption to a service or a reduction in its quality / the practice of restoring normal service operation as quickly as possible after an incident | Permitted language variant. Protected terms in IT service management. Plural form: incidents. In AICC, every outage or failure of a Solution is raised in the incident management of the Bank, which owns the classification and severity of incidents (Solution Lifecycle Model 8.5; AI Policy 5.2; Vocabulary and Style, Severity). An incident is an AI Incident only where it meets that definition: an event in which the use of AI causes or could cause harm, a breach of law or policy, or a loss of control (Vocabulary and Style, AI Incident; AI Policy 5.1). The word in its ordinary sense of an event or occurrence has its ordinary meaning |
| Innovation and Planning week / IP week | In scaled agile delivery, the period at the end of a Program Increment reserved for innovation, learning, the review of the increment, and the planning of the next one. In AICC, the last week of the third Iteration of a Program Increment, or an earlier week that the Calendar Record places, which holds the PI Review and Demo, Inspect and Adapt, Innovation, PI Planning, and the quarterly Steering, as Vocabulary and Style defines it | Fixed form. Protected term in scaled agile delivery and as a fixed short name. IP week is the fixed short form in AICC. IP does not mean Internet Protocol or intellectual property in this context. In scaled agile practice the corresponding period lasts a full iteration; in AICC it is one week (Vocabulary and Style, Short forms; Solution Lifecycle Model 6.1 and 6.2) |
| Inspect and Adapt | In scaled agile delivery, the event at the end of each Program Increment in which the results of the increment are evaluated and improvements are identified through structured problem solving | Context-specific form. Protected term in scaled agile delivery, used only as a whole. In AICC, the Event of the Program Increment loop that solves the main problems of the Program Increment and enters its improvements as Features in the Program Backlog; it is held in the IP week, and in light mode within the PI Review and Demo (Solution Lifecycle Model 6.2 and 6.6). Inspection and adaptation in their general sense have their ordinary meaning |
| Iteration | In agile delivery, a fixed time-box in which a team plans, builds, reviews, and improves an increment of work. In AICC, one calendar month of work of a Team, of four or five whole weeks, named I01 to I12, with a goal and an Iteration Backlog, as Vocabulary and Style defines it | Context-specific form. Protected term in agile delivery and in its defined AICC use. Plural form: Iterations. Iteration is never shortened to IT, which means information technology (Vocabulary and Style, Short forms). Use of the term sprint does not change AICC's Iteration. Iteration in a general sense, such as a repetition of a draft, has its ordinary meaning |
| Iteration Planning | In agile delivery, the event at the start of an iteration in which a team selects the work that it will do and sets the goal of the iteration | Context-specific form. Protected term in agile delivery. In AICC, the Event of the Iteration loop that takes the PI Objectives, selects the Features for the month into the Iteration Backlog, and sets the Iteration goal (Solution Lifecycle Model 6.3) |
| Iteration Retrospective | In agile delivery, the event at the end of an iteration in which a team reviews how it worked and agrees on improvements | Context-specific form. Protected term in agile delivery. In AICC, it ends with one or two improvements, which the Team enters as Work Items or Features; in light mode it is held in the Iteration Review and Demo (Solution Lifecycle Model 6.3 and 6.6). Retrospective, alone, names the corresponding feedback loop (Solution Lifecycle Model 6.9) |
| Iteration Review and Demo | In agile delivery, the event at the end of an iteration in which the working increment is demonstrated and assessed and feedback is gathered | Context-specific form. Protected term in agile delivery. In AICC, the Event that shows the working Features and Solutions to the product owner against their written acceptance criteria, takes the acceptance of the Features and the Capabilities, and reads the four signals of each live Solution with its Domain Owner (Solution Lifecycle Model 6.1, 6.3 and 8.4). Demo is the established short name of the demonstration |
| Kanban | A method of managing the flow of work through explicit workflow policies, visualization, and control of work in progress | Permitted language variant. Protected term in agile and lean delivery, for that method. Kanban identifies the method. The defined names Portfolio Kanban and Program Kanban identify the respective AICC boards. Vocabulary and Style; Solution Lifecycle Model |
| known error | In IT service management, a problem whose cause has been analyzed and documented, usually with a workaround, but which has not yet been resolved | Permitted language variant. Protected term in IT service management. Plural form: known errors. In AICC, the known errors of a Service are kept with its knowledge, together with the user guide, and referenced in the Solution Definition (Solution Lifecycle Model 8.10) |
| lead time / cycle time | Elapsed-time measures with explicitly chosen start and end points | Permitted language variant. Protected terms in lean flow measurement. Solution Lifecycle Model 10.3 specifies the measurement boundaries applicable to AICC. |
| lean | Describing the management practice, originating in manufacturing, that delivers value with the least waste by managing flow, limiting work in progress, and improving continuously | Permitted language variant. Protected term in lean management, for that practice; the word otherwise has its ordinary meaning. The Initiative Brief is the lean business case of an Initiative (Portfolio Management Model 6.1), and AICC's flow measures are those of lean practice. Kanban, value stream and WIP limit derive from that practice |
| MVP | Minimum viable product. A deliberately limited first version used to test a value hypothesis with evidence, rather than to deliver all intended capability | Fixed form. Protected term as a fixed name. Vocabulary and Style; Portfolio Management Model 6.8 and section 7. MVP tests the hypothesis of an Initiative in AICC; pilots and prototypes have their respective purposes |
| PI Objective | In scaled agile delivery, a summary of the business and technical goals that a team intends to achieve in a Program Increment, with the business value planned. In AICC, the intent and direction of the Teams for a Program Increment, with the business value scored by the Domain Owner, as Vocabulary and Style defines it | Context-specific form. Protected term in scaled agile delivery and in its defined AICC use. Plural form: PI Objectives. A PI Objective is not a commitment of scope (Vocabulary and Style, PI Objective; Solution Lifecycle Model 6.2). An OKR or a general goal has its own context |
| PI Planning | In scaled agile delivery, the event at the start of each Program Increment at which the teams and the business owners agree on the objectives, plan the work, and identify the dependencies of the increment | Context-specific form. Protected term in scaled agile delivery. In AICC, the Event that sets the PI Objectives, the Features that the Program Increment aims at, the Program Board, and the proposed Roadmap; the Domain Owner, or the Executive Sponsor for enabling work, scores the business value planned of each PI Objective, and the quarterly Steering that ends the IP week confirms the PI Objectives and the Roadmap (Solution Lifecycle Model 6.2) |
| PI predictability | In scaled agile delivery, a measure of the reliability of planning: the business value achieved in a Program Increment relative to the business value planned | Context-specific form. Protected term in scaled agile delivery. In AICC, the business value achieved divided by the business value planned, summed over the PI Objectives of the Program Increment, read as a band over the Program Increments with no target, because the PI Objectives are not a promise (Solution Lifecycle Model 10.3; AICC Charter 7.3) |
| PI Review and Demo | In scaled agile delivery, the event at the end of a Program Increment at which the delivered increment is demonstrated and the achievement of its objectives is assessed | Context-specific form. Protected term in scaled agile delivery, in its AICC form. In AICC, the Event of the Program Increment loop that shows what the Program Increment delivered, at which the Domain Owner, or the Executive Sponsor for enabling work, scores the business value achieved of each PI Objective; the Quarterly Report is drafted from its data (Vocabulary and Style, Quarterly Report; Solution Lifecycle Model 6.2) |
| pilot | A limited use of a solution by a defined group of users under real conditions, to assess its value and readiness before wider use | Permitted language variant. Not a protected term. Plural form: pilots. The Statement of Intent requires each Solution to be piloted and measured against defined success Measures before it is extended (5.2). In AICC, use by the First users precedes the Release decision (Vocabulary and Style, First users and Release). A pilot differs from an MVP, which tests the hypothesis of an Initiative |
| post-incident review | In IT service management, a structured review held after an incident to establish what happened, why it happened, and which improvements are needed | Permitted language variant. Protected term in IT service management. In AICC, the post-incident review of an AI Incident is held in the incident management of the Bank in the time that it sets; the AICC Lead takes part and records the review in the AI Incident Review (AI Policy 5.8; Vocabulary and Style, AI Incident Review) |
| problem / problem management | In IT service management, a cause or potential cause of one or more incidents / the practice of identifying and managing such causes so as to reduce the likelihood and impact of incidents | Permitted language variant. Protected terms in IT service management. In AICC, problem management takes the incidents or requests that repeat as a Feature in the Program Backlog (Solution Lifecycle Model 8.10). Problem in its ordinary sense, as in the main problems that Inspect and Adapt solves, has its ordinary meaning |
| product owner | In agile delivery, the person accountable for the value of a product, who orders its backlog and accepts the delivered work. In AICC, the person who accepts each Feature and each Capability against its acceptance criteria during development, at the Iteration Review and Demo, as Vocabulary and Style defines it | Context-specific form. Protected term in agile delivery and in its defined AICC use. Written in lower case. The designation is not a Role; a product manager is the same person under another name. While the Team has up to three people the AICC Lead is the product owner (Vocabulary and Style, Product owner). The AICC Lead reorders the Program Backlog (Solution Lifecycle Model 6.4) |
| production | The operating environment serving actual users or business processes | Permitted language variant. Protected term in IT service management, for that operating environment; the word otherwise has its ordinary meaning. The Environment of use rule determines the transition applicable to AICC. |
| Program Board | In scaled agile delivery, a board created at PI Planning that shows the planned delivery of features, the dependencies between teams, and the milestones of a Program Increment | Context-specific form. Protected term in scaled agile delivery. ## 7. Maintenance

7.1. Amendments to this reference shall follow the document maintenance and change provisions of the Document Catalog. Amendments affecting the meaning of a defined AICC concept shall be reconciled with Vocabulary and Style and the relevant governing document.

7.2. Each entry shall identify the universal term, permitted form or forms, meaning and scope of application in the language of its edition, and shall state whether the term is a protected term and, where it is, its protected use. Names with a meaning specific to AICC shall identify that context. Abbreviations, product-name variants and distinctions from related concepts shall be included where relevant.

7.3. Company and product names shall be verified against the provider's documentation. Former names shall be identified as historical aliases where required to interpret an existing Record or quotation.

7.4. A change from a permitted language variant to a fixed shared name, or a change in the protected status or protected use of a term, shall be stated in the revised entry.

7.5. Terminology amendments shall be reflected consistently in affected text, headings, tables, diagrams, navigation, search entries and accessible labels. The corpus and its portal projections shall use equivalent meanings.
