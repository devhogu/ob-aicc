# Innovation portfolio

The innovation portfolio is the Bank's structured mechanism for developing and commercializing new capabilities and revenue streams — covering idea sourcing, hypothesis-driven experimentation, MVP development, stage-gate review, and the scaling of commercially validated innovations into production. Banks face fintech disruption from well-funded competitors and must maintain an innovation pipeline that is both disciplined (stage-gated, capital-efficient) and fast (capable of matching fintech iteration velocity). Without a portfolio view, innovation investment is episodic and difficult to justify at board level. **The opportunity for GenAI is to accelerate experiment analysis, automate stage-gate synthesis, and give portfolio leadership continuous visibility into pipeline health and P&L contribution** — shifting the innovation portfolio from a governance burden to a strategic asset with real-time performance accountability.

## Idea sourcing & stage-gate {#idea-sourcing-stage-gate}

The structured process for capturing, evaluating, and progressing innovation ideas from initial submission through stage-gate reviews to approved experimentation. Stage-gate discipline — with defined criteria at each gate (commercial potential, feasibility, strategic fit, resource availability) — prevents premature capital commitment to unvalidated ideas. Stage-gate reviews consume significant innovation board and sponsor time; the quality of review materials determines the quality of the go/no-go decision.

## Scaling decision {#scaling-decision}

The investment and operational decision to take a validated MVP into full production — specifying the investment required, the expected P&L trajectory at different adoption curves, the operational risk of the scaling approach, and the milestone-based funding structure. Scaling decisions are the highest-stakes investment decisions in the innovation portfolio; under-resourcing a scaling innovation destroys the value demonstrated in the MVP. The decision requires modeling adoption curve uncertainty and comparing the scaling option against alternative investment uses.

## MVP design & hypothesis testing {#mvp-design-hypothesis-testing}

Design and execution of minimum viable products and the structured experiments that test the core commercial and technical hypotheses underlying each innovation. Hypothesis-driven experimentation discipline — specifying the hypothesis, metric, test design, and decision threshold before running the experiment — is the primary quality control mechanism in the pipeline. Weak hypothesis design is the leading cause of inconclusive experiment results that consume resources without generating clear go/no-go signals.

## Portfolio rebalancing {#portfolio-rebalancing}

Ongoing optimization of the innovation portfolio composition — balancing early-stage experimentation, mid-stage MVP development, and late-stage scaling — against available investment capital and strategic priority alignment. Portfolio rebalancing requires continuous visibility into the pipeline value at each stage, the expected capital requirement of scaling candidates, and the strategic contribution of current experiments to declared innovation themes. Without rebalancing, portfolios cluster at early stages (low conversion to scale) or at late stages (insufficient pipeline replenishment).

## Innovation P&L {#innovation-pl}

Financial accountability for the innovation portfolio — tracking revenue generated, cost incurred, and P&L contribution by scaled innovation, against the original business case commitment. Innovation P&L reporting is the mechanism by which the board holds the innovation function accountable for commercial outcomes. Without clear attribution of revenue and cost to individual innovations, the portfolio is managed by effort rather than by outcome. Integrating innovation P&L into the Bank's financial reporting structure requires combining business-line revenue data with innovation cost allocations.

### Innovation P&L Revenue Attribution Brief

- URN: urn:financial-services:scenario:strategic-initiatives/innovation-portfolio/innovation-pl/innovation-pl-revenue-attribution-brief
- Lens: Insights
- Complexity: M
- Intent: The AI agent reads the commercial performance of each scaled innovation alongside the Bank's overall business-line financials and produces a quarterly revenue attribution brief that isolates the incremental revenue contribution of each scaled innovation — net of cannibalization effects and shared infrastructure cost — from business-as-usual performance. The innovation board presents attribution-based P&L evidence to the board rather than gross revenue claims that do not account for substitution effects.
- Problem to solve: Innovation P&L reporting claims the full revenue of customers or transactions that use the innovation, without netting cannibalization of existing products or shared infrastructure costs. The board approves the reported innovation contribution without understanding the net incremental impact. When profitability is assessed holistically, innovation contributions are smaller than reported — and funding decisions for the next cycle are made on overstated prior performance.
- Solution: The AI agent reads scaled innovation revenue data, business-line financials, and shared cost allocations. It produces a quarterly attribution brief with incremental revenue contribution per innovation, net of cannibalization effects and cost allocations. The innovation board and the CFO review the net attribution, and the innovation board presents it to the board; innovation funding decisions are made on net, not gross, contribution.
- OKR: Innovation P&L reporting to the board reflects net incremental contribution, not gross revenue claims.
