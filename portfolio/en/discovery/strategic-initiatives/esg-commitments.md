# ESG commitments

ESG commitments are the Bank's publicly declared obligations across environmental (Scope 1/2/3 emissions reduction, climate transition plan), social (financial inclusion, responsible lending, workforce targets), and governance (board composition, executive accountability, conduct standards) dimensions. Sustainability reporting obligations are maturing: regulators are issuing green taxonomy guidelines and setting out climate risk expectations in line with the TCFD recommendations, and institutional investors and development-finance counterparties increasingly require ISSB-standard disclosure. Commitments made publicly bind the institution — disclosure gaps and target misses attract regulatory scrutiny and reputational cost. **The opportunity for GenAI is to compress the data assembly and narrative production cycle for ESG disclosure** — turning a multi-month annual reporting exercise into a continuously maintained disclosure posture, and accelerating transition-plan scenario analysis from weeks to hours.

## TCFD/ISSB disclosure {#tcfd-issb-disclosure}

Climate-related financial disclosure under the Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures (TCFD) framework and ISSB IFRS S2 standard — covering governance, strategy, risk management, and metrics and targets. Regulators commonly signal alignment to TCFD expectations for systemically important financial institutions. TCFD-aligned disclosure typically includes scenario analysis under at least two climate pathways (1.5°C and 2°C+), producing forward-looking narratives of climate risk impact on the balance sheet. The annual TCFD report is the primary disclosure vehicle reviewed by institutional investors, rating agencies, and supervisory authorities.

## Transition planning {#transition-planning}

The Bank's documented pathway for aligning its loan portfolio and investment activities with climate transition commitments — covering sector engagement plans, financing exclusion policies, green product volume targets, and the sequencing of portfolio reallocation from high-emission to low-emission exposures. Transition planning requires scenario-based modeling of portfolio emissions trajectory under different sector transition speeds, regulatory interventions, and client engagement outcomes. The plan is reviewed annually by the board and increasingly by supervisory authorities.

## Scope 1/2/3 reporting {#scope-1-2-3-reporting}

Measurement and disclosure of the Bank's direct operational emissions (Scope 1), purchased energy emissions (Scope 2), and financed emissions across the lending and investment portfolio (Scope 3 Category 15) — the largest and most complex component for banks. Scope 3 financed emissions calculation requires counterparty-level emissions data or sector-based proxies, cross-referenced with the Bank's exposure data by borrower and sector. The calculation methodology under PCAF (Partnership for Carbon Accounting Financials) is data-intensive and requires annual update as exposure positions shift.

## Regulatory ESG filings {#regulatory-esg-filings}

Mandatory ESG disclosures to the regulator, where it requires them — covering green taxonomy alignment reporting, climate risk assessment submissions, and sustainability-linked financial instrument disclosures. Regulatory ESG filings are distinct from voluntary investor disclosure and carry compliance obligations with defined submission timelines. The filing requirements are evolving rapidly as regulators align to the TCFD and ISSB international standards, creating ongoing adaptation requirements for the sustainability and compliance functions.
