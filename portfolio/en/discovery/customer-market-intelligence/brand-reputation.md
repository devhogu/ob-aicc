# Brand & reputation

Brand and reputation tracks the Bank's standing in public, regulatory, and customer perception — the intangible asset that determines customer trust, employee attraction, and the regulatory goodwill that shapes supervisory discretion. Reputational risk is commonly categorized as a material risk in supervisory frameworks, and conduct-related reputational events carry a direct supervisory consequence. Brand trust in banking is fragile: a single high-profile conduct failure or a social-media amplified service breakdown can move customer acquisition and retention metrics materially within days. **GenAI enables continuous reputation monitoring across all public signals** — from social media to regulatory filings — surfacing reputational threats before they escalate beyond containment.

## Reputational risk early warning {#reputational-risk-early-warning}

A composite early-warning signal for reputational risk — combining social-media sentiment trends, complaint-escalation patterns, regulatory communication volume, and media-tone shifts into a single leading indicator for the board and senior leadership. Reputational risk is distinct from the underlying event that causes it: the early-warning signal is not the event itself, but the pattern of signals that precede material reputational damage. Supervisory frameworks commonly classify reputational risk formally and expect the board to oversee a systematic reputational-risk monitoring process.

## Social & media sentiment {#social-media-sentiment}

Continuous monitoring of brand mentions, sentiment scores, and narrative themes across social-media platforms and news sources — the real-time signal channel for brand perception. Social and media sentiment leads NPS: a viral negative event on social media typically precedes a survey-measured NPS decline by four to six weeks. In many markets, messaging platforms and local social networks are the primary social channels for banking discussion; monitoring these platforms requires local-language NLP capabilities.

## Crisis communication readiness {#crisis-communication-readiness}

Pre-staging response frameworks, key-message libraries, and stakeholder communication templates for the most probable reputational-crisis scenarios — the preparation that determines whether the Bank communicates in hours or days when an event occurs. Crisis scenarios in banking include: a data breach, a major service outage, a regulatory enforcement action, an employee fraud or misconduct case, and a viral social-media complaint. Supervisory guidance commonly expects regulated institutions to maintain documented crisis-communication plans.

## Conduct complaint signals {#conduct-complaint-signals}

Tracking complaint escalation patterns — volume trends, regulator-referred complaints, media-reported complaints, and complaint-outcome patterns — as leading indicators of reputational and conduct risk. A rising complaint volume in a specific product is commonly a conduct-supervision trigger before it becomes a reputational event. Early escalation of complaint signals to leadership enables pre-emptive regulatory engagement rather than reactive response.

### Product Complaint Concentration Analysis

- URN: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-market-intelligence/brand-reputation/conduct-complaint-signals/product-complaint-concentration-analysis
- Lens: Insights
- Complexity: S
- Intent: The AI agent analyzes complaint volume relative to active product book size by product category, identifying which products have disproportionate complaint concentration as a trigger for conduct-risk review.
- Problem to solve: Complaint volume by product is reported in absolute terms. Compliance and the CRO receive the concentration analysis monthly; products with elevated complaint rates are escalated for root-cause investigation ahead of regulator inquiry.
