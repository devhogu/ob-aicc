Open sections: none. The scope, measures, capacity rule, and baseline approval are settled below.

## 1. Hypothesis

For functions with small, repeatable needs in assurance, a Standing Initiative gives each request a recorded owner, priority, acceptance criteria, and outcome while keeping delivery within one Iteration. The intended benefit is prompt, controlled support without a separate Initiative Brief and Service Agreement for every request.

## 2. Business outcomes and leading indicators

The outcome is an accepted result for the requesting function within one Iteration. The initial baseline is zero admitted and zero accepted run-rate Features; no elapsed-time or percentage observation exists before the first delivery. The measures use the Program Backlog and the Iteration Backlog and are reviewed at the Iteration Review and Demo.

| Indicator | Definition | Initial baseline | Target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Delivery within the Iteration | Features admitted to an Iteration and accepted by its end, divided by all run-rate Features admitted to that Iteration | Zero admissions; the rate is not applicable | 100%; work that cannot fit is scoped as an ordinary Initiative |
| Acceptance evidence | Accepted Features with the delivered result, the criteria met, and the product owner's name and date recorded, divided by all accepted Features | Zero acceptances; the rate is not applicable | 100% |

Each Feature states the benefit sought for its function and its acceptance criteria. Any claim about time saved, reduced errors, adoption, or control effectiveness is supported by that Feature's evidence; no achieved benefit is assumed at establishment.

## 3. Scope and the minimum viable product

An assessment, criteria review, tiering record, or oversight follow-up may enter as run-rate work if it meets Business Model 4.7 and 4.8. The service category proposes the mode and the Competence Center Lead confirms it. Work that needs more than one Iteration is scoped as a separate Initiative. The Standing Initiative has no MVP of its own (Portfolio Management Model 5.5).

Each run-rate Feature sits directly under this Initiative in the Program Backlog, is admitted at the Weekly Review within the Team's limits, and records its client, data-use approval where AI is used, acceptance criteria, Dependencies, admission, delivery, and acceptance. The support level is on demand. AI Solution, validation, release, and production-change requirements still apply.

## 4. Cost and value

The Standing Initiatives share the existing the Competence Center Team capacity; no separate headcount, funding, or fixed share of capacity is reserved for a service area. The initial Team limit is one Feature in progress across ordinary and run-rate delivery combined, including a Feature in Review or Waiting. The Ready queue holds at most two Features. The same shared limit applies across lanes and Domains; an urgent request changes the order of work and does not add capacity. The boards record these limits under DR-2026-063.

At each Weekly Review the Competence Center Lead admits the highest-ranked eligible request that fits the remaining Iteration and the shared limit. If none fits, none is admitted. Each request states its expected benefit and acceptance criteria. Financial figures remain in their governed sources. The normal monthly and quarterly reviews may change the limits and work mix within the authority of the charter.

## 5. Risks, dependencies, and Risk Tier

The baseline approval and initial capacity rule are recorded in DR-2026-063; RI-008 is closed and DEP-015 is Met. Each request still requires available capacity within the shared limit, a named client and Domain Owner, and the approval for its purpose and data class where AI is used. The Risk Tier, provider assessment, independent check or validation, and production-change controls apply to each use as the charter requires.
