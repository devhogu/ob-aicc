## 1. Facts

- Until this Decision the pack named the product owner as the person who accepts a delivered outcome: the Domain Owner for an item of a Domain, and the Executive Sponsor for an item that spans Domains or is enabling work (DR-2026-019; Vocabulary 6.1; Solution Lifecycle Model 2.9, clause 7.3). The product owner was therefore the requester, who would have to take part in the Iteration Review and Demo to accept each Feature.
- The Operating Model 4.4(d) barred the AICC Lead from accepting a Solution that the AICC Lead built (DR-2026-042). The AICC Team has one member, who is the AICC Lead and the Solution Engineer (Teams Record; RI-007), so during development no one could accept a Feature of a Solution that the AICC Lead built.
- The head of AICC, who owns the way of working, decided how a Solution is accepted: the requester as business owner accepts the final working prototype against the acceptance criteria; during development the product owner or the product manager accepts; and, because the Team is small, the AICC Lead accepts the final before it is provided and deployed to the business owner for judgment.

## 2. Options

1. Keep the acceptance of every Feature with the requester, and keep the AICC Lead barred from accepting what the AICC Lead built.
2. Accept at two levels: the product owner during development, with the AICC Lead as the product owner while the Team is small, and the requester for the Solution, with the final acceptance of the Team by the AICC Lead before the Solution is deployed to the requester.

## 3. Decision

The AICC Lead takes the second option, and decides the following.

1. D1, the acceptance during development: the product owner accepts each Feature and each Capability against its acceptance criteria, at the Iteration Review and Demo. A product manager is the same person under another name. While the Team has up to three people the AICC Lead is the product owner of the Team, and later the AICC Lead may name another person in the Appointments Record. The product owner is a designation and not a Role.
2. D2, the final acceptance of the Team: before a Solution, whether its working prototype or its final version, is provided and deployed to the Domain Owner for judgment, the AICC Lead accepts the final for the Team. The Solution meets the acceptance criteria of its Solution Definition, its tests are referenced (Solution Lifecycle Model 7.1), and its check or validation is in place. It comes before the first deployment to the first users and before each significant change, and it is recorded in the release block of the Solution Definition with who and the date.
3. D3, the business acceptance: the requester, who is the Domain Owner, or the Executive Sponsor for an item that spans Domains, is enabling work, or is an Experiment with no Domain, judges the working Solution deployed to its first users against the acceptance criteria and accepts it, returns it, or rejects it. It is the acceptance of the Solution and, for an Engagement, of its Outcome Report. The first users are those whom the Domain Owner names for the judgment. The release beyond the first users stays a separate decision, by the Domain Owner for Risk Tier 1 and 2 and by the Executive Sponsor for Risk Tier 3 (Solution Lifecycle Model 7.1, 7.4).
4. D4, the segregation: the AICC Lead may give the acceptance of D1 and D2 for a Solution that the AICC Lead built. The AICC Lead shall not Check or validate it, shall not release it, and shall not give its business acceptance (Operating Model 4.4(d)). Where the AICC Lead is the Domain Owner, the Executive Sponsor gives the business acceptance and the release. This is an accepted limit while the Team is small. The compensating controls are the test by a person other than the builder, the check or the validation by another person, the business acceptance, the release decision, and the monthly sample of the Decisions of the AICC Lead by the Executive Sponsor. It is recorded as RI-033 and in the Appointments Record Part B.
5. D5, the words: the pack keeps Domain Owner, and the Vocabulary says that the Domain Owner is the requester who judges the work, and that business owner is a term not used. The Domain Owner no longer is defined as the product owner of its Solutions.

## 4. Conflicts and advice

Declared: the AICC Lead decides a rule that lets the AICC Lead give the acceptance of the Features and the final acceptance of the Team for a Solution that the AICC Lead built. The business acceptance, the check or the validation, and the release stay with others. The Executive Sponsor reviews the accepted limit with RI-033 at the quarterly Steering that ends 2026-PIQ4, and the monthly sample of the Decisions of the AICC Lead covers this Decision. No record exists of the agreement of the Executive Sponsor. The AICC Lead tells the Executive Sponsor of the change (Document Catalog 4.2).
