# Register (standard mode only)

## Internal

Status updates, internal email, exec memos, project updates: readers share context, and the job is to report what is true.

Cut hard. Delete these outright when they carry no fact beyond themselves:

- Phase or journey openers ("As we enter the next chapter of our growth").
- Unfalsifiable optimism or momentum about an unnamed future ("we remain bullish that these efforts will set us up for success", "momentum continues to build"). A forecast about a named event ("we expect the cutover to go smoothly") is a claim: keep it.
- Effort claims with no outcome ("the team has been going above and beyond").
- Generic closers and update offers ("Let me know if you have any questions", "More to come", "We look forward to keeping you posted").
- Greeting padding ("I hope you all had a great weekend"). A plain greeting that fits the channel ("Hi all,") may stay.

A whole paragraph may go when every sentence in it qualifies. An opener that also names the topic ("I wanted to share an update on the vendor review") is rewritten to keep the topic, not deleted.

Never delete a status, risk, owner, number, date, or real ask. A real ask names an action the reader should take ("approve the budget by Friday"); a generic invitation to get in touch is a closer, not an ask. A status that names nothing ("a number of priorities are being explored") stays, with a `Note:` asking the author to name them.

Sentences you keep still get plain wording: replace stock vocabulary ("pivotal", "instrumental", "key enabler") with a plain word, and mark hollow intensifiers in brackets ("[relentlessly]", "[truly]"), using the table and "Marked words" in `fidelity.md`. A magnitude word ("significant") stays with a `Note:`.

No personality or texture in this register: vary sentence length only where the content varies (`humanize-pro` "Do not humanize": status reports).

Keep qualifiers that carry real uncertainty, stated plainly (`../vendor/humanize-pro/references/channels.md` section 7).

## External

Proposals, marketing copy, landing pages, cold outreach: readers do not yet trust the writer, and the claims are the content.

Cut wording, not claims (`fidelity.md`, "Deletion by mode"). Openers that describe the world rather than the offer ("In a fast-changing market") are not claims and may go. When claims have no proof behind them, say so in a `Note:` line; do not delete the claim and do not invent the proof.

## Ambiguous

Treat as external. Internal text that leaks reads worse than external text that was slightly too careful (`channels.md` section 14).
