# Incident regulation: what to confirm with compliance

Research note of 2026-10-01 for the incident clauses of the AI Policy (section 5). It records what the sources say, so that the compliance function can confirm which requirements apply to the Bank. The Bank is in the Kyrgyz Republic. Whether any EU rule applies to it directly is not established here and is for compliance to decide. The timelines come from secondary sources and must be checked against the text of each regulation.

## DORA, Regulation (EU) 2022/2554

- Article 17: financial entities set up an ICT-related incident management process to detect, manage, and notify incidents. It logs every incident, finds and fixes root causes, classifies by priority, severity, and criticality, allocates roles, sets communication plans, and informs senior management and the management body of at least major incidents.
- Article 19: major incidents are reported to the competent authority in stages (initial notification, intermediate report, final report). Clients whose financial interests are affected are informed without undue delay. Reporting may be outsourced but the entity stays fully responsible.
- Timing is in Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/301 (secondary sources): initial notification within four hours of classifying the incident as major and no later than 24 hours from awareness; intermediate report within 72 hours of the initial notification; final report no later than one month after the latest intermediate report. Classification criteria are in Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/1772 and templates in Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/302.

## EU AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689

- Article 3(49): a serious incident is an incident or malfunctioning of an AI system that directly or indirectly leads to the death of a person or serious harm to health, a serious and irreversible disruption of critical infrastructure, the infringement of obligations under Union law protecting fundamental rights, or serious harm to property or the environment.
- Article 73: providers of high-risk AI systems report serious incidents to the market surveillance authority of the Member State where it occurred: no later than 15 days after the provider or, where applicable, the deployer becomes aware; two days for a widespread infringement or a critical-infrastructure disruption; ten days for a death. The article applies from 2 August 2026.
- Article 26(5): a deployer monitors the system, informs the provider immediately of a serious incident, then the importer or distributor and the authorities. A financial institution meets the monitoring duty by complying with its internal governance rules under financial services law. Article 26(6): logs are kept for at least six months, and a financial institution keeps them within its financial documentation. The source page gives the application dates of Article 26 as 2 December 2027 (Annex III systems) and 2 August 2028 (Annex I systems).

## GDPR, Regulation (EU) 2016/679

- Article 33: the controller notifies the supervisory authority of a personal data breach without undue delay and where feasible within 72 hours of becoming aware, unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to persons. A processor notifies the controller without undue delay. The controller documents every breach.

## Kyrgyz Republic

- The Law on Personal Data No. 58 of 14 April 2008 has no general breach-notification duty like the GDPR (secondary source). The National Bank of the Kyrgyz Republic may set the reporting of banks and request information. No NBKR rule on the reporting of information security incidents was found. This is for compliance to confirm.
