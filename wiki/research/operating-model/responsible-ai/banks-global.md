# How major global banks publish responsible AI principles

Research note for the AI Competence Center, O!Bank Kyrgyzstan. Compiled 30 September 2026 from public web sources.

Scope: 22 banks reviewed (JPMorgan Chase, HSBC, Citi, Goldman Sachs, Barclays, Lloyds, NatWest, BBVA, Santander, ING, UBS, BNP Paribas, Deutsche Bank, Standard Chartered, Wells Fargo, Bank of America, BNY, State Street, Morgan Stanley, CBA, RBC, TD). Only 11 have a public, named set of AI principles that I could confirm. The other banks describe an approach or a governance model, but I found no list.

Reading basis is marked per bank:
- **Read** means I read the bank's own page or document.
- **Summary** means I saw the point only in a search-result summary, not in the source itself.

## Banks with named principles

Principle names below are copied as the bank words them. Where I could confirm only a paraphrase, I say so.

### HSBC (July 2024, read in full)

Seven principles:
1. Act consistently with HSBC's values
2. Protect privacy
3. Start with a clearly defined purpose
4. Address unfair bias and decision-making
5. Be responsible for AI
6. Adapt governance to meet emerging needs
7. Contribute to development of best practice

- **Governance:** the document names no committee. It commits to "clear accountability for our AI systems", testing and monitoring "both before being deployed and on an ongoing basis", and escalation of concerns "to appropriate accountable executives". HSBC's AI page names a first Chief AI Officer, David Rice. It names no committee.
- **Distinctive:** "We do not use AI decision-making for a purpose that could not be justified if pursued through human decision-making." Third-party rule: HSBC applies "the same principles and governance mechanisms" to third-party products as to its own, and vets privacy and security before sharing personal data. It also commits to "meaningful forums for challenging and questioning design or use of AI" and to training staff on AI limitations as well as capabilities.

### NatWest Group (Code of Conduct PDF read in full)

Two sets of principles, described as a "north star".

Data Ethics principles (the bank works towards ensuring that it will):
1. Respect people, their aspirations and their privacy
2. Help customers make informed decisions
3. Work to prevent harm to individuals and benefit society
4. Make sure data we use is accessible and the way we use it is fair and prevents discrimination
5. Are transparent

AI principles (the bank works towards ensuring its AI systems):
1. Are subject to human oversight and promote human agency
2. Are technically robust, resilient, and safe to prevent unintentional harm
3. Comply with privacy and data protection laws throughout the model lifecycle
4. And decisions they make can be explained to customers in a way they will understand
5. Are free from bias and discrimination
6. Consider the social and environmental impacts
7. Provide an opportunity to contest the outcomes of our AI Systems and ways to get redress

- **Governance (Code):** an "Ethical Model Process" evaluates AI systems during design and development. There is a dedicated AI & Data Ethics team, supported by a panel of colleagues. Privacy Impact Assessments are run "for all AI Systems". Training is offered to all colleagues, and some become panel members.
- **Governance (May 2025 article, read):** every AI use case gets an "Ethical AI Impact Assessment". High-risk edge cases go to the "AI and Data Ethics Panel". Members are drawn from a trained pool and each represents a stakeholder group (customers, colleagues, communities, shareholders and investors, future generations). Head of Responsible AI is Dr Paul Dongha.
- **Distinctive:** it is the only bank in this set with a published right to contest and get redress. Colleagues can "review, override or intervene" in AI-powered processes. The code says it is aspirational, not legally binding.

### BBVA (page read; no date shown)

Eight principles, grouped as Analytics, Data, Technology and Global:
1. Transparency
2. Fairness
3. Precision and accuracy
4. Privacy
5. Data quality
6. Technological security
7. Responsibility and training
8. Social and environmental commitment

- **Governance:** the page describes three implementation areas: technology (an operating framework that builds the principles in by design), governance, and people (training and awareness). Governance is tied to the Group Code of Conduct and the EU AI Act, with monitoring indicators and a continuous-improvement cycle. The page names no committee or body. A centralised inventory of AI systems and continuous monitoring are reported in a search summary of BBVA's material, not on the page I read.
- **Distinctive:** human oversight and "literacy and training for everyone involved" sit inside a single principle (7). Sustainability is a full principle (8).

### Santander (page read)

Five principles:
1. Transparency
2. Fairness & bias mitigation
3. Accountability
4. Privacy, data protection & security
5. Contribution to value & well-being

- **Governance:** the Data and Artificial Intelligence Corporate Framework was approved by the Board of Directors. Oversight follows a three-lines-of-defence model, with independent review scaled to risk. Named owners are required across the AI lifecycle. The Chief Data & AI Officer is Ricardo Martin Manjon. Teams combine data, risk and compliance "from day one" under one global standard.
- **Distinctive:** the transparency principle says users must "always know when they are interacting with AI". Responsible AI training is mandatory for every employee working with these systems. Search summaries add a mandatory all-staff AI plan from 2026 with a Responsible AI module, and open-source governance and fairness tools from the Santander AI Lab. The page states no vendor rules.

### UBS (page read; policy detail from summary)

Three principles: "respect for human autonomy, prevention of harm, and a commitment to fairness and transparency". These rest on the risk-aware culture and the Code of Conduct and Ethics.

- **Governance:** a Group AI Policy sets standards. The AI Operating & Risk Committee is among dedicated AI governance bodies. A Chief AI Office sits inside the Group Chief Operating Office with "dedicated accountability for AI governance, regulatory engagement, and AI Capabilities enablement". Divisions own delivery and outcomes. A separate Group Data Ethics Policy (search summary of a 6-K filing) covers staff who process client or personal data through AI.
- **Distinctive:** "All employees receive mandatory annual training in the responsible use of AI." Managers stay "fully accountable" for the final assessment when AI summarises evaluations.

### Standard Chartered (interview read, 18 July 2025)

A "responsible AI standard" in place since 2021, described as principles-based. The interview names fairness, ethics, transparency and model performance, plus cyber security and data privacy risks. A search summary words the pillars as fairness, ethics, transparency and self-accountability. I did not read a published list.

- **Governance:** the Responsible AI Council, chaired by Group Chief Data Officer Mo Rahim, assesses each model against the principles and decides whether to approve it. It meets as needed. A separate data ethics framework exists.
- **Distinctive:** "the human is right, not the machine" and "the individual is accountable for the outcome". Protected data (for example gender, ethnicity, race, political opinions) is generally removed from models. The bank worked with the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) on the FEAT principles and the Veritas initiative. The article describes no vendor process.

### TD (press release read, 30 July 2026)

Seven commitments, not given as a numbered list. Areas named: laws that already apply to financial institutions, transparency and explainability, data use and privacy, fairness, quality and accountability, reliability, security. The grouping is my reading of compressed wording.

- **Governance:** principles are embedded in the AI Risk framework and policy. The AI Risk Management team oversees them. The Trustworthy AI team (inside the Layer 6 AI group) operationalises them. Supporting processes: model risk assessments, privacy impact assessments, independent oversight reviews. Each use case is assessed for explainability, fairness, performance and privacy before deployment, then monitored.
- **Distinctive:** the link to model risk assessment is explicit. Example controls for colleague-facing assistants: "retrieval grounding, human oversight and continuous monitoring". The principles are being added to employee education.

### RBC (page read)

Four principles on the RBC page: Privacy & Security; Accountability; Fairness; Transparency & Responsible Disclosure. A search summary refers to five principles in the 2023 ESG report; I could not reconcile four with five.

- **Governance:** the page gives no committee. A search summary of an investor engagement (Federated Hermes) reports board discussion of AI and human oversight of decisions. The Borealis AI "RESPECT AI" hub is a related research programme.
- **Distinctive:** transparency includes giving affected people enough information to understand the factors behind a decision.

### Commonwealth Bank of Australia (newsroom item read)

Six principles, per the Group AI Policy: environmental and social, fairness, transparency, privacy and data protection, reliability and security, accountability.

- **Governance:** "a dedicated governance forum to oversee AI risk framework development". Built on existing risk frameworks and the Code of Conduct. Toolkits and guardrails include "ongoing monitoring and annual reviews". In February 2026 CBA published "Our Approach to Adopting AI" (dated December 2025); I could not read it, because the PDF is encrypted.
- **Distinctive:** environmental and social impact is the first-listed principle. Search summaries of the 2024 AGM report a chair statement that AI should run on renewable energy and use water within Australia's water governance; I did not verify this against the AGM text. 27,600 employees had engaged with a learning series by end of 2025.

### Lloyds Banking Group (summary only)

Seven AI Ethics principles, as reported in search summaries of the Lloyds AI page: Trust and integrity; People-first development; Inclusive practices; Ethical innovation; Investing in expertise; Accountability and adaptive governance; Optimise for sustainability. The wording and order are from summaries and are not verified. The page returned an error each time I fetched it.

- **Governance (summary):** an AI Assurance Framework covering all AI systems, internal or external, across Plan, Develop and Live stages. A Data & AI Ethics Committee (DAIEC). A Risk Mitigation Template, a central repository of risk guardrails and a central registry of models. Alignment to the EU AI Act, UK AI assurance guidance and ISO 42001. Head of Responsible AI: Suzanne Brink.
- **Distinctive:** a four-persona AI Academy (colleagues, leaders, technical specialists, governance professionals) is reported. Governance tools for agentic AI (automated checks, pre-approved building blocks) are under preparation.

### BNP Paribas (summary only)

Five guiding principles for AI that is "ethical, reliable and rights-respecting". I could confirm only "Skills development" by name, and human oversight described as a fundamental principle: important decisions must be capable of being "understood, monitored and, where necessary, corrected". A sixth pillar on environmental footprint is being introduced.

- **Governance (summary):** a central Group AI Committee with four intermediate committees (IT, risk, value creation, HR). More than 800 AI use cases. Alignment to the EU AI Act and GDPR. About 80,000 employees trained on AI topics in 2025.
- **Distinctive:** at BNP Paribas Cardif, AI cannot automatically decline a claim.

## Common themes

Counted across the 11 banks with named principles (HSBC, NatWest, BBVA, Santander, UBS, Standard Chartered, TD, RBC, CBA, Lloyds, BNP). Counts use the principle names or headline wording; loose mappings are marked. BNP Paribas and Lloyds counts rest on summaries.

| Theme | Banks naming it | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Fairness, bias, non-discrimination | HSBC, NatWest, BBVA, Santander, UBS, StanChart, TD, RBC, CBA; Lloyds loosely ("Inclusive practices") | 10 of 11 |
| Transparency, explainability | HSBC (explainable, transparent about data), NatWest, BBVA, Santander, UBS, StanChart, TD, RBC, CBA, Lloyds ("Ethical innovation") | 10 of 11 |
| Accountability, named ownership | HSBC, BBVA, Santander, StanChart, TD, RBC, CBA, Lloyds | 8 of 11 |
| Privacy, data protection | HSBC, NatWest, BBVA, Santander, TD, RBC, CBA | 7 of 11 |
| Security, reliability, robustness | NatWest, BBVA, Santander, TD, CBA | 5 of 11 |
| Environment, sustainability | NatWest, BBVA, CBA, Lloyds, BNP (sixth pillar) | 5 of 11 |
| Human oversight or agency as a named principle | NatWest, BBVA, UBS ("human autonomy"), BNP | 4 of 11 |
| Skills, training, literacy as a named principle | HSBC, BBVA, Lloyds, BNP | 4 of 11 |
| Purpose, benefit to customers | HSBC, NatWest, Santander, Lloyds | 4 of 11 |
| Harm prevention | UBS, NatWest | 2 of 11 |
| Right to contest and redress | NatWest | 1 of 11 |

Observations:
- Fairness and transparency are near-universal, and they are the core of the FEAT and OECD-style vocabulary.
- Human oversight is a named principle at only four banks. At many others it appears in practice statements instead: Standard Chartered, TD, Barclays, Wells Fargo, Bank of America, Morgan Stanley, HSBC's AI page.
- Training appears at almost every bank as a practice. UBS, Santander and BNY make it mandatory (BNY per summary).
- Sustainability entered the lists recently and is mostly European or Australian.

## Distinctive positions

- **HSBC:** a "justifiable by a human" test. AI decision-making is not used for a purpose that would be indefensible if done by a person. It also applies its own principles to third-party products.
- **NatWest:** the only published right to contest an AI decision and get redress. A stakeholder-representing ethics panel, with members drawn from a trained pool.
- **UBS:** shortest set (three principles), anchored in autonomy, harm prevention and fairness. Mandatory annual training for all employees.
- **Santander:** users must always know they are interacting with AI. The framework is approved by the Board.
- **Standard Chartered:** protected attributes are generally removed from models. A council approves each model.
- **CBA:** environmental and social impact leads the list. It publishes an organisation-wide AI adoption report.
- **BBVA:** human oversight and literacy folded into one principle. Data quality is a separate principle.
- **TD:** principles are tied directly to model risk and privacy impact assessments, with independent oversight reviews.
- **Lloyds (summary):** assurance framework across Plan, Develop and Live with a central registry, aligned to ISO 42001.
- **BNP Paribas (summary):** auto-decline of a claim is barred at its insurance arm.
- **Morgan Stanley (summary):** second-line group validates every system before go-live.
- **Banks with no list:** JPMorgan, Citi, Goldman, BofA, Wells Fargo, State Street, Barclays. They lean on existing model risk and enterprise risk structures, and on executive statements about human accountability. This is a legitimate alternative to a principles document.

## Governance patterns

How principles become practice, where the sources show it:

1. **A named ethics or responsible-AI body.** Standard Chartered (Responsible AI Council, approves models), NatWest (AI & Data Ethics team and panel), Lloyds (Data & AI Ethics Committee, per summary), UBS (AI Operating & Risk Committee), BNP Paribas (Group AI Committee, per summary). Approval authority is explicit at Standard Chartered and NatWest; at others it is not stated.
2. **Link to model risk and enterprise risk.** TD (AI Risk framework, model risk assessments), Santander (three lines of defence), Goldman and Wells Fargo (board risk committees), Morgan Stanley (second-line validation, per summary), Citi (Technology Committee).
3. **Per-use-case impact assessment.** NatWest (Ethical AI Impact Assessment, Privacy Impact Assessment for all AI systems), TD (pre-deployment assessment of explainability, fairness, performance, privacy).
4. **Lifecycle assurance framework.** Lloyds (Plan, Develop, Live), HSBC (test and monitor before and after deployment), BBVA (design-stage controls and monitoring indicators).
5. **Registry or inventory.** Lloyds and BBVA (both from summaries). Neither is stated on the primary pages I read.
6. **Risk-proportionate review.** Santander (independent review scaled to risk), CBA (annual reviews). Formal risk tiers with named tier labels were not found in any source I read.
7. **Human-in-the-loop as a control.** NatWest (review, override, intervene), Standard Chartered (individual accountable), UBS (managers accountable for final assessment), Goldman (human review of assistant output, per summary), Morgan Stanley ("the humans press the button", per summary), Bank of America (2019 remark).
8. **Named senior owner.** HSBC (Chief AI Officer), UBS (Chief AI Office), Santander (Chief Data & AI Officer), Standard Chartered (Group Chief Data Officer chairs the council), NatWest (Head of Responsible AI).
9. **Training as a gate.** UBS (mandatory annual), Santander (mandatory for staff working with these systems), NatWest (accreditation programme, summary), BNY (before use of Eliza, summary), CBA (learning series).
10. **Third-party and vendor rules.** Explicit only at HSBC among the pages I read: same governance applied to third-party products, and vetting before sharing personal data. JPMorgan's supplier letter is about security; its AI-specific reading is a commentator's.
11. **External anchors.** EU AI Act (BBVA, BNP, Lloyds, NatWest indirectly), MAS FEAT and Veritas (Standard Chartered, Deutsche Bank), Australia's national AI ethics framework (CBA), ISO 42001 (Lloyds, summary).

## Confidence and gaps

**Higher confidence (read at source):** HSBC, NatWest, BBVA, Santander, UBS, TD. Principle names and governance descriptions for these are as published.

**Medium confidence:**
- **Standard Chartered:** I read an interview, not a published principles list. The four-pillar wording is from a search summary.
- **RBC:** the page shows four principles; a summary says five. Unresolved.
- **CBA:** the six principle names come from a newsroom item quoting the Group AI Policy. The full report was unreadable, so governance detail is thin. The AGM renewable-energy claim is from a news summary.
- **TD:** the seven commitments are my grouping of compressed wording.
- **BBVA and Santander:** no publication dates are shown on the pages. BBVA governance bodies and inventory are not described on the page.

**Lower confidence (search summaries only):**
- **Lloyds:** all seven principle names, the committee, the registry and the ISO 42001 alignment. The primary page returned an error on every fetch.
- **BNP Paribas:** four of the five principles are not confirmed by name. The committee structure and use-case counts are unverified. The primary page returned 403.
- **UBS policy names** (Group Data Ethics Policy) are from a summary of a 6-K filing.

**Not found:**
- No principles list found for JPMorgan Chase, Citi, Goldman Sachs, Barclays, Bank of America, Wells Fargo, State Street, Morgan Stanley, Deutsche Bank or ING. That does not prove none exist. BNY may have numbered principles behind its "Commitment to the Responsible, Ethical Use of Data and AI" link, which I did not read. Citi's 2020 principles claim is unverified.
