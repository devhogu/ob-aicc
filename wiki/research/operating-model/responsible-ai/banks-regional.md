# Responsible AI principles: Asian, Middle Eastern, CIS and emerging-market banks, and regional regulators

Research note for the AI Competence Center, O!Bank Kyrgyzstan. Research date: 2026-09-30.

- **[P]** a primary page (institution or regulator site) was opened, but the fetch tool returned a machine summary, not verbatim text.
- **[S]** read only through search-result summaries or secondary commentary (law firms, news, vendors). Treat as unverified until the primary text is opened.
- **[2026]** dated 2026 and not confirmed against a primary document.

## 3. Common themes

- **Principles are shared, wording differs.** Across banks and regulators the same core set appears: fairness and non-discrimination, transparency or explainability, accountability with board or senior-management ownership, human oversight, data protection and privacy, and safety, security or reliability. Regional additions: sustainability (RBI, UAE), societal benefit (SDAIA, Grab, Uzbekistan), "innovation" as a principle (RBI).
- **Regulators mostly issue guidance, not law, for banks.** MAS, HKMA, BNM, RBI, CBUAE and the Bank of Russia use principles, circulars or codes. Binding, AI-specific bank rules are rare (Qatar is the clearest; Kazakhstan has a general AI law).
- **Customer-facing safeguards recur.** Tell people they are dealing with AI; offer human review or an opt-out for AI decisions (HKMA, CBUAE, Bank of Russia, QCB, Kazakh law, Kyrgyz draft, Uzbek rules).
- **Accountability stays with the institution.** Board or senior-management accountability (MAS proposals, QCB, CBUAE, RBI). Kazakhstan's Banking Law and the Kyrgyz draft both reject "the algorithm did it" as an excuse.
- **Operating mechanisms named by banks.** Committees and taskforces (DBS, UOB, Sber), an AI register or inventory (DBS, QCB, MAS proposal, RBI), risk materiality tiers, mandatory staff training (DBS), red-team testing (Itaú).
- **Industry collaboration.** MAS Veritas and MindForge, Malaysian AICB framework, Russian Alliance in AI and the Bank of Russia best-practice compendium.
- **Voluntary principles turned into measurable tests.** Veritas (fairness metrics) and DBS PURE (a use-case gate) are the clearest examples.

## 5. Distinctive positions

- **DBS**: "should we" test (PURE) placed above "can we"; pairs principle with a use-case gate and mandatory training.
- **MAS**: moves from principles (FEAT 2018) through measurement tools (Veritas) to proposed supervisory guidelines covering oversight, inventory and capability. Proportionality is explicit.
- **RBI**: includes "Innovation over Restraint" as a principle and proposes shared infrastructure and indigenous models next to risk controls.
- **CBUAE**: consumer-protection framing; right to human review or explanation on request.
- **QCB**: most prescriptive found; prior regulator approval for high-risk AI and outsourcing.
- **SDAIA**: humanity and social or environmental benefit are named principles; enforceable duties come from data and cyber law.
- **Bank of Russia**: soft regulation by design, extension to unsupervised third parties, best-practice compendium developed with industry.
- **Sber**: earliest bank-level principles in the region (2021), governed through the ESG committee.
- **Kazakhstan**: statute with a technology-neutral financial regulator; bank liable for AI decisions.
- **Kyrgyz draft**: strong accountability line (no "hallucination" defence), language and historical-figure provisions, mandatory assessment only for state bodies and high-risk AI.
- **Uzbekistan**: eight-principle registered ethics rules with a sole-basis limit on AI for legally significant decisions.
- **Grab**: openly states a trade-off between individual and ecosystem outcomes.
