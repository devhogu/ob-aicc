# Responsible AI: standards, regulator and international-body frameworks banks align to

Gathered 2026-09-30 from web search and page fetches. Primary documents were read where the note says so under "Confidence and gaps". Everything else rests on search summaries. Nothing here is legal advice, and no Kyrgyz-specific legal review was done.

## Summary table

"Voluntary" means the framework does not bind a Kyrgyz bank by its own force. It can still bind through a home-country law, a contract, or a supervisor's expectations.

## Common themes

Counted across 11 frameworks where I could read a principle list or a summary of requirements: OECD, UNESCO, G7 Code, NIST AI RMF (with 600-1), ISO 42001/23894, EU AI Act, UK five principles, MAS (FEAT and proposed guidelines), HKMA, FSB Sound Practices 2026, EIOPA Opinion. "Partial" means the topic appears but not as a named principle or clear requirement. The mapping is my own judgement from names and summaries, so treat counts as approximate.

| Theme | Count (of 11) | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accountability and governance | 11 | All |
| Transparency and explainability | 11 | All (G7: public reporting of capabilities and limits) |
| Privacy and data governance | 10 | All except UK principles |
| Risk-based, proportionate treatment | 10 | All except UK principles |
| Fairness and non-discrimination | 9, plus 2 partial | Partial: G7 (data measures), FSB (bias named as a risk) |
| Robustness, safety and security | 9, plus 1 partial | Partial: MAS (testing and monitoring) |
| Human oversight | 8, plus 1 partial | Partial: ISO. Not named by G7 or UK principles |
| Testing and ongoing monitoring | 6 | G7, NIST (Measure), ISO, EU, MAS, FSB |
| Third-party and value-chain risk | 5, plus 2 partial | Named: NIST, ISO, EU, MAS, FSB. Partial: HKMA (board accountable for procurement), EIOPA (not checked) |
| Inventory and documentation | 4 | MAS (inventory), FSB (identification, documentation, inventory), EU (technical documentation), ISO (documented information) |
| Incident reporting or sharing | 3 verified | G7 (action 4), EU (Art. 73), FSB (information sharing). NIST and ISO likely, not verified |
| Contestability and redress | 3 | UK, HKMA (opt-out, human intervention), UNESCO (via Ethical Impact Assessment tool) |

Reading across the set: every framework asks for accountable owners, explanation, and proportionate risk treatment. Tiering by materiality is the common mechanism (EU high-risk, MAS materiality, FSB Practice 5, PRA risk classification, HKMA risk-based). Regulators in the UK, EU (EBA, EIOPA), Hong Kong and US say they apply existing law rather than write AI-specific rules, except the EU AI Act.

## What is binding versus voluntary for a Kyrgyz bank and a group with fintech entities

Known from search summaries, not from legal text:

- **Kyrgyz Digital Code.** Adopted by parliament 18 Jun 2025, signed by the President in Aug 2025, in force from about Feb 2026 (one interview source says it took effect in February). It includes AI provisions and personal data rules. Commentary says the AI rules are lighter than the EU's, lean on self-regulatory organisations, and set higher documentation requirements in areas such as healthcare and security. Whether any article imposes a duty on a bank's AI use is unknown to me.
- **Kyrgyz draft AI ethics regulation.** The Ministry of Digital Development published a draft regulation on the ethics of AI for public discussion on 28 Sep 2026 (single source, two days before this note). It is a draft.
- **NBKR.** I found no NBKR rule on how banks use AI. NBKR is building its own AI-enabled supervision (SupTech Concept and Roadmap 2026-2031, reported approved 16 Sep 2026, single source). Existing NBKR requirements on risk management, outsourcing, IT and information security apply to AI use by their general terms. I did not read them.
- **CIS Model Law on AI Technologies.** Kyrgyzstan took part in approving it. A model law is not binding until adopted nationally.

Binding only if a jurisdictional link exists (unknown for this group):

- **Kazakhstan Law No. 230-VIII "On Artificial Intelligence".** Signed 17 Nov 2025, in force 18 Jan 2026. Risk classes low, medium, high, autonomy levels, risk management for high-risk systems, bans on manipulative and social-scoring uses, synthetic content labelling, administrative liability. It applies to any group entity operating in Kazakhstan. Whether the group has such an entity is unknown. Kazakhstan's Digital Code is reported effective 11 Jul 2026 (single source).
- **EU AI Act.** Applies to non-EU deployers or providers when the AI output is used in the Union. Applies to the group only if an entity serves EU persons or feeds EU decisions. Unknown. If it applies, credit scoring of natural persons and life or health insurance pricing are high-risk from 2 Dec 2027; general Article 50 transparency from 2 Aug 2026.
- **GDPR and other data-protection laws** would follow the same jurisdictional test. Not analysed.
- **US, UK, Singapore, Hong Kong rules.** Bind only entities licensed or serving customers there. Nothing found linking the group to them.

Voluntary for the bank (no legal force in Kyrgyzstan on their own): OECD, G7, UNESCO, NIST, ISO 42001, 23894, 27001-adjacent standards, IIA, OWASP, FSB, BIS/FSI, IOSCO, FATF horizon scan, Basel, MAS, HKMA, PRA, SR 26-2, UK principles.

They still matter through:
- correspondent banks, card schemes, cloud and model vendors, and lenders or investors that ask for alignment (contract terms unknown);
- NBKR, which may adopt international practice (Basel standards are the usual route). No sign of this for AI yet;
- FSB practices and the Basel model-risk expectations, which NBKR may use as a reference in supervision. Unknown.

Unknowns to close: which jurisdictions each group entity is licensed in; whether any entity serves EU persons; whether NBKR has issued or plans AI, outsourcing or model-risk rules that reach the bank; contract clauses with vendors and financing parties; whether any fintech entity is a regulated lender, insurer or payment institution under a different regulator.

Timeline that matters most if the EU AI Act applies (Digital Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 Jul 2026): prohibited practices and AI literacy since 2 Feb 2025 (AI literacy softened to "support" rather than guarantee a level); general-purpose AI model obligations since 2 Aug 2025 (Commission enforcement powers from 2 Aug 2026; models on the market before 2 Aug 2025 have until 2 Aug 2027); Article 50 transparency from 2 Aug 2026 (watermarking for systems already on the market to 2 Dec 2026); Annex III high-risk from 2 Dec 2027 (was 2 Aug 2026); Annex I products from 2 Aug 2028; new prohibition on non-consensual intimate imagery and child sexual abuse material from 2 Dec 2026; national sandboxes by 2 Aug 2027. Penalty ceilings (Art. 99): EUR 35m or 7% for prohibited practices; EUR 15m or 3% for most operator duties; EUR 7.5m or 1% for incorrect information.

## Requirements that would need a mechanism in an operating model

Where several frameworks converge, the operating model needs a named mechanism. The mechanism itself is a design choice and is not prescribed by the sources.

| Requirement | Sources that ask for it | What the mechanism must do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Human oversight | EU Art. 14 and 26 (people with competence, training, authority); UNESCO principle "Human oversight and determination"; FSB Practice 10 (oversight scaled to materiality, risk, autonomy, complexity, explainability); MAS lifecycle controls; HKMA (human involvement, opt-out or human intervention for customers) | Decide per use case what level of oversight applies and who is the accountable person; give overseers authority to stop or override; guard against automation bias (named by FSB). |
| Inventory and registry | MAS (current AI inventory); FSB Practice 3 (identification, use case and model inventory, third-party registry); EU (Art. 49 registration applies to public bodies and providers; no deployer registry duty for private banks found) | One register of every AI use case, model, vendor and version, with owner, tier and status, kept current. |
| Risk tiering and materiality assessment | FSB Practice 5 (at inception and thereafter); MAS (impact, complexity, reliance); PRA SS1/23 (model risk classification); EU (high-risk classification, Art. 6 and Annex III); EIOPA (proportionate) | One intake assessment that assigns a tier, with a check against EU Annex III and Kazakhstan high-risk criteria where relevant. Tier drives every other control. Reassess on change. |
| Impact assessment | EU Art. 27 (fundamental-rights impact assessment for credit and life or health insurance deployers); ISO 42001 and ISO/IEC 42005; UNESCO Ethical Impact Assessment | A documented assessment of effects on affected people before high-tier launch. |
| Documentation | EU Arts. 11 and 13 (providers), Art. 26 use per instructions; FSB Practice 3; ISO 42001 documented information; SS1/23 | Standard record per use case: purpose, data, design, testing evidence, limits, owner, approvals. Vendor-supplied documentation collected for bought systems. |
| Logging and record-keeping | EU Art. 26 (keep logs at least six months, financial institutions within their financial-law documentation); FSB (enhanced documentation and logging for generative AI); EIOPA (record-keeping) | Retention of inputs, outputs, overrides and decisions sufficient to reconstruct a decision; retention period set against other law (data protection). |
| Testing and performance monitoring | FSB Practice 9; NIST Measure; G7 action 1 (internal and independent external testing, red-teaming); SS1/23 independent validation; SR 26-2 validation and ongoing monitoring; MAS evaluation and testing | Pre-release testing and independent validation scaled to tier; drift and performance monitoring; revalidation triggers. Generative and agentic AI are outside SR 26-2 scope, so the bank sets its own standard. |
| Security and AI-specific cyber | FSB Practice 11; OWASP LLM Top 10 and Agentic Top 10; ISO 27001 with 27090; NIST 600-1 Information Security; HKMA cyber circular (29 May 2026); G7 action 6 | Add prompt injection, data poisoning, excessive agency, tool and identity abuse to the security review and testing of every generative or agentic use case. |
| Third-party and vendor AI | FSB Practice 12 (performance, transparency, data quality, supply chain, concentration, continuity); SR 26-2 vendor products; MAS third-party risk; NIST Value chain; ECB (concentration in a few non-EU providers); EU Art. 25 value chain | Due diligence and contract terms for model and AI providers; exit and fallback plan; concentration view across the group; vendor model validation. |
| Incident reporting and information sharing | EU Art. 73 (serious incidents: as early as possible, at most 15 days, 10 for death, 2 for widespread infringement) and Art. 26; G7 action 4; FSB (information sharing on AI incidents) | Definition of an AI incident, a route to report internally, and a route to notify regulators, vendors and, where required, customers. Which NBKR or other reporting duties apply is unknown. |
| Customer transparency and contestability | EU Art. 26 (inform persons subject to high-risk decisions), Art. 50; UK Consumer Duty consumer understanding and support; UK principle Contestability and redress; HKMA disclosure and human intervention; ECOA specific reasons for adverse action | Disclosure when a customer is dealing with AI; a way to ask for a human; specific reasons for adverse credit decisions from models that are hard to explain. |
| Fairness testing | OECD, UNESCO, NIST 600-1 Harmful bias, MAS Veritas methodology, EIOPA, HKMA | Bias testing before release and in monitoring for credit, pricing and marketing use cases; note US 2026 rule change to disparate impact does not apply outside the US. |
| AI literacy and capability | EU Art. 4 (softened Omnibus wording); UNESCO Awareness and literacy; MAS capabilities and capacity; FSB Practice 4 | Training for staff by role; skills for overseers and validators. |
| Independent challenge and audit | SS1/23 independent validation; IIA framework; ECB (independent challenge) ; FSB Practice 2 | A function separate from the builders that validates high-tier systems and audits the process. |
| Board oversight and risk appetite | FSB Practice 1; MAS (boards and senior management lead governance); ESMA (management body responsible); HKMA (board accountable, including procurement) | Board-level statement of AI risk appetite and periodic reporting on the register and incidents. |
