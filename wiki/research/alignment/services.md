## 2. Contradictions

### 2.1 AICC Charter
- X1. `about/what-we-do.md` 2.1 cites Charter 3.2 for "not a platform team, not a Control Function, does not deliver at scale". Charter 3.2 lists five limits (AI Platform, business results, Control Function rules, own validation, reserved matters); none of the three claims is in it.
- X2. `authored.json` services_page.not: same misattribution.
- X3. `services/platforms.md`: "AICC builds and runs these as its platforms: the knowledge-base kit, the workflow runtime…" sits badly with Charter 3.2 ("shall not own or operate the AI Platform"), the Vocabulary "AI Platform", and SoI 7.4 ("Execution is carried out by the Domains"). The corpus does not separate an AICC platform from the AI Platform.

### 2.2 Business Model
- X4. `how-we-work.md` 2.1: run-rate work "taken in at the Weekly Review… within the Limits on Work in Progress of the Team" conflicts with BM 3.1 ("An Engagement is an Initiative that has a client function"), BM 7.1–7.2 (intake needs a Strategic Priority fit, a Domain Owner, room under the limit on Active Initiatives), BM 5.1 (Service Agreement issued when the study starts), and the Vocabulary "Initiative". Run-rate is then either an Initiative that breaks the BM 7 controls, or not an Engagement at all.
- X5. `areas/*.md` 2.1 screens on problem, size, Risk Tier, existing package; BM 7.2 and PMM Reviewing require Strategic Priority fit, Domain Owner, room under the limit. The site leaves out the binding criteria.
- X6. `what-we-do.md` §1: "Every Engagement leaves a reusable package"; BM 4.4 only "keeps the lessons and the reusable assets".
- X7. `how-we-work.md` Fig. 1 places the Service Agreement after "Gate: approval"; BM 5.1 and workflow Fig. 1 issue it when the study starts and amend it at approval. `how-to-engage.md` Step 2 is correct.

### 2.3 Solution Lifecycle Model
- X8. `catalog-form.md` 4.1: a Solution entry goes "from Proposed through Discovery, Approved, Active, Review, Accepted to Closed"; SLM 5.1 and 8.1: a Solution is Active from the start of delivery to the end of its life; acceptance sits in the release block (7.3(c)).
- X9. `service-model.md` 2.1: seven states; SLM 8.1 gives the Service Stages as Operate, Evolve, Retire; Vocabulary 4.2–4.3 define "Active" and "Closed" otherwise.
- X10. `service-model.md` 3.2: four signals incl. cost; SLM 8.4: "the monitoring, the incidents, the use, and the notices of the providers". Cost absent from the corpus; provider notices absent from the site.
- X11. `platforms.md` 4.1: a package becomes a Service with a "second consumer"; SLM 8.1: "A Product with many consumers or recurring requests becomes a Service through a business case".
- X12. `platforms.md` 4.1: Service hand-off with a Proposal; SLM 8.1 Service End: "Retired, or cancelled". The Proposal covers only Experiment adoption and the strategy.
- X13. `how-we-work.md` 3.3: Experiment ends in "promote, hand over, or shelve"; SLM 8.1: "accepted with its lessons and closed, a Proposal is made, or it is rejected".
- X14. `oversight.md` 4.1: "the Steering reads the whole each month and each quarter"; SLM 8.2 and C-20: quarterly.
- X15. `service-model.md` 1.1 Owner: "the Solution Engineer for the build and the run"; SLM 8.4: holds only for a Service AICC runs.
- X16. `service-model.md` 1.1 Risk Tier: "Assigned by the AICC Lead"; SLM 5.2 and AI Policy 3.2 add the Executive Sponsor for a Solution the AICC Lead built.
