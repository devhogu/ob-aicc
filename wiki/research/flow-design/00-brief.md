# Research brief: a light portfolio-to-team process for a small AI unit in a bank

## Who needs this
The AI Competence Center (AICC) of O!Bank Kyrgyzstan: an internal consulting and innovation unit that proves and delivers AI solutions for the functions of a bank. The unit is small (up to about three people now, a joint team with people assigned by the functions later). It works in a regulated environment: AI risk tiers, model-risk and information-security clearances, independent testing, data-class approval, audit read access.

## What we run today (so you can judge fit)
- Four levels: Discover (a catalog of scenarios, then a Funnel of needs) -> Portfolio (Initiatives on a Kanban: Funnel, Reviewing, Analyzing, Portfolio Backlog, MVP, Implementation, Done) -> Program (Capabilities as Epics, Features, on a Kanban, monthly Iterations, quarterly Program Increments) -> Team (Stories, tasks, bugs in Jira; daily).
- Decisions today: Intake, Scoped, Approval (a six-section Initiative Brief approved by a Domain Owner or the Executive Sponsor), Pull, Decision after the MVP (continue, pivot, defer, reject), Acceptance. Three recurring Steering forums (monthly, quarterly, yearly), WSJF ranking, WIP limits, strategic priorities with investment envelopes and guardrails.
- Two modes of work: run-rate work (a small repeatable request done within one Iteration, a Feature under a standing Initiative) and an Initiative (anything larger).
- Live work is tracked in Jira; our own site is a thin projection of it plus the static theory. We want one process description ("a project profile") per kind of work and one live "project card" per real project.

## The problem
We suspect our process has more gates, forums and approval layers than SAFe-style lean agile needs for a unit this size. We want to keep what is genuinely best from many disciplines and drop the rest, and describe a light protocol for each level (Discover, Portfolio, Program, Team), including how decisions are taken, by whom, in which forum, with which artefacts kept.

## What to return (plain text, no files; at most 700 words; be concrete)
1. The 5-8 best-fit practices from your body of knowledge, each in one or two sentences, for which LEVEL they fit (Discover / Portfolio / Program / Team / decision-making / controls) and why they suit a very small unit.
2. Decision rights and forums: who decides what, on what evidence, how often, and what is decided by a pre-agreed rule instead of a meeting.
3. Artefacts worth tracking and keeping at each level (and which are paperwork to drop).
4. What to avoid or discard for our size, and why.
5. Any point that matters in a regulated bank.
6. Sources: URL, publisher, year. Prefer public pages that need no login (official guides, standards bodies' summaries, reputable practitioner write-ups, books' public summaries). Mark any claim that comes only from your memory as "unverified". Quote at most 15 words from any source.
