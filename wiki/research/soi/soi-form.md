# Statement of intent: what it should contain

The form of the [AI adoption statement of intent](../../../charter/documents/statement-of-intent.md) and where it comes from. The
charter document carries no references. Gathered 2026-09-30.

## Finding

No published "statement of intent" on AI adoption by a bank or fintech was found. The closest published documents are:

- the Bank of England's internal [AI strategy](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/about/governance-and-funding/staff-codes-and-policies/the-banks-ai-strategy)
  (August 2025), a central-bank document about its own use of AI;
- bank AI principles pages, covered in the [responsible AI alignment](../operating-model/responsible-ai/README.md);
- AI policy and governance templates for financial institutions.

The general form of a statement of intent comes from public-sector accountability practice. The AI-specific content comes
from ISO/IEC 42001 and from AI policy practice.

## Three sources of form

### 1. Statement of intent in public accountability practice

In New Zealand, the Crown Entities Act requires a statement of intent from each Crown entity. Treasury guidance lists what
it must contain (section 141):

| Required element | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Period covered | The period the statement relates to, with start and end dates |
| Strategic objectives | What the entity intends to achieve or contribute to |
| Nature and scope | The nature and scope of its functions and intended operations |
| Management of functions and operations | How it intends to manage them to meet its strategic intentions |
| Organizational health and capability | How it proposes to manage its organization and capability |
| Assessing performance | How it proposes to assess its performance |
| Acquisitions | The process for acquiring interests in other entities |
| Other matters | Anything else needed to understand its intentions and capability |

Its stated purpose is public accountability: it sets out intentions in advance and provides a base against which
performance is later assessed. Source: [Treasury guidance](https://www.treasury.govt.nz/publications/guide/crown-entities-act-statement-intent-guidance)
(PDF text read: [April 2024 edition](https://www.treasury.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2024-04/cea-soi-apr24.pdf)).

### 2. AI policy under ISO/IEC 42001

Clause 5.2 asks top management to establish an AI policy that is appropriate to the organization's purpose, provides a
framework for setting AI objectives, includes a commitment to satisfy applicable requirements, and includes a commitment
to continual improvement. The policy is documented, communicated within the organization, and available to interested
parties as appropriate. Source: [ISO/IEC 42001](https://www.iso.org/standard/42001) and summaries such as
[Advisera](https://advisera.com/articles/iso-42001-clauses-requirements/). The standard's text is paywalled and this
reading is from summaries.

### 3. AI strategy and AI policy practice

- The Bank of England strategy has an executive summary, a definition of AI, how AI can help, AI principles, mission and
  vision, goals and priorities, skills and partnerships, annual review, and next steps. Its later sections were not
  visible.
- AI policy templates for financial institutions list purpose, scope and authority; guiding principles; governance, roles
  and decision rights; risk appetite and tolerance; lifecycle and model risk; controls and operations; standards;
  roadmap and maturity; and approval and review. Board approval gives the strongest mandate, and review is at least
  annual and on material change ([Pertama Partners](https://www.pertamapartners.com/insights/ai-governance-charter-template-documenting-your-approach),
  [MAIN](https://mainms.org/ai-policy-guidance-template-finance/),
  [AegisAI](https://www.aegisaicompliance.com/resources/ai-policy-template-financial-institutions),
  [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/programs/center-for-board-effectiveness/articles/board-of-directors-governance-framework-artificial-intelligence.html)).
  These are vendor and consultancy sources.

## Resulting structure

| Section of the statement | Source of the element |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Purpose and scope | Public-sector form (nature and scope); policy templates (purpose, scope, authority) |
| 2 Summary of intent | Bank of England executive summary |
| 3 Alignment with the Group's vision and strategy | Policy templates (vision, mission, strategic alignment); ISO 42001 (appropriate to purpose) |
| 4 Values, 5 Principles of adoption, 6 Principles of application | Bank of England principles; policy templates (guiding principles); the [responsible AI alignment](../operating-model/responsible-ai/README.md) |
| 7 Governance and accountability | Policy templates (governance, decision rights, risk appetite); public-sector form (management of functions) |
| 8 Areas of application, 9 Strategic priorities | Public-sector form (strategic objectives, nature and scope); ISO 42001 (framework for AI objectives); Bank of England goals and priorities |
| 10 Capability and enablers | Public-sector form (organizational health and capability); Bank of England skills and partnerships |
| 11 Maturity roadmap | Policy templates (roadmap and maturity); [priorities basis](priorities-basis.md) |
| 12 Performance assessment and reporting | Public-sector form (assessing performance) |
| 13 Commitments, review, and approval | ISO 42001 (commitments to requirements and to continual improvement; documented and communicated); policy templates (approval and review) |

The public-sector "acquisitions" element is not relevant and was left out.

## Style

Formal corporate statement: defined terms, numbered clauses, commitments stated with "shall", present tense for
description, third person, no first-person plural. The Bank of England text is in the first person plural and is written
for staff and the public. This statement is written for the Board, regulators, investors, and auditors, so it uses the
third person.

## Confidence and gaps

- The New Zealand element list was extracted from the Treasury PDF, and its wording is summarized here.
- The ISO 42001 reading is from secondary summaries. Confirm against the standard.
- The Bank of England page was only partly visible.
- The section order is our own composition of these three forms.
