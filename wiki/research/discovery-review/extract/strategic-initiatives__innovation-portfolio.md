Idea sourcing & stage-gate
The structured process for capturing, evaluating, and progressing innovation ideas from initial submission through stage-gate reviews to approved experimentation. Stage-gate discipline — with defined criteria at each gate (commercial potential, feasibility, strategic fit, resource availability) — prevents premature capital commitment to unvalidated ideas. Stage-gate reviews consume significant innovation board and sponsor time; the quality of review materials determines the quality of the go/no-go decision.

Scaling decision
The investment and operational decision to take a validated MVP into full production — specifying the investment required, the expected P&L trajectory at different adoption curves, the operational risk of the scaling approach, and the milestone-based funding structure. Scaling decisions are the highest-stakes investment decisions in the innovation portfolio; under-resourcing a scaling innovation destroys the value demonstrated in the MVP. The decision requires modeling adoption curve uncertainty and comparing the scaling option against alternative investment uses.

MVP design & hypothesis testing
Design and execution of minimum viable products and the structured experiments that test the core commercial and technical hypotheses underlying each innovation. Hypothesis-driven experimentation discipline — specifying the hypothesis, metric, test design, and decision threshold before running the experiment — is the primary quality control mechanism in the pipeline. Weak hypothesis design is the leading cause of inconclusive experiment results that consume resources without generating clear go/no-go signals.

Portfolio rebalancing
Ongoing optimization of the innovation portfolio composition — balancing early-stage experimentation, mid-stage MVP development, and late-stage scaling — against available investment capital and strategic priority alignment. Portfolio rebalancing requires continuous visibility into the pipeline value at each stage, the expected capital requirement of scaling candidates, and the strategic contribution of current experiments to declared innovation themes. Without rebalancing, portfolios cluster at early stages (low conversion to scale) or at late stages (insufficient pipeline replenishment).
