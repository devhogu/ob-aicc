Net promoter & brand equity tracking
Tracking NPS, brand-recall, and brand-equity scores over time — the structured measurement of how customers perceive and recommend the bank. Brand equity is a composite measure of awareness, association, loyalty, and differentiation; NPS is the single-metric proxy most commonly used in financial-services board reporting. Under CBR consumer-protection conduct guidance, NPS trend is a monitored indicator for retail banks above a regulatory threshold; declining NPS is a supervisory conversation trigger.

Reputational risk early warning
A composite early-warning signal for reputational risk — combining social-media sentiment trends, complaint-escalation patterns, regulatory communication volume, and media-tone shifts into a single leading indicator for the board and senior leadership. Reputational risk is distinct from the underlying event that causes it: the early-warning signal is not the event itself, but the pattern of signals that precede material reputational damage. Under CBR and NBK supervisory frameworks, reputational risk is formally classified, and the board is expected to oversee a systematic reputational-risk monitoring process.

Continuous monitoring of brand mentions, sentiment scores, and narrative themes across social-media platforms and news sources — the real-time signal channel for brand perception. Social and media sentiment leads NPS: a viral negative event on social media typically precedes a survey-measured NPS decline by four to six weeks. In CIS markets, Telegram and VKontakte are the primary social channels for banking discussion; monitoring these platforms requires local-language NLP capabilities.

A rising complaint volume in a specific product is a conduct-supervision trigger under NBK and CBR frameworks before it becomes a reputational event. ### CARD 13 [Insights|S] Product Complaint Concentration Analysis
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-market-intelligence/brand-reputation/conduct-complaint-signals/product-complaint-concentration-analysis
intent: Agent analyses complaint volume relative to active product book size by product category, identifying which products have disproportionate complaint concentration as a conduct-risk supervision trigger.
Problem to solve: Complaint volume by product is reported in absolute terms. A product with 150 complaints from a 50,000-customer base has a materially different conduct-risk profile from a product with 150 complaints from a 5,000-customer base, but both appear equally in the volume table. Under NBK and ARDFM supervision, complaint rate relative to book size is the metric that triggers conduct-risk escalation, not absolute complaint count.
Solution: Agent reads complaint volumes by product category and active-product-holder counts monthly, computes complaint rate per thousand active customers by product, and flags products above a threshold complaint rate for conduct-risk review. Compliance and the CRO receive the concentration analysis monthly; products with elevated complaint rates are escalated for root-cause investigation ahead of regulator enquiry.
