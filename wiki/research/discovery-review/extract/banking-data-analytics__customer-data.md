Customer identity & KYC data
The legal identity record for each customer — covering government-issued identifiers, beneficial ownership structure for legal entities, sanctions screening status, and KYC risk classification under NBKR AML/CFT Regulation No. 2 and CBR Regulation No. 375-P. KYC data is the compliance foundation for every customer relationship; its currency and accuracy determine the bank's regulatory exposure. KYC refresh cycles — annual for high-risk customers, periodic for standard-risk — generate a recurring data-assembly workload for the compliance team.

intent: Agent assembles the KYC refresh pack for each customer approaching their periodic review date, pulling current identity records, screening results, and relationship activity into a structured dossier for the compliance analyst to review and sign off. The compliance analyst reviews the dossier, applies professional judgment, and signs off on the KYC record.
OKR objective: Compliance analysts conduct KYC periodic reviews from agent-assembled dossiers — current identity records, screening results, and relationship activity — with pre-populated risk indicators and documentation gap flags, directing analyst capacity toward judgment rather than data assembly.

Consent & privacy records
The formal records of customer permissions for data processing, marketing communications, third-party data sharing, and product-specific terms — governed under Kazakhstan's Law No. 94-V on Personal Data and its Protection and Russia's Federal Law No. 152-FZ. Each consent record specifies the processing purpose, the legal basis, and the expiry or withdrawal status. Consent coverage determines the addressable population for data-driven engagement; gaps between the consented and engaged populations create regulatory exposure under data protection enforcement frameworks.

Customer 360 profile
The integrated customer view assembled across all source systems — KYC and identity, product holdings and balances, transaction behaviour, interaction history, and service records — that provides any customer-facing function with a complete picture of the customer at the point of engagement. The customer 360 is not a system but an assembly pattern: pulling the current state from each source on demand. Its utility depends on the completeness and currency of the underlying source data and the speed at which the integrated view can be retrieved.

### CARD 15 [Automation|S] Behavioural Scoring Refresh Trigger
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:banking-data-analytics/customer-data/segmentation-behavioural-data/behavioural-scoring-refresh-trigger
intent: Agent monitors the behavioural feature inputs to credit scoring and product-targeting models and triggers a selective model score refresh when an individual customer's feature values have moved materially since the last full model run. The selective refresh maintains score currency for the highest-movement customers between full model calibration cycles, without the compute cost of a full portfolio re-score. The credit and analytics teams define materiality thresholds by model type.
Problem to solve: Credit and product-targeting scores are regenerated on a scheduled cycle — monthly or quarterly — regardless of whether individual customers' behavioural features have changed materially since the last run. Customers whose transaction behaviour or product usage has shifted significantly carry a stale score until the next full cycle. For credit limit decisions and real-time product offers, a stale score may misrepresent the customer's current risk or propensity profile. A selective refresh mechanism requires a trigger signal to identify which customers warrant an out-of-cycle score update.
Solution: Agent tracks the behavioural feature values — transaction frequency, average balance, payment regularity, product usage intensity — for each customer against the values recorded at the last model score run. When feature movement for a customer exceeds the materiality threshold defined for a given model, the agent generates a selective refresh trigger for that customer and model combination. The credit and analytics model execution teams process the selective refresh queue on a weekly basis. The CDO and model risk teams review trigger volumes to calibrate materiality thresholds.
OKR objective: Credit and product-targeting scores remain current for the highest-movement customers between full model cycles through a structured selective refresh triggered by material feature movement.
OKR KR [Adoption]: Agent monitors feature movement for 100% of active customers against all in-scope models for ≥ 48 weeks within 12 months; materiality thresholds defined and approved by credit and analytics within 60 days of go-live.
OKR KR [Acceptance]: ≥ 75% of selective refresh triggers confirmed as yielding a materially different score on re-run; false-positive trigger rate ≤ 15% within 3 months of go-live.
OKR KR [Cycle]: Selective refresh queue processed on a weekly cadence; score staleness for the highest-movement decile of customers reduced from full model cycle gap (30-90 days) to ≤ 7 days within 6 months.
