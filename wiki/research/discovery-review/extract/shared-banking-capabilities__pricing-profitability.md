Pricing experiment analytics
Synthesis of A/B pricing experiment results across products and segments — measuring conversion impact, margin effect, competitor response, and customer-segment differential — to inform pricing decisions. Pricing experiments across rate levels, fee structures, and bundled-product offers generate structured data that requires statistical testing, segment decomposition, and management narrative before the result can drive a pricing decision. Current experiment-to-decision cycles take four to six weeks from data availability to management sign-off.

### CARD 1 [Enablement|S] Pricing experiment design review
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:shared-banking-capabilities/pricing-profitability/pricing-experiment-analytics/pricing-experiment-design-review
intent: Pre-launch review of proposed pricing experiment designs — evaluating sample size adequacy, segment stratification, control group specification, and measurement period against the proposed learning objective — with a structured design assessment and any recommended adjustments. Design review before launch reduces the rate of underpowered experiments that produce inconclusive results, enabling the pricing team to commit experiment capacity to designs with sufficient statistical power.
Problem to solve: Pricing experiments that are underpowered — insufficient sample size, inadequate control group, or measurement periods that do not capture the full seasonal effect — produce inconclusive results that cannot support a pricing decision, wasting the experiment window and the pricing team's bandwidth. Experiment design review is informal; the statistical adequacy assessment relies on the analyst's individual knowledge rather than a systematic pre-launch check against the experiment's learning objective.
Solution: Agent reads the proposed experiment design — learning objective, pricing treatment, segment, sample size, control group specification, and measurement period — and produces a design assessment covering statistical power, confound risk, and any recommended parameter adjustments before the experiment launches. Pricing team reviews the design assessment, implements the recommended adjustments, and launches the revised experiment with sufficient statistical power for the learning objective.
OKR objective: Pricing experiment designs are reviewed for statistical adequacy before launch so experiments are powered to produce actionable results.
OKR KR [Adoption]: Pre-launch design review used for ≥90% of new pricing experiments within 6 months of go-live.
OKR KR [Acceptance]: Inconclusive experiment rate — experiments producing results below statistical significance — reduced by ≥30% within 18 months of adoption; pricing team confirms design review materially improved experiment design in ≥80% of review sessions.
OKR KR [Cycle]: Design assessment available within 4 hours of design specification submission so experiment refinement and launch can occur within the same planning sprint.

### CARD 2 [Automation|M] Pricing experiment result synthesis
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:shared-banking-capabilities/pricing-profitability/pricing-experiment-analytics/pricing-experiment-result-synthesis
intent: Automated synthesis of A/B pricing experiment results — measuring conversion impact, margin effect, competitor response, and customer segment differential — into a management-ready decision brief with the statistical significance assessment, the pricing recommendation, and the implementation specification. The synthesis step reduces the experiment-to-decision cycle by eliminating the analyst assembly time between data availability and management sign-off.
Problem to solve: Pricing experiments across rate levels, fee structures, and bundled-product offers generate structured data that requires statistical testing, segment decomposition, and management narrative before the result can drive a pricing decision. Current experiment-to-decision cycles take four to six weeks from data availability to management sign-off; slow decision cycles reduce the commercial benefit of pricing experimentation and compress the window for implementing pricing advantage before competitors respond.
Solution: Agent reads the pricing experiment outcome data, applies the prescribed statistical tests — conversion rate significance, margin effect, and segment differential — and drafts the decision brief with the test results, the pricing recommendation, and the product and segment implementation specification. Product pricing management reviews the brief and approves the pricing recommendation, with the statistical evidence and implementation specification available for Finance and Risk sign-off.
OKR objective: Pricing experiment result synthesis and management decision briefs are produced from experiment outcome data within 5 business days of data availability.
OKR KR [Adoption]: Agent-produced result briefs used for ≥80% of pricing experiments within 9 months of go-live.
OKR KR [Acceptance]: Experiment-to-decision cycle reduced from 4 to 6 weeks to ≤2 weeks within 12 months; management sign-off rate on agent-produced briefs ≥85% without additional analysis commission.
OKR KR [Cycle]: Decision brief available within 5 business days of data availability so pricing decisions can be implemented within the same product review cycle as the experiment conclusion.

### CARD 3 [Insights|M] Pricing competitive response signal
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:shared-banking-capabilities/pricing-profitability/pricing-experiment-analytics/pricing-competitive-response-signal
intent: Continuous monitoring of competitor product pricing — deposit rates, lending rates, and fee structures — to identify pricing moves that affect the bank's conversion and retention rate on actively running experiments and the bank's competitive positioning on in-market products. Competitor pricing changes that materially affect experiment conditions are flagged to the pricing team, enabling a decision on whether the experiment should be paused, extended, or its interpretation adjusted.
Problem to solve: Pricing experiments are designed against a competitive context that is current at launch; competitor pricing changes during the experiment window affect conversion rates in both the treatment and control groups, confounding the experiment's ability to isolate the treatment effect. Without systematic competitor pricing monitoring, experiment results affected by in-period competitor moves are not distinguished from results driven by the pricing treatment itself, leading to potentially incorrect pricing recommendations.
Solution: Agent monitors competitor product pricing feeds for the bank's key product markets — deposit and lending rate publications, fee structure disclosures, and promotional rate announcements — and flags changes that affect the competitive context of a running experiment. Pricing team receives the competitor move alert with the estimated impact on experiment conditions and an assessment of whether the change warrants a pause, extension, or interpretive adjustment.
OKR objective: Competitor pricing moves that materially affect running experiment conditions are identified and flagged to the pricing team within 1 business day of the change.
OKR KR [Adoption]: Competitive pricing monitoring covering the bank's primary product markets within 6 months of go-live; alerts used in ≥80% of experiment review sessions.
OKR KR [Acceptance]: ≥75% of competitor move alerts confirmed as materially affecting experiment conditions by the pricing team; experiment interpretation adjusted for competitor-confounded experiments in ≥90% of flagged cases.
OKR KR [Cycle]: Competitor pricing change alerts issued within 1 business day of the change so the pricing team can adjust experiment management before further data accumulates under altered competitive conditions.

### CARD 4 [Insights|S] Segment cost-to-serve benchmarking
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:shared-banking-capabilities/pricing-profitability/segment-profitability-deep-dive/segment-cost-to-serve-benchmarking
intent: Analysis of operating cost allocation by customer segment and product — benchmarking cost-to-serve against the bank's internal segment profitability targets and available peer data — to identify the segments where operating cost is the primary drag on RAROC and the cost components driving the deviation. The analysis supports the business unit's cost efficiency programme prioritisation with segment-level cost-to-serve evidence.
Problem to solve: RAROC underperformance can be driven by NIM compression, credit loss, or operating cost; segment profitability attribution identifies cost as a driver but does not decompose the operating cost to the specific service component or channel allocation that is above the efficiency benchmark. Without segment-level cost-to-serve decomposition, cost efficiency programme effort is allocated without evidence of which service components and channel allocations produce the largest cost-to-RAROC drag in the underperforming segments.
Solution: Agent reads the operating cost allocation for each segment by service component — servicing, origination, collections, and channel cost — and benchmarks cost-to-serve against the bank's internal targets and available external peer data, producing a ranked cost deviation report with the specific components above the benchmark. Business unit head uses the cost deviation report to direct cost efficiency programme effort toward the highest-impact service components in the underperforming segments.
OKR objective: Segment operating cost-to-serve is benchmarked against internal targets and peer data on a quarterly basis and available for cost efficiency programme prioritisation.
OKR KR [Adoption]: Cost-to-serve benchmarking used in ≥4 business unit cost efficiency programme reviews within the first 18 months of go-live.
OKR KR [Acceptance]: Operating cost-to-serve in the bottom-quartile segments improves by ≥10% within 24 months of first cost efficiency cycle informed by the benchmarking analysis.
OKR KR [Cycle]: Benchmarking analysis refreshed quarterly from cost allocation data so efficiency programme decisions reflect the most recent operating cost performance.

### CARD 6 [Insights|M] Segment profitability deviation signal
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:shared-banking-capabilities/pricing-profitability/segment-profitability-deep-dive/segment-profitability-deviation-signal
intent: Continuous monitoring of segment profitability metrics against plan and the RAROC hurdle — detecting intra-quarter deviations by product segment, geographic unit, and customer tier that indicate a material performance trajectory change before the quarterly reporting cycle. Early deviation signals enable business unit heads to commission an attribution analysis and initiate corrective action within the quarter rather than receiving the deviation as a board-reported result.
Problem to solve: Segment profitability deviations are identified at the quarterly reporting cycle; deviations that develop over weeks of intra-quarter trading are not visible to the business unit head until the quarter is already closed. Corrective actions initiated on a closed-quarter deviation can only affect the following quarter; deviations identified intra-quarter allow intervention within the period, reducing the magnitude of the final reported underperformance.
Solution: Agent monitors segment profitability metrics — NIM, credit loss run-rate, fee income, and cost — on a weekly basis against the plan trajectory and the RAROC hurdle, and routes a deviation signal to the business unit head when a segment's intra-quarter trajectory projects a material end-of-quarter variance. Business unit head reviews the deviation signal and commissions a full attribution analysis where the projected variance is above the materiality threshold, enabling intra-quarter corrective action.
OKR objective: Intra-quarter segment profitability deviations that project a material end-of-quarter variance are identified and routed to business unit heads on a weekly monitoring cycle.
OKR KR [Adoption]: Deviation signal covering ≥85% of business unit segment profitability by product and geography within 9 months of go-live.
OKR KR [Acceptance]: ≥70% of deviation signals confirmed as material by business unit heads; intra-quarter corrective action rate on flagged deviations ≥60% within 18 months.
OKR KR [Cycle]: Deviation signal available each Friday from the week's profitability metric data so business unit heads can assess and respond before the following week's trading commences.

FTP & RAROC pricing signal
Continuous comparison of front-book product pricing against RAROC hurdle rates — identifying products and segments where current origination pricing falls below the board-set risk-adjusted return threshold. FTP rates embody the cost of funding and interest rate risk hedging; the RAROC hurdle incorporates expected credit loss, capital charge, and operating cost allocation. Products priced above FTP cost but below the RAROC hurdle consume risk budget at a negative risk-adjusted return.

For experiment synthesis, agent reads experiment data, runs prescribed statistical tests, decomposes results by segment and channel, and generates the management narrative. ### CARD 9 [Automation|M] RAROC hurdle breach alert

intent: Automated daily comparison of front-book origination pricing against the board-set RAROC hurdle rate by product and segment, with breach alerts routed to the product pricing team when a product's current origination pricing falls below the hurdle. Alerts include the specific product, segment, pricing band, and the estimated RAROC implied by the current origination rate, enabling pricing intervention before a material volume of sub-hurdle business is written.
Problem to solve: Products priced above FTP cost but below the RAROC hurdle consume risk budget at a negative risk-adjusted return; under ICAAP and Basel III risk appetite frameworks, persistent below-hurdle origination must be identified and remediated as a capital efficiency issue. Solution: Agent reads the daily origination pricing data and the current FTP and RAROC parameters, computes the implied RAROC for each product-segment pricing band, and routes a breach alert to the product pricing team for any band where origination pricing falls below the board-set hurdle. Product pricing team reviews the alert, determines whether the below-hurdle pricing reflects a deliberate strategic decision or an unintended pricing drift, and initiates a pricing adjustment or obtains board exception approval as appropriate.
OKR objective: Front-book origination pricing that falls below the board-set RAROC hurdle is identified on a daily basis and routed to the product pricing team before material sub-hurdle volume accumulates.
OKR KR [Adoption]: Daily RAROC breach alert covering ≥90% of front-book product-segment origination volume within 6 months of go-live.
OKR KR [Acceptance]: ≥85% of breach alerts actioned by the product pricing team within 5 business days; below-hurdle origination volume as a proportion of total origination reduced by ≥15% within 12 months.
OKR KR [Cycle]: Breach alert routed to product pricing team by 9am each business day so pricing intervention can be initiated before that day's origination volume closes.
