Trading book positions
The intraday and end-of-day record of each financial instrument held in the trading book — covering security positions, derivative contracts, foreign exchange positions, and commodity exposures — marked to market from current pricing feeds. Trading book positions drive VaR calculation, regulatory market risk capital under the Internal Models Approach (IMA) or Standardised Approach (SA), P&L attribution, and collateral margining. Position data flows from trading systems to the risk engine on each intraday cycle; its accuracy and completeness are prerequisites for every downstream risk metric.

### CARD 1 [Automation|S] Trading Book Position Completeness Check
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:banking-data-analytics/risk-position-data/trading-book-positions/trading-book-position-completeness-check
intent: Agent validates trading book position completeness on each intraday cycle — checking that positions from all trading systems have loaded to the risk engine, that the position count and aggregate notional are consistent with the prior cycle within tolerance, and that no instrument classes are missing relative to the expected coverage set. Exceptions are routed to the risk operations team before the risk calculation cycle runs, preventing the VaR and sensitivity calculations from proceeding on an incomplete position set.
Problem to solve: Trading book position data flows from multiple trading systems to the risk engine on each intraday cycle; feed failures, system timeouts, and booking delays can result in incomplete position sets reaching the risk engine without detection. VaR and sensitivity calculations that proceed on an incomplete position set produce understated risk metrics; the understatement is identified only when the position reconciliation is performed at end-of-day, after the risk metrics have been used in intraday limit monitoring and margin call calculations.
Solution: Agent reads the position load confirmation records from the risk engine at the completion of each intraday load cycle and checks: source system feed confirmation for all registered trading systems, position count and aggregate notional within a tolerance band of the prior cycle, and instrument class coverage against the expected set for each trading desk. Missing source system feeds, material count drops, and absent instrument classes are flagged immediately as load exceptions routed to the risk operations team. The risk calculation cycle proceeds only after exceptions are resolved or formally accepted by the risk operations lead.
OKR objective: VaR and sensitivity calculations proceed on a complete, confirmed trading book position set on each intraday cycle, with load exceptions resolved before the calculation runs.
OKR KR [Adoption]: Agent validates position completeness for ≥ 95% of intraday calculation cycles for ≥ 48 weeks within 12 months; all trading system feed confirmations integrated from go-live.
OKR KR [Acceptance]: ≥ 90% of completeness exceptions confirmed as genuine load failures by the risk operations team; VaR understatement events attributable to incomplete position sets eliminated within 3 months of go-live.

### CARD 2 [Enablement|S] Trading Book Boundary Monitoring
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:banking-data-analytics/risk-position-data/trading-book-positions/trading-book-boundary-monitoring
intent: Agent monitors the trading and banking book boundary on a weekly basis, identifying positions that exhibit characteristics inconsistent with their current book assignment — banking book positions showing trading intent indicators, or trading book positions with characteristics suggesting they should be classified as banking book. The risk governance and finance teams use the boundary monitoring report to maintain the integrity of the regulatory book boundary under NBKR and CBR market risk frameworks.
Problem to solve: The trading book/banking book boundary is established at the point of instrument designation and reviewed through the annual regulatory capital assessment. Positions that migrate in character — a banking book holding that develops short-term trading patterns, or a trading book position that has become effectively illiquid — may not be reclassified promptly. Incorrect book classification affects regulatory capital under the FRTB and SA market risk frameworks; boundary breaches identified in supervisory examinations require restatement of capital calculations.
Solution: Agent reads the full position inventory and applies boundary characterisation rules — trading intent indicators for banking book positions (frequency of turnover, mark-to-market designation, desk assignment) and illiquidity indicators for trading book positions (bid-offer spread width, days-to-liquidate estimates, pricing source classification). Positions where the characterisation analysis suggests a potential boundary inconsistency are flagged weekly with the specific indicators driving the flag. The risk governance and finance teams review flagged positions and initiate reclassification through the formal boundary review process where warranted.
OKR objective: The trading/banking book boundary is maintained at current regulatory classification through weekly characterisation monitoring, with reclassification candidates identified before regulatory examination.
OKR KR [Adoption]: Agent produces weekly boundary monitoring reports for 100% of in-scope positions for ≥ 48 weeks within 12 months; characterisation rules approved by risk governance and finance within 60 days of go-live.
OKR KR [Acceptance]: ≥ 75% of flagged boundary inconsistencies confirmed as requiring reclassification review by the risk governance team; boundary restatement events in supervisory examinations eliminated within 12 months of go-live.
OKR KR [Cycle]: Boundary monitoring cycle shifted from annual regulatory capital review to a weekly proactive scan; reclassification candidates identified and processed within the same quarter of characteristic change onset.

Limit & headroom data
The real-time and end-of-day state of the limits hierarchy — from board-level aggregate risk limits through BU, desk, and position sub-limits — showing current utilisation, available headroom, and breach status for each limit. Limit data is maintained in the risk management system and feeds the daily limit monitoring process, intraday trading controls, and the Risk Committee's monthly utilisation report. Limit breach events require escalation to the CRO within a defined response window under the bank's risk governance framework; the escalation notice must include breach magnitude, contributing positions, and proposed remediation.

### CARD 5 [Insights|S] Limit Utilisation Trend Monitor

intent: Agent monitors the rate of limit utilisation build-up across the limits hierarchy on a daily basis, identifying sub-limits where the utilisation trend line projects a breach within a defined forward window under the current trajectory. The risk management team receives a weekly trend alert for limits approaching the forward-breach projection threshold, enabling proactive position management before the breach materialises.
Problem to solve: Limit monitoring focuses on current utilisation against the current limit; the rate at which utilisation is building and the projected time to breach under the current trajectory are not surfaced systematically. The CRO's response window after breach detection is constrained by the breach magnitude and the time required to reduce the offending position.
Solution: Agent reads the daily utilisation time series for each limit in the hierarchy and calculates the trend — linear extrapolation and 7-day moving average — projecting the time to breach at the current trajectory. Limits where the trend line projects a breach within the defined forward window are flagged in a weekly trend alert ranked by proximity to projected breach. The risk management team uses the alert to engage the relevant desk or business unit in position management before the utilisation reaches the breach threshold.
OKR objective: The risk management team receives forward-looking trend alerts for limits approaching projected breach, enabling position management at least 5 business days before the breach materialises.
OKR KR [Adoption]: Agent produces weekly trend alerts covering 100% of the limits hierarchy for ≥ 48 weeks within 12 months; trend projection methodology applied from go-live.
OKR KR [Acceptance]: ≥ 70% of trend alerts result in position management action by the relevant desk within the alert period; projected-breach accuracy within ±2 business days confirmed in ≥ 75% of cases that reach the projected breach date.
OKR KR [Cycle]: Forward breach warning lead time extended from zero (reactive breach detection) to a minimum 5-business-day trend alert in ≥ 80% of breach events within 6 months of go-live.

Risk metrics & sensitivities
Derived risk measures calculated from positions and market data — VaR (parametric and historical simulation), expected shortfall, Greeks (delta, gamma, vega, rho), DV01, CS01, and P&L attribution — that characterise the portfolio's sensitivity to market movements. These metrics are the primary inputs to the daily risk briefing, the limit monitoring process, and regulatory capital reporting. Their accuracy depends on the completeness of the underlying position data and the timeliness of the market data consumed in the calculation.

The market risk quant team reviews the validation output and either adjusts the parameter or documents the basis for retaining the challenged value.
OKR objective: Stress scenario parameter sets are validated for internal consistency and historical calibration before the model run, reducing the risk of producing non-credible results that require revision after board or supervisory review.
