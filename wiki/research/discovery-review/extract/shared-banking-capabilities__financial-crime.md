Transaction monitoring alert triage
Pre-investigation classification and context assembly for transaction monitoring alerts — aggregating per-customer KYC, transaction history, prior alert dispositions, behavioral baseline deviations, and cross-customer ring indicators into an investigator-ready summary. Alert volumes at regulated institutions generate 50-2,000 cases per day; 90-98% are false positives under current monitoring rules, but each carries a mandatory review obligation under NBKR AML/CFT rules and FATF Recommendation 10.

### CARD 1 [Insights|S] Transaction monitoring alert disposition feedback loop
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:shared-banking-capabilities/financial-crime/transaction-monitoring-alert-triage/tm-alert-disposition-feedback-loop
intent: Systematic analysis of alert disposition outcomes — tracking true-positive rate by rule, customer segment, and behavioral profile — to feed the disposition evidence back into the triage prioritisation model and the false-positive rule calibration analysis on a monthly refresh cycle. The feedback loop ensures the triage scoring model incorporates the most recent disposition evidence rather than relying on the initial scoring parameters set at deployment.
Problem to solve: Triage prioritisation models set at deployment use historical base rates; as the customer population, transaction patterns, and monitoring rules evolve, the base rates that drove the initial prioritisation logic drift from the current disposition outcomes. Without a systematic feedback loop from actual dispositions to the triage scoring parameters, the prioritisation model's accuracy degrades over time, reducing the differential between the true-positive rate in the top-ranked alerts and the queue baseline.
Solution: Agent aggregates the monthly disposition outcomes — true-positive rate, SAR conversion rate, and false-positive characteristics — by rule, customer segment, and behavioral profile, and produces a calibration report with the specific parameter updates required to maintain triage scoring accuracy. AML model risk team uses the calibration report to update the triage scoring parameters on the monthly refresh cycle, with the disposition evidence available for the model governance record.
OKR objective: Triage prioritisation model parameters are recalibrated monthly from actual alert disposition outcomes, maintaining scoring accuracy as the customer and transaction population evolves.
OKR KR [Adoption]: Disposition feedback loop calibration used in ≥10 monthly triage model refreshes within the first 12 months of go-live.
OKR KR [Acceptance]: Triage model accuracy — differential between top-quartile true-positive rate and queue baseline — maintained at ≥2.5x throughout the first 12 months; model risk committee confirms calibration documentation completeness on quarterly review.
OKR KR [Cycle]: Calibration report produced within 3 business days of each month-end so parameter updates are deployed before the next month's alert volume accumulates.

### CARD 2 [Automation|M] Transaction monitoring alert pre-investigation pack
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:shared-banking-capabilities/financial-crime/transaction-monitoring-alert-triage/tm-alert-pre-investigation-pack
intent: Pre-assembled investigation context for transaction monitoring alert review — aggregating per-customer KYC profile, recent transaction history, prior alert dispositions, behavioral baseline deviations, and cross-customer ring indicators into an investigator-ready summary before the analyst begins disposition analysis. Pre-assembly reduces alert investigation preparation time, enabling the analyst to begin substantive disposition analysis without manual data aggregation across source systems.
Problem to solve: Alert volumes at regulated institutions generate 50 to 2,000 cases per day; 90 to 98% are false positives under current monitoring rules, but each carries a mandatory review obligation under NBKR AML/CFT rules and FATF Recommendation 10. Manual context assembly — retrieving KYC, transaction history, prior alerts, and behavioral baseline — consumes 15 to 45 minutes per alert before substantive analysis begins, creating a backlog during high-volume periods and compressing the time available for genuine risk investigation.
Solution: Agent reads each new transaction monitoring alert and assembles the investigation context — KYC profile, 90-day transaction history, prior alert and disposition history, behavioral baseline comparison, and related account connections — into a structured investigator pack with source citations. Investigator receives the pre-assembled pack and begins disposition analysis without additional data retrieval, with the context organised to support the prescribed investigation checklist.
OKR objective: Transaction monitoring alert investigation packs are assembled from source systems before the analyst begins disposition review, reducing preparation time per alert.
OKR KR [Adoption]: Pre-assembled packs covering ≥90% of daily alert volume within 9 months of go-live.
OKR KR [Acceptance]: Mean alert preparation time reduced by ≥50% vs. pre-deployment manual aggregation baseline; analyst confirmation of pack completeness ≥90% on quality review.
OKR KR [Cycle]: Investigation pack assembled within 30 minutes of alert generation so it is available when the analyst queue refreshes at the start of each shift.

### CARD 3 [Insights|M] Transaction monitoring alert priority triage scoring
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:shared-banking-capabilities/financial-crime/transaction-monitoring-alert-triage/tm-alert-priority-triage-scoring
intent: Prioritisation scoring for the daily transaction monitoring alert queue — ranking alerts by estimated true-positive probability based on the customer's risk classification, prior alert history, behavioral baseline deviation, and typology pattern match — so investigators can concentrate the most experienced analyst capacity on the highest-risk alerts. The prioritisation reduces the probability that genuine suspicious activity is reached late in the queue when the filing deadline window is most compressed.
Problem to solve: The 90 to 98% false-positive rate in the standard monitoring queue means that genuine suspicious activity is distributed across a large volume of noise; without prioritisation, investigators process alerts in queue order, applying the same attention to high-probability false positives as to the subset of alerts most likely to require SAR filing. Genuine suspicious activity identified late in the investigation cycle — after the majority of the review window has been consumed by false-positive dispositions — compresses the SAR drafting and filing time, increasing the deadline risk for the highest-priority cases.
Solution: Agent scores each alert in the daily queue against the customer's risk classification, prior alert history, behavioral baseline deviation magnitude, and typology pattern match, and reorders the investigation queue with the highest-priority alerts at the front. Investigators work the prioritised queue, with the triage rationale for each alert's ranking available in the pre-investigation pack, enabling senior analysts to be assigned to the top-ranked alerts.
OKR objective: Daily transaction monitoring alert queues are prioritised by estimated true-positive probability so investigators process highest-risk alerts first.
OKR KR [Adoption]: Prioritised queue used by AML investigation team for ≥90% of daily alert volume within 6 months of go-live.
OKR KR [Acceptance]: True-positive alert identification rate in the top quartile of the prioritised queue ≥3x the baseline queue rate within 12 months; SAR filing deadline compliance rate maintained at ≥99%.
OKR KR [Cycle]: Prioritised queue available at the start of each investigation shift so the first alerts worked by each analyst are the highest-risk cases of the day.

### CARD 4 [Enablement|S] AML typology update enrichment
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:shared-banking-capabilities/financial-crime/aml-investigation-pack/aml-typology-update-enrichment
intent: Continuous monitoring of FATF mutual evaluation reports, FinCEN advisories, NBKR AML/CFT circulars, and industry typology publications for emerging transaction and behavior patterns relevant to the bank's customer and product mix, with the findings summarised and routed to the AML team's investigation guidance library. Updated typology guidance enables investigators to apply current pattern recognition rather than relying on the bank's last formal typology training cycle.
Problem to solve: AML typology guidance in the bank's investigation manual reflects the patterns known at the last training or manual update cycle; newly published FATF, FinCEN, and supervisory typology updates are not systematically integrated into the working knowledge available to investigators between training cycles. Investigators applying outdated typology patterns are less likely to correctly identify and document the emerging pattern indicators that characterise current SAR-filing rationale, creating a gap in the institution's AML program quality between training events.
Solution: Agent monitors FATF, FinCEN, NBKR, and relevant industry typology publication feeds for new or updated pattern descriptions relevant to the bank's business profile, extracts the key behavioral indicators, and drafts a summary for the AML investigation guidance library. AML management reviews the summary before publishing to the guidance library, ensuring new typology indicators are available to investigators within days of regulatory publication rather than at the next training cycle.
OKR objective: Emerging AML typology indicators from regulatory and industry publications are extracted and available to investigators in the guidance library within 5 business days of publication.
OKR KR [Adoption]: Agent-produced typology summaries covering ≥90% of material regulatory typology publications within 6 months of go-live.
OKR KR [Acceptance]: ≥85% of AML management typology summary reviews result in publication to the guidance library without material revision; investigators confirm guidance usefulness in ≥75% of quarterly survey responses.
OKR KR [Cycle]: Typology summary available for management review within 2 business days of source publication so the 5-business-day guidance library update target is achievable.

The analyst receives a complete, structured dossier covering all standard evidence components and directs effort to disposition judgment — assessing the typology fit, making the SAR filing decision, and documenting the rationale. OKR KR [Adoption]: Agent-assembled investigation pack available at case-open for ≥95% of AML alerts within 12 months of go-live.

AML supervisors use the quality gap report to direct analyst coaching and documentation calibration, with the evidence pattern available for supervisory examination pre-preparation.
OKR objective: AML alert disposition quality gaps are identified across the full disposition population on a continuous basis and available for analyst coaching and supervisory examination preparation.
OKR KR [Adoption]: Disposition quality analytics covering ≥80% of monthly alert disposition volume within 9 months of go-live; used in ≥4 AML supervision reviews per year.
OKR KR [Acceptance]: Documentation deficiency rate on sampled supervisory review reduced by ≥30% within 18 months; analyst coaching interventions driven by quality analytics rather than exam findings in ≥90% of cases.
OKR KR [Cycle]: Quality gap report refreshed monthly from completed disposition data so coaching decisions reflect the most recent 30 days of disposition quality.

### CARD 7 [Automation|S] False-positive analyst calibration brief

OKR KR [Adoption]: Calibration briefs used in ≥4 team calibration sessions per year within 9 months of go-live.
OKR KR [Acceptance]: Analyst-level disposition disagreement rate on flagged rule-alert combinations reduced by ≥20% within 18 months of first calibration cycle informed by the briefs.
OKR KR [Cycle]: Calibration brief refreshed quarterly from the prior 90 days of disposition data so each calibration session addresses the most recent disagreement patterns.

The analysis covers 12 months of closed alerts, providing the statistical population needed to distinguish systematic tuning opportunities from case-specific noise. The model risk and AML teams use the output to prepare tuning proposals for model risk committee submission under NBKR AML/CFT supervisory expectations.

Tuning proposals resting on manual review of 50 to 100 cases do not satisfy the evidentiary standard for model risk committee approval; the full disposition dataset is required but not accessible without structured analytical tooling. It produces a ranked tuning opportunity list with the estimated alert volume reduction per adjustment and the supporting disposition evidence for each recommendation. The model risk and AML teams use the output to draft tuning proposals for model risk committee submission; false-positive rate by rule and segment is the primary outcome metric tracked post-implementation.
OKR objective: AML false-positive rate by transaction monitoring rule and customer-profile segment is systematically analysed across 12 months of closed alerts, with model-risk-committee-ready tuning proposals produced from the full disposition dataset.
OKR KR [Adoption]: Agent-produced rule analysis covering ≥12 months of closed alerts used to support ≥2 model risk committee tuning submissions per year.
OKR KR [Acceptance]: ≥75% of agent-identified tuning opportunities rated as actionable by the model risk committee based on statistical evidence quality; post-implementation false-positive rate reduction ≥15% per approved tuning cycle.
OKR KR [Cycle]: Population-level false-positive analysis cycle compressed from 3–6 months of senior-analyst availability to ≤4 weeks from commissioning to model risk committee submission.

SAR drafting
Structured drafting of Suspicious Activity Reports in the prescribed format — FinCEN (for US-regulated entities), Rosfinmonitoring (for CBR-regulated entities), or AFMRK (for NBKR-regulated entities) — from completed AML investigation findings. SAR narratives must cover who/what/when/where/why/how under each regulator's guidance, cross-reference related prior SARs, and meet a hard filing deadline (30 days from the determination of suspicion under FinCEN rules; similar under regional equivalents). Drafting from scratch takes 4-8 hours of BSA officer time per SAR.

OKR objective: SAR disclosure completeness is verified by automated pre-filing check before every filing, eliminating regulator-returned SARs for completeness deficiency.
OKR KR [Adoption]: Pre-filing check covering ≥99% of SAR filings within 6 months of go-live.

OKR KR [Cycle]: Quality check result available within 15 minutes of narrative submission so the BSA officer can resolve gaps and submit to filing queue within the same working session.
