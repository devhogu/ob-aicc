RM book management
The portfolio-level management of a relationship manager's assigned client book — tracking revenue concentration, product penetration depth, engagement frequency, and attrition risk across the full set of assigned relationships. Book management discipline is the operational foundation for RM performance: an RM who tracks book health continuously can redirect attention to at-risk relationships before the client reduces the relationship, and can sequence cross-sell activity toward the highest-probability opportunities. Under NBK and NBKR supervisory guidance on relationship banking, RMs are expected to maintain documented evidence of regular client contact at frequency proportional to the client's revenue tier.

### CARD 1 [Automation|S] Book Activity Log Automation
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/relationship-management/rm-book-management/book-activity-log-automation
intent: Agent maintains an automated activity log for each client in the RM's book — recording client interactions, product events, and compliance-relevant milestones from CRM events, product system alerts, and KYC schedule data — and generates a weekly summary for the RM's review. The log reduces the manual CRM maintenance burden for RMs while ensuring the documented contact frequency record meets NBK and NBKR supervisory expectations. CRM activity record completeness rate and RM manual CRM entry time are the primary outcome metrics.
Problem to solve: RMs are required to maintain documented evidence of regular client contact at a frequency proportional to the client's revenue tier; CRM record completeness is a supervisory expectation under NBK and NBKR relationship banking guidance. Manual CRM entry competes with client-facing time; high-book RMs either compress documentation quality or spend disproportionate time on administrative entry. Book activity records that are incomplete reduce the analytical value of CRM data for book health monitoring, cross-sell targeting, and KYC review preparation — creating a compound operational cost for the bank beyond the immediate compliance risk.
Solution: Agent reads CRM interaction logs, product system event feeds, and KYC schedule data for each client in the RM's book, compiling a structured activity record covering interactions, product milestones, and compliance events at each update cycle. The weekly summary is presented to the RM for review and confirmation; the RM adds qualitative interaction notes where the automated record does not capture the full interaction context. CRM activity record completeness rate — the proportion of client relationships with a compliant documented contact record — and RM manual CRM entry time are tracked against the pre-deployment baseline.

### CARD 2 [Insights|M] RM Book Health Intelligence
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/relationship-management/rm-book-management/rm-book-health-intelligence
intent: Agent monitors each RM's client book on a weekly cadence — tracking revenue concentration, product penetration depth, engagement frequency, and attrition risk signals — and produces a book health brief identifying clients at risk of relationship reduction and clients overdue for a cross-sell conversation. The RM reviews the brief at the start of the weekly call planning cycle and directs outreach effort toward the highest-priority clients. RM team head review of book health trends across the team provides the primary input for resource allocation and coaching conversations.
Problem to solve: RM book management is largely reactive; RMs prioritise client outreach based on recency of contact and personal relationship familiarity rather than from a systematic book health signal. Clients whose product holdings are reducing, engagement frequency is declining, or revenue contribution is concentrating without diversification are not systematically identified until the relationship loss is evident. The RM team head cannot identify which books in the team are deteriorating on leading indicators without commissioning CRM analysis per RM, which is not available at weekly cadence.
Solution: Agent reads CRM interaction records, product holding data, transaction revenue attribution, and engagement frequency for each client in the RM's book, computing book health metrics at weekly cadence. The book health brief ranks clients by attrition risk and cross-sell opportunity, and flags clients overdue for outreach against the documented contact frequency standard. The RM team head has access to the team-level book health summary; book health trends across the team are reviewed monthly alongside individual RM performance data. Revenue per book, attrition rate, and contact frequency compliance rate are the primary outcome metrics.

### CARD 3 [Enablement|M] Book Portfolio Rebalancing Brief
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/relationship-management/rm-book-management/book-portfolio-rebalancing-brief
intent: Agent analyses each RM's book structure — revenue concentration, segment distribution, geographic clustering, and KYC review workload — and produces a quarterly rebalancing brief for the RM team head, identifying book assignments that are unbalanced by revenue concentration or review workload relative to the team's capacity. The brief supports the RM team head's book allocation decisions and provides the evidence basis for new client assignments and transition planning. Revenue per RM book, book balance variance across the team, and KYC workload distribution are the primary outcome metrics.
Problem to solve: RM book allocations are reviewed annually or when a departure creates a vacancy; the RM team head does not have a current-state view of book balance across the team — which RMs are carrying disproportionate revenue concentration risk, which have the highest upcoming KYC review workload, and which have capacity for new client assignments. Unbalanced book structures are a risk management concern: over-concentrated books create single-point-of-failure risk for the bank's client relationships; under-resourced books generate service quality gaps that are not identified until client attrition is evident. Book rebalancing decisions made without an analytical view of current book structure produce allocations that are informed by relationship history rather than by portfolio risk and capacity data.
Solution: Agent reads revenue attribution, product holding data, segment assignments, and KYC review schedules for each client across the RM team's book, computing book-level concentration, workload, and capacity metrics at quarterly cadence. The rebalancing brief identifies the highest-priority rebalancing cases — over-concentrated books, upcoming KYC workload peaks, and under-resourced books with capacity for additional client assignments. The RM team head reviews the brief and uses it as the primary evidence basis for book allocation decisions; book balance variance across the team and KYC deadline adherence rate are tracked as outcome metrics.

Client lifecycle stewardship
The proactive management of a client's relationship across its full lifecycle — acquisition, deepening, maturity events, KYC review cycles, and relationship transitions. Lifecycle stewardship differs from reactive client management: the RM acts before the client event rather than responding to it. Under NBK, NBKR, and CBR KYC refresh obligations, all client relationships must be reviewed on a risk-proportionate schedule — typically annually for high-risk and every three to five years for standard-risk clients. Missing a KYC review deadline creates a regulatory breach; missing a product maturity creates a competitor opportunity.

### CARD 4 [Insights|S] Client Lifecycle Event Monitoring
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/relationship-management/client-lifecycle-stewardship/client-lifecycle-event-monitoring
intent: Agent monitors each client across CRM, product maturities, KYC review schedules, and public corporate event feeds, alerting the RM at structured lead times before events requiring proactive outreach. The alert includes a recommended action and a pre-populated brief drawn from the event context and the client's current relationship profile. The RM confirms the action taken and the outcome is recorded in CRM, maintaining a complete interaction audit trail.
Problem to solve: Client lifecycle events — term deposit maturities, credit facility renewals, KYC review deadlines, and corporate leadership changes — are tracked across multiple systems without consolidated alerting. RMs discover impending events with insufficient lead time for a considered response or a competitive retention offer. Missing a KYC review deadline is a regulatory breach under NBKR and CBR CDD requirements; missing a product maturity is a competitor opportunity with a measurable revenue consequence.
Solution: Agent monitors CRM, product system maturities, KYC review schedules, and public corporate event feeds for each client in the RM's book. It generates an alert at 30, 14, and 7 days before each event with the recommended action and a pre-populated brief drawn from relationship history and event context. The RM acts on the alert and records the outcome in CRM; KYC deadline adherence and product retention rates are the primary outcome metrics.
OKR objective: Structured alerts with recommended action and pre-populated brief are available to each RM at 30, 14, and 7 days before client lifecycle events — maturities, KYC deadlines, credit renewals, and corporate changes — maintained from continuous monitoring of CRM, product systems, and public event feeds.
OKR KR [Adoption]: Agent generates lifecycle event alerts for ≥95% of events meeting the defined alert horizon criteria across the full active RM book for ≥48 consecutive weeks.
OKR KR [Acceptance]: ≥85% of alerts confirmed as actionable by the receiving RM; KYC deadline breach rate and product retention rate at maturity tracked as primary outcome metrics.
OKR KR [Cycle]: Lead time from event identification to RM notification extended from same-week or post-event discovery to ≥14 days advance notice for ≥90% of scheduled events.

### CARD 5 [Enablement|S] Client Relationship Continuity Brief
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/relationship-management/client-lifecycle-stewardship/client-relationship-continuity-brief
intent: Agent generates a relationship continuity brief for each client when an RM transition occurs — coverage change due to staff departure, book redistribution, or planned leave — assembling the interaction history, product holdings, open commitments, and upcoming lifecycle events from CRM and product systems. The incoming RM reviews the brief before first contact with the client; no relationship transition occurs without a structured handover document. Client retention rate following RM transitions and time from transition to first incoming RM contact are the primary outcome metrics.
Problem to solve: RM transitions — departures, book redistributions, and extended leave — are a material client retention risk; clients who experience a handover without a briefed incoming RM perceive the relationship as disrupted and are more likely to reduce the relationship or move to a competitor. The current handover process relies on the departing RM's documentation quality and the incoming RM's manual CRM review; where the departing RM's documentation is incomplete, the incoming RM has insufficient context for a credible first interaction. Under NBK and NBKR supervisory guidance on relationship banking, RMs must maintain documented evidence of regular client contact; transitions that produce gaps in the contact record create both a client experience risk and a compliance gap.
Solution: Agent reads CRM interaction history, product holdings, open commitments, KYC review status, and upcoming lifecycle events for each client subject to a relationship transition, and generates a structured continuity brief for the incoming RM. The brief is generated at transition confirmation rather than on the departure date, giving the incoming RM adequate preparation time. Client retention rate — measured as product holding and contact frequency change in the 90 days following the transition — and time from transition to first documented incoming RM contact are the primary outcome metrics.

### CARD 6 [Automation|M] KYC Refresh Workflow Automation
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/relationship-management/client-lifecycle-stewardship/kyc-refresh-workflow-automation

No KYC review deadline is missed without a documented escalation record; the agent maintains the full workflow audit trail. KYC deadline adherence rate and review completion cycle time are the primary outcome metrics.
Problem to solve: KYC refresh deadlines for the RM client book are tracked in the compliance system; RMs are responsible for initiating client document requests at a defined lead time before the deadline. Solution: Agent reads the KYC review schedule for each client relationship and initiates the document request workflow at the defined lead time — typically 60 days before the review deadline — generating the client-facing request and the internal tracking record. It monitors document receipt and flags overdue responses to the RM and compliance team at defined escalation points. On receipt of all required documents, the agent assembles the completed review file for compliance sign-off; the full workflow timeline is maintained in the audit trail. KYC deadline adherence rate — the proportion of reviews completed before the regulatory deadline — and workflow completion cycle time are the primary outcome metrics.

Client interaction & meeting prep
The preparation cycle for RM client interactions — compiling relationship history, recent account events, company news, product holding gaps, and cross-sell prompts into a coherent meeting brief. Meeting quality is the proximate driver of cross-sell conversion and client satisfaction; preparation depth determines meeting quality. In corporate and private banking, the RM with the most current and relevant context consistently captures more wallet share. Under NBKR and CBR suitability frameworks for regulated products, meeting notes and client assessment records must be documented — adding a post-meeting administration burden that compounds the time cost of preparation.

urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/relationship-management/client-interaction-meeting-prep/meeting-outcome-intelligence

The weekly brief identifies the preparation and engagement practices with the strongest outcome correlation and flags RMs where outcome metrics suggest a coaching conversation is warranted. ### CARD 9 [Enablement|M] RM Meeting Brief
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/relationship-management/client-interaction-meeting-prep/rm-meeting-brief
intent: Agent assembles a pre-meeting brief for the RM from CRM history, recent account activity, public corporate or wealth-profile data, and product holding gaps — ready for review before the client interaction. For existing clients the brief emphasises relationship continuity and recent developments; for prospects it synthesises wealth sources and known priorities. The RM reviews the brief, conducts the meeting, and records interaction notes in CRM post-engagement.
Problem to solve: Relationship managers prepare for client and prospect meetings by manually reviewing CRM notes, recent transaction history, company news, and product holdings — thorough preparation for a single meeting is time-consuming, and book size means most clients receive a brief review or none. Under NBKR and CBR suitability frameworks for regulated products, post-meeting documentation adds a further time obligation on the same resource. Inconsistent preparation quality across the book results in uneven client experience and missed cross-sell signals at high-engagement interactions.
Solution: Agent reads CRM interaction history, recent account activity, public corporate filings or wealth profile data where applicable, and current product holdings. It generates a brief covering relationship summary, likely agenda topics, and recommended cross-sell prompts aligned to the client's lifecycle stage and product holding gaps. The RM reviews and acts; post-meeting notes are recorded in CRM to maintain a complete interaction history for compliance and continuity purposes.
OKR objective: A pre-meeting brief — covering relationship summary, likely agenda topics, and recommended cross-sell prompts calibrated to the client's lifecycle stage and product holding gaps — is available to each RM before every scheduled client or prospect interaction, drawn from CRM history, account activity, and public corporate or wealth profile data.
OKR KR [Adoption]: Agent generates a pre-meeting brief for ≥90% of scheduled RM client and prospect meetings for ≥48 consecutive weeks post go-live.
OKR KR [Acceptance]: ≥80% of briefs rated as adequate for meeting preparation by the RM without requiring supplementary manual research; cross-sell conversion rate and NPS from RM-managed relationships tracked as primary outcome metrics.
OKR KR [Cycle]: RM meeting preparation time reduced from 30–60 minutes of manual CRM and news review per client to ≤10 minutes of brief review per meeting.

Relationship deepening & cross-sell
The identification and pursuit of product deepening opportunities within the existing client book — expanding product holdings, increasing wallet share, and strengthening the relationship's economic contribution to the bank. Cross-sell in RM-managed segments is driven by event-triggered relevance: a corporate client restructuring its treasury function needs new transaction banking products; a private banking client with a maturing term deposit needs a reinvestment conversation. Segment propensity models provide population-level signals; per-client opportunity identification requires joining product holding data, transaction signals, and CRM interaction context.

RM team head coaching preparation time and cross-sell conversion rate by product category are the primary outcome metrics.

The RM team head reviews the brief and directs coaching effort toward the highest-gap categories; cross-sell conversion rate improvement by category is the primary outcome metric.

### CARD 11 [Automation|S] Cross-Sell Conversation Brief Automation
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/relationship-management/relationship-deepening-cross-sell/cross-sell-conversation-brief-automation
intent: Agent generates a structured conversation brief for each high-priority cross-sell opportunity identified in the RM's ranked list — covering the product, the supporting rationale, the client's relevant context, and a suggested conversation approach — ready for the RM to review before the client interaction. The RM reviews and adapts the brief; the quality and specificity of the cross-sell prompt reduces the preparation time required before each opportunity conversation. Cross-sell conversion rate for opportunities with a generated brief versus unassisted opportunities is the primary outcome metric.

Cross-sell opportunities that remain in the priority list beyond three weeks without a conversation are unlikely to convert; the preparation burden is a contributing factor to opportunity aging.
Solution: Agent reads each high-priority cross-sell opportunity from the RM's ranked list and generates a structured conversation brief covering the recommended product, the supporting rationale drawn from transaction signals and lifecycle context, and a suggested conversation approach tailored to the client's relationship history. The RM reviews and adapts the brief before the client interaction; the brief is accessible from within the RM's call preparation workflow. Cross-sell conversion rate for opportunities with a generated brief compared to the unassisted baseline is the primary outcome metric; opportunity aging — days from identification to first client conversation — is a secondary indicator.

### CARD 12 [New opps|M] Cross-Sell Opportunity Identification
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/relationship-management/relationship-deepening-cross-sell/cross-sell-opportunity-identification
intent: Agent identifies the highest-probability cross-sell opportunities in each RM's book from product holding gaps, transaction signals, and lifecycle events, ranked by expected revenue contribution. The output provides the recommended product, supporting rationale, and a suggested conversation hook tailored to the client's current circumstances. RMs integrate the ranked list into their weekly call plan.
Problem to solve: Cross-sell targeting within the RM book is driven by segment-level propensity campaigns; RMs receive campaign lists without client-specific context — which client recently restructured its treasury function, or which private banking client has a term deposit maturing in 30 days. High-probability opportunities are missed because the relevant signal is distributed across product systems, CRM, and public data sources with no consolidation at the RM level. Revenue from relationship deepening is systematically below potential when RM outreach is not informed by a current, client-specific opportunity signal.
Solution: Agent reads each client's product holdings, recent transaction patterns, lifecycle events — maturity dates, corporate actions, salary credits — and CRM interaction history. It generates a ranked cross-sell list per RM with the recommended product, supporting rationale, and a suggested conversation hook for each opportunity. RMs integrate the list into their call plan for the week; cross-sell conversion rate and revenue per RM book are the primary outcome metrics.
OKR objective: A weekly ranked cross-sell list per RM — specifying recommended product, supporting rationale, and suggested conversation hook drawn from product holding gaps, transaction signals, and lifecycle events — is available for integration into the RM's call plan.
OKR KR [Adoption]: Agent produces a ranked cross-sell list for ≥95% of active RMs on each weekly cycle for ≥48 consecutive weeks post go-live.
OKR KR [Acceptance]: ≥70% of top-ranked cross-sell opportunities per RM confirmed as commercially appropriate by the RM team lead on monthly review; cross-sell conversion rate and revenue per book tracked as primary outcome metrics.
OKR KR [Cycle]: Cross-sell opportunity identification cycle reduced from quarterly segment campaign lists to weekly client-specific ranked output.
