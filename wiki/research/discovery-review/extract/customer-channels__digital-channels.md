Digital onboarding
The customer-facing process of opening an account, applying for a product, or registering for digital banking through web or mobile. Under NBKR Resolution No. 50/4 and CBR Ordinance No. 4243-U, remote digital onboarding requires biometric identification, document verification, and electronic signing — a multi-step process with significant drop-off risk at each stage. Completion rate is the primary KPI; abandonment decomposed by step, device, and segment determines where the product team should invest improvement effort.

### CARD 1 [Enablement|S] Onboarding Abandonment Recovery
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/digital-channels/digital-onboarding/onboarding-abandonment-recovery
intent: Agent detects digital onboarding session abandonment in real time, identifies the step and likely abandonment reason from the session event log, and generates a personalised re-engagement message for dispatch through the customer's preferred channel within a defined re-engagement window. The digital operations team reviews the re-engagement queue before dispatch; the completion rate of re-engaged applicants versus the cold-start baseline is the primary outcome metric.
Problem to solve: Customers who abandon a digital onboarding journey mid-process represent a high-recovery cohort; many have dropped off due to a specific step failure — document upload error, verification timeout, or session interruption — rather than a change of intent. Without a real-time abandonment detection and re-engagement capability, abandoned journeys are not followed up until the next campaign cycle, by which time the applicant may have opened an account elsewhere. Re-engagement messages sent without step-specific context are less effective than messages that identify and address the specific step the applicant did not complete.
Solution: Agent monitors digital onboarding session events and detects abandonment — session closure without step completion — within the defined re-engagement window. It generates a personalised message identifying the step not completed and providing the appropriate instruction or support content for that step (document upload guidance, browser compatibility check, or a branch appointment option for verification-failed applicants). The digital operations team reviews the queue and releases; abandonment recovery rate — proportion of abandoned applicants who complete the journey within 7 days of re-engagement — is the primary outcome metric.

### CARD 2 [Insights|M] Digital Onboarding Drop-Off Intelligence
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/digital-channels/digital-onboarding/digital-onboarding-drop-off-intelligence
intent: Agent reconstructs digital onboarding journeys from session and KYC event data to locate the steps where applicants abandon, with root-cause attribution by step, device, and segment for the Head of Digital. The analysis distinguishes between document upload failure, session timeout, and identity verification latency as abandonment drivers. The Head of Digital receives a weekly brief with prioritised step-level remediation recommendations.
Problem to solve: Digital onboarding completion rates are tracked at funnel level — aggregate abandonment without step-level, device-level, or segment-level decomposition. Root-cause identification requires ad hoc analytical work that produces findings stale by the time remediation is designed. The absence of current-state step analytics means product and operations teams act on quarterly retrospectives rather than on the week's abandonment pattern.
Solution: Agent reads session event logs and KYC processing timestamps, reconstructs each applicant journey, and maps abandonment to the step, device, and applicant segment where it occurs. It decomposes abandonment drivers — distinguishing upload failure rates, session timeout frequency, and identity verification latency by device and segment combination. The Head of Digital reviews a weekly brief with a prioritised list of step-level interventions ranked by estimated completion-rate recovery.
OKR objective: A weekly brief identifying digital onboarding abandonment by step, device, and segment — with root-cause attribution distinguishing upload failure, session timeout, and identity verification latency — is available to the Head of Digital for remediation prioritisation.
OKR KR [Adoption]: Agent delivers the weekly onboarding drop-off intelligence brief for ≥95% of scheduled weeks for ≥48 consecutive weeks post go-live.
OKR KR [Acceptance]: ≥80% of step-level remediation recommendations confirmed as actionable by the Head of Digital on weekly review.
OKR KR [Cycle]: Onboarding drop-off diagnostic cycle reduced from quarterly ad hoc analytical work to weekly automated brief production within 48 hours of session log refresh.

### CARD 3 [Automation|M] KYC Document Verification Automation
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/digital-channels/digital-onboarding/kyc-document-verification-automation
intent: Agent verifies identity documents submitted during digital onboarding — checking document authenticity signals, expiry, name consistency, and biometric match — and generates a structured verification summary for the KYC operations team before the case is reviewed for approval. Documents falling outside automated confidence thresholds are flagged for manual KYC review with the specific exception noted. KYC review time per case and straight-through processing rate are the primary outcome metrics.
Problem to solve: Digital onboarding identity verification requires the KYC operations team to manually review document images and biometric data for each applicant, checking authenticity, expiry, and name consistency against multiple data points. Under NBKR Resolution No. 50/4 and CBR Ordinance No. 4243-U, each remote onboarding case must have a documented verification record; manual review is the rate-limiting step for completion rate and the primary driver of verification latency. KYC review queue depth during peak onboarding periods extends verification latency beyond the threshold at which applicants abandon the journey, directly reducing completion rate.
Solution: Agent reads the submitted identity documents and biometric data, checks authenticity signals, expiry dates, name consistency across documents, and biometric match score, and generates a structured verification summary for each case. Cases meeting the automated confidence threshold are flagged for expedited KYC review; cases with specific exceptions are queued for manual review with the exception type noted. Straight-through processing rate — cases approved without manual intervention — and KYC review time per case are tracked against the pre-deployment manual review baseline.

Digital support & servicing
Self-service tasks within the digital channel — balance enquiries, transfers, statement requests, dispute initiation, card management — that determine whether the customer resolves the need digitally or transfers to an assisted channel. Servicing deflection rate — the proportion of potential contact center contacts resolved in the digital channel — is the primary channel economics metric. Under NBKR and CBR consumer service standards, digital self-service must provide equivalent outcomes to assisted channels for the categories of transactions it covers.

### CARD 4 [Automation|S] Digital Self-Service Task Automation
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/digital-channels/digital-support-servicing/digital-self-service-task-automation
intent: Agent handles routine self-service requests submitted through the digital channel — balance enquiry, mini-statement, card freeze and unfreeze, and address update — without agent involvement, completing the transaction within the session and generating a confirmation record for the customer and the bank's audit trail. Straight-through completion rate and the contact center volume reduction from automated digital task handling are the primary outcome metrics.
Problem to solve: Routine self-service requests — balance enquiries, statement requests, card management, and address updates — represent a significant share of contact center inbound volume and are submitted through digital channels by customers who expect immediate completion. In the current model, digital channel submissions for some task types are queued for back-office processing or contact center callback rather than completed within the session, creating an inconsistency between the customer's expectation and the actual servicing experience. Under NBKR and CBR consumer service standards, digital self-service must provide equivalent outcomes to assisted channels for covered transaction categories; queued processing does not meet this standard for routine tasks.
Solution: Agent handles routine self-service requests submitted through the digital channel by executing the transaction — balance enquiry, mini-statement generation, card freeze and unfreeze, address update — within the session against the bank's core system, generating a confirmation record immediately. The agent escalates to a human agent where the transaction type, regulatory category, or account status requires human review. Straight-through completion rate per task type and contact center volume reduction from digital task absorption are tracked against the pre-deployment baseline.

### CARD 5 [Enablement|S] Digital Channel Support Brief
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/digital-channels/digital-support-servicing/digital-channel-support-brief
intent: Agent generates a daily digital channel support brief for the digital operations team, summarising the prior day's servicing activity — task completion rates by category, escalation reasons, error events, and any anomalies in session failure rates. The brief replaces manual data extraction from session logs and enables the digital operations team to identify and respond to servicing degradation before it drives contact center volume. Response time to digital servicing anomalies and escalation rate per task category are the primary outcome metrics.
Problem to solve: Digital channel servicing performance — task completion rates, escalation reasons, session error frequency — is monitored through periodic review of session logs; real-time degradation in completion rate for a specific task type is not detected until it shows up in contact center volume or customer complaints. The digital operations team does not have a consolidated daily brief covering servicing performance across all task categories; performance visibility requires manual log extraction that is performed on a reactive basis. Without a proactive daily brief, servicing degradation accumulates for hours or days before the digital operations team can identify and escalate for resolution.
Solution: Agent reads digital channel session event logs and completion records, computes task completion rates and escalation rates by category for the prior day, and identifies any anomalies against the seven-day rolling baseline. The daily brief is delivered to the digital operations team at the start of the working day; anomalies are flagged with the session data supporting immediate investigation. Response time from anomaly detection to investigation initiation and daily escalation rate per task category are tracked as primary outcome metrics.

### CARD 6 [Insights|M] Digital Servicing Deflection Intelligence
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/digital-channels/digital-support-servicing/digital-servicing-deflection-intelligence
intent: Agent maps contact center call reasons to the digital servicing capability inventory, identifying transaction categories that customers are calling about but that could be resolved in the digital channel, and quantifying the deflection opportunity per category. The Head of Digital and Head of Contact Center review the quarterly brief jointly to prioritise digital capability development that will close the deflection gap. Deflection rate by transaction category and contact center volume reduction are the primary outcome metrics tracked after capability deployment.
Problem to solve: Digital servicing deflection rate — the proportion of potential contact center contacts resolved in digital — is tracked at the aggregate level; the specific transaction categories driving assisted-channel volume above the deflection target are not systematically identified. Contact center call reason data and digital channel capability inventory are maintained by separate teams; a joined analysis that identifies which call reasons lack a digital equivalent is not produced at operational cadence. Without category-level deflection gap analysis, digital development investment is prioritised on product roadmap criteria rather than on the highest-volume assisted-channel contacts that could be shifted to digital.
Solution: Agent reads contact center call reason classifications and maps each category against the digital channel capability inventory, flagging categories where a self-service equivalent exists but is not being used and categories where no digital capability exists. It estimates the contact center volume reduction potential for each capability gap, ranked by call volume. The quarterly brief is reviewed jointly by the Heads of Digital and Contact Center; digital capability development priorities are aligned to the highest-volume deflection opportunities identified.

Digital engagement & personalisation
The in-session customer experience — feature discovery, product promotion, and personalised content — that determines whether a logged-in session deepens the customer relationship or ends at a routine transaction. Personalisation is governed by CBR and NBK data protection regulations and the bank's GDPR-aligned consent framework; offers and content must be served within the scope of the customer's marketing consent. The commercial case for personalisation is measured in cross-sell conversion lift, product activation rate, and digital engagement frequency.

### CARD 7 [Automation|S] Consent Scope Enforcement Automation
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/digital-channels/digital-engagement-personalisation/consent-scope-enforcement-automation
intent: Agent maintains a current-state consent scope register for each customer, verified against the consent management system on each session, and blocks any personalisation recommendation whose product category falls outside the customer's active consent scope. Consent scope checks are applied at the recommendation generation step rather than post-hoc, eliminating the regulatory exposure from serving out-of-scope recommendations. The compliance team reviews a weekly consent scope exception report; any breach triggers an incident record.
Problem to solve: Personalisation recommendations are generated from product and transaction signals; consent scope adherence is enforced through a post-hoc compliance review rather than at the point of recommendation generation. Under CBR and NBK data protection regulations, serving marketing content outside the scope of the customer's active consent is a regulatory breach; post-hoc detection means a breach has already occurred before it is identified. Consent scope can change between sessions — a customer withdrawing marketing consent during one session should receive no recommendations in the next — and the current architecture does not propagate consent changes to the personalisation layer in real time.
Solution: Agent reads the current-state consent scope for each customer from the consent management system at session initiation and filters the recommendation set to exclude any product category outside the active scope. Consent scope changes are propagated to the personalisation layer within the session event cycle; no out-of-scope recommendation is generated regardless of model output. The weekly exception report tracks any consent scope boundary events detected by the audit log; each exception triggers an incident record for compliance review.

### CARD 8 [Insights|M] Personalisation Effectiveness Intelligence
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/digital-channels/digital-engagement-personalisation/personalisation-effectiveness-intelligence
intent: Agent analyses recommendation acceptance and conversion outcomes by segment, product type, and session context to produce a weekly personalisation performance brief for the Head of Digital. The brief identifies which recommendation categories are generating conversion lift and which are producing offer fatigue or consent scope boundary contacts, enabling rapid model calibration. The Head of Digital reviews the brief and directs the model calibration cycle from a current-state performance signal rather than from periodic A/B test results.
Problem to solve: Personalisation model performance is reviewed through periodic A/B tests at campaign cadence; the Head of Digital cannot see which recommendation categories are underperforming or generating offer-fatigue responses between test cycles. Model drift — where a recommendation type that was effective at launch loses conversion lift over time — is not detectable until the next scheduled test review. Consent scope boundary contacts — sessions where a recommendation was served outside the customer's consent scope — are only identified through post-hoc compliance review rather than in real time.
Solution: Agent reads session-level recommendation records, customer response events (click, dismiss, conversion), and consent scope flags, and computes weekly conversion rates by recommendation category, segment, and session context. It identifies offer-fatigue signals — declining acceptance rates in high-frequency recommendation categories — and consent scope boundary events for immediate review. The Head of Digital reviews the brief and adjusts model calibration parameters; conversion lift by recommendation category and consent scope adherence rate are the tracked outcome metrics.

### CARD 9 [New opps|L] Digital Engagement Personalisation
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/digital-channels/digital-engagement-personalisation/digital-engagement-personalisation
intent: Agent generates a next-best-action recommendation for each logged-in session — product offer, content, or service prompt — from the customer's product holdings, recent transaction signals, and lifecycle position. Recommendations are constrained by the customer's marketing consent scope and offer-fatigue rules, enforced systematically at session level. Conversion and activation rates against the session-level recommendation measure outcome.
Problem to solve: Digital channel personalisation relies on segment rules set at campaign cadence; customers at different lifecycle stages receive undifferentiated prompts because the system has no mechanism to reflect per-customer signals at session level. Marketing consent scope and offer-fatigue limits are not enforced consistently across the session layer, creating both compliance exposure and customer experience degradation. High-propensity customers at product-eligible lifecycle events — term deposit maturities, salary uplift, savings accumulation — are missed between campaign cycles.
Solution: Agent reads product holdings, recent transaction activity, segment assignment, and lifecycle stage for each session, generating a ranked next-best-action with a confidence score. Consent scope adherence and offer-fatigue constraints are applied at generation time rather than post-hoc, ensuring regulatory compliance and customer experience standards are met systematically. The recommendation is rendered via the digital channel personalisation layer; cross-sell conversion and product activation rates measure commercial outcome.
OKR objective: A next-best-action recommendation — product offer, content, or service prompt — constrained to consent scope and offer-fatigue rules, is generated for each logged-in digital session from per-customer transaction signals and lifecycle position.
OKR KR [Adoption]: Agent generates a session-level next-best-action recommendation for ≥90% of logged-in digital sessions meeting the defined eligibility criteria for ≥26 consecutive weeks post go-live.
OKR KR [Acceptance]: Cross-sell conversion and product activation rates for sessions with agent recommendation ≥20% higher than the pre-deployment segment-rule baseline on 6-month holdout cohort; consent scope adherence rate ≥99.5%.
OKR KR [Cycle]: Personalisation refresh cycle reduced from campaign-cadence segment rule updates (4–6 weeks) to per-session real-time recommendation generation.

Digital adoption tracking
The measurement of how deeply and broadly the customer base uses digital channels — login frequency, feature activation, product self-service usage, and segment penetration. Digital adoption tracking informs channel investment prioritisation, contact center headcount planning, and branch network review. Under NBKR and NBK open banking strategies, digital adoption rate is a supervisory interest metric and a component of financial inclusion reporting. Cohort-level decomposition — by segment, product, and tenure — is the analytical input that makes adoption metrics actionable.

### CARD 10 [Automation|S] Low-Adoption Customer Outreach Automation
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/digital-channels/digital-adoption-tracking/low-adoption-customer-outreach-automation
intent: Agent identifies customers who have registered for digital banking but have not logged in within a defined inactivity window, generates personalised re-engagement messages, and queues them for dispatch within the customer's marketing consent scope. The Head of Digital reviews the outreach queue before dispatch; no message is released without confirmation. Re-engagement rate — proportion of inactive customers who log in within 30 days of outreach — is the primary outcome metric.
Problem to solve: Customers who register for digital banking but do not log in within the activation window represent a high-priority adoption recovery cohort; without systematic identification and outreach, they remain dormant and do not contribute to the digital adoption rate. Manual extraction of inactive registrant lists and outreach message assembly is performed at campaign cadence rather than on a rolling basis, allowing inactivity to compound before outreach is triggered. Marketing consent scope and message personalisation are applied inconsistently in manually assembled campaigns, creating compliance exposure and reducing outreach effectiveness.
Solution: Agent reads digital banking registration records and session event logs, identifies customers inactive beyond the defined window, and generates personalised re-engagement messages tailored to the customer's product holdings and preferred contact channel, within consent scope. Messages are queued for the Head of Digital's review before dispatch; the agent records dispatch date and subsequent session event for re-engagement rate tracking. Re-engagement rate by cohort and time from inactivity threshold to outreach dispatch are tracked against the prior campaign-cadence baseline.

The brief for the Head of Digital includes adoption gap analysis by segment and recommended activation interventions ranked by estimated adoption recovery per cohort. Solution: Agent reads digital channel session data, product holdings, segment assignments, and customer tenure records, and computes adoption rates by cohort at weekly cadence. It identifies cohorts below the bank's adoption target, maps the feature activation sequences associated with sustained high engagement, and ranks intervention options by estimated adoption recovery per cohort. The Head of Digital reviews the weekly brief and directs the digital activation programme toward the highest-opportunity cohorts; segment adoption rate improvement and feature activation rate by cohort are the primary outcome metrics.
