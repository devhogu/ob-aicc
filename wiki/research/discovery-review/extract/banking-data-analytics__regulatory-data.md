Regulatory reporting datasets
The structured datasets assembled from source systems for each regulatory submission cycle — covering the population, field definitions, transformation logic, and validation rules required for NBKR Form 700 (prudential returns), CBR Form 0409 (credit risk reporting), ARDFM capital adequacy returns, and AML/CFT transaction reporting. The dataset assembly process is the first step of each reporting cycle; its completeness and accuracy determine whether the submission passes regulatory validation checks on first filing or requires restatement.

### CARD 1 [Enablement|S] Regulatory Submission Variance Brief
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:banking-data-analytics/regulatory-data/regulatory-reporting-datasets/regulatory-submission-variance-brief
intent: Agent compares the current cycle's regulatory dataset to the prior cycle's submission and generates a variance brief — identifying material movements by form field, classifying variances by likely cause (portfolio growth, risk grade migration, rate movement, structural change), and flagging unexplained residuals for analyst investigation. The regulatory reporting team uses the brief as the primary quality review input before approving the dataset for submission.
Problem to solve: Regulatory reporting quality review requires the team to compare the current cycle's dataset to the prior submission, explain material movements in key fields, and confirm that unexplained residuals do not indicate data errors. The comparison is performed manually for each form; the variance commentary is drafted by the analyst after identifying the movements. For complex forms with many fields, the manual comparison consumes a substantial portion of the available review window, compressing the time available for investigating genuine anomalies.
Solution: Agent reads the current and prior cycle datasets, calculates field-level variances, and classifies each material variance against known drivers — portfolio balance movements, risk grade changes, market rate movements, and FX revaluation — using data available from the credit, market risk, and finance systems. Variances that can be attributed to a documented driver are summarised in the variance brief; residuals that exceed a defined unexplained threshold are flagged for analyst investigation. The brief is structured to align with the form's section structure, enabling the review to proceed field-by-field.
OKR objective: The regulatory reporting team completes the variance review for each cycle with a structured, pre-populated brief, directing analytical effort to unexplained residuals rather than variance identification.
OKR KR [Adoption]: Agent produces variance briefs for ≥ 3 form types for ≥ 12 consecutive submission cycles within the first year; variance driver classification logic validated by the regulatory reporting team within 60 days of go-live.
OKR KR [Acceptance]: ≥ 80% of attributed variances confirmed as correctly classified by the regulatory reporting team; unexplained residual flags confirmed as requiring investigation in ≥ 85% of cases.
OKR KR [Cycle]: Variance review time reduced from 2-3 days of manual comparison to ≤ half a day of brief review and residual investigation per cycle.

Data lineage & BCBS 239 compliance
The documented trace of each CDE from its source system through every transformation step to its final position in the regulatory report — the evidence required by BCBS 239 Principles 2 and 3 (data accuracy and completeness) for supervisory review. For systemically important banks under NBKR and CBR supervision, BCBS 239 lineage documentation is a supervisory examination requirement; gaps in the lineage map are findings that require remediation within defined timelines. Maintaining current lineage as ELT pipelines evolve is a continuous governance discipline, not a periodic documentation exercise.

Data residency & localization controls
The governance controls that ensure personal and financial data of Kazakhstan and Russia citizens is stored and processed within domestic jurisdictions, as required by Kazakhstan's Law No. 94-V and Russia's Federal Law No. 242-FZ. Data residency compliance requires maintaining a current data inventory mapped to storage locations, monitoring cross-border data flows for personal data, and maintaining the legal basis register for any cross-border transfers that are permitted under the data protection framework. Cloud adoption and cross-border API integrations create a continuously evolving data flow landscape that periodic mapping exercises cannot fully track.
