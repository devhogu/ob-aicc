Branch network planning
The strategic configuration of the branch network — site selection, branch density, format allocation, and consolidation decisions — governed by NBK/NBKR/CBR licensing frameworks and consumer access obligations. Network planning cycles are annual; the inputs — demographic data, digital adoption metrics, competitor positions, and regulatory access maps — change continuously. Branch network decisions commit capital and headcount for three to five years, making the planning exercise high-stakes and analytically demanding.

### CARD 1 [Automation|S] Branch Regulatory Notification Drafting
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/physical-branches/branch-network-planning/branch-regulatory-notification-drafting
intent: Agent drafts regulatory notifications for branch network changes — new openings, format conversions, and closures — under NBK, NBKR, and CBR licensing requirements, populating the mandatory fields from the approved change record and generating a complete draft for compliance review before submission. The compliance team reviews and submits; preparation time per notification and the completeness rate on first submission are the primary metrics.
Problem to solve: Branch network change notifications to NBK, NBKR, and CBR require structured submissions with mandatory fields drawn from the approved change record — location, format, capacity, access provisions, and implementation timeline. Manual drafting of regulatory notifications from change records is a consistent administrative bottleneck; regulatory deadlines for notification submission — typically 30-90 days before implementation depending on jurisdiction — are linked to the implementation timeline, and late notifications delay the entire network change programme. Notification completeness on first submission is a quality control concern; regulatory notifications returned for amendment introduce further delay.
Solution: Agent reads the approved branch change record — location, format type, capacity, consumer access provisions, and implementation dates — and generates the regulatory notification in the format required by the applicable jurisdiction. The compliance team reviews and submits; any fields requiring judgement beyond the structured change record are flagged for human completion before submission. Notification preparation time, completeness rate on first submission, and days from change approval to notification submission are tracked against the pre-deployment manual drafting baseline.

### CARD 2 [Insights|M] Branch Network Performance Intelligence
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/physical-branches/branch-network-planning/branch-network-performance-intelligence
intent: Agent monitors demographic, digital adoption, and competitor density data at catchment level on a quarterly cadence, updating the branch network performance model and flagging locations where the economics case for the current format has shifted materially since the last annual review. The Head of Branches and Head of Network Planning review the quarterly signal brief before determining whether an off-cycle network review is warranted. The brief provides a continuous monitoring layer between annual network planning cycles.
Problem to solve: Branch network planning relies on an annual review cycle; demographic shifts, digital adoption rate changes, and competitor branch closures or openings affect network economics on a continuous basis but are not incorporated into the network model between annual reviews. A branch whose catchment demographics have shifted materially — new residential development, a competitor closure, or a significant digital adoption increase in the serving segment — may remain in the current configuration for up to 12 months before the annual review identifies the case for reconfiguration. Under NBK and NBKR licensing frameworks, network changes require regulatory engagement with defined lead times; early identification of cases for reconfiguration allows the bank to complete the regulatory process within the annual capital allocation cycle.
Solution: Agent reads demographic data, digital adoption metrics, competitor branch location changes, and the bank's current network configuration, and updates the catchment-level branch performance model on a quarterly cadence. It flags branches where the economics case has shifted materially from the prior annual review baseline and estimates the network planning implication — reconfiguration, format change, or regulatory engagement — for each flagged location. The Head of Branches reviews the quarterly brief and determines whether an off-cycle review process should be initiated; branches flagged and acted upon in advance of the annual cycle are tracked as the primary efficiency outcome.

### CARD 3 [New opps|M] Branch Site Selection Modelling
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/physical-branches/branch-network-planning/branch-site-selection-modelling
intent: Agent models the commercial case for candidate branch locations from demographic data, digital adoption rates, competitor density, and the bank's existing customer distribution in each catchment. The output ranks candidate sites by projected revenue contribution and customer acquisition potential over a three-year horizon, with the assumption set transparent for network planning review. The Head of Network Planning reviews the model output before site selection decisions are made.
Problem to solve: Branch site selection models are constructed manually by the network planning team from demographic data, competitor maps, and internal customer distribution data — a process that limits the number of candidate sites that can be evaluated within a planning cycle. Sites that are commercially viable but outside the team's established catchment knowledge are not systematically evaluated; the constraint is model production capacity rather than data availability. Under NBK and NBKR financial inclusion frameworks, new branch openings in underserved catchments can receive regulatory credit; the commercial model for these locations requires a financial inclusion overlay that is not currently built into the standard site assessment.
Solution: Agent reads demographic and income data by catchment, digital adoption rates, competitor branch density, and the bank's existing customer distribution, and generates a three-year commercial model for each candidate site covering projected revenue, customer acquisition potential, and financial inclusion credit assessment. Sites are ranked by commercial return and flagged where financial inclusion regulatory credit applies. The Head of Network Planning reviews the ranked site list before capital allocation; sites evaluated and actioned from agent output are tracked against the prior manual assessment capacity.

Branch performance reporting
The monthly and quarterly reporting cycle that measures branch network performance against targets — customer volume, transaction mix, revenue per visit, queue time, cross-sell conversion, and complaint rate. Regional managers use the pack to calibrate branch manager coaching and resource allocation. Under NBK and NBKR supervisory expectations, branch-level performance data is a component of the consumer access reporting submitted during on-site inspections. The reporting cycle consumes significant network team time in collation and currently delivers results that are already several weeks stale by the time review conversations occur.

### CARD 4 [Automation|S] Branch Performance Data Automation
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/physical-branches/branch-performance-reporting/branch-performance-data-automation
intent: Agent automates the extraction, transformation, and consolidation of branch performance data from operational source systems into the reporting data model on a daily cadence, replacing the manual export and reconciliation cycle. The Head of Branches and regional managers can access current-state branch metrics at any point in the month rather than waiting for the end-of-period manual pack. Data freshness — the lag between operational event and reporting availability — is the primary technical outcome metric.
Problem to solve: Branch performance reporting data is extracted manually from multiple operational systems at period end; intra-month performance is not visible to regional managers or the Head of Branches except through ad hoc requests to the BI team. The manual extraction process introduces transformation errors and reconciliation steps that compound the 10-15 day delay between period close and pack delivery. Regional managers who need a current-state view of a branch's performance during the month cannot access it without commissioning a bespoke BI extract, which is not available at operational cadence.
Solution: Agent runs daily extraction jobs from each operational source system — transaction system, CRM, queue management, complaint log — transforming and loading the data into the branch performance reporting model with a defined reconciliation check at each step. Any source system extraction failure or reconciliation discrepancy is flagged to the BI team for immediate resolution. Data freshness — the maximum lag between operational event and reporting availability — and extraction failure rate are tracked as the primary technical outcome metrics; intra-month data availability replaces the end-of-period manual extract model.

### CARD 5 [Enablement|S] Regional Manager Coaching Brief
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/physical-branches/branch-performance-reporting/regional-manager-coaching-brief
intent: Agent generates a pre-meeting coaching brief for each regional manager's branch review session from the branch performance data — identifying the two to three branches with the most significant performance variances, the metric drivers behind each variance, and a structured agenda for the review conversation. The Head of Branches reviews the brief before the regional manager meeting cycle; the brief replaces manual review preparation by the regional manager team. Coaching session preparation time and the proportion of review sessions identifying a specific branch management intervention are the primary outcome metrics.
Problem to solve: Regional managers prepare for branch review sessions by manually reviewing performance data for each branch in their portfolio — a preparation process that compresses the quality of coaching conversations when review cycles are frequent and the branch count is high. Without a structured brief that surfaces the highest-priority performance variances and their drivers, review conversations focus on branches the regional manager is already tracking rather than on the branches with the most acute performance gaps. The Head of Branches has no visibility into which regional manager review sessions are producing actionable outcomes and which are being conducted without a structured performance basis.
Solution: Agent reads branch performance data for each regional manager's portfolio and identifies the two to three branches with the most significant variances against target across the key metrics — queue time, cross-sell conversion, revenue per visit, and complaint rate. For each flagged branch it provides the metric driver breakdown and a structured agenda point for the review conversation. The Head of Branches reviews the brief before the regional manager meeting cycle; review session preparation time and the rate of sessions producing a documented branch management intervention are tracked as outcome metrics.

Teller & frontline operations
The staffing, scheduling, and queue management of the teller line and frontline service staff — the primary cost driver for an assisted branch interaction. Teller operations are governed by consumer service standards and NBK/NBKR consumer protection guidance on maximum queue times at banking points. Optimal staffing requires matching shift capacity to intraday footfall patterns that vary by branch, day of week, and local event calendar. Manual scheduling from historical averages is insufficient to manage intraday variance.

### CARD 7 [Automation|S] Teller Schedule Generation
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/physical-branches/teller-frontline-operations/teller-schedule-generation
intent: Agent generates the weekly teller staffing schedule for each branch from the footfall demand forecast, contracted shift patterns, and staff availability, minimising idle staffing while maintaining the queue-time service level at each intraday interval. The branch manager reviews and confirms the schedule before publication; any override is recorded with a reason code for schedule accuracy improvement. Schedule efficiency — the ratio of productive teller time to total paid hours — and queue-time service-level adherence are the primary outcome metrics.
Problem to solve: Teller schedules are built manually by branch managers from historical average footfall data; the schedule does not reflect intraday demand variability from payroll cycles, local events, or day-of-week patterns that are stable and predictable from operational data. Under NBK and NBKR consumer protection guidance, maximum queue times are a service standard; manual scheduling from averages generates both understaffed intervals — where queue times exceed the standard — and overstaffed intervals — where teller idle time generates unnecessary payroll cost. Branch managers spend 2-4 hours per week on manual schedule construction, a burden that grows with branch size and does not benefit from analytical optimisation.
Solution: Agent reads the weekly footfall demand forecast, contracted shift patterns, and staff availability, and generates an optimised teller schedule minimising idle staffing while maintaining the queue-time service level at each 30-minute interval. The branch manager reviews and confirms before publication; overrides are recorded with reason codes. Schedule efficiency ratio and queue-time service-level adherence rate are tracked weekly against the prior manual schedule baseline; branch manager scheduling time is a secondary efficiency outcome.

Cash management
The daily management of vault cash across the branch network — replenishment ordering, intraday balancing, and end-of-day reconciliation. Cash management ties up working capital at the cost of overnight treasury funding; excess vault cash reduces net interest income while insufficient cash creates service failures. Under NBKR and CBR cash circulation regulations, branches must maintain minimum denominations for customer service. Replenishment logistics are managed through armoured carrier contracts with fixed visit frequencies, making over-ordering costly to unwind.

### CARD 10 [Automation|S] Branch Cash Replenishment Forecast
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/physical-branches/cash-management/branch-cash-replenishment-forecast
intent: Agent forecasts branch vault cash requirements from historical draw patterns, calendar events, and local deposit mix, generating the weekly replenishment order for treasury operations review. The forecast carries a confidence range per branch, accounting for payroll cycles, public holidays, and local demand variability that manual orders cannot model accurately. Branch managers review the recommendation before submission, retaining decision authority while eliminating the manual calculation burden.
Problem to solve: Branch cash replenishment orders are calculated manually by branch managers from recent actuals, with fixed safety margins that do not reflect demand variability. Over-ordering ties up working capital at overnight funding cost; under-ordering creates service failures at peak demand periods. Demand variability from payroll cycles, public holidays, and local events exceeds what a manager can model accurately from recent history alone.
Solution: Agent reads historical vault cash draw data, local transaction patterns, and calendar inputs to generate a weekly replenishment order per branch with a confidence range. The branch manager reviews and confirms before submission to treasury operations. Out-of-service events from vault depletion and average idle cash balance are the primary outcome metrics tracked against the pre-deployment baseline.
OKR objective: A weekly branch vault replenishment order with a per-branch confidence range is available for treasury operations review, generated from historical draw patterns, calendar events, and local deposit mix.
OKR KR [Adoption]: Agent produces replenishment recommendations for ≥95% of branches on each weekly ordering cycle for ≥48 consecutive weeks post go-live.
OKR KR [Acceptance]: ≥85% of agent-generated replenishment orders confirmed by branch managers without material volume amendment before submission to treasury.
OKR KR [Cycle]: Branch manager order calculation time reduced from 45–90 minutes of manual actuals review per branch to ≤10 minutes of recommendation confirmation.

Branch managers review and attest digitally; the complete reconciliation record is maintained in the document management system for supervisory access. Reconciliation preparation time per branch and the rate at which discrepancies above tolerance are detected and escalated on the day of occurrence are tracked against the pre-deployment manual baseline.

### CARD 12 [Insights|M] Branch Cash Demand Intelligence
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-channels/physical-branches/cash-management/branch-cash-demand-intelligence
intent: Agent produces a weekly branch cash demand brief decomposed by location, day-of-week pattern, and calendar event, surfacing the branches and periods where current vault cash levels and replenishment intervals create the highest service failure risk. The brief identifies branches where demand has shifted materially from the prior-period baseline and quantifies the working capital cost of current over-ordering patterns. The Head of Treasury Operations reviews the brief and directs replenishment logistics adjustments before the next ordering cycle.
Problem to solve: Branch vault cash management is optimised at the branch level through manager-initiated replenishment orders; a portfolio-level view of cash demand patterns, idle cash concentration, and service failure risk across the branch network is not available to treasury operations. Branches with elevated service failure risk — where demand variability exceeds the current replenishment interval — are not distinguishable from lower-risk locations without discrete analytical work per branch. The working capital cost of network-wide over-ordering is material but is not quantified in the current reporting model; treasury operations cannot identify where idle cash reduction would have the greatest impact without a consolidated demand intelligence view.
Solution: Agent reads vault cash draw records and replenishment history across the branch network, computes weekly demand forecasts by location, and produces the portfolio-level brief covering service failure risk by branch, idle cash concentration, and branches where demand has shifted from the prior baseline. The brief quantifies the working capital cost of current over-ordering patterns and estimates the impact of targeted interval reductions at high-idle-cash locations. The Head of Treasury Operations reviews the brief and directs replenishment interval adjustments; out-of-service events from vault depletion and aggregate idle cash balance across the network are tracked as primary outcome metrics.
