Market share by product
The bank's share of each product market — retail deposits, consumer credit, mortgages, SME lending, corporate facilities, card-spend volume — measured against total market size from regulator and industry-association data. Product-level market share is the primary output of competitive positioning analysis: it reveals where the bank is gaining, stable, or ceding ground to peers and non-bank alternatives. In Kazakhstan, NBK publishes monthly banking-sector statistics with a six-to-eight-week lag; in Russia, CBR quarterly reports are the primary source.

### CARD 1 [Automation|S] Product Market-Share Monitor
urn: urn:financial-services:scenario:customer-market-intelligence/market-share-positioning/market-share-by-product/market-share-monitor-product
intent: Agent monitors NBK, NBKR, and CBR regulatory data releases, extracts product-level banking-sector statistics on publication, and produces a market-share narrative for strategy and IR leadership on each new release.
Problem to solve: Regulatory data releases from NBK, NBKR, and CBR arrive on irregular schedules and require manual extraction, peer comparison, and narrative production before leadership can read the market-share picture. The process consumes two to three analyst days per release and is often completed late relative to when the strategy team needs the information.
Solution: Agent monitors regulator publication schedules, extracts relevant product-level statistics on each release, computes the bank's position against the peer set, and produces a market-share narrative for strategy and IR leadership. Board pack commentary on market position is sourced directly from the agent-generated output.

OKR KR [Acceptance]: ≥80% of market-share narratives confirmed as materially accurate and publication-ready by the strategy lead without requiring manual reanalysis.
OKR KR [Cycle]: Market-share narrative production cycle reduced from 3–5 days of manual regulatory data extraction and commentary drafting to ≤24 hours of automated extraction and agent-assisted review.

Peer product benchmarking
Systematic comparison of the bank's product features, pricing tiers, and service terms against the peer set — the input that informs product-refresh decisions and reveals where the bank has feature gaps or pricing vulnerabilities. Peer benchmarking must cover the full competitive set: state-bank incumbents, private domestic peers, foreign-bank subsidiaries, and digital challengers. In KZ and KG markets, the digital-challenger set now includes several mobile-only neobanks with pricing structures that undercut traditional banks on fee transparency.

Market share by segment
Market share disaggregated by customer segment — retail mass, emerging affluent, SME micro, SME core, corporate — revealing where the bank holds a defensible position and where it is underweight relative to its strategic priorities. Segment market share is the harder measurement: regulatory data is product-level, not segment-level, so segment share requires combining regulatory data with customer-research estimates and internal portfolio composition. Strategy teams rely on annual commissioned research for segment-share estimates in the absence of an automated assembly process.

Competitor strategic moves
Monitoring and synthesising strategic announcements, M&A activity, regulatory filings, and investor-communication signals from peer institutions — the intelligence layer that reveals where competitors are placing strategic bets before the impact is visible in market-share data. In KZ and RU markets, competitor strategic moves are disclosed through stock exchange announcements for listed banks, NBK and CBR regulatory filings, and press releases. Synthesising this signal requires multi-source aggregation that most strategy teams perform manually and inconsistently.
