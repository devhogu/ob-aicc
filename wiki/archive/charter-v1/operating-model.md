# Operating Model

## 1. Purpose and scope

1.1. This Operating Model sets out how the AI Competence Center ("AICC") operates: its structure, its management of the
Portfolio, its Delivery Program, and its cadence.

1.2. It applies to AICC and to the Domains and Control Functions of the Bank and of each Participating Entity that work
with AICC.

1.3. It applies the values and principles of the Statement of Intent. The terms used in it are defined in the Vocabulary
and Style.

1.4. The procedures that carry out this Operating Model are set out in the Governance Forums, the Decision Management, the
Portfolio Management, the Service Catalog, the Registers, the Reporting, and the Artifact Standards.

## 2. Overview of AICC

2.1. AICC is the governance framework and program office for the adoption of AI across the Bank and the Group.

2.2. AICC provides two things to the Bank and the Group: a managed Portfolio of Initiatives and Use Cases, and a delivery
capability, consisting of a Delivery Program together with the standards, practices, and enablement on which it
depends.

2.3. AICC does not own the AI Platform, the Solutions, or the business results of the Domains. Execution is carried out
with the Domains by Domain Experts, who adopt and promote Solutions; AI Solution Engineers, who build and run them; and
Domain Owners, who act as product owners and own the results. AICC directs and guides this work, and may supply AI
Solution Engineers.

2.4. AICC comprises the AICC Hub, which is described in section 9, and the Roles in the Domains, which are described in
section 10.

## 3. Values and principles of AICC

3.1. The AICC Values are alignment, openness, respect for people, and relentless improvement.

3.2. The Values and the Principles of Adoption and of Application are stated in the Statement of Intent. AICC applies them
in its Stages, policies, and decisions.

3.3. AICC works in accordance with the following Principles of Delivery:

(a) decisions are taken on an economic view, by value and cost of delay relative to effort;

(b) work is organized around business value, that is, the products and services of the Bank and the Group, and not
around projects or technology;

(c) all work is made visible, work in progress is limited, and flow is managed;

(d) delivery is incremental and based on evidence: a Solution is piloted, measured against defined success Measures, and
then scaled;

(e) planning follows a common cadence and is aligned across Domains each quarter, decisions are taken at the lowest
competent level, and only strategy, cross-Domain trade-offs, and control decisions are taken centrally;

(f) delivery proceeds at product speed within bank-grade control; and

(g) people are given training, purpose, and autonomy, and the way of working is improved continuously.

## 4. Organizational positioning

4.1. AICC is a first-line function. It provides the governance framework and the program office for the adoption of AI in
the Bank and in the Participating Entities.

4.2. Each Entity retains its own regulatory obligations, accountability, Control Functions, and data. Data and decisions
remain within the Entity unless a Group Arrangement permits sharing.

4.3. The Control Functions are independent of AICC. Each Control Function names a Control Function Contact for each Entity
and regulatory regime concerned. AICC does not set the rules of a Control Function and does not validate its own work.

4.4. AICC reports to the Executive Sponsor. The Board Committee receives a periodic report on the Portfolio.

## 5. Sponsorship and steering

5.1. The Executive Sponsor holds the mandate of AICC and the investment funding for the Portfolio, appoints the AICC
Lead, and answers for AICC to executive management.

5.2. The Executive Sponsor decides the Strategic Priorities, the Investment Envelopes, and the Investment Guardrails on the
recommendation of the AI Steering Committee. The AI Steering Committee directs the Portfolio, as the Roles and
Responsibilities and the Governance Forums state.

5.3. The Board Committee oversees AI on behalf of the Board. It receives the periodic report of AICC on the Portfolio,
its risks, and its benefits.

5.4. The Executive Sponsor, the AI Steering Committee, and the Board Committee are Roles defined in the Roles and
Responsibilities. The Terms of Reference of the AI Steering Committee, and of every other Forum, are in the Governance
Forums. The Decisions that they take follow the Decision Management.

## 6. Portfolio management

6.1. The Portfolio is governed in three parts.

6.2. **Strategy and investment.** The Executive Sponsor decides a small number of Strategic Priorities from the Statement of
Intent and an Investment Envelope for each, on the recommendation of the AI Steering Committee. Funding is allocated to Strategic Priorities
and to the capacity of Delivery Teams, and not to individual projects. The Investment Guardrails determine the amount
that may be committed without further approval, the Initiatives that require an Initiative Brief and approval by the AI
Steering Committee, and the investment mix expected across new Solutions, enabling work, and Solutions in operation.

6.3. **Portfolio operations.** The Portfolio and Value Delivery Office conducts intake, ranking, capacity balancing,
cadences, and reporting.

6.4. **Control.** Control requirements form part of the Portfolio Flow as explicit policies at each Stage. Evidence is
drawn from the records of the AI Platform. The Control Function Contacts take part in Intake, in validation, and in the
Control Review.

## 7. Work structure and backlogs

7.1. The work of AICC is structured in four levels. The backlogs and the Roadmaps are kept as Records of the Portfolio, in
accordance with the Portfolio Management and the Artifact Standards.

| Level | Description | Backlog | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Strategic Priority | A direction stated in the Statement of Intent, with an Investment Envelope | Portfolio priorities | Executive Sponsor |
| Initiative | A larger investment that may cross Domains, Entities, or quarters, described in an Initiative Brief | Portfolio Backlog | Domain Owner, with the Portfolio Manager |
| Use Case | A deliverable for one Domain with a measurable benefit | Delivery Backlog | Domain Owner |
| Work Item | A task of a Delivery Team | Team board | AI Solution Engineer and Domain Expert |

7.2. Work Items are managed by the Delivery Team on its own board.

7.3. Enabling work supports Use Cases and is ranked in the same backlogs. It comprises AI Platform capabilities,
architecture, governance and policy work, and exploration.

7.4. A Domain is a business function or a product line of the Bank or of an Entity.

7.5. The Portfolio Manager ranks the backlogs by the value and urgency of each item relative to its effort, taking into
account risk reduction and the opportunities the item opens. The ranking sequences the work. It is combined with
evidence for the benefit, available capacity, dependencies, and the option to stop.

## 8. Portfolio Flow

8.1. Every Initiative and every Use Case moves through the Stages below. Each Stage has explicit entry and exit policies
that are set by Risk Tier and are stated in the Portfolio Management.

| Stage | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Intake | Ideas and needs are captured, checked for fit with the Strategic Priorities, assigned a Risk Tier, and sized |
| Discovery | Benefits, cost, and risks are assessed; an Impact Assessment is made for a Use Case that affects customers or has a higher Risk Tier; and an Initiative Brief is written where the Investment Guardrails require one. Approved work waits, ranked, until capacity is free |
| Pilot | A narrow trial with a trained Domain Expert in real work, or a controlled experiment with defined success Measures |
| Scale | Adoption across the Domain after the decision of the Domain Owner |
| Operate | The Solution runs, is monitored, and is improved by the Delivery Team that built it. An AI Incident is handled in accordance with the AI Incident Policy |
| Retire | The Solution is withdrawn or replaced |

## 9. The AICC Hub

9.1. The AICC Hub is a small, dedicated team led by the AICC Lead, whose Role is defined in the Roles and Responsibilities.

9.2. The AICC Hub has three groups.

9.3. **The Portfolio and Value Delivery Office** combines three responsibilities:

(a) portfolio management, comprising intake, ranking, funding recommendations, capacity balancing, and the retirement of
Initiatives;

(b) the agile program office, comprising the visual board, the policies for moving work between Stages, the Limits on
Work in Progress, the regular cadences, the tracking of dependencies and risks, reporting, and lean guidance, and
including the Functional Direction of AI Solution Engineers; and

(c) value delivery, comprising the definition, tracking, and realization of benefits and outcome reporting to the AI
Steering Committee.

9.4. **Architecture and Engineering** is responsible for the architecture of Solutions, the technical guardrails and standards
kept in the Standards Record, and the approval of the design of Solutions. Where AICC supplies the AI Solution Engineer, it also builds the Solution for the
Domain.

9.5. **The Enablement Group** is responsible for coaching, training, Communities of Practice, templates, and the portal.
It trains Domain Experts, supports AI Solution Engineers with practices and standards, and moves the Bank and the Group
toward self-sufficient Domains. It is the agile center of excellence of AICC and focuses on coaching and learning rather
than control.

## 10. Roles in the Domains

10.1. Delivery in a Domain rests on the Roles described in this section. They sit in the Domains or in the technology
function, outside the AICC Hub, except where AICC supplies the AI Solution Engineer. Each Role is defined in the Roles
and Responsibilities.

10.2. **The Domain Owner** owns the results of AI adoption in the Domain and acts as the product owner of its Use Cases.
For a product line, the Domain Owner is the owner of that product. The Domain Owner decides whether the Domain takes part
in an Initiative, names the Domain Expert, funds the Use Cases of the Domain, accepts the Solution into use, decides its
rollout, and confirms at each Quarterly Planning and Review Event the value achieved.

10.3. **The Domain Expert** is a specialist of the Domain, appointed by the Domain Owner, who acts as early adopter. The
Domain Expert explains the work and its requirements, is trained by the Enablement Group, tries the Solution in real
work, and then scales adoption and trains colleagues. The Domain Expert remains in the reporting line of the Domain.

10.4. **The AI Solution Engineer** is a technical specialist who builds, releases, and runs Solutions for a Domain. The
same AI Solution Engineer builds and operates a Solution, so that what is learned in operation informs its design. The
AI Solution Engineer is an engineer and not a specialist of the Domain, and works in or for the Domain that uses the
Solution.

10.5. An AI Solution Engineer is supplied under one of three arrangements.

| Arrangement | Line management | Direction of AI work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AICC: the AICC Lead or a member of the AICC Hub, working for a Domain | AICC Lead | AICC, with the Domain Owner for priorities |
| Allocated from the technology function to a Domain or a Use Case | Technology function | Functional Direction by AICC |
| Dedicated technology specialist of the Domain | Domain Owner | Functional Direction by AICC |

10.6. Under Functional Direction, AICC sets the priorities, standards, architecture guardrails, and working practices for
the AI work of the AI Solution Engineer, and reviews that work. Line management, employment, and performance remain with
the line manager, who consults AICC.

## 11. Control Functions and the AI Platform

11.1. The Control Functions are the model risk, compliance, information security, data protection, legal, and internal
audit functions. Each names a Control Function Contact for each Entity and regulatory regime concerned. The Control
Function Contact is a member of the Control Function and not of AICC.

11.2. The duties of the Control Function Contact are defined in the Roles and Responsibilities. The decisions of the Control
Function Contact are recorded in the Portfolio.

11.3. The AI Platform is provided and operated by the Platform Owner outside AICC. AICC states the requirements that the
use of AI places on it in the Standards Record.

## 12. Delivery Program

12.1. The Delivery Teams of all Domains operate as one Delivery Program on a common cadence. Each Delivery Team pulls work
continuously from the Delivery Backlog within the Limits on Work in Progress. The Delivery Program is aligned by the
following events.

12.2. **The Quarterly Planning and Review Event.** The Domain Owners state the business context and priorities, the Lead
Architect states the technical direction, and the Delivery Lead facilitates. The Delivery Teams commit to Objectives for
the quarter. At the review, the Domain Owners score the value achieved against each Objective, the Delivery Teams review
flow and quality Measures, and agreed improvements enter the Delivery Backlog. Risks are reviewed with the Control Function
Contacts.

12.3. **The Delivery Review** takes place every two weeks. The Delivery Teams demonstrate working Solutions to the Domain
Owners and Domain Experts, and the next items are chosen from the Delivery Backlog.

12.4. **The Communities of Practice** enable Domain Experts and AI Solution Engineers to share practice across Domains,
supported by the Enablement Group.

## 13. Engagement of a Domain

13.1. AICC engages a Domain in the following sequence.

(a) The AICC Lead discusses adoption with the Domain Owner and identifies routine work that the Domain wishes to automate.

(b) The Domain Owner agrees and names a Domain Expert, usually a person who performs that routine work. The first Use
Case is narrow. Other Domain Experts and needs join later.

(c) The AICC Lead secures an AI Solution Engineer under one of the arrangements in section 10.5.

(d) The Domain Expert explains the work and its requirements. The AI Solution Engineer, the Domain Expert, and the AICC
Lead define the technical Solution. The Use Case is assigned a Risk Tier with the Control Function Contacts.

(e) The AI Solution Engineer builds the Solution. The Control Function Contacts validate it in accordance with the Risk
Tier.

(f) The Domain Expert is trained on the Solution and tries it in real work.

(g) The Domain Owner decides whether the Solution works, accepts it, and decides its adoption across the Domain.

(h) The Domain Expert supports colleagues in adopting the Solution. The AI Solution Engineer operates and improves it. The
Portfolio and Value Delivery Office tracks benefits, and the Use Case is scaled, adjusted, or retired.

## 14. Processes and cadence

14.1. The Governance Forums define every Forum, with its purpose, Chair, members, and frequency. This section states the
cadence of the work.

14.2. **Continuous.** Work flows through the Stages of the Portfolio Flow. Each Stage and each Domain has a Limit on Work
in Progress. New work is pulled only when capacity is free. Blocked work is escalated.

14.3. **Continuous delivery.** The same Delivery Team designs, builds, tests, releases, and operates a Solution. Solutions
are evaluated automatically before release, released when ready, and monitored in operation. AI Incidents and drift are
handled by the Delivery Team, with the Control Function Contacts consulted. Controls apply by Risk Tier, so that a change
of low risk proceeds at product speed.

14.4. **Yearly.** The AI Steering Committee holds its yearly session in the first quarter, as the Governance Forums state. The
Strategic Priorities, the Investment Envelopes, the Investment Guardrails, and the roadmap are set, and the Operating Model,
the Evolution Plan, the AI Risk Appetite Statement, and the Corpus are reviewed.

14.5. **Quarterly.** The Quarterly Planning and Review Event and the Control Review are held, and the AI Steering
Committee reviews the Portfolio and the roadmap.

14.6. **Every two weeks.** Replenishment and the Delivery Review are held.

14.7. **Daily.** The AICC Hub and the AI Solution Engineers hold the Stand-up to clear blockers.

14.8. **As required.** The Design Review is held when a Solution requires approval of its design.
