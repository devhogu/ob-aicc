# Corpus Assessment

## 1. Purpose and scope

1.1. This document defines how the documents in the charter folder, taken singly and as a whole, are assessed for readiness. It
sets the criteria, the checklist, the scoring, the Readiness Levels, the categories of Finding, and the records of an
Assessment.

1.2. It applies to every document in the charter folder and to the Corpus that they form. It also applies to the Records of the
Portfolio and to the published copies of the documents, as far as the criteria in section 6 state.

1.3. The Assessment is the standing authority for accepting a document, and for accepting the Corpus, as ready. A document or
the Corpus is not accepted or applied as ready until the Assessment shows the Readiness Level that the acceptance rule in
section 9 requires.

## 2. Principles

2.1. The Assessment is uniform: the same criteria and the same checklist apply to every document and to the Corpus.

2.2. The Assessment is evidence-based. Each Finding states the evidence, and each score follows from the checklist.

2.3. The Assessment is repeatable. Checks that can be carried out mechanically are stated as such, so that they can be
repeated in the same way each time.

2.4. The Assessment is independent. A document is not assessed by its author.

2.5. The Assessment is proportionate. It tests that the Corpus is not more complex than the operation of AICC requires.

## 3. Roles

3.1. The Delivery Lead holds the Assessor duty. The Portfolio Manager assesses the documents that the Delivery Lead has written.

3.2. The AICC Lead, as owner of every document, answers each Finding and carries out the actions.

3.3. The Executive Sponsor accepts the readiness of the Corpus and activates the documents that the Document Catalog assigns to
the Executive Sponsor.

3.4. Internal audit may sample the Assessment each year and has read access to every report.

3.5. A person who holds the Assessor duty does not assess a document that the person wrote. The Control Function Contact of
Internal audit, or another Control Function Contact, assesses that document. The holder of the AICC Lead Role does not assess a
document that the holder wrote, whichever other Role the holder holds.

## 4. When the Assessment is made

4.1. A document is assessed before it is activated.

4.2. The whole Corpus is assessed each quarter, in advance of the AI Steering Committee. The result is reported in the Quarterly
Report.

4.3. The Corpus, or the documents affected, is assessed when an active document that the Document Catalog assigns to the Executive Sponsor is amended; when a finding of audit or
of a supervisor concerns the documents; and when the structure of the Operating Model changes.

## 5. Readiness Levels

5.1. Each document has one Readiness Level.

| Readiness Level | Name | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R0 | Absent | A document required by the Operating Model or another document does not exist |
| R1 | Stub | The document records its title and intent only |
| R2 | Drafted | The document is written, and gaps or inconsistencies remain |
| R3 | Defined | The document is complete for its purpose, conforms, and is consistent, and is ready for activation |
| R4 | Active | The document has been activated, and it is published and in effect |
| R5 | Operating | The document is applied in practice, with Records that show its use, and is reviewed on its interval |

5.2. A document that is a Template, a Register, or a Record seeded by design is assessed on the criteria that apply to it, and
its placeholders are not counted as gaps.

5.3. The Readiness Level of the Corpus is stated as the number of documents at each Readiness Level, together with the score of
each Corpus criterion.

## 6. Criteria and checklist

6.1. Each document is assessed on ten criteria, D-1 to D-10. Each criterion has checklist items. An item passes or fails. A
checklist item that cannot be assessed fails. The method of an item is mechanical (M), which means it can be repeated by a
program or by counting, or judgment (J). The severity is the severity of a Finding if the item fails.

## 7. Scoring

7.1. Each criterion is scored from the items that fail.

| Score | Name | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | Fully met | No failed item is above Minor, and at most two Minor items fail |
| 2 | Largely met | No Blocker item fails, and at most two Major items fail |
| 1 | Partly met | A Blocker item fails, or more than two Major items fail |
| 0 | Not met | The criterion could not be assessed, or the document is absent |

7.2. A document is a stub if its status is stub. A stub scores 0 on D-3 and D-8, and is assigned Readiness Level R1.

7.3. The Readiness Level of a document that is not a stub follows the scores.

| Readiness Level | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| R2 | The default for a written document |
| R3 | D-1 to D-5 score 3; D-6 to D-10 score at least 2; and no Blocker Finding is open |
| R4 | R3 is met, no Major Finding against the document is open, and the document has been activated |
| R5 | R4 is met, D-8 scores 3 with Records that show its use, and the document is reviewed on time |

## 8. Complexity budget

8.1. The Corpus stays within the following limits. A limit is changed only by a Decision of the Standards category.

| Construct | Limit | Active at Maturity Level 1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Roles | 15 | Those in the Register of Appointments |
| Forums | 8 | No more than 5 |
| Registers | 8 | No more than 4 |
| Templates | 25 | No more than 10 |
| Defined terms | 100 | Not applicable |
| Rows of the Decision Rights | 50 | Not applicable |
| Decision Categories | 7 | Not applicable |
| Documents of the charter folder | 30 | Not applicable |
| Lines in a document | 350 | Not applicable |
| Content restated in more than one document | 5 percent of the clauses | Not applicable |

## 9. Acceptance

9.1. A document is accepted for activation when its Readiness Level is R3 or higher and no Finding above Minor is open against it.

9.2. The Corpus is accepted as ready for activation when every document that the Document Catalog assigns to the Executive Sponsor, and every document of the
categories MND, ORG, and GOV, is at R3 or higher; no document that the Operating Model requires is at R0 or R1; every Corpus
criterion scores at least 2; and no Blocker Finding is open.

9.3. The Corpus is accepted as ready to operate when those documents are at R4 or higher, every Corpus criterion scores at least 2,
C-9 scores 3, and no Major Finding is open.

9.4. The Executive Sponsor accepts the readiness of the Corpus by a Decision of the Strategic category, on the recommendation of
the AICC Lead and the Assessor.

## 10. Findings

10.1. A Finding has an identifier F-nnn, a category, a severity, the documents affected, the evidence, and a recommendation
with an owner and a date.

10.2. The categories of Finding are:

(a) gap: required content or a required document is missing;

(b) ghost: something is referred to that does not exist;

(c) orphan: a document is referred to by no other document;

(d) legacy: a superseded name, path, or Role remains;

(e) drift: two copies of the same content differ;

(f) duplication: the same content is stated in more than one place;

(g) misalignment: two documents state the same thing differently;

(h) incoherence: a document does not fit its own purpose or the structure of the Corpus;

(i) overengineering: a construct exists that the operation of AICC does not need at its Maturity Level;

(j) overcomplexity: a construct is more elaborate than its purpose needs; and

(k) non-conformance: a document does not follow the Vocabulary and Style or the Document Catalog.

10.3. The severities are Blocker, Major, and Minor. A Blocker Finding prevents a Readiness Level of R3. A Major Finding prevents
activation. A Minor Finding is corrected in the ordinary course.

10.4. An open Finding is tracked as an issue in the Risk and Issue Register, with the identifier of the Finding, until it is
closed.

## 11. Records

11.1. Each Assessment produces the following Records, kept in the assessments folder of the Records of the Portfolio, in a folder
named with the date of the Assessment.

| Record | Content | Template |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Corpus Assessment Report | The summary, the Readiness Level of each document, the maturity matrix, the scores of the Corpus criteria, the Findings, the complexity measures, and the recommendations | Corpus Assessment Report |
| Document Assessment Report | For each document: its Readiness Level, the score on each criterion, the Findings, and the recommendations | Document Assessment Report |

11.2. The maturity matrix shows each document against each criterion, with its score and its Readiness Level.

11.3. The Quarterly Report states the Readiness Level of each document, the score of each Corpus criterion, and the Findings
that are open.

11.4. Each Corpus Assessment Report states the traceability from the requirements of the Operating Model and the Statement of
Intent to their carriers, as criteria C-1.2 and C-7.2 require.

## 12. Mechanical checks

12.1. The following checks are mechanical and are repeated in the same way at each Assessment: completeness of the header; numbering
of sections and clauses; use of excluded terms; lower-case use of defined terms; capitalized terms that are not defined; defined
terms that are not used; checks that links and cross-references resolve; agreement of counts and lists between documents and
tables; agreement of the metadata blocks with the Document Catalog, and of the revised date with the change log; agreement of the Document Catalog, the Operating Model, and the Artifact Standards with the
documents that exist; legacy names, paths, and Roles; agreement of the Decision Rights with the Roles and Responsibilities; the
counts against the complexity budget; and the agreement of the published copies with the active text.

12.2. The other checks are judgments. The report states the evidence for each.
