# Roles and Responsibilities

## 1. Purpose and scope

1.1. This document defines the Roles used in the Operating Model and in the Decision Rights, with the responsibilities and
authority of each.

1.2. A Role is a set of responsibilities and authority. A Position is a job in the organization chart. This document
defines Roles only. The Register of Appointments records the Positions and Holders of each Role.

1.3. One person may hold several Roles, and one Role may have several Holders, subject to the rules of separation in
section 8.

1.4. The Roles apply to the Bank and to each Participating Entity. Where an Entity has its own regulator, Control
Functions, or products, the Roles of the Domain and of the Control Functions are held within that Entity.

## 2. Mandate and oversight

### 2.1. Executive Sponsor

2.1.1. **Purpose.** The Executive Sponsor holds the mandate of AICC and answers for it to executive management.

2.1.2. **Responsibilities.** The Executive Sponsor:

(a) chairs the AI Steering Committee;

(b) activates the documents that the Document Catalog assigns to the Executive Sponsor;

(c) approves the yearly strategy, the roadmap, and the Investment Envelopes;

(d) approves the AI Risk Appetite Statement;

(e) accepts the readiness of the Corpus;

(f) appoints the AICC Lead; and

(g) reports to the Board Committee.

2.1.3. **Authority.** The Executive Sponsor decides on the mandate, the Investment Envelopes, and the appointment of the
AICC Lead.

2.1.4. The Executive Sponsor resolves disputes that the AI Steering Committee cannot resolve.

### 2.2. AI Steering Committee

2.2.1. **Purpose.** The AI Steering Committee is the collective body that directs the Portfolio.

2.2.2. **Responsibilities.** The AI Steering Committee:

(a) recommends the Strategic Priorities, the Investment Envelopes, and the Investment Guardrails to the Executive Sponsor;

(b) approves Initiatives above the threshold set by the Investment Guardrails;

(c) confirms the priorities of the Portfolio each quarter;

(d) resolves conflicts between Domains and between Entities;

(e) reviews benefits;

(f) decides the retirement of an Initiative; and

(g) decides the release of a Risk Tier 4 Use Case.

2.2.3. **Authority.** The Executive Sponsor decides the Strategic Priorities, the Investment Envelopes, and the Investment
Guardrails on the recommendation of the AI Steering Committee. The AI Steering Committee decides the Initiatives that require
its approval.

2.2.4. The members of the AI Steering Committee are stated in the Governance Forums.

### 2.3. Board Committee

2.3.1. **Purpose.** The Board Committee oversees AI on behalf of the Board.

2.3.2. **Responsibilities.** The Board Committee receives the periodic report on the Portfolio, its risks, and its
benefits.

2.3.3. **Authority.** The Board Committee has oversight and challenge. It does not take part in delivery.

## 3. AICC Hub

### 3.1. AICC Lead

3.1.1. **Purpose.** The AICC Lead leads AICC as a working lead: the lead technical authority of AICC and the accountable
leader of its governance framework and program office. The Role is not administrative.

3.1.2. **Responsibilities.** The AICC Lead:

(a) proposes the strategy and the roadmap;

(b) discusses adoption with Domain Owners and identifies routine work suitable for automation;

(c) secures an AI Solution Engineer for each Use Case;

(d) is accountable for Intake, the way of working, the tracking and reporting of benefits and flow, the Functional
Direction of AI Solution Engineers, the Register of Appointments, the AI Incident Register, and the Exception Register;

(e) is the owner of every document of the charter folder, decides the Templates, and activates the documents that the Document
Catalog assigns to the AICC Lead;

(f) prepares the Quarterly Report; and

(g) reports to the AI Steering Committee and, through the Executive Sponsor, to the Board Committee.

3.1.3. **Authority.** The AICC Lead decides Intake, sets the way of working, and directs AI work under Functional
Direction.

3.1.4. The AICC Lead may hold the Lead Architect, Delivery Lead, and AI Solution Engineer Roles until they are separated.

3.1.5. The AICC Lead shall not validate or stop a Use Case within the remit of a Control Function.

### 3.2. Lead Architect

3.2.1. **Purpose.** The Lead Architect holds the design authority for Solutions built or directed by AICC.

3.2.2. **Responsibilities.** The Lead Architect:

(a) sets the architecture of Solutions, the technical standards, and the technical guardrails, and states in the Standards
Record the requirements that the use of AI places on the AI Platform;

(b) defines the technical Solution for each Use Case with the AI Solution Engineer and the Domain Expert; and

(c) approves the design of Solutions against the Standards Record.

3.2.3. **Authority.** The Lead Architect decides on architecture and technical standards for work directed by AICC. The
Lead Architect does not decide the Templates.

### 3.3. Portfolio Manager

3.3.1. **Purpose.** The Portfolio Manager is responsible for portfolio management and value delivery.

3.3.2. **Responsibilities.** The Portfolio Manager:

(a) takes in and triages Use Cases;

(b) maintains the Portfolio Backlog, is consulted on the Delivery Backlog, and ranks the items by value and urgency relative to effort;

(c) balances capacity;

(d) assists Domain Owners in writing Initiative Briefs;

(e) prepares funding and retirement recommendations;

(f) defines benefit Measures with the Domain;

(g) tracks and reports benefits and outcomes and provides them to reviews of the Portfolio;

(h) keeps the Portfolio Backlog, the Portfolio Roadmap, the Service Portfolio, the Decision Register, and the Benefits
Register;

(i) holds the Secretary duty of the AI Steering Committee, the Design Review, and the Control Review; and

(j) assesses the documents that the Delivery Lead has written.

### 3.4. Delivery Lead

3.4.1. **Purpose.** The Delivery Lead is responsible for the agile program office and keeps work flowing across Domains.

3.4.2. **Responsibilities.** The Delivery Lead:

(a) maintains the visual board of all Use Cases and the policies for moving work between Stages;

(b) sets and monitors the Limits on Work in Progress;

(c) facilitates the Quarterly Planning and Review Event and conducts the fortnightly cadences;

(d) coordinates delivery across Domains;

(e) tracks dependencies, blockers, and risks;

(f) reports flow Measures;

(g) carries out the Assessment of the documents as the Assessor;

(h) keeps the Delivery Backlog, the Program Roadmap, the Roster, and the Risk and Issue Register;

(i) carries out the Functional Direction of AI Solution Engineers on behalf of the AICC Lead; and

(j) holds the Secretary duty of the Quarterly Planning and Review Event and of Replenishment.

3.4.3. **Authority.** The Delivery Lead decides how work is pulled and sequenced within the limits and priorities set by
the Portfolio.

### 3.5. Enablement Coach

3.5.1. **Purpose.** The Enablement Coach is responsible for the agile center of excellence of AICC.

3.5.2. **Responsibilities.** The Enablement Coach:

(a) trains and coaches Domain Experts and AI Solution Engineers;

(b) conducts the Communities of Practice; and

(c) maintains training material, the Templates that the AICC Lead decides, and the portal.

## 4. Domains

### 4.1. Domain Owner

4.1.1. **Purpose.** The Domain Owner owns the results of AI adoption in a Domain and acts as the product owner of its Use
Cases.

4.1.2. **Responsibilities.** The Domain Owner:

(a) decides whether the Domain takes part in an Initiative;

(b) states the outcome wanted;

(c) appoints Domain Experts and standing AI Solution Engineers;

(d) funds the Use Cases of the Domain;

(e) accepts a Solution into use and decides its rollout across the Domain;

(f) takes part in quarterly planning and scores the value achieved against the Objectives at each Quarterly Planning and
Review Event; and

(g) owns the results and the risks of the Use Cases of the Domain.

4.1.3. **Authority.** The Domain Owner decides on participation, funding, business acceptance, and rollout for the Domain.

4.1.4. The Domain Owner may delegate day-to-day work on a Use Case to a Domain Expert and remains accountable.

### 4.2. Domain Expert

4.2.1. **Purpose.** The Domain Expert is an early adopter and helper in a Domain, typically a person who performs the
routine work to be automated.

4.2.2. **Responsibilities.** The Domain Expert:

(a) explains the routine work and its requirements to the AI Solution Engineer;

(b) works with the AI Solution Engineer on the Solution;

(c) is trained on the Solution and tries it in real work;

(d) tests its fitness for the work of the Domain;

(e) scales adoption and trains colleagues once the Domain Owner has decided on rollout;

(f) brings further needs to Intake; and

(g) helps to define benefit Measures.

4.2.3. **Authority.** The Domain Expert has no authority over funding, acceptance, or control decisions.

4.2.4. The Domain Expert remains in the reporting line of the Domain.

### 4.3. AI Solution Engineer

4.3.1. **Purpose.** The AI Solution Engineer builds and runs Solutions for a Domain. The Role may be filled by the AICC
Lead, a member of the AICC Hub, a specialist from the technology function, or a dedicated technology specialist of
the Domain.

4.3.2. **Responsibilities.** The AI Solution Engineer:

(a) defines the technical Solution with the Domain Expert;

(b) develops, tests, releases, and operates Solutions, so that what is learned in operation informs the design;

(c) records Use Cases, models, and agents in the AI Registry;

(d) monitors live Solutions and handles AI Incidents;

(e) follows the standards and practices of AICC; and

(f) works to the priorities set through Functional Direction.

4.3.3. **Authority.** The AI Solution Engineer decides technical matters within the standards of AICC.

4.3.4. The AI Solution Engineer is line-managed by the AICC Lead, by the technology function, or by the Domain Owner, in
accordance with the arrangements in the Operating Model, and takes Functional Direction from AICC.

## 5. Control Functions

### 5.1. Control Function Contact

5.1.1. **Purpose.** The Control Function Contact is the named member of an independent Control Function who advises,
validates, and signs off.

5.1.2. **Responsibilities.** The Control Function Contact:

(a) advises on requirements at Intake;

(b) confirms the Risk Tier within the remit of the Control Function;

(c) validates Solutions against the standards of the Control Function;

(d) may stop a Use Case for a control breach; and

(e) records decisions in the Portfolio.

5.1.3. **Authority.** The Control Function Contact decides within the remit of the Control Function, for the Entity and the
regulatory regime that the Contact serves.

5.1.4. The Control Function Contact is not a member of AICC.

### 5.2. Remit of each Control Function

5.2.1. The remit of each Control Function is set out in the following table.

| Control Function | Remit of the Control Function Contact |
| --- | --- |
| Model risk | Risk Tier and model validation, including the validation of AI and language models |
| Compliance | Conformance with the regulation, conduct, consumer protection, and anti-money-laundering rules of the Entity |
| Information security | Security requirements, access, and protection against attacks specific to AI |
| Data protection | Lawful use of personal data, retention, cross-border transfer, and sharing between Entities |
| Legal | Contractual, intellectual property, partner, and liability matters |
| Internal audit | Independent assurance over AICC and the Portfolio |

## 6. AI Platform

### 6.1. Platform Owner

6.1.1. **Purpose.** The Platform Owner provides and operates the AI Platform.

6.1.2. **Responsibilities.** The Platform Owner:

(a) builds and operates the AI Platform to the requirements that AICC states;

(b) keeps the AI Registry current;

(c) provides the evidence, in the form of logs and traces, on which the Stages rely; and

(d) keeps the data of each Entity separated on the AI Platform as the Data Classification Policy requires.

6.1.3. **Authority.** The Platform Owner decides on the design of the AI Platform within those requirements.

6.1.4. The Platform Owner sits outside AICC.

## 7. Codes

7.1. The codes of the Roles are stated in the Decision Rights.

## 8. Rules of separation

8.1. No person validates, or signs off on control grounds, a Use Case that the person built, owns, or benefits from.

8.2. A Control Function Contact shall not hold the Domain Expert or AI Solution Engineer Role for a Use Case that the
Contact reviews.

8.3. The AICC Lead and the members of the AICC Hub shall not hold the Control Function Contact Role.

8.4. The Lead Architect shall not approve the design of a Solution that the Lead Architect built, and shall not validate a
Solution on control grounds.

8.5. The Control Function Contact of Internal audit shall not take part in delivery or in the operating decisions of the
Portfolio.

8.6. The Platform Owner shall not accept or validate Solutions built on the AI Platform.

8.7. Where the AICC Lead or another member of the AICC Hub holds the AI Solution Engineer Role for a Use Case, that person
shall not take part in validating or accepting the Use Case.

8.8. A person shall not assess a document that the person wrote. The Corpus Assessment states who assesses in that case.

## 9. Duties held by Roles

9.1. A duty is a task that a Role holds in addition to its responsibilities. A duty is not a Role. The Register of
Appointments does not record a duty.

9.2. The duties are as follows.

| Duty | Content | Held by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chair | Presides over a Forum | The Role that the Terms of Reference of the Forum name |
| Proposer | Prepares a Decision and puts it to the Decider | The Role that prepares the Decision |
| Decider | Takes a Decision | The Role that the Decision Rights show as accountable |
| Secretary | Prepares the agenda, records the Decisions, and issues the minutes of a Forum | The Role that the Terms of Reference of the Forum name |
| Assessor | Carries out an Assessment | The Delivery Lead, and the Portfolio Manager for the documents that the Delivery Lead wrote |

9.3. The Secretary issues the agenda before the meeting, records the attendance, the Decisions, the actions, and the risks raised,
writes the Decision Records, issues the minutes, and keeps them in the Records of the Portfolio, within the periods that the
Governance Forums and the Decision Management state.

9.4. The Assessor assesses each document and the Corpus in accordance with the Corpus Assessment and reports the Findings to the
AICC Lead.
