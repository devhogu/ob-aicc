# Selection approach

Start with the service problem and the outcome that would justify changing the current way of working. AI is one possible means of achieving that outcome.

## Questions to resolve

| Question | What the assessment should establish |
| --- | --- |
| What needs to improve? | The affected service or journey, the people involved and evidence of the problem. |
| What would success mean? | A measurable outcome, a credible baseline and the source of the evidence. |
| Who owns the outcome? | The business owner, the people who understand the process and the intended users. |
| What are the options? | Process improvement, deterministic automation, analytics and AI where it adds value. |
| Can the idea be evaluated? | Available sources, permitted data use, integration dependencies and a bounded evaluation. |
| What could go wrong? | Customer impact, data exposure, incorrect decisions, operational failure and limits on action. |
| What would it take to sustain? | Delivery effort, operating cost, support, adoption and continuing ownership. |

## Basis for selection

A useful selection record states the proposed scope, expected benefit, principal assumptions, dependencies and the reason to proceed. It also records what remains uncertain and what evidence would justify continuing, changing direction or stopping.

An indicative benefit or complexity estimate is a starting assumption. Assessment replaces it with evidence from the intended use and operating context.
