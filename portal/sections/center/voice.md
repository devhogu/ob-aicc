# Voice and labels

Provisional. The style guidelines of the practitioner replace this page when they are issued, and the rules below are the working proposal until then.

## Voice

1. The site speaks as a department describes itself: declarative, in the present tense, in the third person. "the Competence Center explores, trials, and proves the use of AI with the functions of the Bank."
2. A statement that rests on a rule names the clause: "Operating Model 6.6".
3. There are no calls to action, no second person, no marketing wording, and no exclamation. A reading route states a purpose, not an invitation.
4. A defined term of the Vocabulary is written as the Vocabulary writes it, with a capital letter, and links to its entry.
5. American spelling. A short sentence is preferred to a long one, and one idea to a paragraph.
6. The authored text of the site restates no rule. It introduces, orders, and links.

## Labels

1. A label is a noun or a noun phrase that names what is there: About the Competence Center, Organization, Responsible AI, Library, Reference.
2. A label of a section does not use "Resources", "Support", "Community", or an instruction.
3. A page title is the title of its source document, workflow, guide, or template, without change.
4. The same word names the same thing everywhere: the section label, the page title, the breadcrumb, and the link.

## Stamps

Every page shows its baseline revision, its date, and its owner, taken from the document, and the footer shows the baseline of the whole charter.
