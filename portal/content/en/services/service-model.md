# The service model

A service of the Competence Center is not a promise in the air. It is composed of defined parts, it has a life with service steps and gates, it is operated under named practices, and it leaves records. This page states the model that every service of the Competence Center follows, whatever its area and category. It is the template; the services in operation are its instances and are kept in the Portfolio.

## 1. The composition of a service

1.1. Every service of the Competence Center, whatever its category and whether run-rate work or an Initiative, is described by the same fields.

| Field | Meaning | Where it is set |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Area and category | One of the fifteen categories in the four areas (advise and formulate; build and run; enablement; assurance) | The Services section |
| Mode | Run-rate work, a small, repeatable request done within one Iteration as a Feature under the Standing Initiative of its service area; or an Initiative, with a business case and an MVP | Proposed by the category and confirmed by the Competence Center Lead at intake (Business Model 7.2) |
| Client | The function, represented by its Domain Owner; the Executive Sponsor for enabling work | Service Agreement; the Feature for run-rate work |
| Solution type | Experiment, Product, or Service, which sets the life after delivery | Solution Definition |
| Support level | None, on demand, agreed response targets, or run by the Competence Center; on demand only for run-rate work | Service Agreement |
| Risk Tier | 1, 2, or 3, which sets the review, the validation, the human oversight, and the speed of release | Assigned by the Competence Center Lead, or by the Executive Sponsor for a Solution that the Competence Center Lead built; AI Registry |
| Owner | The Domain Owner for the results; the Solution Engineer for the build, and for the run of a Service that the Competence Center runs; the product owner for acceptance during development | Appointments Record; Solution Definition |
| Package | The Package the service leaves, where one results, by its Package Definition | The catalog, in the Portfolio |
| Records | The Initiative Brief, the Service Agreement, the Solution Definition, the Control Sign-Off, the Acceptance Checklist, the Outcome Report, and the Service Management records, as the mode and the type require | The Registry |

## 2. The life of a service

2.1. A service is born as a need at the front door and lives through the phases of an Engagement: study, proof, delivery, support. A Service that the Competence Center runs then passes through the service steps that the page The life of a Service describes: Admitted, Catalogued, In service, Improving, Transition planned, Migrating, and Handed over or Retired, each with its question, its signals, its action, and its gate. The service steps refine the Stages Operate, Evolve, and Retire and are not states: the Service stays Active until it is Closed.

2.2. A Product lives in the hands of its consumer, supported on demand and revised through the Portfolio Backlog. An Experiment lives for its time-box and then goes to review: it is accepted with its lessons and closed, a Proposal is made, or it is rejected; when a Receiver accepts its Handover, it is closed and the Competence Center oversees the Adopted Solution.

## 3. The operation of a service

3.1. A Service that the Competence Center runs is operated under the practices that the page Service operations describes: request and incident handling by class of service, problem management, change enablement, knowledge, service levels against the Service Agreement, the run cost against the business case, and the fallback and the exit of each supplier, each in proportion to the Risk Tier of the Service.

3.2. The health of a Service is read at each Iteration Review and Demo in four signals, service levels, incidents, use, and cost, with the notices of its providers, by the Domain Owner, or the Executive Sponsor for a Service across Domains. The signals feed the sunset rule of the Service and the Quarterly Report.

## 4. The records of a service

4.1. The Solution Engineer enters each service step and the date on which it was reached in the Solution Definition and the AI Registry, and each decision leaves its record in the form of the templates of the Knowledge base. The site states the forms; the Registry and the Portfolio hold the instances; the Quarterly Report reads them.

## 5. The catalog

5.1. The catalog of the Competence Center has two parts: the Solutions, each by its Solution Definition, and the Packages, each by its Package Definition. The page Service catalog states the form of each entry. The live catalog is kept in the Portfolio (Business Model 4.4).
