# Questions people ask

Short answers to the questions people bring, grounded in the charter; each answer names where the rule is. The answers explain; the documents prevail.

## 1. Using AI at work

**May I use an AI assistant for my work?** Yes, if it is a Solution approved for your data class and your purpose and recorded in the AI Registry, and you have completed the training that AICC sets for it, or use it first under supervision as training. The Domain Owner approves the use of a Solution in the Domain. AI Policy 2.1.

**May I paste customer data, or any confidential data, into an assistant?** Only into a Solution approved for that data class. Data of a class may not be sent to a model or a service not approved for it, at any point, including discovery. AI Policy 2.2.

**Am I responsible for what the assistant produces?** Yes. A person reviews AI output before it is relied on, except where the Risk Tier allows otherwise, and the person who relies on the output or signs it is accountable for it. AI Policy 2.3.

**May I record or transcribe a meeting with AI?** Only with the consent of all participants. AI Policy 2.5.

**Can I use AI to get around a limit or a control?** No. Nobody uses AI to bypass a control, a limit, or a decision of a Control Function. AI Policy 2.5.

## 2. Starting work with AICC

**How do I bring a need to AICC?** In any form, to the AICC Lead: a conversation, a message, or a ticket in Service Management. A small, repeatable request that can be done within one Iteration is taken in as run-rate work at the Weekly Review; any other need is taken in as an Initiative if it fits a Strategic Priority, has a Domain Owner, and the limit on the Active Initiatives permits. Business Model 4.7, 4.8, and 7.2; Portfolio Management Model 5.5.

**Do I need funding or a plan of my own to start?** No. AICC does not charge the functions; the Domain pays from its Envelope for the run, the licenses, and the provider costs of a Solution. Run-rate work needs no Initiative Brief, Service Agreement, or Outcome Report of its own; an Initiative has a one-page Initiative Brief. AICC Charter 4.1; Business Model 4.8 and 6.2.

**What does AICC commit to, and what do I commit to?** AICC commits in a Service Agreement, on a best-effort basis within its capability; the function commits to nothing, and what AICC relies on from the function is written as an Assumption. Business Model 5.

**Who approves a business case?** The Domain Owner, within one Domain and below an Investment Guardrail; the Executive Sponsor above a guardrail, across Domains, or for enabling work; cleared first by the Control Function Contacts when Risk Tier 2 or 3 is expected. Portfolio Management Model 6.3 and 6.4.

**Why does AICC build a small version first?** Every Initiative is a hypothesis, probed as an MVP before the decision to continue, so that the Bank invests in what works. The approver decides to continue, pivot, defer, or reject on the leading indicators. Portfolio Management Model 7.

## 3. Risk and approval

**What is a Risk Tier, and who assigns it?** The classification of a Solution by its data class, its influence on a decision, whether it reaches a customer, and its autonomy, from 1 to 3; it sets the validation, oversight, testing, monitoring, disclosure, and release that apply. The AICC Lead assigns it when the Solution is defined; the Executive Sponsor for a Solution the AICC Lead built. A Control Function Contact may raise it, and only the Contact of model risk may lower it. AI Policy 3.

**Who checks or validates a Solution before people use it?** A person other than its builder: the Checker checks a Risk Tier 1 Solution, and the Control Function Contacts validate a Risk Tier 2 or 3 Solution, before the first real users or data. AICC does not validate its own work. AI Policy 3.3; Solution Lifecycle Model 7.1.

**Who signs before a Solution is released to everyone?** Three acceptances in order, then the release: the product owner accepts the Features; the AICC Lead gives the final acceptance of the Team; the Domain Owner accepts the working Solution as the requester; then the Domain Owner releases it beyond the first users for Risk Tier 1 and 2, the Executive Sponsor for Risk Tier 3 and where the AICC Lead is the Domain Owner, with the Acceptance Checklist signed. Solution Lifecycle Model 7.3 and 7.4.

**Can a Control Function stop a Solution?** Yes, within its remit, finally; nobody overrides a stop. Operating Model 5.4.

**What happens when AI causes an incident?** It is handled in the incident management of the Bank with the AICC Lead as a stakeholder, reviewed afterwards in an AI Incident Review, and the Executive Sponsor tells the Board Committee of a major incident without waiting for any report. AI Policy 5; AICC Charter 7.2.

**Can an exception to a control be granted?** A departure from the AI Policy is an Exception, decided by the Control Function Contact for the remit concerned, or by the AICC Lead for a requirement that AICC alone set; it states the date on which it expires, is entered in the Risks and Issues Record, and is reviewed by the Executive Sponsor each month, and an expired Exception is decided at once. A risk beyond the AI Risk Appetite Statement is not an Exception: only the Executive Sponsor may accept it, and tells the Board Committee of it without waiting for any report. AI Policy 6.1; AICC Charter 5.4.

## 4. The unit

**Who decides what?** The person who does the work, on the facts; a decision rises only when it affects another Domain or outside the Bank, cannot be reversed, exceeds a guardrail or changes a priority, or accepts a risk beyond appetite or concerns Risk Tier 3. Operating Model 5.

**Does AICC run the AI Platform?** No. The Platform Owner provides and operates it outside AICC, to the requirements AICC states. AICC Charter 3.2; Statement of Intent 10.3.

**Does AICC operate Solutions at scale?** No. It is not a platform team of the Bank and does not operate a Solution at the scale of the Bank: it proves and builds, a Solution that the Bank adopts at scale is handed to its Receiver, and a Service that AICC runs is handed over to an IT function of the Bank when the Bank should run it at scale. AICC Charter 3.2; Business Model 2.5.

**Where are the live records?** In the Registry, Jira, Confluence, and Service Management; this site holds the rules and the explanations, not the records. Operating Model 7; Records and systems, in Reference.

**Where is the person behind a Role?** In the Appointments Record of the Registry; the charter names Roles only. Operating Model 4.6.
