# MITRE ATLAS and the AI Incident Database

Two knowledge bases a bank that uses AI should know. ATLAS catalogs the techniques adversaries use against AI systems, in the matrix form security teams know from MITRE's attack framework, with case studies. The AI Incident Database collects the public record of harms and failures of AI systems, so that others can learn before they repeat them.

## 2. What it publishes

2.1. ATLAS: tactics and techniques against machine learning and AI systems, prompt injection, model extraction, data poisoning, evasion, with case studies and mitigations, mapped to the terms of the OWASP list. The AI Incident Database: incidents reported in the public record, classified by harm, sector, and system, with a taxonomy and a submission process.

## 3. Why it matters to the Bank

3.1. The security test of a Risk Tier 2 or 3 Solution against the attacks specific to AI needs a threat model, and ATLAS is its vocabulary. The AI Incident Review of the Bank learns from the incidents of others; the database is where they are collected, and the financial sector's incidents are among them.

## 4. How to take it, and for what

4.1. For threat modeling before a build and for the security test, use ATLAS with the OWASP list: which techniques apply to this Solution, which mitigations are in place. For the Lab's guardrails and for the AI Incident Review, read the database's incidents in finance and in customer service, and ask whether the same failure could happen here. Information security and the Checker use ATLAS; the Competence Center Lead uses the database in the quarterly risk check.

## 5. Cautions

5.1. ATLAS describes attacks; using its techniques against any system needs the authorization the Bank's rules require. The database holds public reports of varying quality; an incident is read as reported, not as established.
