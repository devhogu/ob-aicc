# McKinsey, the McKinsey Global Institute, and QuantumBlack

The most-cited source on the adoption and the value of AI in business: a yearly state-of-AI survey, the Global Institute's estimates of the economic potential of generative AI by function and industry, and a steady stream of pieces on AI in banking. It sets much of the vocabulary executives use, and it is read as the view of a firm that sells what it describes.

## 2. What it publishes

2.1. The yearly state-of-AI survey of adoption, use cases, value, and risk practices; the Global Institute's reports on the economic potential of AI and generative AI, with estimates by function; banking practice pieces on AI in operations, risk, and customer service; QuantumBlack's technical and organizational pieces on scaling AI, on AI agents, and on responsible AI; and case material.

## 3. Why it matters to the Bank

3.1. The surveys are the benchmark a Board or an investor is likely to quote; the Bank should know them. The function-level estimates of value are a useful first cut when a function asks where AI could matter, and the banking pieces describe what peer banks are doing. The organizational material on scaling AI, platforms, talent, operating model, maps onto the questions the Competence Center answers for the Bank.

## 4. How to take it, and for what

4.1. For the state of practice and the benchmark figures, read the yearly survey and quote it as a survey of respondents, which is what it is. For where value may lie in a function, use the Global Institute's function estimates as a starting hypothesis for a scenario, to be tested by the Bank's own MVP. For how organizations scale AI, read the operating-model pieces beside the Bank's own Operating Model and note where they differ. Do not take a case as a fact about the Bank.

## 5. Cautions

5.1. A consulting house publishes to sell; its surveys are of its clients and respondents; its estimates are potentials, not results. Read as a well-made view, verify with the Index and the institutions, and test with the Bank's own evidence.
