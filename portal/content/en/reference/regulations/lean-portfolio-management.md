# Lean portfolio management practice

The body of practice that treats a portfolio as a flow to be managed rather than a list of projects to be approved: strategic themes with investment envelopes and guardrails, a portfolio Kanban with limits on work in progress, a lean business case probed by a minimum viable product, and governance that is continuous and light. Its best-known codification is the Lean Portfolio Management competency of the Scaled Agile Framework.

## 2. What it sets

2.1. Three dimensions that interlock: strategy and investment funding, which translates strategic themes into investment and sets guardrails so that decisions can be local; agile portfolio operations, which coordinates the flow of large items through a portfolio Kanban with a regular sync and limits on work in progress; and lean governance, which carries compliance, audit, and forecasting continuously through the business case, the measures, and the review. The devices: the epic hypothesis statement and lean business case, the minimum viable product with a persevere or pivot decision, weighted shortest job first for ranking, participatory budgeting across value streams, and guardrails by investment horizon, capacity allocation, and approval thresholds.

## 3. Whom it reaches

3.1. Any organization that adopts it. It binds no one.

## 4. Relevance to the Bank

4.1. It is the practice that the Portfolio of AICC is built on, adapted to a small unit in a bank: a yearly frame instead of a rolling one, a single Team instead of many, and the clearances of the Control Functions written into the business case.

## 5. How the charter relates to it

5.1. The strategic loop with its Envelopes and Guardrails is the first dimension; the portfolio sync and the backlog care with the Kanban are the second; the portfolio review with the clearances and the measures is the third. The Portfolio Management Model states the model using the terminology established in the corpus.
