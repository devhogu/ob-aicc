# OECD Principles on Artificial Intelligence

The first intergovernmental standard on AI, adopted by the OECD members and adhering countries and taken up by the G20. It states what trustworthy AI is and what governments should do to foster it, and it supplies the definition of an AI system that most later instruments adopted.

## 1. Identity

| Item | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Kind | Intergovernmental principles |
| Issued or overseen by | Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development; adhering governments |
| Jurisdiction | Global |
| Status | Adopted 2019, revised 2024; the reference definition of an AI system used by the EU and others |

## 2. What it sets

2.1. Five principles for trustworthy AI: inclusive growth and well-being; human-centred values and fairness; transparency and explainability; robustness, security, and safety; and accountability. Five recommendations to governments on research, ecosystems, policy environment, skills, and international cooperation. The OECD AI Policy Observatory tracks national policies against them.

## 3. Whom it reaches

3.1. Governments that adhere; through them, the organizations they regulate. The principles bind no company directly, and they are the common ground that the laws and the standards of the field build on.

## 4. Relevance to the Bank

4.1. The principles are the shortest statement of what a regulator or an investor will expect of the Bank's use of AI. Where the Bank states its own principles, they should be recognizable against these.

## 5. How the charter relates to it

5.1. The Principles of Application of the Statement of Intent restate the OECD principles in the Bank's own terms: accountability, fairness, transparency and explainability, privacy, security and reliability, human oversight, and proportionate control.
