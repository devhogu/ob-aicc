# United States supervisory guidance on model risk and on AI in credit

The reference practice for the management of model risk in banks, and the position of the consumer regulator that a lender must explain a credit decision to the consumer in specific terms even when the decision was made with complex AI.

## 1. Identity

| Item | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Kind | Supervisory guidance of the federal banking and consumer agencies |
| Issued or overseen by | Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System; Office of the Comptroller of the Currency; Consumer Financial Protection Bureau |
| Jurisdiction | United States |
| Status | Model risk management guidance since 2011; guidance on adverse action notices with AI since 2022 and 2023 |

## 2. What it sets

2.1. Model risk management: a model inventory, independent validation by people without a stake in the model, documentation, ongoing monitoring, and a named owner, with the intensity proportionate to the materiality of the model. On AI in credit: the duty to give the specific and accurate reasons for an adverse action does not change because the decision used a complex model, and a lender that cannot explain its model may not use it.

## 3. Whom it reaches

3.1. Banks and lenders of the United States. It binds no bank in the Kyrgyz Republic, and it is the practice that model risk management everywhere descends from.

## 4. Relevance to the Bank

4.1. The independence of validation and the explainability of credit decisions are the two expectations a bank will meet from any supervisor. The Bank's own validation and explanation requirements are built on this practice.

## 5. How the charter relates to it

5.1. The independent check or validation of the AI Policy, the rule that the Competence Center does not validate its own work, the Control Sign-Off, and the disclosure, explanation, and contestability requirements of the higher Risk Tiers follow this practice.
