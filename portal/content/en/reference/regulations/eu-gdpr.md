# EU General Data Protection Regulation

The reference regime for the protection of personal data, and the model for the personal data laws of many countries, among them those of the region. It governs the processing and the transfer of personal data and gives persons rights over decisions taken about them by automated means.

## 1. Identity

| Item | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Kind | Regulation of the European Union |
| Issued or overseen by | European Data Protection Board and the national data protection authorities |
| Jurisdiction | European Union |
| Status | In force since 2018 |

## 2. What it sets

2.1. Lawful basis, purpose limitation, minimization, accuracy, retention, security, and accountability for the processing of personal data; rights of access, correction, erasure, and objection; limits on automated decision-making with legal or similarly significant effects, with a right to human intervention and an explanation; data protection impact assessments for high-risk processing; conditions for transfers outside the Union.

## 3. Whom it reaches

3.1. Organizations established in the Union, and organizations outside it that process the data of persons in the Union in connection with offering them goods or services or monitoring their behavior. It reaches a provider or a partner of the Bank that processes such data.

## 4. Relevance to the Bank

4.1. The personal data law of the Kyrgyz Republic and those of Kazakhstan and the Russian Federation share its concepts, and the providers of the Bank are built to comply with it. Its rules on automated decisions and impact assessments are the pattern for the Bank's own handling of AI that touches personal data.

## 5. How the charter relates to it

5.1. The data classification rules applied to AI in the AI Policy, the minimization rule, the Data Sharing Arrangement of the AI Competence Center Charter for data that leaves the Bank, and the contestability principle of the Statement of Intent follow the same pattern.
