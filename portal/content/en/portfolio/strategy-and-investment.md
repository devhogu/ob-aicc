# Strategy and investment

The Portfolio starts from a frame that is set once a year: the Strategic Priorities the Bank pursues, the Investment Envelope of each, and the Investment Guardrails that say what may be decided where. Within that frame, decisions are taken close to the work; outside it, they rise to the Executive Sponsor. This part explains the frame and the decision rights that follow from it.

## 1. The Strategic Priorities

1.1. The Strategic Priorities are the strategic themes of the Portfolio. The Executive Sponsor sets them with the Board, each with an Investment Envelope, from the strategy of the Bank and the priorities that the Domain Owners put forward, and the Statement of Intent states those in force for AI, from customer intelligence to software engineering. An Initiative is taken in only if it fits a Strategic Priority; the priorities are the first filter of the funnel and the first line of the business case.

## 2. The Investment Envelopes

2.1. An Investment Envelope is the investment the Bank sets for a Strategic Priority for the year. Funding goes to the priorities and to the Teams, not to Initiatives one by one: The Competence Center does not charge the functions, the Executive Sponsor owns the budget of the Competence Center itself, and the Domain pays from its Envelope for the run, the licenses, and the provider costs of its Solutions. A business case is affordable when its cost is within the Envelope of its priority, and each quarter the benefit confirmed by the Domain Owners is set against the Envelope, which informs the next year's decision.

2.2. The Envelope changes the nature of a decision to start. It is no longer a negotiation over funds for one piece of work; it is a decision that this need, under this priority, within this Envelope, is worth a place under the limit now. The figures themselves stay in the financial planning of the Bank; the Priorities Record points to them.

## 3. The Investment Guardrails

3.1. The Investment Guardrails state the amount that may be committed without the Executive Sponsor and the Initiatives that need the approval of the Executive Sponsor. They are the device that lets decisions be local without losing control. An Initiative that stays within one Domain and below a guardrail is approved by the Domain Owner; an Initiative that exceeds a guardrail, spans Domains, or is enabling work of the Competence Center is approved by the Executive Sponsor. The same rule decides who takes the decision after the MVP. The outcome is accepted by the Domain Owner, or by the Executive Sponsor for an item that spans Domains, is enabling work, or is an Experiment that has no Domain, whatever its size (Solution Lifecycle Model 7.3(c)).

3.2. Guardrails are governance, not its absence. A lean budget without guardrails is a blank check; guardrails are the written limits within which a Domain Owner may act alone, and the amounts and the kinds of Initiative at which the Executive Sponsor must.

## 4. Who decides what

| Decision | Within one Domain and below a guardrail | Above a guardrail, across Domains, or enabling work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Strategic Priorities, the Envelopes, the Guardrails | The Executive Sponsor, with the Board, each year | The same |
| The business case of an Initiative | Domain Owner | Executive Sponsor |
| The decision after the MVP | Domain Owner | Executive Sponsor |
| Acceptance of the outcome and confirmation of the benefit | Domain Owner, whatever the guardrail | Domain Owner; the Executive Sponsor across Domains or for enabling work |
| Intake, ranking, the limit on the Active Initiatives, pulling into work | Competence Center Lead, within the mix the Executive Sponsor sets | Competence Center Lead |
| Clearance of a business case that expects Risk Tier 2 or 3 | Control Function Contacts | Control Function Contacts |
| The mix of Initiatives for the next Program Increment | Executive Sponsor, at the quarterly Steering | The same |

## 5. How the frame is renewed

5.1. The frame is set at the yearly Steering, which is the monthly Steering of December, from the Quarterly Report of the third Program Increment and the findings of the year. It reads the benefit against each Envelope and the results against each priority, reviews the documents and the AI Risk Appetite Statement, and renews or adjusts the priorities, the Envelopes, and the Guardrails for the next year. A change that the fourth quarter calls for is taken at the first quarterly Steering of the new year. The Strategy page of About the Competence Center sets this in the strategy of the Bank.
