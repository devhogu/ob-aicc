# International Monetary Fund and the World Bank on fintech and AI

The institutions that advise the governments and the central banks of the region on digital finance, inclusion, and, increasingly, AI. Their fintech notes, working papers, and country work set the policy context in which the Bank operates, and their inclusion agenda is one the Bank shares.

## 1. Identity

| Item | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Kind | International financial institutions; policy and country work |
| Who | The International Monetary Fund and the World Bank Group, through their fintech, digital finance, and inclusion programs and their country teams |
| Access | Free; publications on their sites |

## 2. What it publishes

2.1. Fintech notes and departmental papers on digital money, payments, open finance, and AI in finance; working papers on the economics of AI; the Bali Fintech Agenda and its follow-up; the World Bank's work on financial inclusion, digital identification, and payment systems, including the Global Findex data; and country reports and technical assistance that reach the region's authorities.

## 3. Why it matters to the Bank

3.1. The region's policy on digital finance, identification, and inclusion is shaped with these institutions. The Bank's Strategic Pillar of financial inclusion and its priorities on onboarding, identification, and responsible lending sit in that context. The Findex data is the reference for inclusion figures.

## 4. How to take it, and for what

4.1. For the policy context, read the fintech notes on AI and on digital finance, and the country work on the Kyrgyz Republic and its neighbors where it is public. For inclusion figures, use the Global Findex. For the strategy pages of the Bank, cite these sources where a regional or inclusion figure is needed. The AICC Lead and the Strategy and governance category use them when a function's strategy touches inclusion or digital payments.

## 5. Cautions

5.1. The work is at the level of countries and systems, not of a bank's design choices; it tells where the policy goes, not what to build.
