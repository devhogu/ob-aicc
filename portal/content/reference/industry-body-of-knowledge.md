# Standards and frameworks

The charter of AICC is its own, and it draws on the common body of knowledge of the field: the frameworks for managing the risk of AI, the standards for managing it as a system, the principles that governments have agreed, and the practice of model risk, service management, and agile and lean delivery. This page lists the sources that matter to the Bank, in one line each, with where the charter uses them.

This is the first edition, a curated list for orientation, kept by the AICC Lead. Each source has a page under Reference; the laws and the supervisors are on the page Regulators and acts.

How to take them: a principle says why and binds no one; a standard is a yardstick an auditor or a partner may hold the Bank to, certifiable or not; a framework is a method to borrow; a practice is what the field does. The charter adopts them in its own terms and names none of their vocabulary, so that the Bank's rule stays readable on its own and can be mapped to any of them when asked.

## 2. The standards and frameworks for AI

| Source | Issued by | What it is | Where the charter uses it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0), with its Generative AI Profile](regulations/nist-ai-rmf.md) | National Institute of Standards and Technology, United States | A voluntary framework to govern, map, measure, and manage the risks of AI systems across their life cycle | The Risk Tiers and the gates before use (AI Policy 3); the AI Incident review (AI Policy 5); the measures of the Maturity Levels |
| [ISO/IEC 42001, Artificial intelligence management system](regulations/iso-iec-ai-standards.md) | International Organization for Standardization and International Electrotechnical Commission | Requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and improving an AI management system in an organization | The control loops and the controls (Operating Model 6 and 8); the AI Policy; the records and evidence (Operating Model 7) |
| ISO/IEC 23894, Guidance on AI risk management | ISO and IEC | Guidance on integrating risk management into AI activities, built on ISO 31000 | The Risk Tiers (AI Policy 3); the AI Risk Appetite Statement (AICC Charter 5) |
| ISO/IEC 22989, AI concepts and terminology | ISO and IEC | The vocabulary of the field | The Vocabulary of the charter |
| [The risk-based approach of the EU Artificial Intelligence Act](regulations/eu-ai-act.md) | European Union | Obligations graded by the risk of the use, from prohibited practices to high-risk systems with conformity, oversight, and transparency duties | The reasoning behind the Risk Tiers (AI Policy 3), as a reference and not as an obligation of the Bank |
| [OWASP Top 10 for Large Language Model Applications](regulations/owasp-top-10-llm.md) | Open Worldwide Application Security Project | The ten security risks of applications built on language models: prompt injection, sensitive information disclosure, supply chain, data and model poisoning, improper output handling, excessive agency, system prompt leakage, vector and embedding weaknesses, misinformation, unbounded consumption | The security test against attacks on AI in the validation of Risk Tier 2 and 3 (AI Policy 3.3); the guardrails of a Solution |
