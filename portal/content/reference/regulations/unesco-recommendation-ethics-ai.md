# UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence

The global normative instrument on the ethics of AI, adopted by the member states of UNESCO. It frames AI in terms of human rights, human dignity, the environment, diversity, and peaceful societies, and it asks states to act through policy, assessment, and education.

## 1. Identity

| Item | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Kind | Normative instrument of an international organization |
| Issued or overseen by | UNESCO; adopted by its member states |
| Jurisdiction | Global |
| Status | Adopted 2021; the member states, among them the Kyrgyz Republic, report on its implementation |

## 2. What it sets

2.1. Values and principles for the whole life cycle of AI: proportionality and do-no-harm, safety and security, fairness and non-discrimination, sustainability, privacy, human oversight and determination, transparency and explainability, responsibility and accountability, awareness and literacy, and multi-stakeholder governance. Policy areas for states, among them ethical impact assessment, data governance, and education.

## 3. Whom it reaches

3.1. States, through their policies; organizations indirectly, as states implement it. It binds no company directly.

## 4. Relevance to the Bank

4.1. It is the ethical reference that the Kyrgyz Republic has adhered to, and the one a public institution or a regulator in the country is most likely to cite. Its emphasis on literacy, oversight, and impact assessment matches what the Bank asks of its own people.

## 5. How the charter relates to it

5.1. The values of the Statement of Intent, integrity, prudence, and respect for people, and its Principles of Application are consistent with the Recommendation; the training requirement and the human oversight of the AI Policy answer its literacy and oversight principles.
