# Council of Europe Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence

The first international treaty on AI. It commits the states that ratify it to ensure that the activities within the life cycle of AI systems are consistent with human rights, democracy, and the rule of law, in the public sector and, as each state decides, in the private sector.

## 1. Identity

| Item | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Kind | International treaty |
| Issued or overseen by | Council of Europe; open to non-member states |
| Jurisdiction | Global |
| Status | Adopted and opened for signature in 2024; binds the states that sign and ratify it |

## 2. What it sets

2.1. Obligations on states to adopt measures on human dignity, equality and non-discrimination, privacy, transparency and oversight, accountability, reliability, and safe innovation, and to provide remedies and procedural safeguards for persons affected by AI systems.

## 3. Whom it reaches

3.1. The states that ratify it, and through their measures the organizations within them. It does not bind a company directly, and it reaches the private sector only as each state provides.

## 4. Relevance to the Bank

4.1. It sets the treaty-level expectation that persons affected by AI have rights and remedies, which is the direction national laws take. For a bank, the practical echo is contestability: a customer can reach a person and contest a decision.

## 5. How the charter relates to it

5.1. The contestability and human oversight principles of the Statement of Intent and the disclosure, explanation, and contestability requirements of the AI Policy for the higher Risk Tiers answer the same expectation.
