# EU Artificial Intelligence Act

The first comprehensive law on AI and the reference regime of the field. It grades obligations by the risk of the use: a short list of prohibited practices, a list of high-risk uses with full duties, transparency duties for systems that interact with people or generate content, and obligations for the providers of general-purpose models.

## 1. Identity

| Item | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Kind | Regulation of the European Union |
| Issued or overseen by | European Parliament and Council; the European AI Office of the European Commission and the national market surveillance authorities |
| Jurisdiction | European Union |
| Status | In force since 2024, applied in phases: prohibitions and literacy from 2025, general-purpose models from 2025, general application from 2026; the duties of high-risk systems deferred by the 2026 amendment to 2027 and 2028 |

## 2. What it sets

2.1. For the high-risk uses, among them the credit scoring of natural persons and the pricing of life and health insurance, the providers must have a risk management system, data governance, technical documentation, logging, transparency to deployers, human oversight, and accuracy, robustness, and cybersecurity, and the deployers must use the system as intended, keep logs, assign oversight to competent people, and, for certain deployers, assess the impact on fundamental rights. Everyone who uses AI must ensure the literacy of their staff.

## 3. Whom it reaches

3.1. Providers and deployers of AI placed on the market or used in the European Union, and providers outside the Union whose system's output is used in the Union. It does not bind a bank in the Kyrgyz Republic as such; it reaches the Bank through the providers and partners it uses that serve the Union, and through the models and services designed to comply with it.

## 4. Relevance to the Bank

4.1. It is the regime against which the industry now builds its products and its documentation, so the models and the services the Bank buys arrive shaped by it. Its risk-based logic, its treatment of credit scoring as high risk, and its human oversight and literacy duties are the clearest statement of where regulation is going, and a bank that aligns with them is prepared for its own supervisor.

## 5. How the charter relates to it

5.1. The Risk Tiers of the AI Policy follow the same risk-based reasoning; the rule that a use the law treats as high risk is at least Risk Tier 2, the human oversight and disclosure requirements of the higher tiers, and the training requirement of the Bank answer the same expectations in the Bank's own terms.
