# The financial standard-setters on AI: FSB, BCBS, BIS

The bodies that set the standards national banking supervisors implement have published their view of AI in finance: its benefits, its risks to institutions and to the system, and what supervisors should expect. Their reports are the best indication of what the supervisor of the Bank will ask in time.

## 1. Identity

| Item | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Kind | Reports and principles of the standard-setters of finance |
| Issued or overseen by | Financial Stability Board; Basel Committee on Banking Supervision; Bank for International Settlements and its Innovation Hub |
| Jurisdiction | Global |
| Status | Reports on AI in finance and on the digitalisation of finance, 2017 onward and renewed in 2024; implemented through national supervisors |

## 2. What it sets

2.1. The themes that recur: model risk and the governance of models; third-party and concentration risk in the providers of AI; data quality and privacy; operational resilience and cyber risk; explainability and consumer protection; the risk of herding and correlated behavior across institutions; and the need for supervisors to build their own capability.

## 3. Whom it reaches

3.1. The standard-setters bind no bank directly; national supervisors translate their work into regulation and supervisory practice.

## 4. Relevance to the Bank

4.1. The expectations of the National Bank of the Kyrgyz Republic and of the regional supervisors will follow these themes. Aligning early with them is cheaper than catching up.

## 5. How the charter relates to it

5.1. The AI Risk Appetite Statement of the AI Competence Center Charter, the provider rules of the AI Policy, the quarterly risk check, and the reporting to the Board Committee address the themes the standard-setters name.
