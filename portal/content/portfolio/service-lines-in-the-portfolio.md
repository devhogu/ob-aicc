# The service lines in the Portfolio

Every service of AICC enters through the same front door and the same Portfolio. What differs is the lane: run-rate work is small and repeatable and flows in days; a program is an Initiative with a business case and an MVP and flows through the portfolio loops. This page states how the nine lines map to the two lanes, how an item is screened, and what the Portfolio shows at a glance.

## 1. The two lanes

| Lane | What flows in it | How it is decided | How long | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Run-rate | A single document or runbook, an automation, a view or a consolidation, a training, an assessment, a digest | Taken in by the AICC Lead at the Weekly Review, as a Feature under the Initiative of the line, ranked by value and urgency; the Domain Owner approves the use for its data class | Days to one Iteration | Within the Limits on Work in Progress of the Team and the class of service |
| Program | A charter pack, a knowledge base, a process rewrite, a pipeline that feeds a report of the Bank, a document engine, a trial | An Initiative: study, business case, approval by the Domain Owner or the Executive Sponsor, MVP, decision after the MVP | Two Iterations to a Program Increment and more | Within the limit on the Active Initiatives |

1.1. The run-rate lane is carried by one standing Initiative per line, of enabling work where no single function is the client, so that every run-rate item has a place in the Portfolio and a record, without a business case of its own.

## 2. The lines by lane

| Line | Run-rate | Program |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strategy and governance office | A readiness and source audit | A charter pack and a portal; a strategy paper; a governance package |
| Normative documents and processes | A document, a runbook, a review of a set, a process drawing | A corpus rewritten |
| Knowledge services | A source set added to an existing base | A new knowledge base |
| Workplace automation | An automation in the catalog | A set of automations for a process |
| Information and decision support | A view, a consolidation, an analysis pack | A pipeline that feeds a recurring report of the Bank |
| Content and document engines | A template added to an engine | An engine for a document family |
| Enablement at the workplace | Training, a clinic, a skill library | A program of adoption for a Domain |
| Assurance and governance support | An assessment, an evaluation, a registry entry | A platform packaging |
| Watch, research, and partnering | A digest, a watch item | A trial |

## 3. Screening at the front door

3.1. An item is taken in when it fits a Strategic Priority, has a Domain Owner, and the limit permits it. It is screened on four questions, in this order: which problem lens it answers (insight, enablement, automation, optimization, a new opportunity); what size it is (small, medium, large, extra large, by the effort and the number of sources and systems it touches); which Risk Tier it is likely to carry; and whether a package exists that answers it already. A small item with an available package goes to the run-rate lane; a large item, a Risk Tier 2 or 3 item, or an item with no package goes to the program lane.

3.2. The Portfolio checks each proposed Initiative against the catalog, so that the Portfolio reuses what exists and does not duplicate it.

## 4. The Portfolio at a glance

4.1. The Portfolio is read in four groups, in the Portfolio Backlog of the Registry and in the Steering Summary: the Active Initiatives, with their lane, line, Domain, and state; the candidates in the funnel and in Discovery, with their lens, size, and likely Risk Tier; the enabling work of AICC, with its standing Initiatives; and the use-case backlog by Domain, screened and ranked, from which the funnel is fed. The live portal of AICC will present this view; this site states its structure.
