# Measures and tracking

A Portfolio that cannot say whether its Initiatives worked, or whether its work flows, is a list of hopes. This part states how the Portfolio is measured and tracked, in three layers: the outcome of each Initiative, read as objectives and key results; the flow of the work, read with the measures of lean practice; and the control of the whole, read at each loop. The measures exist to control and to improve, not to rank people, and a figure of the Bank stays in its source system.

## 1. The outcome of an Initiative: objectives and key results

1.1. The Portfolio applies the discipline of objectives and key results to every Initiative, in the Bank's own terms. The Initiative Brief states the objective as a hypothesis: the business outcome for a named function, in words. It states the key results as leading indicators: measurable results with a source system, a baseline, and a target. The MVP is the first test of the key results: the probe is built to move the indicators, and the decision after it reads them. The Capabilities of the Initiative are the means by which the key results are achieved in full, and each Capability is accepted against the result it was to produce. The benefit that the Domain Owner confirms at the Outcome Report and each quarter is the lagging confirmation that the objective was met.

| Layer | In the words of the charter | What it answers | Where it is written and read |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Objective | The hypothesis of the Initiative Brief: the value for a named function | Why are we doing this? | Initiative Brief; read at approval and after the MVP |
| Key results | The leading indicators, each with source system, baseline, and target | How will we know, in numbers? | Initiative Brief; read at the end of the MVP and at each quarterly Steering |
| First test of the key results | The MVP, the smallest version that can move the indicators | Does the hypothesis hold? | Solution Definition and the result of the probe; the decision after the MVP |
| Means to the key results | The Capabilities and their Features | What must be built to reach the results? | Program Backlog; accepted at the Iteration Review and Demo |
| Confirmation | The benefit confirmed by the Domain Owner against the benefit claimed and the Envelope | Did the value arrive? | Outcome Report; Quarterly Report |

1.2. Three rules make the objectives honest. A key result is a measure of a change in the business, adoption, time, errors, cost, revenue, and not a measure of activity: a delivered Feature is not a key result. Every key result has a baseline before the MVP starts, or the MVP's first job is to establish it. And the key results of an Initiative are few, two to four, so that the decision after the MVP can be read on one page.

1.3. For a scenario of the use-case backlog, before it becomes an Initiative, the same shape is used in brief: an objective, and three key results on adoption, acceptance, and cycle, so that the scenarios of a Domain can be compared and ranked.

## 2. The flow of the work: the measures of lean practice

2.1. The Portfolio runs on Kanban, so it measures flow and does not measure velocity, story points, or the output of a person. The measures are those of lean practice, and each has a definition, a source, and a place where it is read.

| Measure | Definition | What it tells | Read at |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Work in progress | The Active Initiatives against the limit; the items in each step | Whether the Portfolio is doing too much at once; the limit is the lever | Monthly Steering |
| Lead time | Days from Approved to Accepted for an Initiative; for a proposal, days from the funnel to its approval | How long a function waits for a result; the measure the function feels | Monthly and quarterly Steering |
| Cycle time | Days from Active to Completed, or to Review in light mode | How long the work itself takes once started | Weekly Review |
| Throughput | Initiatives accepted per Program Increment; Features accepted per Iteration | The rate of delivery; with work in progress, it gives the expected lead time | Quarterly Steering; Iteration Review and Demo |
| Aging | The age of the oldest item in the funnel; the days an approved Initiative waits; the days an item is Waiting | Where the flow is stuck, before lead time shows it | Weekly Review; monthly Steering |
| Blockers and returns | Items Waiting on a dependency; business cases returned at a gate; items returned at the check | Where the system, not the people, makes the work slow | Weekly Review; monthly Steering |
| Quality at the gate | The share that meets the check or the validation first time; defects found in operation after the check | Whether quality is built in or inspected in | Weekly Review; Iteration Review and Demo |
| Predictability | PI Objectives achieved against planned, with the business value scored | Whether the plan of a quarter can be trusted; a trend, without a target | PI Review and Demo |

2.2. The measures are read together. Work in progress, throughput, and lead time move as one: a Portfolio that takes in more than it finishes sees its lead time grow for every function at once, which is why the limit on the Active Initiatives is the first control and not the last. Aging and blockers are the early signals; lead time is the late one. The targets are set once there is a baseline, by the Team with the product owner for the flow of delivery and by the Steering for the flow of the Portfolio.

## 3. The control of the whole: what each loop reads

| Loop | What it reads | From | Decides on it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Backlog care, weekly | Aging of the funnel, business cases in progress, blockers, cycle time of the work | Dashboard | Triage, re-rank, raise items at a gate |
| Portfolio sync, monthly | Active against the limit, time in each step, returns at the gates, a sample of the decisions, lead time from proposal to approval | Steering Summary, Portfolio Backlog | Decide at the gates, pull into work, adjust the limit |
| Portfolio review, quarterly | For each Active Initiative, the key results against the plan and the benefit confirmed; the quarterly risk check; throughput and predictability; the benefit against each Envelope | Quarterly Report, Registry Snapshot | Continue, pivot, defer, or reject; the mix; the report to the Board Committee |
| Strategic, yearly | The benefit against each Envelope over the year; the results of each Strategic Priority; the Measures of the Maturity Levels | Quarterly Reports of the year | The Priorities, the Envelopes, the Guardrails for the next year |

3.1. The Measures of the Maturity Levels of the Statement of Intent sit above the Portfolio: the Domains and employees using approved Solutions, the processes with AI embedded and the change in time and errors against baseline, the benefits realized against the Envelope, the predictability of delivery, the incidents and the findings. The Portfolio feeds them, and the yearly Steering reads them when it confirms the Maturity Level of each priority.

## 4. How it is tracked

4.1. The definitions and the formulas of every measure named here, and the profit and loss view of the Portfolio per Strategic Priority, are stated on the page Portfolio measures: definitions and formulas. Three records carry the tracking, and no other is added. The Portfolio Backlog holds every Initiative with its state, its rank, its key results, and its dates, from which lead time, aging, and work in progress are read. The Dashboard shows the flow measures each week. The Steering Summary each month and the Quarterly Report each quarter carry the figures the loops read, with each figure pointing to its source. The live portal of AICC will present them; this site states what they are.
