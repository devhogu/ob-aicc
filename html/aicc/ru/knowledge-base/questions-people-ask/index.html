Short answers to the questions people bring, grounded in the charter; each answer names where the rule is. The answers explain; the documents prevail.

1. Using AI at work

May I use an AI assistant for my work? Yes, if it is a Solution approved for your data class and your purpose and recorded in the AI Registry, and you have had the training for your role. The Domain Owner approves the use of a Solution in the Domain. AI Policy 2.1.

May I paste customer data, or any confidential data, into an assistant? Only into a Solution approved for that data class. Data of a class may not be sent to a model or a service not approved for it, at any point, including discovery. AI Policy 2.2.

Am I responsible for what the assistant produces? Yes. A person reviews AI output before it is relied on, and remains accountable for what they sign. AI Policy 2.3.

May I record or transcribe a meeting with AI? Only with the consent of all participants. AI Policy 2.5.

Can I use AI to get around a limit or a control? No. Nobody uses AI to bypass a control, a limit, or a decision of a Control Function. AI Policy 2.5.

2. Starting work with AICC

How do I bring a need to AICC? In any form, to the AICC Lead: a conversation, a message, or a ticket in Service Management. It is taken in if it fits a Strategic Priority, has a Domain Owner, and the limit on the Active Initiatives permits. Business Model 3; Portfolio Management Model 5.

Do I need a budget or a project to start? No. AICC does not charge the functions; the Domain pays from its Envelope for the run, the licenses, and the provider costs of a Solution. A small, repeatable need is run-rate work and needs no business case of its own; a program has a one-page Initiative Brief. AICC Charter 4.1; Business Model 6.2.

What does AICC commit to, and what do I commit to? AICC commits in a Service Agreement, on a best-effort basis within its capability; the function commits to nothing, and what AICC relies on from the function is written as an Assumption. Business Model 5.

Who approves a business case? The Domain Owner, within one Domain and below an Investment Guardrail; the Executive Sponsor above a guardrail, across Domains, or for enabling work; cleared first by the Control Function Contacts when Risk Tier 2 or 3 is expected. Portfolio Management Model 6.3 and 6.4.

Why does AICC build a small version first? Every Initiative is a hypothesis, probed as an MVP before the decision to continue, so that the Bank invests in what works. The approver decides to continue, pivot, defer, or reject on the leading indicators. Portfolio Management Model 7.

3. Risk and approval

What is a Risk Tier, and who assigns it? The classification of a Solution by its data class, its influence on a decision, whether it reaches a customer, and its autonomy, from 1 to 3; it sets the validation, oversight, testing, monitoring, disclosure, and release that apply. The AICC Lead assigns it when the Solution is defined; the Executive Sponsor for a Solution the AICC Lead built. AI Policy 3.

Who checks a Solution before people use it? A person other than its builder: the Checker for Risk Tier 1, the Control Function Contacts for Risk Tier 2 and 3, before the first real users or data. AICC does not validate its own work. AI Policy 3.3; Solution Lifecycle Model 7.1.

Who signs before a Solution is released to everyone? Three acceptances in order, then the release: the product owner accepts the Features; the AICC Lead gives the final acceptance of the Team; the Domain Owner accepts the working Solution as the requester; then the Domain Owner releases it beyond the first users for Risk Tier 1 and 2, the Executive Sponsor for Risk Tier 3, with the Acceptance Checklist signed. Solution Lifecycle Model 7.3 and 7.4.

Can a Control Function stop a Solution? Yes, within its remit, finally; nobody overrides a stop. Operating Model 5.4.

What happens when AI causes an incident? It is handled in the incident management of the Bank with the AICC Lead as a stakeholder, reviewed afterwards in an AI Incident Review, and a major incident is reported to the Board Committee without waiting for the next report. AI Policy 5; AICC Charter 7.2.

Can an exception to a control be granted? Only by the Control Function concerned, recorded in the Risks and Issues with its reason and its end date, limited in time. AI Policy 6.

4. The unit

Who decides what? The person who does the work, on the facts; a decision rises only when it affects another Domain or outside the Bank, cannot be reversed, exceeds a guardrail or changes a priority, or accepts a risk beyond appetite or concerns Risk Tier 3. Operating Model 5.

Does AICC run the AI Platform? No. The Platform Owner provides and operates it outside AICC, to the requirements AICC states. AICC Charter 3.2; Statement of Intent 10.3.

Does AICC deliver at scale? No. It proves and builds; a Solution the Bank should run at scale is handed to a platform team or an IT function. AICC Charter 3.2.

Where are the live records? In the Registry, Jira, Confluence, and Service Management; this site holds the rules and the explanations, not the records. Operating Model 7; Records and systems, in Reference.

Where is the person behind a Role? In the Appointments Record of the Registry; the charter names Roles only. Operating Model 4.6.
