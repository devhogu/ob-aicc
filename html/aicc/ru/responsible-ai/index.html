Ответственный ИИ

The Bank uses AI under rules that are proportionate to risk, and it asks its people to understand the technology they use. This section is a short course and the rules in one place: what AI is today, from machine learning to generative AI and agents; where the value appears in a bank and what the evidence says; what the fintech landscape does with AI and what that means for a digital bank in this region; the risks that came with the technology and the ones it added; what the industry and the regulators mean by responsible AI; how the Bank applies it through its risk appetite, its Risk Tiers, and its gates; and the terms of the field in plain language. The AI Policy and the AI risk and control workflow state the rules; the course explains them.

    Understanding AI today

    Artificial intelligence is not one thing. It is a family of techniques that let a computer do work that used to need human judgment: recognizing, predicting, ranking, generating, and now acting. The techniques differ in how they are built, in what they are good at, and in how they fail, and the difference matters for anyone who decides whether and where to use them. This page explains the family in plain terms, with banking examples, as the first step of a short course on the subject.

  • Страница

    The opportunities

    Every wave of technology promises more than it delivers at first and more than anyone expected in the end. Generative AI is in its first years, and the honest view is that its value is real, uneven, and earned by the organizations that do the unglamorous work of governance, data, and adoption. This page states where the value is appearing in banking, what the evidence says, and where the Bank has chosen to look first.

  • Страница

    AI in fintech and digital banking

    Digital banks and fintech firms were the first in finance to use AI at scale, because their model made it natural: they are data-rich, built on software and interfaces rather than branches, and they grow by handling volume with few people. This page sets the Bank's use of AI in that context: what the fintech landscape does with AI, what it means for a mobile-first bank in this region, what the financial supervisors watch, and what the Bank takes from it.

  • Страница

    The risks and challenges

    Every capability of generative AI has a failure mode attached to it, and most of the failure modes are new to the people who have to manage them. This page sets them out in plain terms, in three groups: the risks that machine learning always carried, the risks that generative AI added, and the risks that agents compound. It ends with the challenges that are not about the technology at all, which are the ones that decide most outcomes.

  • Страница

    What responsible AI means

    "Responsible AI" is the phrase the industry settled on for a simple proposition: an organization that uses AI remains answerable for what the AI does, and it organizes itself so that the answer is a good one. Behind the phrase stand a small number of principles that governments, standard-setters, and regulators have converged on over the last decade, and a set of practices that turn the principles into daily work. This page explains both, and shows where the Bank's own principles come from.

  • Страница

    How the Bank applies it

    The principles of responsible AI become the Bank's rules in the AI Policy and in the AI Risk Appetite Statement, and they become practice in the way AICC and the functions work. This page explains the rules in plain terms for the person who will use AI, build it, or approve it. The AI Policy is the rule and prevails; this page is the explanation.

    AI terms explained

    The vocabulary of the field, in plain terms, for the reader of this course. The Vocabulary of the charter defines the terms that the documents use as rules; this page explains the terms of the technology that the documents assume. Where a term is both, the charter's definition prevails.
