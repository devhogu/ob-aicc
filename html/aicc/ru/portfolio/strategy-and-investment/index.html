Strategy and investment

Текст этой страницы пока доступен только на английском языке. Русский перевод ещё не готов.

The Portfolio starts from a frame that is set once a year: the Strategic Priorities the Bank pursues, the Investment Envelope of each, and the Investment Guardrails that say what may be decided where. Within that frame, decisions are taken close to the work; outside it, they rise to the Executive Sponsor. This part explains the frame and the decision rights that follow from it.

1. The Strategic Priorities

¶1.1. The Strategic Priorities are the strategic themes of the Portfolio. The Executive Sponsor sets them with the Board, each with an Investment Envelope, from the strategy of the Bank and the priorities that the Domain Owners put forward, and the Statement of Intent states the seven in force at the baseline, from customer intelligence to software engineering. An Initiative is taken in only if it fits a Strategic Priority; the priorities are the first filter of the funnel and the first line of the business case.

2. The Investment Envelopes

¶2.1. An Investment Envelope is the investment the Bank sets for a Strategic Priority for the year. Funding goes to the priorities and to the Teams, not to Initiatives one by one: AICC does not charge the functions, the Executive Sponsor owns the budget of AICC itself, and the Domain pays from its Envelope for the run, the licenses, and the provider costs of its Solutions. A business case is affordable when its cost is within the Envelope of its priority, and each quarter the benefit confirmed by the Domain Owners is set against the Envelope, which informs the next year's decision.

¶2.2. The Envelope changes the nature of a decision to start. It is no longer a budget negotiation for a project; it is a decision that this need, under this priority, within this Envelope, is worth the capacity now. The figures themselves stay in the financial planning of the Bank; the Priorities Record points to them.

3. The Investment Guardrails

¶3.1. The Investment Guardrails state the amount that may be committed without the Executive Sponsor and the Initiatives that need the approval of the Executive Sponsor. They are the device that lets decisions be local without losing control. An Initiative that stays within one Domain and below a guardrail is approved by the Domain Owner; an Initiative that exceeds a guardrail, spans Domains, or is enabling work of AICC is approved by the Executive Sponsor. The same rule decides who takes the decision after the MVP and who accepts the outcome.

¶3.2. Guardrails are governance, not its absence. A lean budget without guardrails is a blank cheque; guardrails are the written limits within which a Domain Owner may act alone, and the thresholds at which the Executive Sponsor must.

5. How the frame is renewed

¶5.1. The frame is set at the yearly Steering, which is the monthly Steering of December, from the Quarterly Report of the third Program Increment and the findings of the year. It reads the benefit against each Envelope and the results against each priority, reviews the documents and the AI Risk Appetite Statement, and renews or adjusts the priorities, the Envelopes, and the Guardrails for the next year. A change that the fourth quarter calls for is taken at the first quarterly Steering of the new year. The Strategy page of About AICC sets this in the strategy of the Bank.

6. Rule source

Portfolio Management Model 2, 4.2, and 6.3 to 6.6; AI Competence Center Charter 4; Statement of Intent 9 and 10.4.
