How to engage

Текст этой страницы пока доступен только на английском языке. Русский перевод ещё не готов.

A function does not need a form, a budget line, or a project to start with AICC. It brings a need. From there the Engagement follows six steps, each with a record and a decision that the function can see.

1. The six steps

Step What happens Who Record
1. Contact A head of function raises a need, in a conversation, a message, or a ticket in Service Management The function; the AICC Lead takes it in An entry in the Portfolio Backlog
2. Study The need is scoped and the business case is written; the Control Function Contacts clear it when Risk Tier 2 or 3 is expected AICC Lead with the Domain Owner Initiative Brief; Service Agreement issued
3. Service Agreement AICC states what it commits to, the support level, and the working agreement; amended when the business case is approved AICC Lead; the function is notified Service Agreement
4. Delivery The Solution is probed as an MVP, then built, verified, and deployed to its first users after the decision to continue Solution Engineer with the Domain Expert; the AICC Lead gives the final acceptance of the Team Solution Definition; Control Sign-Off; release block
5. Outcome Report The outcome is reported with its evidence, and the Domain Owner accepts it as the requester AICC Lead issues; the Domain Owner accepts Outcome Report
6. Support The Solution is supported at the agreed level; a check-in at each Iteration decides follow-on or end Solution Engineer; the AICC Lead with the Domain Owner Service Management records

2. The commitment

¶2.1. AICC commits in a Service Agreement. It is a working agreement and not a legal document, and it needs no signature chain. AICC works toward the outcome on a best-effort basis, within the capability that it has available. The function commits to nothing; what AICC relies on from the function is written as an Assumption.

¶2.2. The scope is an intent and a backlog ordered by value within the Limits on Work in Progress. Either side may end or redirect the Engagement at the end of an Iteration.

¶2.3. AICC does not charge the functions. The Domain pays from its Investment Envelope for the run, the licenses, and the provider costs of a Solution.

3. Service levels

Support level What AICC does Solution type
None Hands the Solution over, with the Outcome Report Experiment, or a Product handed over
On demand Answers requests and issues new versions when asked Product
At agreed response targets Supports to the targets that the Service Agreement states Service
Run by AICC Runs the Solution for its whole life, with its run cost and its sunset rule Service

4. What AICC does not do

¶4.1. AICC does not own or operate the AI Platform, own the business results of a Domain, set the rules of a Control Function, validate its own work, or decide a matter that the regulation of the Bank reserves to the Board, to the management, or to a Control Function.

¶4.2. AICC does not deliver at scale. It proves and builds, and it relies on the platform teams and the IT functions of the Bank to run what the Bank adopts.

5. Rule source

Business Model 3 to 7; AICC Charter 3.2 and 4; Solution Lifecycle Model 7.3 and 8; the Engagement workflow and guide.

  • Service line

    Advisory

    AICC advises a function of the Bank on where and how to use AI: which needs are worth an Initiative, what a Solution would take, what it would cost to run, which Risk Tier it would carry, and which provider or platform fits. Advisory is the study phase of an Engagement, and it is how most work with AICC starts.

  • Service line

    Proof and prototyping

    Before the Bank invests in a Solution, AICC proves it. A proof is a probe: an Experiment, a proof of concept, a minimum viable product, or a trial of a platform or a concept, run for a stated number of Iterations against leading indicators, and ending in evidence and a decision.

  • Service line

    Solution delivery

    AICC builds Solutions for the functions of the Bank: knowledge bases on governed sources, assistants for a team, and the automation of routine work that a function does by hand today. A Solution of this kind is a Product: a version built for one consumer, handed over, and supported on demand.

  • Service line

    AI services run by AICC

    Some of what AICC builds it also runs: portals, engines, pipelines, and knowledge services that serve a function or the Bank for as long as they are worth running. These are the Services of AICC. Each has a business case that states its run cost and its sunset rule, a Domain Owner who accepts and reviews it, and support through Service Management.

  • Service line

    Enablement

    AI reaches the Bank through its people. AICC trains employees before they use AI, coaches the Domain Experts who carry adoption in their Domains, runs the communities of practice, and keeps the playbooks and the reusable assets of its Engagements so that the next function starts further ahead.

  • Service line

    Oversight of Adopted Solutions

    Not every AI Solution of the Bank is built by AICC. When a Proposal is adopted and a Domain or a platform team delivers it, or when a function brings in a Solution of its own, AICC oversees it: it keeps it in the AI Registry with its Risk Tier, follows its checks and its incidents, and reports on it with the rest of the Portfolio.

  • Service line

    Research and partnering

    AICC watches the field so the Bank does not have to: the models, the platforms, the methods, and the rules that change every quarter. It tests what matters in trials, keeps the Bank's reference of the industry body of knowledge and of the regulators and acts, and works with other organizations and with providers where the Bank gains from it.
