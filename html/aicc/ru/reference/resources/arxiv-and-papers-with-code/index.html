arXiv and Papers with Code

Текст этой страницы пока доступен только на английском языке. Русский перевод ещё не готов.

Where AI research appears first, as preprints, before and often instead of peer review, and the index that connects a paper to its code, its datasets, and its reported results. They are the primary source for a method and the place to check a claim that a vendor or an article makes.

1. Identity

Item Entry
Kind Preprint repository and research index
Who arXiv, run by Cornell University, for the preprints; Papers with Code, a community index linking papers to code and results
Access Free

2. What it publishes

¶2.1. arXiv: the preprints of the field, in the categories of artificial intelligence, computation and language, machine learning, and computer vision, searchable and with daily listings. Papers with Code: the same papers linked to their implementations, their datasets, and leaderboards of results by task.

3. Why it matters to the Bank

¶3.1. When a provider or an article claims a method or a result, the paper is the evidence, and the code and the leaderboard show whether it holds. A Solution Engineer who designs a retrieval or an evaluation reads the method where it was published. A Control Function Contact who validates a model can read what its builders published about it.

4. How to take it, and for what

¶4.1. For a claim, find the paper and read its evaluation section and its limitations. For a method, read the paper beside its code. For the state of a task, read the leaderboard with care. Follow a few categories or authors rather than the daily firehose. The Research and exploration category of AICC keeps the watch and digests what matters.

5. Cautions

¶5.1. A preprint is not peer-reviewed and may be wrong; results on benchmarks do not transfer to the Bank's cases without evaluation on them. Reading is free; nothing of the Bank is submitted.

  • Справочный материал

    MITRE ATLAS and the AI Incident Database

    Two knowledge bases a bank that uses AI should know. ATLAS catalogs the techniques adversaries use against AI systems, in the matrix form security teams know from MITRE's attack framework, with case studies. The AI Incident Database collects the public record of harms and failures of AI systems, so that others can learn before they repeat them.
