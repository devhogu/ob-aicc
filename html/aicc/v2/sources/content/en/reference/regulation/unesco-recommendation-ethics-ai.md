## Briefly {#short}

The Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence is the first global standard on AI ethics. All 193 UNESCO Member States adopted it in November 2021, the Kyrgyz Republic among them. It is a recommendation, not a treaty: it does not bind anyone directly but serves as a reference for countries when they prepare their own AI laws, strategies, and rules.

## What it means in practice {#practice}

For the organization: the Kyrgyz Republic is part of this document, so a government body preparing rules on AI is likely to refer to it. Its main ideas, such as proportionality, human oversight, data protection, and fairness, match what we call [[responsible-ai|responsible AI]]. If our rules build on them, new requirements will not catch us off guard.

For an employee using AI at work:

- Use AI where the task really needs it, and no further. There is no point handing AI something that can be done without extra risk to the customer.
- An important decision about a person stays with a person. AI can prepare an option, but an employee decides and is responsible.
- If you are about to put customer data into an AI tool, first check that this is allowed, and use only the tools approved in your organization.
- If you notice that an AI result puts some group of customers at a disadvantage, that is [[bias]]: do not use the result, and report it.

## Key ideas {#ideas}

- **Four values.** Respect for human dignity and human rights; living in peaceful, just, and interconnected societies; diversity and inclusiveness; a flourishing environment and ecosystems.
- **Ten principles.** Proportionality and do no harm; safety and security; the right to privacy and data protection; multi-stakeholder governance of AI; responsibility and accountability; transparency and [[explainability]]; human oversight and determination; sustainability; awareness and [[ai-literacy|AI literacy]]; fairness and non-discrimination.
- **Proportionality.** AI is used only when and as far as a legitimate aim requires, choosing the way that carries the least risk to people.
- **Responsibility stays with people.** An AI system cannot be made responsible in place of a person: a person or an organization is always accountable for its use.
- **[[impact-assessment|Impact assessment]].** UNESCO proposes assessing, before an AI system is introduced, how it will affect people, their rights, and society, and taking that into account in the decision.
- **Eleven policy areas.** The Recommendation turns the values and principles into action: from ethical impact assessment and data policy to education, the economy and labor, health, and the environment.

## Good to know {#details}

- Adopted in November 2021 by all 193 UNESCO Member States.
- It is addressed to countries, but its values and principles concern everyone involved in the AI lifecycle, including companies.
- It is a voluntary benchmark: there are no penalties for not following it.
- To help countries, UNESCO runs the Global AI Ethics and Governance Observatory, which brings together information on how ready countries are to use AI ethically and responsibly.
- There is an official Russian text.
