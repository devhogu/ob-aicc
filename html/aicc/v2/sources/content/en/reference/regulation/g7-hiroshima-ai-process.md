## Briefly {#short}

The G7 Hiroshima AI Process began in May 2023, on the direction of G7 leaders at the Hiroshima Summit. Its main outcome is the Comprehensive Policy Framework: guiding principles and a code of conduct for organizations developing the most advanced AI systems. G7 digital and tech ministers agreed it in December 2023, and G7 leaders endorsed it the same month. All of it is voluntary: there is no law here, and companies decide for themselves whether to sign up to the code.

## What it means in practice {#practice}

For the organization, this is first of all a benchmark for choosing a model provider. The code lists what a responsible developer does and publishes: how it tests a model before release, what the model can and cannot do, how it manages risks and reports problems. So when choosing a model, it is worth asking: does the provider publish this information, and does it take part in the reporting framework run by the OECD? The Kyrgyz Republic is a member of the Hiroshima AI Process Friends Group, so our government supports this benchmark too.

For an employee using AI at work:

- Read what the provider intends the tool for and what limitations it names itself. Do not use the tool for tasks the provider considers unsuitable.
- If you publish text or images created by AI, follow the internal rules on how to label such content.
- If you notice a tool behaving strangely or dangerously, report it: the developer and the organization look into such cases together.
- Use only the tools approved in your organization.

## Key ideas {#ideas}

- **Advanced AI systems.** The code is about the most powerful systems, above all the most advanced [[foundation-model|foundation models]] and [[generative-ai|generative AI]] systems.
- **Eleven actions in the code.** Among them: test the model before release and throughout its lifecycle, including with deliberate attacks; watch for vulnerabilities and misuse after release; publish reports on the model's capabilities and limitations; share information about incidents; disclose risk management policies; protect the model; label content created by AI; protect personal data and intellectual property.
- **Matched to risk.** All actions follow a [[risk-based-approach|risk-based approach]]: the higher the risk, the more testing and disclosure.
- **Principles for all AI actors.** A separate document extends the guiding principles to those who do not develop models but use them, which includes organizations like ours.
- **Reporting framework.** The OECD runs a voluntary reporting framework in which companies publish how they follow the code. You can read such a report before talking to a provider.

## Good to know {#details}

- The process started in May 2023. The principles and code for developers were set out with the G7 Leaders' Statement of 30 October 2023; the Comprehensive Policy Framework was agreed and endorsed in December 2023.
- The reporting framework was launched on 7 February 2025. The OECD published the first company reports in April 2025, and the responses to the second version of the framework on 2 October 2026, submitted by 31 companies.
- The Hiroshima AI Process Friends Group was set up in May 2024 for countries that support the process. As of August 2026, its members include the Kyrgyz Republic and Kazakhstan.
- The code is voluntary, with no penalties. The documents are described as "living": they are to be reviewed as the technology develops.
- The official documents are published in English and Japanese; we did not find an official Russian translation.
