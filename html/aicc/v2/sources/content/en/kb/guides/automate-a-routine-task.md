This guide is for an employee with recurring work: a weekly report, a summary of customer requests, checking documents against a list. By the end, you will have walked through the five steps from manual work to automation and will know which step your task is at now.

[[workspace-automation|Workspace automation]] is when AI prepares recurring work (collecting data, a report, filling in a form, sorting emails), and a person checks it and decides. Start small: one task that you do every week and know well.

## 1. Describe the task {#describe}

Write the task down in three lines: what goes in, what should come out, and who reads it. For example:

- **Input:** the weekly export of overdue loans in Excel.
- **Output:** a one-page summary with totals, changes over the week, and the five largest cases.
- **Who reads it:** the head of the department, on Mondays before lunch.

Also write down how long the task takes now: you'll need it in step five. If you can't describe the task in three lines, it's too early to automate it.

## 2. Do it with Claude {#manual}

Do the task with Claude in an approved work account three or four times on real data, only data that may be shared (see [What you can and cannot share with AI](page:kb/guides/what-to-share)). This way you will find the wording of the prompt that reliably gives the result you need, and you will see where Claude makes mistakes.

## 3. Make it stick {#fix}

Choose the simplest method that fits:

- **Prompt template:** save the prompt that worked and drop new data into it.
- **Project:** if the task always relies on the same documents (see [Projects in Claude](page:kb/guides/claude-projects)).
- **Skill:** if it's a procedure that needs to be done the same way in different conversations (see [Skills: teach Claude your procedure](page:kb/guides/claude-skills)).
- **Scheduled task in Cowork:** if the result is needed regularly and Claude can prepare a draft on its own (see [Scheduled tasks](page:kb/guides/claude-work-complete-guide#cowork-schedule)).

If the task touches work systems or the whole department, we help you make it stick: that way it comes to us as [[run-rate|run-rate work]].

## 4. Keep a human check {#review}

Automation prepares; a person checks and sends. This matters most for anything that goes to a customer, to another department, or affects a decision. You may remove the check only where a mistake costs nothing and that decision is written down.

## 5. Measure {#measure}

Compare the time and the number of corrections before and after. If there is no gain, that is a result too: the task is better left as it is.

## Try it now {#try}

Take one of your weekly tasks and describe it in three lines, as in the example above: input, output, who reads it. Add a fourth line with how long it takes you. You will use this description to write your first skill.
