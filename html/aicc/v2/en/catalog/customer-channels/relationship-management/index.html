Relationship management

Relationship management is the primary revenue-generating channel for corporate, SME, and high-value retail segments, and for private banking where the Bank offers it — the relationship manager (RM) is the Bank's commercial face to its most profitable customers. RM productivity is constrained by administrative overhead: meeting preparation, CRM documentation, report assembly, and cross-sell list management consume time that should be directed at client interaction. Under the regulator's conduct requirements, RMs handling investment products, complex lending, and wealth management carry individual obligations — client suitability, conflict of interest management, and product disclosure — that add documentation burden. The opportunity for generative AI is to restore RM capacity to client-facing time — generating pre-meeting briefs in minutes, monitoring book health continuously, identifying cross-sell opportunities from transaction signals, and alerting RMs to lifecycle events before they become urgent.

Insights & analytics

RM book health — revenue concentration, product penetration depth, client engagement frequency, and at-risk relationship signals — is visible at team level in quarterly performance reviews. Individual RMs do not have a continuous, portfolio-level view of which clients are drifting, which books are over-concentrated, or where cross-sell capacity is accumulating unused.

Enablement

Meeting preparation for client calls and reviews is performed manually by RMs: reviewing CRM notes, pulling account data, reading company news, and assembling a cross-sell prompt from the product team's campaign list. Thorough preparation for a corporate client meeting takes 30–60 minutes; RM book size means most meetings receive significantly less preparation time.

Automation

RM performance reporting, book composition summaries, and cross-sell pipeline tracking are assembled manually by team heads from CRM exports and finance data at period end. The recurring, structured nature of each report makes it a candidate for AI-driven assembly.

New business opportunities

An RM operating with a continuously monitored book — receiving alerts on at-risk relationships, cross-sell opportunities surfaced from transaction signals, and pre-generated meeting briefs — serves a larger effective book size without adding headcount. The commercial leverage is higher revenue per RM, not more RMs. In competitive corporate and private banking markets, the RM who arrives best-prepared consistently captures wallet share from the RM who arrives with generic talking points.

RM book management

The portfolio-level management of a relationship manager's assigned client book — tracking revenue concentration, product penetration depth, engagement frequency, and attrition risk across the full set of assigned relationships. Book management discipline is the operational foundation for RM performance: an RM who tracks book health continuously can redirect attention to at-risk relationships before the client reduces the relationship, and can sequence cross-sell activity toward the highest-probability opportunities. Supervisory guidance on relationship banking commonly expects RMs to maintain documented evidence of regular client contact at a frequency proportional to the client's revenue tier.

Book Activity Log Automation

The AI agent maintains an automated activity log for each client in the RM's book — recording client interactions, product events, and compliance-relevant milestones from CRM events, product system alerts, and KYC schedule data — and generates a weekly summary for the RM's review. The log reduces the manual CRM maintenance burden for RMs while ensuring the documented contact frequency record meets supervisory expectations. CRM activity record completeness rate and RM manual CRM entry time are the primary outcome metrics.

S
Problem to solve

RMs are required to maintain documented evidence of regular client contact at a frequency proportional to the client's revenue tier; CRM record completeness is commonly a supervisory expectation for relationship banking. Manual CRM entry competes with client-facing time; high-book RMs either compress documentation quality or spend disproportionate time on administrative entry. Book activity records that are incomplete reduce the analytical value of CRM data for book health monitoring, cross-sell targeting, and KYC review preparation — creating a compound operational cost for the Bank beyond the immediate compliance risk.

Solution

The AI agent reads CRM interaction logs, product system event feeds, and KYC schedule data for each client in the RM's book, compiling a structured activity record covering interactions, product milestones, and compliance events at each update cycle. The weekly summary is presented to the RM for review and confirmation; the RM adds qualitative interaction notes where the automated record does not capture the full interaction context. CRM activity record completeness rate — the proportion of client relationships with a compliant documented contact record — and RM manual CRM entry time are tracked against the pre-deployment baseline.

Acceptance criteria

An automated activity log for each client in the RM's book — interactions, product events, and compliance-relevant milestones compiled from CRM events, product system alerts, and KYC schedule data — is available to each RM with a weekly summary for review and confirmation, keeping the documented contact record complete.

Adoption

The AI agent maintains the activity log and delivers the weekly summary for ≥95% of active RM books for ≥48 consecutive weeks post go-live.

Acceptance

≥85% of weekly summaries confirmed by the RM without correction of automated entries; CRM activity record completeness rate ≥95% of client relationships.

Cycle

RM manual CRM entry time reduced by ≥50% against the pre-deployment baseline within 6 months of go-live.

RM Book Health Intelligence

The AI agent monitors each RM's client book on a weekly cadence — tracking revenue concentration, product penetration depth, engagement frequency, and attrition risk signals — and produces a book health brief identifying clients at risk of relationship reduction and clients overdue for a cross-sell conversation. The RM reviews the brief at the start of the weekly call planning cycle and directs outreach effort toward the highest-priority clients. RM team head review of book health trends across the team provides the primary input for resource allocation and coaching conversations.

M
Problem to solve

RM book management is largely reactive; RMs prioritize client outreach based on recency of contact and personal relationship familiarity rather than from a systematic book health signal. Clients whose product holdings are reducing, engagement frequency is declining, or revenue contribution is concentrating without diversification are not systematically identified until the relationship loss is evident. The RM team head cannot identify which books in the team are deteriorating on leading indicators without commissioning CRM analysis per RM, which is not available at weekly cadence.

Solution

The AI agent reads CRM interaction records, product holding data, transaction revenue attribution, and engagement frequency for each client in the RM's book, computing book health metrics at weekly cadence. The book health brief ranks clients by attrition risk and cross-sell opportunity, and flags clients overdue for outreach against the documented contact frequency standard. The RM reviews the brief at the start of the weekly call planning cycle; the RM team head has access to the team-level book health summary, and book health trends across the team are reviewed monthly alongside individual RM performance data. Revenue per book, attrition rate, and contact frequency compliance rate are the primary outcome metrics.

Acceptance criteria

A weekly book health brief — ranking clients by attrition risk and cross-sell opportunity and flagging clients overdue for outreach against the contact frequency standard — is available to each RM at the start of the weekly call planning cycle, with a team-level summary for the RM team head.

Adoption

The AI agent delivers the book health brief for ≥95% of active RM books on each weekly cycle for ≥48 consecutive weeks post go-live.

Acceptance

≥75% of flagged at-risk and overdue clients confirmed as outreach priorities by the RM; revenue per book, attrition rate, and contact frequency compliance rate tracked as primary outcome metrics.

Cycle

Book health visibility moved from CRM analysis commissioned per RM to weekly automated delivery at the start of each call planning cycle.

Book Portfolio Rebalancing Brief

The AI agent analyzes each RM's book structure — revenue concentration, segment distribution, geographic clustering, and KYC review workload — and produces a quarterly rebalancing brief for the RM team head, identifying book assignments that are unbalanced by revenue concentration or review workload relative to the team's capacity. The brief supports the RM team head's book allocation decisions and provides the evidence basis for new client assignments and transition planning. Revenue per RM book, book balance variance across the team, and KYC workload distribution are the primary outcome metrics.

M
Problem to solve

RM book allocations are reviewed annually or when a departure creates a vacancy; the RM team head does not have a current-state view of book balance across the team — which RMs are carrying disproportionate revenue concentration risk, which have the highest upcoming KYC review workload, and which have capacity for new client assignments. Unbalanced book structures are a risk management concern: over-concentrated books create single-point-of-failure risk for the Bank's client relationships; under-resourced books generate service quality gaps that are not identified until client attrition is evident. Book rebalancing decisions made without an analytical view of current book structure produce allocations that are informed by relationship history rather than by portfolio risk and capacity data.

Solution

The AI agent reads revenue attribution, product holding data, segment assignments, and KYC review schedules for each client across the RM team's book, computing book-level concentration, workload, and capacity metrics at quarterly cadence. The rebalancing brief identifies the highest-priority rebalancing cases — over-concentrated books, upcoming KYC workload peaks, and under-resourced books with capacity for additional client assignments. The RM team head reviews the brief and uses it as the primary evidence basis for book allocation decisions; book balance variance across the team and KYC deadline adherence rate are tracked as outcome metrics.

Acceptance criteria

A quarterly rebalancing brief — identifying over-concentrated books, upcoming KYC review workload peaks, and under-resourced books with capacity for additional client assignments — is available to the RM team head as the evidence basis for book allocation decisions.

Adoption

The AI agent delivers the rebalancing brief covering ≥95% of RM books for ≥4 consecutive quarters post go-live.

Acceptance

≥75% of priority rebalancing cases confirmed as actionable by the RM team head; book balance variance across the team and KYC deadline adherence rate tracked as primary outcome metrics.

Cycle

Book balance review moved from annual or vacancy-driven reallocation to quarterly delivery within 2 weeks of quarter close.

Client lifecycle stewardship

The proactive management of a client's relationship across its full lifecycle — acquisition, deepening, maturity events, KYC review cycles, and relationship transitions. Lifecycle stewardship differs from reactive client management: the RM acts before the client event rather than responding to it. Under KYC refresh obligations, all client relationships must be reviewed on a risk-proportionate schedule — typically annually for high-risk and every three to five years for standard-risk clients. Missing a KYC review deadline creates a regulatory breach; missing a product maturity creates a competitor opportunity.

Client Lifecycle Event Monitoring

The AI agent monitors each client across CRM, product maturities, KYC review schedules, and public corporate event feeds, alerting the RM at structured lead times before events requiring proactive outreach. The alert includes a recommended action and a pre-populated brief drawn from the event context and the client's current relationship profile. The RM confirms the action taken and the outcome is recorded in CRM, maintaining a complete interaction audit trail.

M
Problem to solve

Client lifecycle events — term deposit maturities, credit facility renewals, KYC review deadlines, and corporate leadership changes — are tracked across multiple systems without consolidated alerting. RMs discover impending events with insufficient lead time for a considered response or a competitive retention offer. Missing a KYC review deadline is a regulatory breach under customer due diligence requirements; missing a product maturity is a competitor opportunity with a measurable revenue consequence.

Solution

The AI agent monitors CRM, product system maturities, KYC review schedules, and public corporate event feeds for each client in the RM's book. It generates an alert at 30, 14, and 7 days before each event with the recommended action and a pre-populated brief drawn from relationship history and event context. The RM acts on the alert and records the outcome in CRM; KYC deadline adherence and product retention rates are the primary outcome metrics.

Acceptance criteria

Structured alerts with recommended action and pre-populated brief are available to each RM at 30, 14, and 7 days before client lifecycle events — maturities, KYC deadlines, credit renewals, and corporate changes — maintained from continuous monitoring of CRM, product systems, and public event feeds.

Adoption

The AI agent generates lifecycle event alerts for ≥95% of events meeting the defined alert horizon criteria across the full active RM book for ≥48 consecutive weeks.

Acceptance

≥85% of alerts confirmed as actionable by the receiving RM; KYC deadline breach rate and product retention rate at maturity tracked as primary outcome metrics.

Cycle

Lead time from event identification to RM notification extended from same-week or post-event discovery to ≥14 days advance notice for ≥90% of scheduled events.

Client Relationship Continuity Brief

The AI agent generates a relationship continuity brief for each client when an RM transition occurs — coverage change due to staff departure, book redistribution, or planned leave — assembling the interaction history, product holdings, open commitments, and upcoming lifecycle events from CRM and product systems. The incoming RM reviews the brief before first contact with the client; no relationship transition occurs without a structured handover document. Client retention rate following RM transitions and time from transition to first incoming RM contact are the primary outcome metrics.

S
Problem to solve

RM transitions — departures, book redistributions, and extended leave — are a material client retention risk; clients who experience a handover without a briefed incoming RM perceive the relationship as disrupted and are more likely to reduce the relationship or move to a competitor. The current handover process relies on the departing RM's documentation quality and the incoming RM's manual CRM review; where the departing RM's documentation is incomplete, the incoming RM has insufficient context for a credible first interaction. Supervisory guidance on relationship banking commonly expects RMs to maintain documented evidence of regular client contact; transitions that produce gaps in the contact record create both a client experience risk and a compliance gap.

Solution

The AI agent reads CRM interaction history, product holdings, open commitments, KYC review status, and upcoming lifecycle events for each client subject to a relationship transition, and generates a structured continuity brief for the incoming RM. The brief is generated at transition confirmation rather than on the departure date, giving the incoming RM adequate time to review it before first contact with the client. Client retention rate — measured as product holding and contact frequency change in the 90 days following the transition — and time from transition to first documented incoming RM contact are the primary outcome metrics.

Acceptance criteria

A relationship continuity brief for each client subject to an RM transition — interaction history, product holdings, open commitments, KYC review status, and upcoming lifecycle events — is available to the incoming RM before first contact with the client.

Adoption

The AI agent generates a continuity brief for ≥98% of clients subject to a confirmed RM transition within 2 working days of transition confirmation, for ≥12 consecutive months post go-live.

Acceptance

≥85% of briefs confirmed by the incoming RM as sufficient for first contact without supplementary manual CRM review; client retention in the 90 days following the transition tracked as primary outcome metric.

Cycle

Time from transition to first documented incoming RM contact reduced from several weeks of handover dependent on the departing RM's documentation to ≤10 working days.

KYC Refresh Workflow Automation

The AI agent manages the KYC refresh workflow for each client relationship — generating the client information request at the defined lead time before the review deadline, tracking document receipt, flagging overdue responses, and assembling the completed review file for compliance sign-off. No KYC review deadline is missed without a documented escalation record; the AI agent maintains the full workflow audit trail. KYC deadline adherence rate and review completion cycle time are the primary outcome metrics.

M
Problem to solve

KYC refresh deadlines for the RM client book are tracked in the compliance system; RMs are responsible for initiating client document requests at a defined lead time before the deadline. Manual tracking across a large book means some review deadlines are noticed late, compressing the time available for client document collection and compliance review. Under customer due diligence requirements, a missed KYC review deadline is a regulatory breach; the current model's reliance on RM manual calendar management produces a preventable compliance risk at scale.

Solution

The AI agent reads the KYC review schedule for each client relationship and initiates the document request workflow at the defined lead time — typically 60 days before the review deadline — generating the client-facing request and the internal tracking record. It monitors document receipt and flags overdue responses to the RM and compliance team at defined escalation points. On receipt of all required documents, the AI agent assembles the completed review file for compliance sign-off; the full workflow timeline is maintained in the audit trail. KYC deadline adherence rate — the proportion of reviews completed before the regulatory deadline — and workflow completion cycle time are the primary outcome metrics.

Acceptance criteria

Every KYC refresh in the RM client book runs through a tracked workflow — client information request issued at the defined lead time, document receipt monitored, overdue responses escalated, and the completed review file assembled — so that the compliance team receives a complete file for sign-off before the review deadline.

Adoption

The AI agent initiates the document request workflow at the defined lead time for ≥98% of scheduled KYC reviews for ≥12 consecutive months post go-live.

Acceptance

≥90% of assembled review files accepted by the compliance team for sign-off without additional document requests; KYC deadline adherence rate ≥98%, with a documented escalation record for every exception.

Cycle

Review initiation moved from RM manual calendar tracking, with deadlines noticed late, to automatic initiation 60 days before the review deadline; workflow completion cycle time tracked against the pre-deployment baseline.

Client interaction & meeting prep

The preparation cycle for RM client interactions — compiling relationship history, recent account events, company news, product holding gaps, and cross-sell prompts into a coherent meeting brief. Meeting quality is the proximate driver of cross-sell conversion and client satisfaction; preparation depth determines meeting quality. In corporate banking, and in private banking where the Bank offers it, the RM with the most current and relevant context consistently captures more wallet share. Under suitability requirements for regulated products, meeting notes and client assessment records must be documented — adding a post-meeting administration burden that compounds the time cost of preparation.

Meeting Outcome Intelligence

The AI agent analyzes RM meeting outcomes recorded in CRM — cross-sell conversion, commitment follow-up completion, and relationship satisfaction signals — and produces a weekly brief identifying the preparation quality and meeting type patterns associated with the highest commercial outcomes. The brief supports the RM team head's coaching cycle with evidence on what differentiates high-outcome meeting preparation from lower-outcome patterns. RM team heads review the brief and direct coaching effort toward the preparation and engagement practices with the strongest outcome correlation.

S
Problem to solve

Meeting outcome data — whether a cross-sell conversation converted, whether commitments were followed up, whether the client expressed a concern — is recorded in CRM interaction notes but is not aggregated into a pattern analysis at RM team level. The RM team head has no current-state view of which preparation practices or meeting types are generating the highest commercial outcomes across the book without commissioning bespoke CRM analysis. Coaching conversations on meeting preparation quality are directed from the team head's observation rather than from a systematic performance signal.

Solution

The AI agent reads CRM meeting records — preparation time logged, meeting type, cross-sell topics raised, conversion outcome, and follow-up completion rate — and correlates preparation quality indicators with commercial outcomes across the RM population. The weekly brief identifies the preparation and engagement practices with the strongest outcome correlation and flags RMs where outcome metrics suggest a coaching conversation is warranted. RM team heads review the brief and direct coaching sessions toward the evidence-ranked improvement opportunities; RM-level cross-sell conversion rate and follow-up completion rate are the primary outcome metrics.

Acceptance criteria

A weekly meeting outcome brief — identifying the preparation and engagement practices with the strongest correlation to cross-sell conversion and follow-up completion, and flagging RMs where a coaching conversation is warranted — is available to RM team heads for the coaching cycle.

Adoption

The AI agent delivers the meeting outcome brief for ≥95% of scheduled weeks for ≥48 consecutive weeks post go-live.

Acceptance

≥70% of evidence-ranked improvement opportunities confirmed as actionable by RM team heads; RM-level cross-sell conversion rate and follow-up completion rate tracked as primary outcome metrics.

Cycle

Meeting outcome pattern analysis moved from bespoke commissioned CRM analysis to weekly automated delivery.

Post-Meeting CRM Update Automation

The AI agent generates a structured CRM interaction record from the RM's post-meeting voice note or written summary, populating the standard fields — meeting date, participants, topics discussed, commitments, and cross-sell outcomes — ready for RM review and confirmation. The RM reviews, edits, and submits; no CRM record is committed without human confirmation. CRM documentation completion rate and after-meeting administration time per RM are the primary outcome metrics.

S
Problem to solve

Post-meeting CRM documentation is a manual administrative step that RMs perform under time pressure between client meetings; documentation quality varies with the RM's administrative discipline and available time. Under suitability requirements for regulated products, meeting notes for advisory interactions must meet a minimum standard of completeness; inconsistent documentation creates supervisory risk and limits the value of CRM data for book management analytics. CRM records that are incomplete or delayed reduce the quality of client interaction intelligence available for meeting preparation, creating a self-reinforcing cycle of poor documentation and poor preparation.

Solution

The AI agent reads the RM's post-meeting summary input — voice note transcription or written notes — and generates a structured CRM record with standard fields populated: meeting date, participants, topics, commitments made by each party, and cross-sell outcomes. The RM reviews and confirms before the record is committed to CRM; any field requiring regulatory completeness for advisory interaction records is highlighted if absent. CRM documentation completion rate — the proportion of meetings with a compliant interaction record within 24 hours — and after-meeting administration time per RM are tracked against the pre-deployment manual documentation baseline.

Acceptance criteria

A structured CRM interaction record — meeting date, participants, topics discussed, commitments, and cross-sell outcomes — generated from the RM's post-meeting voice note or written summary is available for RM review and confirmation after every client meeting.

Adoption

The AI agent generates a structured CRM record for ≥90% of client meetings with a submitted voice note or written summary for ≥48 consecutive weeks post go-live.

Acceptance

≥85% of generated records confirmed by the RM without substantive edit; ≥95% of meetings hold a compliant interaction record within 24 hours.

Cycle

After-meeting administration time reduced from 15–20 minutes of manual CRM entry per meeting to ≤5 minutes of review and confirmation.

RM Meeting Brief

The AI agent assembles a pre-meeting brief for the RM from CRM history, recent account activity, public corporate or wealth-profile data, and product holding gaps — ready for review before the client interaction. For existing clients the brief emphasizes relationship continuity and recent developments; for prospects it synthesizes wealth sources and known priorities. The RM reviews the brief, conducts the meeting, and records interaction notes in CRM post-engagement.

M
Problem to solve

Relationship managers prepare for client and prospect meetings by manually reviewing CRM notes, recent transaction history, company news, and product holdings — thorough preparation for a single meeting is time-consuming, and book size means most clients receive a brief review or none. Under suitability requirements for regulated products, post-meeting documentation adds a further time obligation on the same resource. Inconsistent preparation quality across the book results in uneven client experience and missed cross-sell signals at high-engagement interactions.

Solution

The AI agent reads CRM interaction history, recent account activity, public corporate filings or wealth profile data where applicable, and current product holdings. It generates a brief covering relationship summary, likely agenda topics, and recommended cross-sell prompts aligned to the client's lifecycle stage and product holding gaps. The RM reviews and acts; post-meeting notes are recorded in CRM to maintain a complete interaction history for compliance and continuity purposes.

Acceptance criteria

A pre-meeting brief — covering relationship summary, likely agenda topics, and recommended cross-sell prompts calibrated to the client's lifecycle stage and product holding gaps — is available to each RM before every scheduled client or prospect interaction, drawn from CRM history, account activity, and public corporate or wealth profile data.

Adoption

The AI agent generates a pre-meeting brief for ≥90% of scheduled RM client and prospect meetings for ≥48 consecutive weeks post go-live.

Acceptance

≥80% of briefs rated as adequate for meeting preparation by the RM without requiring supplementary manual research; cross-sell conversion rate tracked as primary outcome metric.

Cycle

RM meeting preparation time reduced from 30–60 minutes of manual CRM and news review per client to ≤10 minutes of brief review per meeting.

Relationship deepening & cross-sell

The identification and pursuit of product deepening opportunities within the existing client book — expanding product holdings, increasing wallet share, and strengthening the relationship's economic contribution to the Bank. Cross-sell in RM-managed segments is driven by event-triggered relevance: a corporate client restructuring its treasury function needs new transaction banking products; where the Bank serves private banking clients, a client with a maturing term deposit needs a reinvestment conversation. Segment propensity models provide population-level signals; per-client opportunity identification requires joining product holding data, transaction signals, and CRM interaction context.

Cross-Sell Conversion Intelligence

The AI agent analyzes cross-sell activity across the RM book — opportunities identified, conversations initiated, and conversion outcomes by product category and segment — and produces a weekly brief for the RM team head identifying the product categories with the lowest conversion rate and the client characteristics associated with high-conversion outcomes. The brief enables the RM team head to direct coaching and product knowledge investment toward the highest-conversion-gap categories. RM team head coaching preparation time and cross-sell conversion rate by product category are the primary outcome metrics.

S
Problem to solve

Cross-sell conversion rate is tracked at the aggregate RM level; the RM team head cannot identify which product categories are generating the most unrealized opportunity — where opportunities are identified but conversations are not converting — without discrete CRM analysis per RM and product. Product categories with low conversion may reflect a pricing problem, a suitability issue, or a presentation skills gap; distinguishing between these root causes requires outcome pattern analysis that is not available in the current reporting model. Coaching resources directed to cross-sell improvement are allocated by the team head's intuition rather than by a current-state category-level conversion signal.

Solution

The AI agent reads CRM records of cross-sell opportunities identified, conversations initiated, and conversion outcomes, and computes weekly conversion rates by product category and segment across the RM population. It identifies the product categories with the widest opportunity-to-conversion gap and the client characteristics associated with high-conversion outcomes. The RM team head reviews the brief and directs coaching effort toward the highest-gap categories; cross-sell conversion rate improvement by category is the primary outcome metric.

Acceptance criteria

A weekly cross-sell conversion brief — conversion rates by product category and segment, the categories with the widest opportunity-to-conversion gap, and the client characteristics associated with high-conversion outcomes — is available to the RM team head to direct coaching and product knowledge investment.

Adoption

The AI agent delivers the cross-sell conversion brief for ≥95% of scheduled weeks for ≥48 consecutive weeks post go-live.

Acceptance

≥75% of the highest-gap categories identified in the brief confirmed as coaching or product knowledge priorities by the RM team head; cross-sell conversion rate by product category tracked as primary outcome metric.

Cycle

Category-level conversion analysis reduced from discrete CRM analysis per RM and product to weekly automated delivery; RM team head coaching preparation time tracked against the pre-deployment baseline.

Cross-Sell Conversation Brief Automation

The AI agent generates a structured conversation brief for each high-priority cross-sell opportunity identified in the RM's ranked list — covering the product, the supporting rationale, the client's relevant context, and a suggested conversation approach — ready for the RM to review before the client interaction. The RM reviews and adapts the brief; the quality and specificity of the cross-sell prompt reduces the preparation time required before each opportunity conversation. Cross-sell conversion rate for opportunities with a generated brief versus unassisted opportunities is the primary outcome metric.

S
Problem to solve

RMs receive a ranked cross-sell list from the opportunity identification model but must manually construct the specific conversation approach for each client — researching the relevant context, determining the product fit rationale, and planning the conversation hook. Per-opportunity conversation preparation adds 20–40 minutes to the cross-sell cycle for each client; across a large book with multiple active opportunities, this preparation burden compresses the volume of cross-sell conversations an RM can pursue in a week. Cross-sell opportunities that remain in the priority list beyond three weeks without a conversation are unlikely to convert; the preparation burden is a contributing factor to opportunity aging.

Solution

The AI agent reads each high-priority cross-sell opportunity from the RM's ranked list and generates a structured conversation brief covering the recommended product, the supporting rationale drawn from transaction signals and lifecycle context, and a suggested conversation approach tailored to the client's relationship history. The RM reviews and adapts the brief before the client interaction; the brief is accessible from within the RM's call preparation workflow. Cross-sell conversion rate for opportunities with a generated brief compared to the unassisted baseline is the primary outcome metric; opportunity aging — days from identification to first client conversation — is a secondary indicator.

Acceptance criteria

A structured conversation brief for each high-priority cross-sell opportunity in the RM's ranked list — the recommended product, the supporting rationale, the client's relevant context, and a suggested conversation approach — is available to the RM before the client interaction.

Adoption

The AI agent generates a conversation brief for ≥90% of high-priority opportunities on each RM's ranked list within 24 hours of list publication, for ≥48 consecutive weeks post go-live.

Acceptance

≥75% of briefs used by RMs without substantive rework; cross-sell conversion rate for opportunities with a generated brief ≥15% higher than the unassisted baseline.

Cycle

Per-opportunity conversation preparation reduced from 20–40 minutes of manual research to ≤10 minutes of brief review; ≥85% of opportunities reach a first client conversation within three weeks of identification.

Cross-Sell Opportunity Identification

The AI agent identifies the highest-probability cross-sell opportunities in each RM's book from product holding gaps, transaction signals, and lifecycle events, ranked by expected revenue contribution. The output provides the recommended product, supporting rationale, and a suggested conversation hook tailored to the client's current circumstances. RMs integrate the ranked list into their weekly call plan.

M
Problem to solve

Cross-sell targeting within the RM book is driven by segment-level propensity campaigns; RMs receive campaign lists without client-specific context — which client recently restructured its treasury function, or, where the Bank serves private banking clients, which of them has a term deposit maturing in 30 days. High-probability opportunities are missed because the relevant signal is distributed across product systems, CRM, and public data sources with no consolidation at the RM level. Revenue from relationship deepening is systematically below potential when RM outreach is not informed by a current, client-specific opportunity signal.

Solution

The AI agent reads each client's product holdings, recent transaction patterns, lifecycle events — maturity dates, corporate actions, salary credits — and CRM interaction history. It generates a ranked cross-sell list per RM with the recommended product, supporting rationale, and a suggested conversation hook for each opportunity. RMs integrate the list into their call plan for the week, and the RM team head reviews the top-ranked opportunities monthly; cross-sell conversion rate and revenue per RM book are the primary outcome metrics.

Acceptance criteria

A weekly ranked cross-sell list per RM — specifying recommended product, supporting rationale, and suggested conversation hook drawn from product holding gaps, transaction signals, and lifecycle events — is available for integration into the RM's call plan.

Adoption

The AI agent produces a ranked cross-sell list for ≥95% of active RMs on each weekly cycle for ≥48 consecutive weeks post go-live.

Acceptance

≥70% of top-ranked cross-sell opportunities per RM confirmed as commercially appropriate by the RM team head on monthly review; cross-sell conversion rate and revenue per book tracked as primary outcome metrics.

Cycle

Cross-sell opportunity identification cycle reduced from quarterly segment campaign lists to weekly client-specific ranked output.
