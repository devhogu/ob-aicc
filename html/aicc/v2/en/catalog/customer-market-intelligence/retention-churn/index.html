Retention & churn

Retention and churn analytics tracks the rate at which customers deepen or reduce their banking relationship, and identifies the drivers that push customers toward exit. In retail banking, customers who use the bank as their main bank most often leave because they move their salary payments to another bank, switch to a digital competitor, or are unhappy with product fees — all of which produce detectable behavioral signals weeks before formal account closure. Generative AI enables the Bank to move from retrospective churn reporting to early identification of at-risk customers, compressing the window from signal to intervention from quarters to weeks.

Churn cohort analysis

Retrospective analysis of which customer cohorts have churned, at what rate, and with what pattern — the foundation for understanding which segment and product combinations have chronic retention problems. Cohort analysis by origination vintage, product bundle, and acquisition channel reveals structural retention differences that aggregate churn rates conceal. Churn cohort analysis must distinguish salary switches to another bank (high-value, fast) from product-fee-driven attrition (gradual, price-sensitive).

Churn Rate Automated Report

Automated monthly churn-rate report by segment, product, and origination vintage — produced from core banking data without manual extraction — delivered to commercial leadership as part of the standard management pack.

S
Problem to solve

Monthly churn reporting requires analyst extraction from core banking and CRM systems, standardized into a consistent format, with commentary written against a template. The process takes one to two analyst days per cycle and the output structure is unchanged month to month. Late delivery of the churn report delays the commercial review meeting preparation.

Solution

The AI agent reads account-closure and product-exit events from core banking monthly, computes churn rates by segment, product, and origination vintage, and produces the standard management report with trend commentary. Analysts review the AI-generated output and add forward-looking commentary; the data-assembly and formatting step is eliminated.

Acceptance criteria

Commercial leadership receives the monthly churn-rate report by segment, product, and origination vintage, with trend commentary, as part of the standard management pack and in time for commercial review preparation.

Adoption

The AI agent delivers the churn-rate report for ≥95% of scheduled monthly cycles for ≥12 consecutive months post go-live.

Acceptance

≥85% of reports accepted by analysts without correction of computed rates; ≥80% accepted by commercial leadership as the basis for the review without supplementary data requests.

Cycle

Churn report production reduced from one to two analyst days of extraction and formatting per cycle to ≤2 hours of analyst review and commentary.

Cohort Cross-Sell Window Analysis

The AI agent analyzes product-deepening rates by cohort, segment, and tenure to identify the post-onboarding windows when cross-sell conversion probability is highest for each product category.

M
Problem to solve

Cross-sell campaigns are timed to product launch cycles and seasonal calendars. The windows within the customer lifecycle when each segment is most receptive to each product — inferred from cohort conversion data — are not used to sequence outreach. Badly timed contact dilutes the commercial offer without increasing reach.

Solution

The AI agent reads product-holding events and contact-outcome records across customer cohorts, identifying the post-onboarding windows at which conversion probability peaks by product and segment. Product heads confirm the windows, and campaign and relationship management teams re-sequence the cross-sell program to match them; campaign efficiency improves without increasing contact volume.

Acceptance criteria

The post-onboarding windows of highest cross-sell conversion probability — by cohort, segment, and tenure for each product category — are available to campaign and relationship management teams from quarterly cohort product-deepening analysis.

Adoption

The AI agent refreshes the cross-sell window analysis for ≥90% of active product categories on the scheduled quarterly cadence for ≥4 consecutive quarters.

Acceptance

≥70% of recommended cross-sell windows confirmed as commercially actionable by product heads on quarterly review.

Cycle

Cross-sell window identification cycle reduced from annual retrospective analysis to quarterly automated cohort output.

Churn Driver Attribution by Cohort

The AI agent attributes churn events by cohort to primary driver categories — price sensitivity, service failure, loss to digital competitors, and salary switch to another bank — and produces a quarterly attribution report for commercial and retention strategy.

M
Problem to solve

Churn is tracked as a rate by segment. Why customers from specific cohorts exit — the driver rather than the rate — is understood only from exit surveys, which capture a voluntary sample of departing customers with inherent selection bias. Cohorts with high churn rates from service-failure drivers and cohorts churning due to competitor price actions require different retention responses, but the current reporting does not distinguish them.

Solution

The AI agent reads churn events with pre-exit behavioral signals, contact history, and where available exit-survey data, attributes each cohort's churn to primary driver categories using a classification model, and produces a quarterly attribution report. Commercial and retention teams design cohort-specific interventions based on driver attribution rather than rate alone.

Acceptance criteria

Commercial and retention teams receive a quarterly attribution report assigning each cohort's churn to primary driver categories — price sensitivity, service failure, loss to digital competitors, and salary switch to another bank — so that interventions are designed by driver rather than by rate alone.

Adoption

The AI agent produces the driver-attribution report for ≥90% of scheduled quarterly cycles for ≥4 consecutive quarters post go-live, covering all cohorts with churn events in the period.

Acceptance

≥75% of cohort driver attributions confirmed as plausible by commercial and retention teams on quarterly review; attribution consistent with exit-survey responses in ≥70% of cases where survey data exists.

Cycle

Churn-driver understanding moved from exit-survey samples to full-cohort attribution within 3 weeks of quarter close.

Save-program design

The structured process of designing retention interventions — by cohort, trigger signal, channel, offer type, and timing — that constitute the Bank's proactive churn-prevention program. Save-program ROI is determined by which customers are contacted, with which offer, through which channel, and at what point in the at-risk trajectory. In retail banking, save programs typically combine a fee-waiver or rate offer with a personal outreach from a relationship manager — a combination that is effective only for high-value customers and counterproductive for lower-value cohorts.

Save-Program Contact Scheduler

The AI agent generates a weekly save-program contact schedule from the current at-risk list, assigning each customer to the recommended intervention type, channel, and timing based on the cohort brief and relationship-manager capacity constraints.

S
Problem to solve

Save-program contact scheduling is performed manually by the retention operations team, who take the at-risk list, apply the cohort brief, and allocate customers to relationship managers and outreach channels while managing capacity constraints. The scheduling process takes one to two working days per week and produces a static list that is out of date by mid-week as new at-risk customers enter the scoring cycle.

Relationship managers receive their weekly queue automatically; retention operations reviews and approves the schedule rather than building it. The schedule updates when new at-risk customers enter mid-week.

Acceptance criteria

Relationship managers receive their weekly save-program contact queue — each at-risk customer assigned an intervention type, channel, and timing within capacity constraints — from a schedule that retention operations reviews and approves rather than builds.

Adoption

The AI agent generates the contact schedule for ≥95% of weekly cycles for ≥48 consecutive weeks post go-live and updates it for new at-risk customers entering mid-week.

Acceptance

≥85% of AI-generated schedules approved by retention operations with minor amendment or none.

Cycle

Schedule preparation reduced from one to two working days per week to ≤2 hours of retention operations review.

Save-Program Cohort Brief

The AI agent generates a cohort-level intervention brief for each at-risk cluster, specifying recommended offer type, channel, timing, and expected save rate based on prior-cycle efficacy data.

M
Problem to solve

Save-program interventions are designed once per cycle applying a standard offer set to all at-risk customers regardless of churn driver or cohort profile. Fee-waiver offers go to customers who would have stayed anyway; relationship-manager calls are allocated to price-sensitive cohorts better served by a self-service offer.

Solution

The AI agent reads cohort profiles, driver attribution from the at-risk model, and prior-cycle save-program outcome data, then produces a cohort brief specifying recommended intervention type, channel, timing, and expected save rate per at-risk cluster. The save-program team reviews the brief with the retention team lead and executes against the cohort-specific recommendations.

Acceptance criteria

A cohort-level intervention brief — specifying recommended offer type, channel, timing, and expected save rate from prior-cycle efficacy data — is available to the save-program team at the start of each save-program cycle.

Adoption

The AI agent generates a cohort intervention brief for ≥95% of at-risk clusters identified in each scoring cycle for ≥48 consecutive cycles post go-live.

Acceptance

≥80% of cohort intervention briefs confirmed as sufficient for program execution by the retention team lead without supplementary manual design work.

Cycle

Save-program cohort brief preparation cycle reduced from 5–7 days of manual cohort analysis and brief drafting to ≤24 hours of automated production.

Save-Offer Effectiveness Insights

The AI agent analyzes the outcomes of the prior three save-program cycles to identify which offer types, delivery channels, and contact timings produced the highest save rates by churn driver and cohort, and produces a design brief for the next cycle.

M
Problem to solve

Save-program design relies on accumulated experience and practitioner judgment. Which offer produced the best save rate for salary-switch churn in the SME segment in the last quarter, relative to the cost of the offer, is not available as a systematic analytical input. The program is adjusted through informal observation rather than structured outcome analysis.

Solution

The AI agent reads save-program contact records, offer details, cost data, and 90-day retention outcomes from the prior three cycles. It identifies offer-channel-timing combinations with above-average save rates by churn driver and cohort, and produces a design brief specifying the recommended combination set for the next program cycle. The save-program team designs the next cycle from the brief, which replaces informal program calibration with an evidence base.

Acceptance criteria

The save-program team receives a design brief before each program cycle, built from the outcomes of the prior three cycles, specifying the offer, channel, and timing combinations with the highest save rates by churn driver and cohort.

Adoption

The AI agent produces the design brief for ≥90% of save-program cycles for ≥4 consecutive cycles post go-live, drawing on contact records, offer details, cost data, and 90-day retention outcomes.

Acceptance

≥75% of recommended combinations adopted by the save-program team in the next cycle's design.

Cycle

Program calibration moved from informal observation to a structured design brief available ≥1 week before each program cycle starts.

At-risk customer identification

Forward-looking identification of currently-active customers displaying the behavioral precursors to churn — the operational output that enables proactive save-program targeting. At-risk scoring depends on multi-signal aggregation: no single signal (usage decline, support-ticket spike, payment failure) is sufficient; the combination determines risk level. Banks that can score their entire active base weekly rather than sample quarterly contact the right customers before the exit decision is made.

Segment-Specific At-Risk Profiling

The AI agent applies segment-specific at-risk thresholds to the weekly at-risk scoring output, producing separate prioritized lists for the SME, mass retail, and emerging-affluent segments calibrated to each segment's churn-signal profile.

S
Problem to solve

The at-risk model produces a single ranked list calibrated on the full active base. Segments with different behavioral profiles — SME customers, whose primary churn signal is credit-facility utilization decline rather than transaction-frequency drop — receive the same scoring threshold as retail customers. SME at-risk customers are systematically under-flagged in the unified model output.

Solution

The AI agent reads the weekly at-risk score output and applies segment-specific thresholds calibrated on historical churn patterns per segment. The SME relationship team, the mass retail save-program team, and the affluent relationship team each receive a segment-specific at-risk list with thresholds appropriate for their customer profile. SME at-risk customers are no longer under-flagged by a threshold calibrated on the full active base.

Acceptance criteria

The SME relationship team, the mass retail save-program team, and the affluent relationship team each receive a weekly at-risk list scored against thresholds calibrated to their segment's churn-signal profile.

Adoption

The AI agent produces all three segment-specific at-risk lists for ≥95% of weekly scoring cycles for ≥48 consecutive weeks post go-live.

Acceptance

≥75% of customers on each segment list confirmed as requiring intervention by the receiving team; share of SME churners flagged before exit raised to ≥70%.

Cycle

Segment-specific lists available within 24 hours of each weekly at-risk scoring run, replacing a single unified list.

At-Risk Scoring and Triage

The AI agent scores the full active customer base for churn probability weekly, surfacing a prioritized at-risk list for save-program and relationship-manager action.

M
Problem to solve

At-risk identification relies on relationship managers recognizing disengagement signals in their own books, supplemented by occasional analytics-team cohort studies. The multi-signal pattern that precedes churn — usage decline, support escalation, reduced transaction frequency, failed direct debit — is not assembled systematically across the full base before the customer exits.

Solution

The AI agent reads usage, support, payment, and digital-activity signals weekly for every active customer, scores churn probability using the multi-signal pattern, and produces a triage list ranked by risk score and segment priority. The retention team lead reviews the top-ranked flags weekly, and save-program teams and relationship managers work from the AI-generated list rather than reactive referrals.

Acceptance criteria

A weekly at-risk score covering the full active customer base is available to save-program teams and relationship managers, giving retention teams a prioritized intervention queue calibrated to current churn probability.

Adoption

The AI agent produces the ranked at-risk list for ≥95% of weekly scoring cycles for ≥48 consecutive weeks within 12 months of go-live.

Acceptance

≥75% of top-decile at-risk flags confirmed as requiring intervention by the retention team lead on weekly review.

Cycle

At-risk identification moved from relationship-manager referrals and occasional cohort studies to weekly automated scoring of the full active base within 90 days of go-live.

Early-Warning Signal Calibration

The AI agent analyzes historical churn events to identify the combination of behavioral signals that preceded exit at each lead time — 2 weeks, 4 weeks, 8 weeks — and produces a signal-calibration report for the at-risk model team.

M
Problem to solve

The at-risk model uses a fixed signal set selected at initial build. The relative predictive weight of signals changes as the customer base evolves — digital adoption increases, new product features alter usage patterns — but the model signal calibration is not reviewed until the next full model rebuild cycle, which occurs annually. Between rebuilds, the model quietly loses accuracy.

Solution

The AI agent reads confirmed churn events from the prior 12 months, traces the behavioral signal pattern in the weeks preceding each exit, and produces a signal-calibration report showing which signals carry the strongest lead-time predictive value at 2, 4, and 8 weeks before churn. The model team uses the AI-generated calibration report as the primary input to the next recalibration cycle.

Acceptance criteria

The at-risk model team receives a signal-calibration report showing which behavioral signals carry the strongest predictive value at 2, 4, and 8 weeks before churn, as the primary input to the next recalibration cycle.

Adoption

The AI agent produces the signal-calibration report for ≥90% of scheduled calibration reviews from go-live, covering all confirmed churn events from the prior 12 months.

Acceptance

≥80% of signal rankings confirmed as statistically valid by the model team; the report used as the primary input in 100% of recalibration cycles.

Cycle

Signal-calibration review cycle reduced from once per annual model rebuild to at least two interim calibration reports per year.

Intervention efficacy tracking

Systematic measurement of which save-program interventions worked — for which cohorts, in which channels, at which points in the at-risk trajectory — so that future intervention design is informed by accumulated evidence rather than intuition. Efficacy tracking is the feedback loop that makes the save program a learning system. Without it, the same ineffective interventions are repeated each cycle while effective ones are not scaled.

Intervention Efficacy Trend Report

The AI agent tracks the save rate trend for each intervention type across consecutive program cycles, surfacing whether a previously effective intervention type is decaying in performance and requires redesign.

S
Problem to solve

Save-program interventions are reviewed for efficacy in the current cycle but not trended across cycles. An intervention that produced a 55% save rate in Q1 and is now delivering 38% in Q3 is declining materially, but without a trend view the Q3 number looks adequate in isolation and the intervention is not queued for redesign.

Solution

The AI agent reads efficacy outcomes from consecutive program cycles, computes trend direction and statistical significance for each intervention type, and flags intervention types with declining save-rate trends for design review. The save-program team uses the trend report alongside the current-cycle efficacy output to distinguish genuinely effective interventions from those in decline.

Acceptance criteria

The save-program team receives a save-rate trend view for each intervention type across consecutive program cycles, with interventions in statistically significant decline flagged for design review.

Adoption

The AI agent produces the trend report for ≥95% of program cycles for ≥4 consecutive cycles post go-live, covering every active intervention type.

Acceptance

≥75% of decline flags confirmed as warranting design review by the save-program team.

Cycle

Cross-cycle efficacy trends, previously not tracked, are reported alongside each current-cycle efficacy output.

Intervention ROI by Cohort

The AI agent calculates the net ROI of each save-program intervention type by cohort — saved revenue minus offer cost and relationship-manager time — and produces a ranked ROI table for program investment decisions.

M
Problem to solve

Save-program efficacy is measured as save rate: the proportion of contacted at-risk customers who remained active 90 days after contact. Save rate does not account for offer cost or the value of the customer saved. A 60% save rate on a fee-waiver offer for a cohort with low customer lifetime value (CLV) may produce negative ROI; a 30% save rate on a rate offer for a high-CLV cohort may generate substantial value. The program is optimized for save rate rather than economic return.

Solution

The AI agent reads save-program outcomes alongside offer costs, relationship-manager time allocation, and CLV data for contacted customers, computes net ROI per intervention type per cohort, and produces a ranked ROI table. The save-program team allocates the next-cycle intervention budget to positive-ROI cohort-offer combinations and retires negative-ROI ones.

Acceptance criteria

The save-program team receives a ranked table of net ROI per intervention type per cohort — saved revenue minus offer cost and relationship-manager time — as the basis for next-cycle intervention budget allocation.

Adoption

The AI agent produces the ROI table for ≥90% of save-program cycles for ≥4 consecutive cycles post go-live, covering all cohort-offer combinations with completed 90-day outcomes.

Acceptance

≥80% of ROI rankings accepted by the save-program team as the basis for budget allocation; negative-ROI combinations retired or redesigned in ≥75% of cases.

Cycle

Program evaluation moved from save rate alone to net ROI per cohort-offer combination, available within 2 weeks of each 90-day outcome window closing.

Contact-pattern indicators

Monitoring customer contact patterns — rising contact frequency, unresolved multi-contact chains, channel-switching after failed service interactions — as leading indicators of churn risk. A customer who contacts the Bank three times in a month about the same issue and receives no resolution is materially more likely to churn than the contact-frequency number alone suggests. Under conduct-supervision requirements, systematic tracking of unresolved-contact chains is commonly a component of complaint-monitoring obligations.

Channel-Switching Churn Signal

The AI agent identifies customers who switch to an assisted channel — phone, branch — after a failed digital-channel interaction, flagging the pattern as a composite churn precursor for retention prioritization.

S
Problem to solve

Channel-switching after digital failure is a known churn precursor: a customer who cannot complete a task in the mobile app and subsequently calls the contact center is materially more likely to exit than a customer who calls without prior digital failure. This pattern is not detected systematically because digital-failure events and subsequent contact-center contacts are logged in separate systems with no cross-system linkage.

Solution

The AI agent reads mobile-app error and incomplete-journey events alongside contact-center contact logs, links events by customer and temporal proximity, and identifies the channel-switch pattern. Customers matching the pattern are added to the retention at-risk queue with a higher churn-precursor weight than usage-decline signals alone, and the retention team reviews a sample of the flags.

Acceptance criteria

Customers who switch to phone or branch after a failed digital-channel interaction are identified and added to the retention at-risk queue as a composite churn precursor, weighted above usage-decline signals alone.

Adoption

The AI agent links mobile-app error and incomplete-journey events to contact-center contacts for ≥95% of weekly at-risk cycles for ≥48 consecutive weeks post go-live.

Acceptance

≥70% of flagged channel-switch customers confirmed as warranting retention attention by the retention team on sampled review.

Cycle

The channel-switch pattern, previously undetected because the events were logged in separate systems, enters the at-risk queue within one week of the triggering contact.

Contact Frequency Trend Monitor

The AI agent tracks weekly contact frequency per customer against a personalized baseline, flagging customers whose contact rate has risen significantly for service-quality review and retention alert.

S
Problem to solve

Contact-center management tracks aggregate contact volume per week. Individual customer-level contact frequency trends are not monitored. A customer whose contact rate triples in a four-week window — typically indicating a persistent unresolved issue — appears only as a marginal increment in the aggregate weekly contact count and receives no special attention.

Solution

The AI agent reads customer-level contact logs weekly, computes contact frequency relative to each customer's own baseline over the prior 90 days, and flags customers showing a material acceleration. The service-quality team reviews flagged customers and escalates where the accelerated contact indicates an unresolved issue; the at-risk model receives the elevated contact-frequency signal as a churn precursor input.

Acceptance criteria

The service-quality team receives a weekly list of customers whose contact rate has accelerated materially against their own 90-day baseline, and the at-risk model receives the same signal as a churn-precursor input.

Adoption

The AI agent computes customer-level contact frequency against baseline for ≥95% of weekly cycles for ≥48 consecutive weeks post go-live.

Acceptance

≥70% of flagged customers confirmed by the service-quality team as having an unresolved issue requiring escalation.

Cycle

Contact-frequency monitoring moved from aggregate weekly volume to customer-level flags within 24 hours of week close.

Multi-Contact Chain Detection

The AI agent links customer contacts across channels by topic and time proximity weekly, identifies unresolved multi-contact chains, and surfaces customers with repeat unresolved issues for prioritized intervention before they reach formal complaint or churn.

M
Problem to solve

Customer contacts are logged per interaction across phone, chat, email, and branch channels with individual ticket IDs. A customer who calls three times about the same unresolved issue appears as three separate contacts in the system. The unresolved chain — the most reliable precursor to formal complaint or churn — is invisible in the ticket-level data.

Solution

The AI agent reads all customer interaction logs, links contacts by customer, topic, and temporal proximity, identifies chains of two or more interactions on the same unresolved issue, and produces a weekly prioritized list of customers with open multi-contact chains. Customer-experience and operations teams triage the list before formal complaints or exit behaviors materialize.

Acceptance criteria

Customer-experience and operations teams receive a weekly prioritized list of customers with open multi-contact chains — two or more interactions on the same unresolved issue across channels — for triage before formal complaint or churn.

Adoption

The AI agent links contacts across phone, chat, email, and branch and delivers the prioritized chain list for ≥95% of weekly cycles for ≥48 consecutive weeks post go-live.

Acceptance

≥80% of detected chains confirmed as a single unresolved issue by the triaging team; ≥70% of listed customers contacted within 5 business days.

Cycle

Unresolved multi-contact chains, previously invisible in ticket-level data, appear in a weekly list within 24 hours of week close.
