Strategy

Our strategy for using AI grows out of what has already been proven in practice. It builds on the business strategy and is made up of strategic themes and the Hub's own goals, and progress is marked by maturity levels.

Business strategy

The business's mission is to improve people's lives with digital products and services. The strategy rests on four strategic directions, and AI serves each of them through the strategic themes described below.

Strategic direction How AI helps Through which themes
Innovation leadership AI as a working tool for every employee; data helps us understand customers better Customer understanding; AI at the workplace
Financial inclusion Remote onboarding and identification of customers, service in Kyrgyz and Russian, responsible lending decisions Customer understanding; AI in flows, operations, and systems
Shareholder value Higher productivity, lower costs of routine work, better management decisions, and better information for investors Management analytics; AI in the work of departments; Maintenance and support
ESG goals Responsible lending, fair treatment of customers, data protection, good governance practice All themes, through our values and principles

Strategic themes

Strategic themes are the areas where AI brings the most value. They are listed in order of priority, and each one develops at its own pace. Every initiative in the portfolio serves at least one theme.

  1. Customer understanding. A complete picture of each customer across all products: verification, behavior, changing needs, and the difficulties they run into. The result: risk-aware service and help for customers before they ask for it.
  2. Management analytics. Reporting and analysis, forecasts and scenarios, investor materials; every figure traces back to a verified source. The result: faster, better-grounded decisions.
  3. AI in the work of departments. Document drafts, summaries, search, translation, and automation of routine work, shaped by each department's priorities. The result: more time for important work and consistent quality.
  4. AI at the workplace. An assistant built right into an employee's work: accurate answers from up-to-date sources, help preparing reports and forms. The result: consistent answers, faster onboarding for newcomers, and less routine.
  5. AI in flows, operations, and systems. AI prepares case files and descriptions, suggests corrections to documents, and spots deviations, while an employee makes the final decision. The result: work moves faster and with fewer errors.
  6. Maintenance and support. Detecting and analyzing incidents, finding root causes, assessing the risk of changes, supporting users. The result: systems run more reliably and recover faster after failures.

All themes rest on the same foundation: people who learn to work with AI before they start using it; data and knowledge that have owners; a shared AI platform; and vetted suppliers with a fallback option.

Our goals

The Hub sets its goals so that they can be checked against project cards and flow measures.

Goal How we'll know it's working
Proven value. Every initiative ends with a measurable result The share of completed initiatives where the business owner confirmed the intended outcome
Faster from idea to result. A good idea doesn't wait for months Lead time and the age of work in the funnel
Built once, works for many. A successful solution leaves a ready-made asset behind The number of reusable packages and how many times other departments have used them
People know how to work with AI. Training comes before use How many employees are trained and use the solutions in their work
Reliability. No solution without a risk tier and someone accountable for it The share of solutions with a risk tier and a business owner; AI incidents analyzed
A common foundation. Shared components and knowledge matter more than individual solutions The share of solutions on the shared platform and with knowledge sources that have an owner

Maturity levels

AI use grows level by level. Each theme climbs the levels at its own pace, and a maturity level counts as reached when its measures are met.

  1. Foundation AI is brought under management before its use grows: controlled experiments and one assistant for a pilot group.
  2. Controlled adoption Employees get safe access to assistants, and the first knowledge bases and theme pilots appear.
  3. Integration AI works in selected processes and systems: it suggests and prepares, and an employee decides.
  4. Scaling Different departments use the shared platform, and AI becomes normal practice across all themes.
  5. Agentic use Clearly bounded tasks are handed over to AI agents under continuous evaluation and human oversight.

The platform grows along with the levels. First comes a single point of access to models, the model gateway, then shared knowledge search, and then the tool gateway, through which AI gets access to systems: read-only at first, later with a limited right to take actions. Guardrails move from rules on paper to checks built into the platform, and monitoring of AI's work moves from manual to continuous.

How to assess progress

  • Foundation: all known uses of AI are recorded, each one has a risk tier, and baseline values of the measures have been taken.
  • Controlled adoption: how many employees have access to an assistant and have been trained, how many important processes have an up-to-date knowledge source, and how the first pilots are going.
  • Integration: how many processes AI works in, how turnaround times and error counts have changed, and how often employees correct AI.
  • Scaling: the share of solutions on the shared platform, reuse of components, and value compared with investment.
  • Agentic use: the share of suitable tasks handed over to AI agents, how many of their actions were stopped by people or by the platform's guardrails, and how quickly incidents are resolved.

How the strategy is updated

Once a year, the themes and goals are checked against the business strategy and against what completed initiatives have shown: some are added, some are closed. Once every program increment, the product management forum looks at how each theme is progressing and decides where to direct the next initiatives. Changes are reflected on this page.
