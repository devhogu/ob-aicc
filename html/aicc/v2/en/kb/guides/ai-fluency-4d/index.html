This guide is for anyone who has already tried working with an assistant. By the end, you will be able to approach any work task with four questions: what to hand to AI, how to describe it, how to check the result, and what you remain responsible for yourself.

Being good at working with AI isn't about knowing which buttons to press. The AI Fluency course, which Anthropic also uses to train its own staff, boils this ability down to four skills. All four names start with D, hence 4D.

  1. Delegation (what to hand over)Decide whether to take on a task with AI at all, which part to hand to it and which to keep for yourself.
  2. Description (how to explain it)Explain the goal, the context and the result you need so that AI understands the task. That is what a good prompt is.
  3. Discernment (judging the result)Judge critically how useful and correct the result is, and notice where AI got it wrong.
  4. Diligence (owning the result)Take responsibility for what you do with AI: what data you share, what you pass on, and whom you tell that you worked with AI.

Example: a memo on a new regulation

Your manager asks for a memo on what a new regulation from the regulator changes for your department.

Delegation

The assistant summarizes the changes and drafts the memo. You draw the conclusions for the department and make the recommendations yourself.

Description

Who you are, who the memo is for, the length and the format. You paste in the text of the regulation and ask it to refer to its clauses.

Discernment

You check every date and clause number against the original and look for exceptions it missed.

Diligence

You don't share internal data beyond what is allowed; you sign the memo yourself and, if needed, say that the draft was prepared with AI.

Describe and evaluate, round and round

The two middle skills work together. You describe the task, evaluate the answer, refine the description, and repeat several times. A poor answer most often means the description was incomplete, not that AI “can't do it”.

Three ways to work with AI

Automation

AI carries out a specific assignment following your instructions: translate, shorten, extract data. For example, shorten meeting minutes to five points.

Augmentation

AI is a thinking partner: together you look for ideas, test arguments and improve a text. For example, think through the structure of a memo and possible objections.

Agency

You set AI up so that from then on it carries out tasks for you on its own, for example answering questions from a knowledge base.

The more independence AI has, the more the fourth skill matters: decide in advance where a person checks the result and can stop the work.

Try it today

Take one work task for this week (a report, an email, a meeting summary) and write it up in four lines, one for each D: what you hand to AI, how you will describe the task, how you will check the result, and what you will tell colleagues about how it was produced. Separately, note what you kept for yourself.

For each skill in detail, with exercises, see the guide AI Fluency: the complete guide.
