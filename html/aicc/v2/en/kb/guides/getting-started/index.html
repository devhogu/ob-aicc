This guide is for people who have never worked with AI before. In five minutes you will pick your first task, start a conversation with an assistant and check its answer against the original.

An AI assistant is a program you talk to: you write to it in plain language, and it quickly reads, writes and summarizes text. It knows nothing about your work until you tell it, and it can be wrong with full confidence. So start with a small, real task whose result you can easily check yourself.

The examples in this Hub are for Claude, the AI assistant made by Anthropic. Use the assistant only through a work account approved in your organization. If you don't know whether you have one, ask your manager or write to us.

Pick your first task

A good task is one where you already know the right answer or can check it quickly:

  • shorten a long email or meeting minutes to five points;
  • rewrite a text more simply or more formally, for example a draft internal memo;
  • draft a reply to a typical customer question, without names or account numbers;
  • come up with options for a headline, questions for a meeting or an outline for a document.

Don't start with tasks where a mistake is costly: decisions about customers or money, or legal wording.

For your first conversations, use text without the names of customers or colleagues, account numbers, phone numbers or internal figures, for example a general mailing or your own draft. For details on what you can and cannot give an assistant, see the guide What you can and cannot share with AI.

Start the conversation

What you write to an assistant is called a prompt. A good first prompt comes together in four steps:

  1. Say who you are and what you do: “I'm a specialist in the operations department.”
  2. Describe the task and the result you need: “I need a half-page summary for my manager.”
  3. Give it the material: paste the text into the conversation window or attach a file, if that data may be shared.
  4. Read the answer and refine it: “shorter”, “no introductory phrases”, “add a table”.

Refining is normal: the second or third answer is usually better than the first.

Try it now

Take a long email with no personal or confidential data and write to the assistant:

I'm a specialist in the operations department. Below is an email about a new working procedure. Shorten it to five points, one sentence each. Don't add anything of your own.

Then check the answer against the original: is every point actually in the email, and has anything important been lost, such as a deadline, an amount or an exception to the rule? If something is wrong, tell the assistant and ask it to fix it.

What to expect

  • The assistant is good at drafts, summaries, rewording and ideas.
  • It can make up a fact, a figure or a link. A confident mistake like this is called a hallucination. Check everything you pass on: to colleagues, your manager or a customer.
  • You remain responsible for the result: the assistant suggests, you decide.

To learn how to word prompts more precisely, see the guide How to brief the assistant.
