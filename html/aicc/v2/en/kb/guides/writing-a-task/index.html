This guide is for anyone who has already tried the assistant on simple tasks and wants the right answer the first time. By the end, you will be able to build any work prompt from five parts.

A prompt is everything you write to the assistant: the request, the explanations, any text you paste in. The assistant does what it understood from the prompt and doesn't know anything you didn't write. The more precise the prompt, the less rework. In the examples the assistant is Claude; use it only through a work account approved in your organization.

The five parts of a prompt

  1. Who and for whomyour role and who will read the result
  2. What to doone task, as a verb: “shorten”, “compare”, “draft”
  3. From whata text, a file, data, an example of a good result
  4. In what formlength, structure, tone, language
  5. How to checkask the assistant to say where its statements come from and what it isn't sure about

You don't have to write all five parts at length: often one sentence each is enough. Paste in materials only if they may be shared — see What you can and cannot share with AI.

Before and after

Before After
“Write about loans” “I work in the contact center. Draft a reply to a customer who is asking why their loan application was declined. Polite tone, 5–6 sentences, no promises. Don't give the reason for the decline: the lending department communicates it.”
“Make a report” “Here is the export of customer requests for the week (table below). Group them by topic and show the five most frequent topics with the number of requests. Format: a table and three one-sentence conclusions. Separately, list the rows you couldn't assign to any topic.”
“Write a memo” “I'm a specialist in the operations department; the head of the division will read the memo. Draft an internal memo about moving the reconciliation deadline from the 5th to the 10th of the month, because the accounting system is being upgraded. My notes are below. Formal style, no more than half a page. At the end, list anything you added yourself that isn't in my notes.”

Look at the “After” column and find all five parts in each prompt. Sometimes “from what” is pasted text, and sometimes it's the situation described in the prompt itself.

Techniques that help

  • Give a sample. One example of the result you want works better than a long description.
  • Break up a big task. First a plan, then each item separately.
  • Ask for options. “Give me three options for the email subject line” — and pick the best one.
  • Set boundaries. “Use only the attached document”; “if the data isn't enough, say so.”
  • Save prompts that work. You can reuse a good prompt for a recurring task again and again.

Try it now

  1. Find your last prompt to the assistant whose answer you had to redo.
  2. Rewrite it using the five parts: who and for whom, what to do, from what, in what form, how to check.
  3. Send both versions in new conversations and compare the answers.
