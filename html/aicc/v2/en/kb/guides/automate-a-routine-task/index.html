How to automate a routine task

This guide is for an employee with recurring work: a weekly report, a summary of customer requests, checking documents against a list. By the end you will have gone through the five steps from a manual task to automation and will see which step your task is at now.

Workspace automation is when AI prepares recurring work (collecting data, a report, filling in a form, sorting emails), and a person checks it and decides. Start small: one task that you do every week and know well.

  1. Describewhat goes in, what comes out, who reads it
  2. Do it with Claudeseveral times, on real data that may be shared
  3. Make it sticka prompt template, a project, a skill, or a scheduled task
  4. Checka person looks at the result before it goes further
  5. Measurehow much time is saved and how many corrections are needed

1. Describe the task

Write the task down in three lines: what goes in, what should come out, and who reads it. For example:

  • Input: the weekly export of overdue loans in Excel.
  • Output: a one-page summary with totals, changes over the week, and the five largest cases.
  • Who reads it: the head of the department, on Mondays before lunch.

Also write down how long the task takes now: you'll need it in step five. If you can't describe the task in three lines, it's too early to automate it.

2. Do it with Claude

Do the task with Claude in an approved work account three or four times on real data, only data that may be shared (see What you can and cannot share with AI). This way you will find the wording of the prompt that reliably gives the result you need, and you will see where Claude makes mistakes.

3. Make it stick

Choose the simplest method that fits:

  • Prompt template: save the prompt that worked and drop new data into it.
  • Project: if the task always relies on the same documents (see Projects in Claude).
  • Skill: if it's a procedure that needs to be done the same way in different conversations (see Skills: teach Claude your procedure).
  • Scheduled task in Cowork: if the result is needed regularly and Claude can prepare a draft on its own (see Scheduled tasks).

If the task touches work systems or the whole department, we help make it stick: that way it comes to us as run-rate work.

4. Keep a human check

Automation prepares; a person checks and sends. This matters most for anything that goes to a customer, to another department, or affects a decision. You may remove the check only where a mistake costs nothing and that is written down.

5. Measure

Compare the time and the number of corrections before and after. If there is no gain, that is a result too: the task is better left as it is.

Try it now

Take one of your weekly tasks and describe it in three lines, as in the example above: input, output, who reads it. Add a fourth line with how long it takes you. You will use this description to write your first skill.
