Learning map: Prompting Claude

Ask Claude for what you need and get it right the first time, from a simple request to a complex multi-step scenario.

Who it's for: for everyoneSteps: 12 · about 6 h
0 of 12
Level 1

Beginner

  1. 1
    How to brief the assistantguide · 6 min

    Build a prompt from five parts: who and for whom, what to do, from what, in what form, and how to check it.

    Try this: Rewrite your last prompt using the five parts and compare the answers.

  2. 2
    Be clear and explain whyguide · 4 min

    Apply the colleague test and explain what the result is for.

    Try this: Add one line to a weak prompt saying who the result is for and why it is needed, then compare the answers.

  3. 3
    Show an exampleguide · 4 min

    Set the tone and format with examples.

    Try this: Give Claude three of your emails that worked well, with no personal data, as models for a new one.

Checkpoint. Your prompts are clear, with a role, a task, materials, and a format, and you include examples.

Level 2

Practitioner

  1. 4
    Role and structure: separate instructions from dataguide · 4 min

    Separate instructions from data with tags, and set a role.

    Try this: Summarize a new regulation for colleagues, separating the role, the document, and the instruction with tags.

  2. 5
    Answer format: ask for what you needguide · 4 min

    Get an answer of the form and length you need.

    Try this: Turn a data export into a table with the columns you specify, plus three conclusions.

  3. 6
    Long documents: text first, question lastguide · 5 min

    Work with long documents: order, tags, and quotes.

    Try this: Compare two contracts, asking Claude to pull out quotes first.

Checkpoint. You control structure and format, and you work with long documents.

Level 3

Advanced

  1. 7
    Let it think: thinking and effort levelguide · 4 min

    Choose the effort level and ask Claude to think things through where it matters.

    Try this: Check an internal memo against a checklist at two effort levels and compare the results.

  2. 8
    How to reduce made-up answersguide · 5 min

    Use the seven techniques for reducing made-up answers.

    Try this: Ask a question about a document, let Claude say “I don’t know,” and check the answer against quotes.

  3. 9
    A big task as a chain of promptsguide · 5 min

    Break a large task into a “draft, check, revise” chain.

    Try this: Prepare a reply to a customer complaint in three prompts (draft, check, revise), with no personal data.

Checkpoint. You cut down on made-up answers and handle complex tasks with chains of prompts.

Level 4

Expert

  1. 10
    How to write prompts for Claude: the complete guideguide · 35 min

    Build a complex prompt on a framework and refine it against 5–10 examples.

    Try this: Make a template for one of your weekly tasks and test it on past examples.

  2. 11
    Prompting best practices ↗documentation, EN · 60 min

    Work from the primary source and know the specifics of each model.

  3. 12
    Anthropic’s interactive prompt engineering tutorial ↗hands-on, EN · 240 min

    Work through nine chapters with exercises.

Checkpoint. You write templates for recurring tasks and improve them based on test results.
