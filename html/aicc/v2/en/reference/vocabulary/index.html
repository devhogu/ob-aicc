Vocabulary

The terms we use and what they mean.

TermWhat it means
acceptance criteriaConditions written down in advance: what the solution must do, and must not do, for it to be fit for use.
AI agentA system in which a model plans and acts toward a goal using tools: it gets data, calls services, fills in forms, and runs code.
AI incidentAn event in which a use of AI causes or may cause harm, or leads to a breach of rules or a loss of control. This includes cases where the harm was prevented.
AI literacyUnderstanding what AI can and can’t do. Everyone who uses AI needs it.
alternative dataData other than credit history that is used to assess a customer: transactional, mobile, behavioral. It raises particular questions of fairness and consent.
appetiteHow much effort we are willing to put into an initiative before we stop and look at the result.
backlogAn ordered list of work items waiting their turn.
benchmarkA standard test for comparing models. It helps you choose a model but says nothing about how it will handle your task.
biasSystematic unfairness in results, learned from the data or built in during design.
business caseA short justification: what the problem is, what the result will be, how we will measure it, and what it will cost.
business ownerThe person in a department who needs the result. Answers for why we are doing the work and decides whether it is worth continuing.
CapabilityA large piece of an initiative’s result that can be shown and checked. It is made up of Features.
class of serviceA way of handling a piece of work by its urgency and size: run-rate work, an initiative, or urgent work.
community of practiceEmployees from different departments who regularly share their experience of applying AI to similar work.
context windowThe amount of text a model takes into account at once. Whatever doesn’t fit, the model doesn’t see.
cycle timeHow long a work item spends in active development.
data leakageData ending up where it shouldn’t be: in a vendor’s training set, in an answer to another user, or in a log.
data lineageThe traceable path of every figure from its source to the report: where it came from, how it was transformed, and who is responsible for it.
data poisoningDeliberately corrupting the data a model learns from, or the documents it draws information from, to change its behavior.
decision logA record on the project card: what was decided, by whom, and when.
doingThe state of an initiative that is being worked on.
doneThe state of an initiative whose work is finished and whose result is recorded.
driftA decline in a model’s quality as the real world moves further away from the data the model was trained on.
embedded financeFinancial services inside a non-financial product or app, provided through a partner bank.
embeddingA numerical representation of text that lets a system find passages close in meaning to a question. Such representations are kept in a vector store.
evaluation setA set of real-world cases with expected results. A solution is checked against it before launch and after every noticeable change.
excessive agencyA situation in which an AI agent has more functions, access rights, or autonomy than its task needs.
exit criterionA condition, written down in advance, under which a step counts as complete.
explainabilityThe ability to explain why a system produced a given result, in a way that the affected person, a supervisor, or an auditor can rely on.
FeatureA small, complete part of a Capability that a team builds in one iteration.
fine-tuningAdditional training of an existing model on the data of a specific task or organization.
foundation modelA large general-purpose model that is trained for a wide range of tasks and then adapted to specific ones.
funnelThe entry level: every proposal and need lands here until it has been reviewed and a decision made on what to do next.
generative AIAI that creates content, such as text, images, program code, or audio, rather than only a forecast or a class.
governanceHow decisions and responsibilities around work with AI are organized: strategy and way of working, documents and processes, rules and criteria, risks, checks, and monitoring of solutions in operation.
groundingTying a model’s answers to verified sources, so that every answer can be checked.
guardrailsRules, filters, and limits around a model: what it may and may not answer, and which actions it is allowed to take.
hallucinationA smooth, confident, and wrong model output: a fact, figure, or reference that doesn’t exist. It is also called confabulation.
high-risk AIUses of AI that the law classes as high risk, such as credit scoring of individuals under the EU AI Act. They must meet the full set of requirements.
human in the loopAn approach in which a person checks every AI result before it is used and confirms every action.
human on the loopAn approach in which a person does not confirm every AI action but watches the system and can step in and stop it.
impact assessmentAn assessment of how a higher-risk use of AI will affect people and their rights. It is done before launch.
independent reviewA review of a solution by a person who was not involved in building it. In the medium and high risk tiers, it is done together with risk, security, and legal specialists.
initiativeWork that delivers a new result and needs its own start decision. Anything bigger than a small recurring task.
investment decisionThe decision to commit a set amount of effort to an initiative’s next step. It is made after shaping and revisited at each review point.
iterationA short, fixed-length period of work, at the end of which there is something to show.
jailbreakSpecially crafted input that makes a model ignore its guardrails.
KanbanA board on which work moves through states from left to right, and the number of work items in each state is limited.
knowledge baseDocuments and rules gathered in one place, each with an owner and a review date. AI answers from them and shows where the answer comes from.
KYCKnow your customer: establishing and verifying a customer’s identity and assessing the related risk, at onboarding and throughout the relationship.
lead timeHow long it takes from the moment a work item appears until it is finished.
leading indicatorA sign that shows up early and suggests the work is heading toward the intended result, before the result itself can be measured.
liveness checkA check that a document is presented by a live person who is actually there, not by a photo or a recording of them.
LLMLarge language model: a model trained on very large amounts of text to predict the next piece of text. The basis of generative AI for working with language.
machine learningA way to build a model from examples rather than by writing out its rules: the model finds a pattern in the examples and applies it to new cases.
maturity levelA stage on the path to adopting AI, from the first controlled experiments to AI agents at work. A stage counts as complete when its indicators are met.
model cardA short description of a model or system: what it is, what it is for, how it was tested, and when it should not be used.
model collapseThe degradation of models that learn more and more from content created by other models.
model gatewayA single point of access to models: request routing, cost tracking, logs, and data protection in one place.
model risk managementThe banking practice of validating, documenting, and monitoring models. AI governance extends it to new kinds of systems.
MVPMinimum viable product: the smallest version of a solution that you can put in front of users to learn whether to continue.
open bankingAccess by licensed third parties to account data and to payment initiation through interfaces, with the customer’s consent.
over-relianceUsers accept AI results without checking them, because the system is usually right.
persevere, pivot, or stopThe decision at a review point: carry on as we are, change direction, or stop the work.
portfolioThe level at which selected proposals become initiatives and get their place in the work queue.
practice checklistA short list of what is usually checked in this kind of work, with whom, and against which materials. It is a reminder, not a Hub requirement.
product management forumA regular meeting that looks at the funnel and the portfolio: what is waiting, what is stuck, and what it is time to start or stop.
product managerAnswers for what exactly we do and in what order: defines the value and makes sure the result is useful.
programThe level at which an initiative is broken down into Capabilities and Features, and the work runs in short iterations.
program decision forumA regular meeting that settles program questions: the order of work, dependencies, and what to do when something slips.
program incrementSeveral iterations in a row, at the end of which the program plans its next stretch.
project cardA living record of a project: what we are doing, why, who is responsible, and what stage it is at. The project manager maintains it, and the site shows it as is.
project managerRuns the project day by day and keeps its card up to date.
project profileA ready-made description of a kind of work: how it is organized, which stages it goes through, which roles it needs, and what is usually checked.
promptThe instructions and context a model receives at the moment it is used. The system prompt is set by the developer and applies all the time.
prompt injectionText in the input that the model carries out as an instruction, against the developer’s intent. It can be hidden in a document, an email, or a web page.
proof of conceptA short hands-on check that an idea works: the solution is tried on a small scale, and the result shows whether to continue.
pullThe principle that work is taken on when there is free capacity, not pushed down from above when there is no room.
RAGRetrieval-augmented generation: an approach in which, at the moment of a question, the model receives relevant passages from verified documents and answers from them with references, rather than from memory.
readyThe state of an initiative when everything is clear and the start decision can be made as soon as there is room.
real-time scoringA score that a model produces while the transaction or request itself is still in progress, for example in fraud detection.
regtechTechnology, including AI, used in compliance work: screening, monitoring, reporting, and tracking regulatory change.
responsible AIPrinciples and practices that make whoever uses AI accountable for what the AI does and able to vouch for its results.
reusable packageWhat remains after a piece of work and can be used again: a method, templates, a toolkit, or a software component. With it, the next department gets its result in days rather than weeks.
risk tierAn assessment of how serious the consequences of an AI solution’s mistake would be. It determines how deep the checks go.
risk-based approachThe principle that the extent of checks and controls is proportionate to the risk of the use.
run-rate workSmall recurring work done within one iteration, following a clear template.
sandboxAn isolated environment for experiments: data is read-only, every action is logged, and nothing reaches production systems.
shadow AIAI tools that employees use at work in place of the ones the organization provides for that kind of data.
shapingThe state of an initiative in which the problem, the intended outcome, and the boundaries are clarified, so that we can decide whether to take it on.
skill libraryReady-made prompts and techniques gathered for one kind of work, which employees use and add to themselves.
start decisionThe decision to take an initiative into work. The business owner makes it together with the product manager, following a rule known in advance.
stop thresholdA signal, written down in advance, that the work should be stopped or reconsidered.
strategic themeAn area in which AI should bring the business the most value in the coming years. Every initiative contributes to at least one theme.
support levelAn agreement on how a solution is supported after launch: not at all, on request, within agreed times, or we run it ourselves.
team levelThe level of the teams’ day-to-day work. It lives in Jira; the Hub only links to it.
throughputHow many work items are finished in a period.
time-boxA time limit set in advance for a piece of work or an experiment. When the time is up, you look at the result instead of quietly extending the work.
tokenThe unit of text a language model works with: a word or part of one. Costs and limits are counted in tokens.
tool gatewayThe point through which an AI system gets access to information systems and services, with separate permissions and limits for each tool.
urgent workWork that can’t be put off: it jumps the queue, and whatever it pushes back is reported right away.
WIP limitHow many work items can be in one state at the same time. New work starts when room frees up.
workspace automationAn AI assistant that runs on an employee’s computer and takes over routine work, such as preparing reports and filling in forms.
WSJFWeighted shortest job first: a way of ordering work items of equal importance, starting with whatever gives the most value in the least time. It is used only to choose between equals.
