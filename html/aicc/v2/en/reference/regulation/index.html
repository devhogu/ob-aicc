Regulators and legal acts

Who regulates what in AI and data: here, in the region, and worldwide. In short: what each document is and what it means for us. This is a starting point for discussion, not legal advice; for the exact text, go to the original source and to the lawyers.

Kyrgyz Republic

Central bank and banking supervisorapplies to us

National Bank of the Kyrgyz Republic

Acts on banking, information security, outsourcing, operational risk, and payment systems.

For us. Any use of AI that touches a banking process or the data of customers or third parties falls under its requirements.

Codeapplies to us

Digital Code of the Kyrgyz Republic (Цифровой кодекс Кыргызской Республики)

In force since February 2026, it brings together the rules on digital services, electronic signatures, biometric identification, personal data protection, and AI systems. It replaced the 2008 Law “On Personal Information.”

For us. It applies to every AI solution that works with customer or employee data: which data, for what purpose, with what consent, and where to process it. It applies to AI systems themselves through the requirements for assessing their hazard.

Read more →

Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 770 of 2 December 2025applies to us

Requirements for the hazard assessment of AI systems and for AI systems of increased hazard (Требования к оценке опасности систем AI и к системам повышенной опасности)

Adopted to implement the Digital Code: a method for assessing the hazard of AI systems; risk management for systems of increased hazard; and their transparency, understandability, controllability, accuracy, reliability, data quality, and technical documentation.

For us. It applies to owners of AI systems, whatever their form of ownership. Our risk tiers and checks need to be compared with this method.

Law and financial intelligence unitapplies to us

Anti-money laundering and countering the financing of terrorism

Customer due diligence, transaction monitoring, and suspicious activity reports. Supervised by the State Financial Intelligence Service under the Ministry of Finance.

For us. Where AI is used in customer due diligence, monitoring, and list screening, the checks the law requires and the decision trail stay in place.

Ministry, acts, and programsapplies to us

Digital development

The country’s digitalization agenda and the rules for government information systems and electronic services; a draft regulation on AI ethics was put out for public consultation in September 2026.

For us. It sets the framework for AI adoption in the country and for working with the state, including on identification.

Kazakhstan

Law and national conceptreference point

Law “On Artificial Intelligence” (Закон «Об искусственном интеллекте»)

The first dedicated AI law in the region, with a classification of systems by risk.

For us. A neighboring country’s example, useful for checking our approach to risk tiers; it concerns partners operating in Kazakhstan.

Legal grounds for processing, consent, the rights of data subjects, cross-border transfer, and in-country storage of certain data.

For us. It concerns partners and suppliers that process the data of Kazakhstan residents.

Russian Federation

Federal lawconcerns partners

Federal Law No. 152-FZ “On Personal Data” (Федеральный закон «О персональных данных» (152-ФЗ))

Requires the personal data of Russian citizens to be stored in databases located in Russia.

For us. It matters when choosing suppliers and partners that work with the data of Russian citizens.

Goals to 2030, and regimes in which AI is tested under temporary exemptions from the rules; since 1 September 2026, the first federal AI law (No. 243-FZ) is in force.

For us. It affects the region and suppliers, and shows how to set up a regulated experiment.

EU regulationreference point

EU Artificial Intelligence Act (AI Act)

Obligations by level of risk: prohibited practices, high-risk uses, and transparency requirements.

For us. Suppliers build their products to meet it; its risk-based logic is the basis of our risk tiers.

The reference regime for personal data protection and the model for the laws of many countries, including those of the region.
