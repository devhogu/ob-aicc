Regulators and legal acts

Who regulates AI and data, and how: here, in the region, and worldwide. In short: what each document is and what it means for us. This is a starting point for discussion, not legal advice; for the exact text, go to the original source and to the lawyers.

Kyrgyz Republic

Central bank and banking supervisorapplies to us

National Bank of the Kyrgyz Republic

Acts on banking, information security, outsourcing, operational risk, and payment systems.

For us. Any use of AI that touches a banking process or the data of customers or third parties falls under its requirements.

Codeapplies to us

Digital Code of the Kyrgyz Republic (Цифровой кодекс Кыргызской Республики)

In force since February 2026, it brings together the rules on digital services, electronic signatures, biometric identification, personal data protection, and AI systems. It replaced the 2008 Law “On Personal Information.”

For us. It applies to every AI solution that works with customer or employee data: which data, for what purpose, with what consent, and where to process it. It applies to AI systems themselves through the requirements for assessing their hazard.

Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 770 of 2 December 2025applies to us

Requirements for the hazard assessment of AI systems and for high-hazard AI systems (Требования к оценке опасности систем AI и к системам высокой степени опасности)

Adopted to implement the Digital Code: a method for assessing the hazard of AI systems, and risk management for high-hazard systems, with their transparency, understandability, controllability, accuracy, reliability, data quality, and technical documentation.

For us. It applies to owners of AI systems whatever their form of ownership. Our risk tiers and checks need to be reconciled with this method.

Law and financial intelligence unitapplies to us

Anti-money laundering and countering the financing of terrorism

Customer due diligence, transaction monitoring, and suspicious activity reports.

For us. Where AI is used in customer due diligence, monitoring, and list screening, the checks the law requires and the decision trail stay in place.

Ministry, acts and programsapplies to us

Digital development

The direction of the country’s digitalization and the rules for government information systems and electronic services; a draft regulation on AI ethics was put out for public consultation in September 2026.

For us. It sets the framework for adopting AI in the country and the way to work with the state, including in identification.

Kazakhstan

Law and national conceptreference point

Law “On Artificial Intelligence” (Закон «Об искусственном интеллекте»)

The first dedicated AI law in the region, with a classification of systems by risk.

For us. An example from a neighboring country that is worth comparing our approach to risk tiers against; it concerns partners operating in Kazakhstan.

Lawconcerns partners

Law “On Personal Data and Their Protection” (Закон «О персональных данных и их защите»)

Legal grounds for processing, consent, the rights of data subjects, cross-border transfer, and in-country storage of certain data.

For us. It concerns partners and suppliers that process the data of Kazakhstan residents.

Central bank and financial supervisorreference point

National Bank of Kazakhstan and the Agency for Regulation and Development of the Financial Market (Национальный Банк и Агентство по регулированию финансового рынка)

Regulation of banks and financial organizations, including their technology, data, and suppliers.

For us. Supervisory practice in the neighboring market with the most developed fintech in the region.

Financial center with its own regulationreference point

Astana International Financial Centre (AIFC)

Its own rules for financial services, data, and technology, and a regulatory sandbox.

For us. A reference point for experiments in a regulated environment and a possible venue for partners.

Russian Federation

Federal lawconcerns partners

Federal Law “On Personal Data” (No. 152-FZ) (Федеральный закон «О персональных данных» (152-ФЗ))

Requires the personal data of Russian citizens to be stored in databases located in Russia.

For us. It matters when choosing suppliers and partners that work with the data of Russian citizens.

Presidential decree and federal lawsreference point

National AI development strategy and experimental legal regimes

Goals up to 2030, and regimes in which AI is tested with temporary exemptions from the rules.

For us. It affects the region and suppliers; an example of how to set up a regulated experiment.

Regulator’s reports and recommendationsreference point

Bank of Russia on AI in the financial market

The central bank’s position on the benefits, risks, and regulation of AI in finance.

For us. Practice in the largest neighboring market, which is likely to influence the region’s regulators.

Voluntary industry codereference point

AI Code of Ethics (Кодекс этики в сфере AI)

Ethical commitments of AI developers and users.

For us. A reference point for ethical practice, in the language of the region’s industry.

European Union

EU regulationreference point

EU Artificial Intelligence Act (AI Act)

Obligations by level of risk: prohibited practices, high-risk uses, and transparency requirements.

For us. Suppliers build their products to meet it; its risk-based logic is the basis of our risk tiers.

EU regulationreference point

General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)

The reference regime for personal data protection and the model for the laws of many countries, including those of the region.

For us. The region’s laws rest on the same concepts, and suppliers rely on complying with it.

EU regulation and EBA guidelinesreference point

Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA)

ICT risk, incident reporting, resilience testing, and third-party risk.

For us. The clearest example of what supervisors expect on incidents, resilience, and suppliers.

United States

Supervisory guidancereference point

Model risk and AI in lending

The reference practice of model risk management; a credit decision is explained in specific terms, whatever the model.

For us. Independent model review and explainable credit decisions are requirements you meet under any supervisor.

Executive orders, procurement rules, state lawsreference point

US AI policy

Federal policy changes with each administration; states pass AI laws to protect consumers.

For us. The direction of the largest market for the models and services we use.

International principles

Intergovernmental principlesreference point

OECD AI Principles

The first intergovernmental standard for trustworthy AI.

For us. The shortest statement of what regulators and investors will expect from the use of AI.

International organization documentreference point

UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence

AI ethics from the standpoint of human rights, dignity, diversity, and the environment.

For us. The Kyrgyz Republic has joined it; a government body is likely to refer to it.

International treatyreference point

Council of Europe Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence

The first treaty on AI: human rights, democracy, and the rule of law across the whole AI lifecycle.

For us. It sets the expectation that people affected by AI have rights and remedies.

Voluntary commitments of developersreference point

G7 Hiroshima AI Process

Principles and a code of conduct for the developers of the most powerful models.

For us. It defines what safety and transparency information to expect from model providers.

Reports of standard-setting bodiesreference point

FSB, Basel Committee and BIS on AI

The position of the bodies whose standards national regulators adopt: the benefits and risks of AI for institutions and for the system.

For us. The region’s regulators will develop their requirements along these topics; preparing early costs less.

Standards and frameworks

Voluntary frameworkreference point

NIST AI Risk Management Framework

The characteristics of trustworthy AI and step-by-step risk management, with a separate profile for generative AI.

For us. The most practical framework; we rely on it when we explain risk tiers and checkpoints.

International standardsreference point

ISO/IEC standards on AI (including ISO/IEC 42001)

An AI management system: policy, roles, risk assessment, control, and improvement.

For us. A partner or regulator will most likely use them to judge how completely AI governance is set up.

List of security risksreference point

OWASP Top 10 for Large Language Model Applications

The ten main risks of applications built on large language models: prompt injection, data leakage, excessive permissions, and others.

For us. The list of attacks you need to be able to test for in generative AI solutions.
