ISO/IEC standards on AI (including ISO/IEC 42001)

← All regulators and actsStandards and frameworksInternational standardsreference point

Briefly

Standards on AI are published jointly by ISO (the International Organization for Standardization) and IEC (the International Electrotechnical Commission). The main one is ISO/IEC 42001, the world's first AI management system standard, published in December 2023. The standards are voluntary: an organization decides for itself whether to apply them and whether to get certified. The texts are sold, so here we summarize them from ISO's public descriptions.

What it means in practice

ISO/IEC 42001 is not about a particular model but about how an organization as a whole manages AI: whether there is a policy, who is responsible for what, how risks are assessed, and how the work is checked and mistakes fixed. A partner or regulator will most likely judge how mature our AI governance is by exactly these questions.

What certification means. An organization builds an AI management system that meets the standard's requirements, and then an independent certification body audits it. If the requirements are met, the organization receives a certificate, which stays valid through regular follow-up audits. A certificate says that the processes for managing AI are in place and working; it does not guarantee that every AI system is free of errors. The requirements for the certification bodies themselves are set by a separate standard, ISO/IEC 42006.

For suppliers. When an AI service supplier says it is certified to ISO/IEC 42001, that is a useful sign, but it doesn't replace our own review: what matters is what the certification covers and how the service will handle our data.

For employees. The standard doesn't set rules for each employee directly, but its logic is the same as in our everyday work: use only approved tools, don't give AI more data than needed, check the result, and report errors.

Key ideas

  • AI management system. The policy, objectives, roles, and processes needed to develop, provide, or use AI responsibly. It suits organizations of any size and sector.
  • The plan–do–check–act cycle. As in other management system standards (for example, on information security), the work goes in a cycle: plan, do, check, and fix.
  • AI risk management. ISO/IEC 23894 explains how to build AI risks into ordinary risk management: assess a risk, decide what to do about it, monitor, and review.
  • A common language. ISO/IEC 22989 sets out AI concepts and terms so that developers, lawyers, managers, and regulators understand each other the same way.
  • Impact assessment. ISO/IEC 42005 helps carry out an impact assessment: understand how an AI system may affect people, groups, and society, and write it down.

Good to know

  • The main standards: ISO/IEC 22989 (concepts and terminology, 2022), ISO/IEC 23894 (AI risk management, 2023), ISO/IEC 42001 (AI management system, December 2023), ISO/IEC 42005 (impact assessment, 2025), and ISO/IEC 42006 (requirements for audit and certification bodies, 2025).
  • Using them is voluntary. Organizations are certified to ISO/IEC 42001; the other standards listed are guidance and supporting documents for it.
  • The texts are sold in the ISO store on iso.org and through national standards bodies. Each standard's page on iso.org has a free short description, and the opening sections of many standards can be previewed on the ISO Online Browsing Platform.
  • The AI standards are developed by the joint ISO/IEC committee JTC 1/SC 42.
