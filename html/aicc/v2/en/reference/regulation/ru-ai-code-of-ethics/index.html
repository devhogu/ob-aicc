AI Code of Ethics

← All regulators and actsRussian FederationVoluntary industry codereference point

Briefly

The AI Code of Ethics (Кодекс этики в сфере искусственного интеллекта) was signed on 26 October 2021 in Moscow at the forum “AI Ethics: The Beginning of Trust.” It was developed on the platform of the AI Alliance, an association of major technology companies, together with academics and with the support of the Presidential Administration, the Analytical Center for the Government, and the Russian Ministry of Economic Development. The code is advisory, joining it is voluntary, and it covers only civilian, not military, uses of AI.

What it means in practice

For us this is a reference point, not binding rules. But it is useful: the code is a shared ethical language for the industry in the region. Its participants include Russia’s largest technology companies, so many potential suppliers and partners have already committed to following it. In 2025, organizations from 22 countries joined the code, including from Kyrgyzstan.

When choosing an AI supplier, a simple and useful question is whether it has joined the code and who is responsible for AI ethics there. The answer says something about how the supplier treats risks, data, and transparency.

The code’s ideas match our responsible AI rules. For an employee, the main one is this: a person is always responsible for the result of AI’s work. Check what AI produces before using it, and do not give in to over-reliance. If a customer is dealing with AI, they should know it.

Key ideas

  • People come first. AI should serve people, respect their freedom of choice, and not discriminate against people by sex, nationality, age, beliefs, or other characteristics. To that end, algorithms and data are checked for bias.
  • Attention matched to risk. The higher the risk an AI use poses to people, the more attention it gets — this is a risk-based approach. For critical uses, the code advises bringing in a neutral third party to assess the risks.
  • Be honest that it is AI. Users are told that they are dealing with AI when this concerns their rights or important areas of their lives, and they are given the chance to end the interaction.
  • A person bears responsibility. Responsibility for decisions cannot be shifted onto AI. Human oversight is needed, along with the ability to cancel an important AI decision — this is close to the human on the loop approach.
  • Data and security. Laws on personal data and protected secrets are followed, data is protected from unauthorized access, and data sets are of good quality and lawfully obtained.
  • An ethics officer. An organization that joins the code appoints an AI ethics officer, preferably within two months, or sets up an ethics commission.

Good to know

  • Signed on 26 October 2021; the text of the code and the register of participants are published on the website of the commission that implements the code.
  • It is a voluntary document: it is not a law, and there are no penalties for breaching it. By joining, an organization agrees to follow its recommendations.
  • The code is supported by the commission that implements it; its secretariat is run by the AI Alliance. The register of participants is public.
  • More than 900 organizations have joined the code, more than 80 of them from abroad.
  • Sector codes have grown out of it: for medicine, education, and the financial market (the last one prepared by the Bank of Russia).
