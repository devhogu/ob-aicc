Bank of Russia on AI in the financial market

← All regulators and actsRussian FederationRegulator’s reports and recommendationsreference point

Briefly

The Bank of Russia is not writing a separate AI law for the financial market. It has chosen soft regulation: it publishes consultation papers for discussion with the market (in November 2023 and November 2025), the Code of Ethics for AI in Finance (July 2025), and methodological recommendations on information security when using AI (June 2026). The regulator’s approach is risk-based and technology-neutral. These documents are advisory and addressed to Russian financial institutions.

What it means in practice

For us this is a reference point, but one very close to our work: it is the practice of the banking regulator of the largest neighboring market, set out in Russian. The region’s regulators are likely to look to it, and Russian partners already work to these recommendations.

For the organization, the code is a ready list of what is worth doing when introducing AI: tell customers when AI is serving them, let them reach an employee and have an AI decision reviewed, keep a single register of models with their risk level, and record cases where something went wrong. The code also advises making sure that contractors who develop or supply AI follow the same rules.

For an employee, the main thing is confidentiality. The code speaks directly about protecting data when employees and customers use generative models. So the rule is the same: work only in tools approved by the organization, and do not paste customer data into AI outside an approved service. For more, see the guide What you can and cannot share with AI.

Key ideas

  • Human-centricity. A customer may decline to deal with AI and speak to an employee, and may ask for a decision made with the help of AI to be reviewed by a person.
  • Fairness. Decisions made with AI do not take into account nationality, language, race, party membership, or religion, and training data is checked for completeness and reliability — this reduces bias.
  • Transparency. Customers are told when a service is provided to them with the help of AI, if this is not obvious, and texts, images, and videos created by generative models are labeled.
  • Reliability. The quality of data and models is checked before deployment and regularly afterward. If AI is temporarily unavailable, employees must be able to carry out important functions.
  • Risk management. AI risks are part of the overall risk management system: a single register of models with an assigned risk level, employee oversight of decisions made by high-risk AI, and a database of risk events. This is close to model risk management and to our risk tiers.
  • Security. The 2026 recommendations advise that critical operations involving AI, such as payments, be confirmed by an employee, that is, working with a human in the loop. Institutions develop their own threat models for AI and check the security of suppliers’ AI services.

Good to know

  • 3 November 2023 — the first consultation paper, “Artificial intelligence in the financial market”; the results of its discussion were published on 22 May 2024.
  • 9 July 2025 — the Code of Ethics (an annex to Bank of Russia Information Letter No. IN-016-13/91). Five principles: human-centricity, fairness, transparency, security and reliability, and responsible risk management.
  • 20 November 2025 — a new consultation paper: one in five financial market organizations already uses AI, and another third plan to introduce it within three years.
  • 28 May 2026 — the results of the discussion: soft regulation and trusted platforms for sharing data; in 2026 the regulator and the market are preparing a collection of best practices under the code.
  • 16 June 2026 — methodological recommendations on information security when using AI.
  • All of these documents are recommendations, not binding requirements. They apply only in Russia; for us they are a reference point.
