Requirements for the hazard assessment of AI systems and for AI systems of increased hazard

← All regulators and actsKyrgyz RepublicCabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 770 of 2 December 2025applies to us

Briefly

This is Resolution No. 770 of the Cabinet of Ministers of the Kyrgyz Republic of 2 December 2025. It was adopted under the Digital Code and spells out the Code's general rules on AI: how to assess the hazard of any AI system, and which requirements apply to increased-hazard systems (the term the resolution and the Code use). The resolution is binding on all owners of AI systems used in the country, whatever their form of ownership. It took effect 15 days after the Digital Code came into force.

What it means in practice

For the organization. Every AI system we use has to be assessed for hazard, using our own method built to these requirements. The assessment is done at design, before launch, and at any significant change to the system or the conditions it works in. The method and the results are published on the owner's website in plain language. If a system turns out to be hazardous, it needs risk management for its whole life, data requirements, technical documentation, and operating logs, and a declaration of conformity before launch. Our risk tiers and checks need to be compared with this method.

Owner or user. The Code distinguishes the owner of an AI system from its user. If we take a ready-made service and use it as intended, we are its user: we work by the instructions, look after the quality of the data, provide human control, and keep logs. But if we release a system under our own name, change its purpose, or substantially rework it, the owner's duties pass to us.

For employees. Don't use an AI tool for a new task, especially for decisions about customers or employees, until that use has been approved and assessed. If a tool behaves oddly or could cause harm, stop and report it: for users of hazardous systems this is an explicit duty.

Key ideas

  • Protected interests. The assessment asks whether the system could harm people's life and health, human rights and freedoms, the environment, national defense, national security, or public order.
  • Hazard assessment. Its goal is to identify risks of harm, estimate their likelihood and size, and decide whether the system is an increased-hazard one. Among other things, it looks at the system's purpose and possible misuse, whether it makes significant decisions about people without a human, whether it processes personal data or secrets protected by law, whom it affects, and whether there is a risk of discrimination. The owner makes the decision.
  • Increased-hazard system. A system that, compared with other ways of doing the task, raises the risk of harm so much that the risk has to be managed. This is close to the idea of high-risk AI. If AI only assists a person and does not raise the risk, it is not classed as increased-hazard.
  • Six characteristics. A hazardous system must be open, explainable, under human control, accurate, reliable, and resilient to failures and attacks.
  • Data quality. The requirements cover the data used to train, test, and run the system: it must be lawfully obtained, reliable, representative, and protected against leaks.
  • Building on international practice. When developing its method, the owner takes into account the standards and recommendations of the OECD, ISO, NIST, the European standardization organizations, and the Council of Europe.

Good to know

  • Resolution No. 770 was adopted on 2 December 2025 and published in the Erkin-Too newspaper on 5 December 2025. It took effect 15 days after the Digital Code came into force (5 February 2026).
  • It has five annexes: requirements for the hazard assessment method, for risk management, for the six characteristics of hazardous systems, for data quality, and for technical documentation.
  • The Cabinet of Ministers set the content of the declaration of conformity separately, in Order No. 1181-t of 31 December 2025. The declaration is signed with a qualified digital signature and posted on the owner's website.
  • Oversight of how the resolution is carried out is assigned to the Presidential Administration.
  • The resolution has no list of kinds of AI that are “hazardous by default”: the owner draws the conclusion from the assessment.
