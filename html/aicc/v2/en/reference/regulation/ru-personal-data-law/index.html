Federal Law No. 152-FZ “On Personal Data”

← All regulators and actsRussian FederationFederal lawconcerns partners

Briefly

Federal Law No. 152-FZ “On Personal Data” (Федеральный закон «О персональных данных») was adopted on 27 July 2006 and has been amended many times since, most recently in 2025 and 2026. It sets out on what grounds information about people may be collected and processed, what rights people have, and what whoever processes the data must do. The law is binding on everyone who processes personal data in Russia; Roskomnadzor, the federal communications and data regulator, oversees compliance.

What it means in practice

In Kyrgyzstan we have our own personal data rules, above all the Digital Code. The Russian law becomes important when the work involves partners or suppliers that process the data of Russian citizens: for example, a Russian contractor, a cloud platform or AI service from Russia, or a shared service whose customers include Russian citizens.

In that case, it is worth finding out three things from the partner in advance. Where the data is stored: the law requires the data of Russian citizens to be collected and stored in databases located in Russia. Whether the partner may transfer the data to us or to another country: before transferring data abroad it must notify Roskomnadzor, which may prohibit or restrict the transfer. Whether its AI makes decisions about people without a human involved: for decisions that affect a person’s rights, the law requires written consent and the right to object.

An employee only needs to remember a simple rule: customers’ personal data, including that of Russian citizens, must not be pasted into an AI tool unless it is an approved work service for such data. Otherwise it is data leakage. For more, see the guide What you can and cannot share with AI.

Key ideas

  • Operator. The organization or person that decides why and how personal data is processed. The operator is the one responsible for complying with the law.
  • A basis for processing. Data is processed with the person’s consent or on another basis set out in the law, for example to perform a contract with them. In some cases written consent is required.
  • Storage in Russia. When the data of Russian citizens is collected, it is recorded, stored, and updated in databases located in Russia. This rule has applied since 2015, and since 1 July 2025 using databases abroad for this has been expressly prohibited.
  • Transfer abroad. Before starting to transfer data to another country, the operator notifies Roskomnadzor. Roskomnadzor keeps a list of countries with adequate data protection and may prohibit or restrict a transfer within ten business days.
  • Decisions without a human. A decision with legal consequences for a person may not be made by automated means alone without the person’s written consent or a direct provision of law. The person is told how the decision was made and given the chance to object; an objection must be answered within 30 days.
  • Fines for data breaches. Since 30 May 2025 fines have risen considerably, and for repeated breaches they are calculated from the company’s revenue.

Good to know

  • Adopted on 27 July 2006 (No. 152-FZ); for the current version, see the Garant or ConsultantPlus legal databases.
  • Roskomnadzor oversees compliance with the law.
  • New rules on transferring data abroad have applied since 1 March 2023; since 26 July 2026 the way the list of countries with adequate protection is drawn up has changed.
  • The ban on using databases abroad when collecting the data of Russian citizens has applied since 1 July 2025.
  • Since 30 May 2025, repeated data breaches can bring a fine of 1% to 3% of the company’s revenue.
  • The law is amended regularly. Before a project involving the data of Russian citizens, check the current version together with the legal team.
