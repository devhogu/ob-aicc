Digital Code of the Kyrgyz Republic

← All regulators and actsKyrgyz RepublicCodeapplies to us

Briefly

The Digital Code of the Kyrgyz Republic (Цифровой кодекс Кыргызской Республики) is a single law on Kyrgyzstan's digital environment: digital services and signatures, identification, data processing, including personal data, and artificial intelligence systems. Code No. 178 of 31 July 2025 was adopted by the Jogorku Kenesh and has been in force since 5 February 2026. It is binding on everyone who works with data in the country, banks included. It replaced the 2008 Law “On Personal Information.”

What it means in practice

For the organization, the Code answers four questions about any AI solution that touches customer or employee data: which data we take, for what purpose, on what basis (a contract, the law, consent, and so on), and where it is processed. On top of that, every AI system has to be assessed for hazard, and hazardous systems must meet separate requirements.

If you paste customer data into an AI tool, that is already processing of personal data. It needs a purpose and a lawful basis, and no more data than the task requires. The company whose service processes the data is a processor under the Code: we need a contract with it, and we answer for how it handles the data. If the server is abroad, that is a cross-border transfer, which has its own rules. So use only approved tools and don't give AI more than it needs (see What you can and cannot share with AI).

If an AI system talks to customers, the customer must know they are not talking to a person, unless that is already obvious. Images or videos created or altered by AI so that they could pass for real must be labeled as artificial.

If AI helps make decisions about people (for example, it assesses an application), an impact assessment is needed before launch, and the system goes through a hazard assessment. The details are on the page about Resolution No. 770.

Key ideas

  • Principles of data processing. Lawfully and openly, only for a purpose set in advance, no more data than needed, accurately, no longer than needed, and securely. The organization must be able to show that it follows these principles.
  • Bases for processing. A contract with the person, a duty under the law, protecting someone's life, a task in the public interest, or the organization's legitimate interest. If none of these fits, consent is needed: voluntary, specific, and informed, and it can be withdrawn.
  • Hazard assessment of an AI system. The owner of every AI system used in the country assesses whether it could harm life and health, people's rights, or other protected interests: at design, before launch, and at any significant change.
  • Increased-hazard systems. This is the Code's name for high-risk AI. A system that only assists a person and does not raise the risk of harm is not one of them. If a system is, its owner runs risk management, keeps technical documentation and operating logs, and confirms compliance with a declaration before launch.
  • Principles for AI. Risk reduction, openness, explainability, human control, accuracy, reliability, and protection against outside interference.

Good to know

  • Code No. 178 of 31 July 2025 was brought into force by a separate Law No. 179, six months after that law was published; it has been in force since 5 February 2026.
  • The State Agency for Personal Data Protection under the Cabinet of Ministers oversees the personal data rules. It also publishes procedures and guidance on the Code.
  • Since 23 November 2025, the Code on Offenses has set fines for breaking the personal data rules.
  • An organization with more than ten employees appoints a person responsible for personal data processing and trains its staff in the rules.
  • The Cabinet of Ministers set the detailed requirements for increased-hazard AI systems in Resolution No. 770 of 2 December 2025.
  • We did not find an official English translation of the Code.
