United States federal and state AI policy

The policy of the largest market for AI models and services, set at the federal level by executive orders and procurement rules that have changed with the administration, and at the state level by laws on high-risk AI and on consumer privacy.

1. Identity

Item Entry
Kind Executive policy and state legislation
Issued or overseen by The federal executive and the Office of Management and Budget; state legislatures
Jurisdiction United States
Status Federal executive orders and memoranda on AI, changed in 2025; state laws on AI and on privacy in force or in preparation in several states

2. What it sets

¶2.1. At the federal level, the policy on the development and the federal use of AI, which shapes the providers through procurement and the standards institute; at the state level, duties for developers and deployers of high-risk AI, among them impact assessments, notices to consumers, and protections against algorithmic discrimination, and consumer privacy laws with rights over automated profiling.

3. Whom it reaches

¶3.1. Federal agencies and their suppliers; the organizations that operate in the states concerned. It binds no bank in the Kyrgyz Republic.

4. Relevance to the Bank

¶4.1. The direction of the market the Bank buys from. The state laws show where regulation of consumer-facing AI tends: impact assessment, notice, and protection against discrimination, which match the Bank's own principles.

5. How the charter relates to it

¶5.1. The Principles of Application of the Statement of Intent, fairness, transparency, and contestability, and the testing for bias of the higher Risk Tiers are consistent with this direction.

    NIST AI Risk Management Framework

    This United States reference framework for managing AI risks is voluntary, non-sector-specific, and used far beyond the United States. It names the characteristics of trustworthy AI and organizes AI risk management into four functions. Its generative profile names the risks that generative AI adds or amplifies.

    United States supervisory guidance on model risk and on AI in credit

    The reference practice for the management of model risk in banks, and the position of the consumer regulator that a lender must explain a credit decision to the consumer in specific terms even when the decision was made with complex AI.
