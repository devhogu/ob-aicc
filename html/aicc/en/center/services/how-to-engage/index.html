How to engage

How to engageEngagement workflowGuide: Engagement

A function does not need a form, funding of its own, or a plan to start with the Competence Center. It brings a need. From there the Engagement follows six steps, each with a record and a decision that the function can see.

1. The six steps

Step What happens Who Record
1. Contact A head of function raises a need, in a conversation, a message, or a ticket in Service Management The function; the Competence Center Lead takes it in An entry in the Portfolio Backlog
2. Study The need is scoped and the business case is written; the Control Function Contacts clear it when Risk Tier 2 or 3 is expected Competence Center Lead with the Domain Owner Initiative Brief; Service Agreement issued
3. Service Agreement Competence Center states what it commits to, the support level, and the working agreement; amended when the business case is approved Competence Center Lead; the function is notified Service Agreement
4. Delivery The Solution is probed as an MVP, then built, verified, and deployed to its first users after the decision to continue Solution Engineer with the Domain Expert; the Competence Center Lead gives the final acceptance of the Team Solution Definition; Control Sign-Off; release block
5. Outcome Report The outcome is reported with its evidence, and the Domain Owner accepts it as the requester Competence Center Lead issues; the Domain Owner accepts Outcome Report
6. Support The Solution is supported at the agreed level; a check-in at each Iteration decides follow-on or end Solution Engineer; the Competence Center Lead with the Domain Owner Service Management records

2. The phases of a service, and the three types of Solution

¶2.1. Whatever the category, an Engagement runs through the same phases: a study, which is the exploration, the scoping, and the business case; a proof, which is the trial of a Solution as an MVP or an Experiment, with its Outcome Report and Proposal; delivery, which is the build and the release; and support after delivery. Run-rate work does not run through these phases: it is a Feature under the Standing Initiative of its service area, done within one Iteration, with no Initiative Brief, Service Agreement, or Outcome Report of its own (Business Model 4.8).

¶2.2. What an Engagement delivers is a Solution of one type: an Experiment, a time-boxed proof that ends in a Proposal; a Product, a version built for one consumer that the Competence Center supports as agreed; or a Service, which the Competence Center runs for its whole life with a run cost and a sunset rule. The Services in operation are listed in the catalog in the Portfolio, whose form the page Service catalog states.

3. The commitment

¶3.1. The Competence Center commits in a Service Agreement. It is a working agreement and not a legal document, and it needs no signature chain. The Competence Center works toward the outcome on a best-effort basis, within the capability that it has available. The function commits to nothing; what the Competence Center relies on from the function is written as an Assumption.

¶3.2. The scope is an intent and a backlog ordered by value within the Limits on Work in Progress. Either side may end or redirect the Engagement at the end of an Iteration.

¶3.3. The Competence Center does not charge the functions. The Domain pays from its Investment Envelope for the run, the licenses, and the provider costs of a Solution.

4. Support levels

Support level What the Competence Center does Solution type
None Hands the Solution over, with the Outcome Report Experiment, or a Product handed over
On demand Answers requests and issues new versions when asked Product
At agreed response targets Supports to the targets that the Service Agreement states Service
Run by the Competence Center Runs the Solution for its whole life, with its run cost and its sunset rule Service

5. What the Competence Center does not do

¶5.1. The Competence Center does not own or operate the AI Platform, own the business results of a Domain, set the rules of a Control Function, validate its own work, or decide a matter that the regulation of the Bank reserves to the Board, to the management, or to a Control Function.

¶5.2. The Competence Center is not a platform team of the Bank and does not operate a Solution at the scale of the Bank (AI Competence Center Charter 3.2). It proves and builds: a Solution that the Bank adopts at scale is handed to its Receiver, and a Service that the Competence Center runs is run until its Handover to an IT function of the Bank or its retirement (Business Model 2.5).

6. Rule source

Business Model 2.5 and 3 to 7; AI Competence Center Charter 3.2 and 4; Solution Lifecycle Model 7.3 and 8; the Engagement workflow and guide.

  • Service category · 4 parts

    Strategy and governance

    The Competence Center builds for any unit of the Bank what it built for itself: a strategy, a charter and mandate, an operating and governance model with its roles, decisions, control loops, and controls, and the portal and repository that publish them. The Competence Center develops these with AI in weeks and maintains them with it afterward. The Competence Center drafts them for the function, and the function owns their substance; The Competence Center decides none of it (Business Model 4.10).

  • Service category · 5 parts

    Knowledge services

    The documents of a function, and the state and regulator documents it works with, turned into a governed knowledge base that it can ask. Legal, compliance, accounting, operations, and any function with a corpus get an assistant that answers from approved, current, cited sources, for its own people and, where the function decides, for its clients.

  • Service category · 2 parts

    Training and knowledge sharing

    AI reaches the Bank through its people. The Competence Center trains employees before they use AI, coaches them in the workplace, builds prompt and skill libraries for each line of work, runs communities of practice, and publishes what it learns so the next function starts further ahead.

  • Service category · 4 parts

    Policies, controls, criteria

    The use of AI in a function answers to the AI Policy of the Bank, to the controls of the Competence Center, and to criteria that say when a Solution is good enough and safe enough to use. The Competence Center helps a function state its own rules of use within the policy, map the controls that apply to its Solutions, and define the acceptance and evaluation criteria before any build, so that compliance is designed in and not inspected in.

  • Page · 2 parts

    Service catalog

    The catalog of the Competence Center lists what the Competence Center runs, has delivered, is proving, and can re-deploy. This page states the form of the catalog: the two kinds of entry, the fields of each, and the state or the status of each. The live catalog is an instance of this form, kept in the Portfolio of the Competence Center (Business Model 4.4); this site states the form only (Document Catalog 5.4). One entry of each kind is shown as an illustration.
