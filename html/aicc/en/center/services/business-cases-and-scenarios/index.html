Business cases and scenarios

Strategy and governanceNormatives and processesResearch and explorationBusiness cases and scenarios

Before the Bank invests in a use of AI, it needs to know what the use is, what it would take, what it would return, and what it would risk. The Competence Center does this work with the function: it finds and shapes the needs, writes each as a scenario with its problem, its solution, and its measures, assesses the readiness of the function and its sources, and writes the business case that the Portfolio decides on.

1. What it is

¶1.1. The category covers the discovery of needs with a function; the shaping of each need as a scenario, with the problem to solve, the proposed Solution, the people who review its output, the leading indicators, and the size; the readiness and source audit of the function, what documents, systems, and owners it has, and what that allows; the business case of an Initiative, with its run cost, its leading indicators, and its expected Risk Tier; and the screening and ranking of a backlog of scenarios by lens, size, and value.

¶1.2. A scenario is the unit of the backlog of scenarios of the Bank. It is written before any build, it is the hypothesis of the MVP, and it is kept with its results so that the Bank learns from what did and did not work.

2. Examples of what a function asks for

The following are examples that illustrate the category, not a list of commitments. What the Competence Center commits to is stated in the Service Agreement of each Engagement.

  • A discovery of needs with a function, ending in a ranked backlog of scenarios
  • A scenario written for one need: problem, Solution, people who review the output, leading indicators, size
  • A readiness and source audit of a function
  • An Initiative Brief: the business case with its run cost, its leading indicators, and its expected Risk Tier, cleared by the Control Function Contacts where Risk Tier 2 or 3 is expected
  • A roadmap for a Domain: its scenarios ranked by value and effort and mapped to the Strategic Priorities and the Maturity Levels, as a Proposal

3. What the function receives

¶3.1. A scoped need with its intended outcome and its success Measures; the scenarios of the function in one form; an Initiative Brief ready for the approval of the Domain Owner, or of the Executive Sponsor above a guardrail, across Domains, or for enabling work; and a recommendation: continue to a proof, adopt what exists, or stop.

4. How it runs

¶4.1. A head of function brings the need; the Competence Center Lead takes it in. For an Engagement, the Competence Center Lead issues a Service Agreement that covers the study. The study runs in one Iteration for a single scenario and in two to three for a Domain, with the Domain Owner and a Domain Expert. The business case goes to the approver; an approved Initiative goes on to its MVP.

5. What it leads to

¶5.1. Decisions taken on evidence, a backlog of scenarios by Domain that feeds the funnel of the Portfolio, and a Bank that invests in what works and learns from what does not.

6. The Package

¶6.1. The scenario form, the readiness audit, the Initiative Brief template, and the screening lenses of the Portfolio.

7. Run-rate work or an Initiative

¶7.1. Run-rate work for a single scenario or an audit; an Initiative for a Domain roadmap. The study itself is the first phase of every Initiative.

8. Who decides

¶8.1. The Competence Center Lead takes the need in. The Domain Owner approves the business case; the Executive Sponsor approves it above a guardrail, across Domains, or for enabling work. The Control Function Contacts clear it when Risk Tier 2 or 3 is expected.

9. Rule source

Business Model 3 and 4.1; Portfolio Management Model 2, 5, and 6; AI Policy 3; the Engagement workflow and guide.

  • Service category · 5 parts

    Knowledge services

    The documents of a function, and the state and regulator documents it works with, turned into a governed knowledge base that it can ask. Legal, compliance, accounting, operations, and any function with a corpus get an assistant that answers from approved, current, cited sources, for its own people and, where the function decides, for its clients.

  • Service category · 2 parts

    Training and knowledge sharing

    AI reaches the Bank through its people. The Competence Center trains employees before they use AI, coaches them in the workplace, builds prompt and skill libraries for each line of work, runs communities of practice, and publishes what it learns so the next function starts further ahead.

  • Service category · 4 parts

    Policies, controls, criteria

    The use of AI in a function answers to the AI Policy of the Bank, to the controls of the Competence Center, and to criteria that say when a Solution is good enough and safe enough to use. The Competence Center helps a function state its own rules of use within the policy, map the controls that apply to its Solutions, and define the acceptance and evaluation criteria before any build, so that compliance is designed in and not inspected in.

  • Page · 3 parts

    How to engage

    A function does not need a form, funding of its own, or a plan to start with the Competence Center. It brings a need. From there the Engagement follows six steps, each with a record and a decision that the function can see.
