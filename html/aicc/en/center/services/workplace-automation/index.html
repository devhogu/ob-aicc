Workplace automation

Knowledge servicesWorkplace automationAnalytics and decision supportContent managementPlatforms

The routine work of a function done with AI and reviewed by a person: routing requests, reading and filling forms, processing reports, consolidating inputs, and producing documents from templates. The person reviews and decides; the assistant prepares.

1. What it is

¶1.1. The category covers automations in the tools the function already uses: the triage and routing of requests and messages, the extraction and the filling of forms, the processing and the summary of reports, the consolidation of inputs from several sources, the drafting of standard documents from templates with the information collected, and case assistance under limited authority.

¶1.2. Every automation has a person who reviews its output before it takes effect, in proportion to the Risk Tier. An automation that would act on systems or funds without review is not in this category.

2. Examples of what a function asks for

The following are examples that illustrate the category, not a list of commitments. What the Competence Center commits to is stated in the Service Agreement of each Engagement.

  • Triage and routing of requests, complaints, and messages
  • Extraction from forms and documents, and the filling of forms
  • Processing and summary of recurring reports; reconciliation break triage
  • Consolidation of inputs from several sources into one working file
  • Documents from templates with the information collected, for a person to review
  • Case assistance under limited authority: related contacts detected, the permitted next step proposed, a grounded explanation drafted

3. What the function receives

¶3.1. Working automations, each with its Solution Definition, its Risk Tier, its review step, and its owner in the function; a short guide for the people who use it; and an entry in the catalog of automations so that another function can take it.

4. How it runs

¶4.1. Run-rate work: a need comes through the front door, is scoped in days, built in one Iteration with the Domain Expert, checked, and handed over. A set of automations for a whole process is an Initiative with a study first.

5. What it leads to

¶5.1. Time released for higher-value work, more consistent output, and a growing catalog of automations that functions re-use.

6. The Package

¶6.1. The catalog of automations: each a Package with its connectors, its prompts and rules, its review step, and its deployment notes.

7. Run-rate work or an Initiative

¶7.1. Run-rate work for single automations; an Initiative for a process.

8. Who decides

¶8.1. The Domain Owner approves the use for its data class; the owner in the function reviews the output; the Competence Center Lead assigns the Risk Tier.

9. Rule source

Statement of Intent 9.4; AI Policy 2 and 3; Solution Lifecycle Model 7 and 8.

  • Page · 5 parts

    Advise and formulate

    The strategy, governance, and process work that the Competence Center drafts with AI for a function, which owns the substance: the direction and the mandate of a unit and its normative documents and processes; with them the watch on the field, and the business cases and scenarios that the Portfolio decides on.

  • Service category · 4 parts

    Strategy and governance

    The Competence Center builds for any unit of the Bank what it built for itself: a strategy, a charter and mandate, an operating and governance model with its roles, decisions, control loops, and controls, and the portal and repository that publish them. The Competence Center develops these with AI in weeks and maintains them with it afterward. The Competence Center drafts them for the function, and the function owns their substance; The Competence Center decides none of it (Business Model 4.10).

  • Service category · 2 parts

    Training and knowledge sharing

    AI reaches the Bank through its people. The Competence Center trains employees before they use AI, coaches them in the workplace, builds prompt and skill libraries for each line of work, runs communities of practice, and publishes what it learns so the next function starts further ahead.

  • Service category · 4 parts

    Policies, controls, criteria

    The use of AI in a function answers to the AI Policy of the Bank, to the controls of the Competence Center, and to criteria that say when a Solution is good enough and safe enough to use. The Competence Center helps a function state its own rules of use within the policy, map the controls that apply to its Solutions, and define the acceptance and evaluation criteria before any build, so that compliance is designed in and not inspected in.

  • Page · 3 parts

    How to engage

    A function does not need a form, funding of its own, or a plan to start with the Competence Center. It brings a need. From there the Engagement follows six steps, each with a record and a decision that the function can see.

  • Page · 2 parts

    Service catalog

    The catalog of the Competence Center lists what the Competence Center runs, has delivered, is proving, and can re-deploy. This page states the form of the catalog: the two kinds of entry, the fields of each, and the state or the status of each. The live catalog is an instance of this form, kept in the Portfolio of the Competence Center (Business Model 4.4); this site states the form only (Document Catalog 5.4). One entry of each kind is shown as an illustration.
