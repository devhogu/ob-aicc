What we do

The Competence Center is the Bank's internal consulting and innovation lab for AI. Its work spans advice and formulation, building and running, enablement, and assurance across strategy, Initiatives, Solutions, and ways of working. The Competence Center is a small unit that proves and builds. It does not operate a Solution at the scale of the Bank: a Solution that the Bank adopts at scale is handed to its Receiver (Business Model 2.5).

1. The four areas and the fifteen categories

Advise and formulate

Category Coverage
Strategy and governance Strategy, charter, operating and governance model, and the portal and repository for a function or an Initiative, drafted with AI as the Competence Center did for itself; the function owns the substance
Normatives and processes Policies, procedures, regulations, runbooks, and process descriptions drafted, aligned, and maintained with AI for a function
Research and exploration Regulatory and technology watch with digests, trials in the Lab, and partnerships with organizations and providers
Business cases and scenarios Needs discovery, scenarios that identify their problem, Solution, and leading indicators, readiness and source audits, and the business case on which the Portfolio decides

Build and run

Category Coverage
Knowledge services A function's corpus and the state and regulator documents it works with, organized as a governed knowledge base it can query
Workplace automation Routing, forms, reports, consolidation, documents from templates, and case assistance, done with AI and reviewed by a person
Analytics and decision support Pipelines, dashboards, analyses, and research tools that prepare the factual base for decisions, with lineage to governed sources
Content management Public, investor, and management material generated from governed data and templates, and the templates, editions, versions, and languages behind it
Platforms The shared engines and environments that the Competence Center builds, the Competence Center's requirements for the AI Platform, and the Handover of an engine to an IT function of the Bank

Enablement

Category Coverage
Training and knowledge sharing Training by role, workplace coaching, skill libraries, communities of practice, playbooks, and publications
Adoption and lifecycle management Domain Experts and adoption plans for each Domain; the Handover, support, revision, and retirement of Solutions; the lifecycle and operation of a Service

Assurance

Category Coverage
Policies, controls, criteria Rules of use within the AI Policy, control maps, acceptance and evaluation criteria, and guardrails, stated before the build
Assessments and evaluations Evaluations of Solutions and providers against the function's cases before use, and assessments of the function's readiness; provider checks remain with the Control Function Contacts (AI Policy 4.1)
Risk tiering The Risk Tier assigned, recorded in the AI Registry, explained, and reassessed when the use changes
Oversight Tracking Adopted Solutions, reviews of Solutions in use, AI Incidents, and changes through the Registry, with reporting in the Quarterly Report

The categories describe the services the Competence Center provides, with examples on each page. The Service Agreement for each Engagement states the Competence Center's commitments. Each Engagement leaves its lessons and, when it produces one, a Package described in a Package Definition. The catalog of Solutions and Packages lets a small unit serve the whole Bank (Business Model 4.4). The Services section describes the categories, the service model, how a function engages the Competence Center, and the form of the catalog.

2. What the Competence Center is not

¶2.1. The Competence Center is not a platform team of the Bank, not a Control Function, and not the owner of the business results of a Domain. It does not own or operate the AI Platform, set the rules of a Control Function, validate its own work, or operate a Solution at the scale of the Bank. The AI Competence Center Charter 3.2 states these limits.

    How we work

    The Competence Center works as a consulting unit works. A function brings a need; The Competence Center takes it in, studies it, commits to it, proves it, builds it, and supports it, and each step leaves a record and a decision that the function can see. The Portfolio decides what the Competence Center works on. Delivery builds it in small steps on a common cadence, with quality and control inside the flow rather than after it.
