Events and rituals

OverviewFlow of valueBacklogs and boardsCadenceEventsLoopsQuality and releaseLife cycleMeasuresRoles and records

Each level of the cadence has its events, and each event has one purpose, named participants, an input, an output, and a record. Nothing else is held. This part states them one by one, from the day to the quarter, and then the two Steerings that take delivery's results into the governance of the unit.

1. The events, by level

Event Level and time Purpose Who Input Output and record
Daily Stand-up Day; each working day, short Share progress, surface blockers The Team The Iteration Backlog and the Team board Blockers entered in the Work Item; no other record
Weekly Planning Week; Monday Set the focus of the week; check the Dependencies The Team; the AICC Lead The Iteration Backlog, the Program Board The focus of the week; Dependencies confirmed or raised
Weekly Review Week; Friday Control the flow: read the Program Kanban, the limits, the Dependencies; reorder the Program Backlog; care for the funnel The AICC Lead; the Team The boards, the Dashboard The Dashboard current; the Program Board current; items at a gate raised to the monthly Steering

2. The two Steerings as interfaces

¶2.1. Delivery does not report to a committee of its own; it reports through the Steerings that govern the unit and the Portfolio. The monthly Steering, in the review week, takes the results of the Iteration Review and Demo: the acceptances, the live reviews, the gate decisions due, a sample of at least three Decisions of the AICC Lead chosen by the Executive Sponsor, and the deficiencies. The quarterly Steering, in the IP week after PI Planning, takes the results of the PI Review and Demo: the value scored, the risk check, the Maturity Level, the decision on each Active Initiative, and the confirmation of the PI Objectives and the Roadmap. The Quarterly Report that the AICC Lead prepares goes to the quarterly Steering, and the Executive Sponsor approves it and may bring it to the Board Committee or the Board (AICC Charter 7.2).

3. The habits behind the events

¶3.1. Four habits carry the events: the demonstration is of working software against written criteria; the plan of a quarter is objectives with a business value, scored afterwards; the retrospective and Inspect and Adapt end with an improvement that enters the backlog as work; and the events are held on their day or the working day before. The Cadence guide states the IP week in order and the treatment of a day that is not available.

4. Rule source

Solution Lifecycle Model 6.2 to 6.7 and 7.3; Operating Model 6; the Cadence workflow and guide.

    The Experiment workflow: the Lab

    Before the Bank invests, AICC proves. An Experiment is a time-boxed trial of a Solution, a platform, or a concept, run on governed data in a bounded environment, measured against leading indicators, and sent to review at the close of its time-box: it is accepted with its lessons and closed, a Proposal is made, or it is rejected. This page states the workflow of an Experiment and the rules of the environment in which it runs, the Lab of AICC (Solution Lifecycle Model 8.13). The six steps below detail the Stage Trial of an Experiment as explanation; the Stages of an Experiment are Trial, Proposal, and Handover (Solution Lifecycle Model 8.1).

  • Page

    The life of a Service

    A Service that AICC runs lives after its release. The Solution Lifecycle Model gives it three Stages, Operate, Evolve, and Retire, and refines them into seven service steps, each with the question that is asked at it, the signals that answer it, the action that follows, and the gate to the next step (Solution Lifecycle Model 8.8). The service steps are not states: the Service stays Active until it is Closed, and the Solution Engineer enters each step and the date on which it was reached in the Solution Definition and the AI Registry.
