9. Strategic Priorities

¶9.1. The Bank has seven Strategic Priorities, stated in order. Each rests on the governed foundation set out in section 10.

9.2. Customer intelligence

  • Objective. To develop an end-to-end understanding of each customer across every service and product of the Bank.
  • Scope. Know-your-customer procedures establish the identity and risk profile of the customer and keep them current throughout the customer lifecycle. The understanding extends to customer behavior, changes in customer needs, and difficulties encountered in the use of services. A view that draws on data from outside the Bank is established only under a Data Sharing Arrangement.
  • Intended outcome. Risk-aware service, earlier detection of problems in customer journeys, and proactive support to customers.

9.3. Business intelligence

  • Objective. To provide AI-supported analysis to those who direct the business.
  • Scope. Monthly and quarterly financial reporting and analysis; forecasting and scenario analysis; benchmarking against peers and competitors from public sources; operational analysis; and information for investors, lenders, and the Board, provided on request. Each figure is traceable to a governed source, and publication requires human approval.
  • Intended outcome. Better-founded and faster decisions, and consistent communication with investors.

9.4. Adoption within Domains

  • Objective. To enable each Domain to use AI in support of its own processes.
  • Scope. Drafting, summarization, search, analysis, translation, and the automation of routine operations, in accordance with the priorities of each Domain.
  • Intended outcome. Time released for higher-value work and more consistent output.

9.5. Expertise at the point of work

  • Objective. To make the Bank's current and compliant expertise available to each employee and, where appropriate, to each customer, at the point where it is needed.
  • Scope. Policies, procedures, product rules, and regulations are maintained as governed knowledge with named owners, review cycles, access control, and source citation.
  • Intended outcome. Consistent and compliant service and decisions, faster proficiency of new employees, retention of institutional knowledge, and a reliable basis for later AI agents.

9.6. AI in banking operations and systems

  • Objective. To embed AI in the flow of banking operations and in the banking systems that carry them, with decisions remaining with qualified persons.
  • Scope. AI assists with the assembly of credit files, the drafting of case narratives, the proposal of payment repairs, and the detection of anomalies. Qualified persons take the decision, and the system records both the AI contribution and the decision. The Risk Tier determines whether any action may be taken without human intervention.
  • Intended outcome. Shorter cycle times and fewer errors, with accountability retained.

9.7. Information technology operations and service lifecycle

  • Objective. To apply AI to the daily operation of technology, separately from the development of software.
  • Scope. Incident detection and triage, root cause analysis, change and release risk, service desk, resource planning and resilience, and security operations. Operational actions are taken under approval.
  • Intended outcome. Greater stability, faster recovery, and reduced manual effort in the operation of systems.

9.8. Software engineering

  • Objective. To apply AI throughout the software engineering lifecycle.
  • Scope. Coding assistance, code review, testing, and the analysis and documentation of legacy code. Coding agents are introduced later under review controls.
  • Intended outcome. Faster and safer delivery of the Bank's own Solutions.
