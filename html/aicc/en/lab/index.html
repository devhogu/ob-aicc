Concept

AI Lab

Experiments with AI scenarios

The AI Lab runs continuously on the Bank's own infrastructure, isolated from its production systems. A scenario can enter at any time as an Experiment; data arrives as read-only extracts from the Bank's systems and never leaves the Bank; the AI-agent workflow is built, measured, and then accepted, turned into a Proposal for delivery, or rejected. The Lab is where the Bank tries generative AI before it commits to it.

Guardrails

The Lab runs under seven guardrails. The Competence Center Lead keeps them with their evidence in the Standards record, and the quarterly Steering reviews them.

  1. LAB-001

    The Lab is isolated from the systems of the Bank, and nothing is written back to them

    Evidence: The environment record of each Experiment

  2. LAB-002

    Access to the Lab is by role, and every action is logged

    Evidence: The access list and the log

  3. LAB-003

    Data enters as read-only extracts under the classification of the Bank, each with a named owner

    Evidence: The data extract record of each Experiment

  4. LAB-004

    Personal data is minimized and assessed with the Control Function Contact of data protection before it enters

    Evidence: The Control Sign-Off

  5. LAB-005

    A cloud or external environment used by the Lab is checked as a provider (AI Policy 4)

    Evidence: The provider check

  6. LAB-006

    Each Experiment has an evaluation set and checks for grounding, drift, and bias before its result is read

    Evidence: The build notes in the Solution Definition

  7. LAB-007

    An Experiment ends at the close of its time-box, with its Outcome Report

    Evidence: The Outcome Report

How people and AI agents work together

People steer: they set the intent and carry accountability. AI agents gather signals, analyze, reason, execute, and learn. The two work in parallel, joined by one intent loop and a trust perimeter that is not negotiable.

◆ System A

People

Authority · Judgment · Intent

  1. A.1

    Strategic direction

    Sets the intent, the risk appetite, and the boundary of acceptable action.

  2. A.2

    Ethics & regulation

    Owns the relationship with regulators, customers, and the public.

  3. A.3

    Final decision rights

    Keeps authority over high-stakes, irreversible, and reputational decisions.

  4. A.4

    Accountability

    Answers to the Board, the regulator, the shareholder, the customer.

  5. A.5

    Review & recalibration

    Checks the Bank against its purpose; resets the intent when the context changes.

● System B

AI agents

Monitoring · Analysis · Execution · Learning

  1. B.1

    Continuous monitoring

    Watches signals on customers, markets, risk, operations, and staff in real time.

  2. B.2

    Multi-angle analysis

    Weighs decisions across competing factors — risk, return, customer, conduct.

  3. B.3

    Options for decision

    Presents worked-out options with confidence, trade-offs, and sources.

  4. B.4

    Execution & automation

    Carries out authorized actions and routine work, and keeps the audit trail.

  5. B.5

    Learning from results

    Feeds results back into models; flags drift; proposes adjustments to people.

↔The intent loop

What this means

Decision rights stay with people. AI agents make decisions faster, wider in scope, and better evidenced. The Bank does not hand over judgment; it equips it.

The intent loop

How people and AI agents work together: intent → insight → action → audit, continuously

1

Intent

People set the outcome, the constraints, the risk appetite, and the ethical limits. Direction, not instructions.

2

Insight

AI agents analyze the data from several angles and present options for decision, with confidence and trade-offs.

3

Action

People authorize. AI agents execute or hand over. Every step has an owner and limits, and can be reversed where possible.

4

Audit

Results are logged, evaluated, challenged if needed, and used to sharpen the next intent. Each turn makes the loop more precise.

Lab practice maturity

How far good practice is applied in each control area of the Lab, measured against what the Bank expects of a production system. The Lab has not yet been assessed: no Experiment has run, and its guardrails are not yet due. Each area shows the practice the Lab intends; point at its status to see what it must show to reach each level.

0–25% · Not in place
Done case by case or not at all; no defined practice.
26–50% · Basic
A minimal practice exists, but it is informal, manual, or not applied to every Experiment.
51–75% · Established
Defined and applied to every Experiment, with evidence kept; some gaps against what production requires.
76–100% · Strong
Applied consistently, evidenced, and checked; close to what the Bank requires of production systems.
