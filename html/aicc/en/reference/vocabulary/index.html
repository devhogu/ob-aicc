Vocabulary and Style

Defined meanings, interpretation and document style

1. Purpose and scope

¶1.1. This document establishes the meanings and rules of interpretation of defined AICC concepts and the style of the charter corpus. The definitions identify the scope, responsibilities, decision rights and distinctions needed to apply the governing documents consistently.

2. Precedence

¶2.1. Where two documents differ, the higher prevails: the Statement of Intent on the Adoption of Artificial Intelligence, the AICC Charter, the Business Model, the Operating Model, the Portfolio Management Model, the Solution Lifecycle Model, the AI Policy, the Document Catalog. This Vocabulary prevails on terms and style. Templates and Records yield to the documents. The workflows and the guides explain the documents and state no rule of their own.

¶2.2. Definitions shall be applied together with the requirements and decision rights of the governing documents. An interpretive clarification or an entry in the shared terminology reference does not amend those requirements.

¶2.3. Corresponding language editions shall preserve the same scope, responsibilities, conditions and exceptions. Language alone does not establish precedence. A discrepancy shall be reconciled against the governing clauses under the Document Catalog.

3. Style

¶3.1. Documents shall use short numbered clauses, with one requirement in each. A duty shall be expressed by "shall"; a fact or authority shall be stated in the present tense.

¶3.2. English-language text shall use American spelling and plain, precise wording.

¶3.3. A defined term shall be capitalized when it identifies an AICC concept and shall be interpreted within its defined scope. The Interpretation and distinctions column explains relationships, limits and differences between concepts. A related expression is equivalent only to the extent expressly stated; a Role, Record or state retains its defined identity.

¶3.4. A defining document states what is and what is to be done. It holds no provenance, no open question, and no reference to a file.

¶3.5. A document refers to another by its title, not by a file path, and does not restate it. The Statement of Intent repeats a few definitions because it is read alone.

¶3.6. A Role is named for its responsibility, not for a person. The Appointments Record names persons.

¶3.7. Headings use sentence case. Document titles use title case. Each table has an introducing clause, except a change log.

¶3.8. Established business, finance, delivery and technology terms shall be used where they accurately identify the concept. A local designation shall be used where the corpus defines a distinct responsibility, Record, state or other concept. Shared Business and Technology Terminology records international names, language-specific names and professional explanations; this Vocabulary governs the functional interpretation of defined AICC concepts. Each edition provides its own-language definitions with equivalent scope. The Standards Record maps external frameworks to the AICC model.

¶3.9. The pages that the AICC portal adds to explain the charter use the terms of this Vocabulary and state no rule of their own, and the documents prevail over them. The Document Catalog 5.4 states how they are kept.

4. Defined terms

¶4.1. The following table defines AICC concepts and their interpretation within the governing corpus. Responsibilities and decision rights shall be read together with the applicable governing clauses; a name or related expression alone does not constitute an appointment, approval or authorization.

Term Meaning Interpretation and distinctions
Bank O!Bank
Board The board of directors of the Bank
Board Committee The committee of the Board that oversees AI, as the Board names it
Data Sharing Arrangement An arrangement that permits the sharing of data or decisions outside the Bank, with another company Group membership alone does not authorize sharing; the arrangement must cover the data or decisions concerned.
AICC The AI Competence Center: the internal consulting and innovation lab of the Bank for the adoption of AI, a joint team led by the AICC Lead and formed of the people whom the functions assign to it
Role A set of responsibilities and authority, held by one or more persons A position or job title does not by itself establish the responsibilities or decision rights of an AICC Role.
Hat A temporary responsibility assigned within the AICC team, such as maintaining the Program Backlog. It is performed as part of the work and is not a separate Role.
Executive Sponsor The Role that holds the mandate and funding of AICC The term identifies the defined AICC Role, rather than any person providing general sponsorship or support.
Holder The person who holds a Role, named in the Appointments Record The Role defines responsibility and authority; the Holder is the person appointed to exercise them.
AICC Lead The Role of the lead engineer and architect of AICC, accountable for its way of working
Solution Engineer The Role of the person who owns a Solution end to end: designs it, decides its architecture, builds it, deploys it, and runs it with the Domain. The AICC Lead, or a person whom a function assigns to AICC, holds it AI engineer, developer and similar job titles describe professional work; appointment to this Role carries the defined end-to-end responsibility.
AI Steering Committee The heads of the business, technology, risk, and compliance functions, who advise the Executive Sponsor SteerCo may denote the committee in context. Its advice is distinct from the Executive Sponsor's decision and from the Steering meeting.
Steering The meeting chaired by the Executive Sponsor, prepared by the AICC Lead, attended by the Domain Owners concerned and the Control Function Contacts, and advised by the AI Steering Committee. The monthly Steering carries the control loop and the portfolio sync, the quarterly Steering carries the assurance loop and the portfolio review, and the yearly Steering carries in addition the direction loop and the strategic loop
Yearly Steering The monthly Steering of December, held in the first two weeks of December, for the next year. It is not an extra meeting: it carries the control loop and the portfolio sync of the month, and in addition the direction loop and the strategic loop. Its input is the Quarterly Report of PIQ3 and the findings of the year to that date
Domain A business function or a product line of the Bank A business unit or business line may be a Domain when it meets this definition; the label alone does not identify its Domain Owner.
Product owner The person who accepts each Feature and each Capability against its acceptance criteria during development, at the Iteration Review and Demo. A product manager is the same person under another name. While the Team has up to three people the AICC Lead is the product owner of the Team, and later the AICC Lead may name another person in the Appointments Record. The designation is not a Role
Domain Owner The Role that owns the results of AI adoption in a Domain. The Domain Owner is the requester who judges the work, and accepts the Solution once it works for its first users A business owner is the Domain Owner only when assigned the responsibilities of this Role. Development acceptance remains with the product owner.
Domain Expert The Role of a specialist of the Domain who acts as early adopter A champion or ambassador may support adoption; those descriptions do not by themselves constitute appointment to this Role.
Control Function The model risk, compliance, information security, data protection, or legal function, which sets the rules of its remit, clears, validates, decides Exceptions, and may stop. Internal audit is not a Control Function and gives independent assurance only The second line performs the control responsibilities stated here. Internal audit provides independent assurance and does not issue operational clearances.
Three lines The division of the duties of risk in the Bank: AICC and the Domains own the risks of their work; the Control Functions set the rules of their remit, clear, validate, decide Exceptions, and may stop; internal audit gives independent assurance The first line owns the risks of its work, the second line exercises its control remit, and the third line provides independent assurance. The distinction does not transfer their decision rights.
Control Function Contact The Role of the named member of a Control Function who advises, validates, and may stop A liaison or point of contact has this authority only when designated to act for the relevant Control Function.
Platform Owner The Role that provides and operates the AI Platform
AI Platform The shared platform that provides the model gateway, the knowledge layer, the tool gateway, guardrails, human oversight, and observability, and that feeds the AI Registry of AICC A technology stack is a technical composition. The AI Platform additionally has the shared functions and operating responsibilities established by the corpus.
Platform guardrails The guardrails of the AI Platform: the checks that filter the input and the output of a model and limit the actions of an AI agent. A Guardrail, alone, is an Investment Guardrail Platform guardrails are technical controls; Investment Guardrails limit investment commitments and decision authority.
Model An artificial intelligence model that produces output from input, such as a language model
AI agent An AI system that takes actions through tools, within the limits set for it
Assistant An AI system that answers or drafts for a person on request, and takes no action beyond its output
Provider A company outside the Bank that supplies a model, an AI service, or a Solution Vendor is a general commercial designation; the AI Policy determines the requirements applicable to a Provider of a model, AI service or Solution.
AI output Content that AI drafted or produced, as the AI Policy 2.4 states
Data class A class of data under the data classification rules of the Bank. A Solution is approved for each data class that it uses
Human oversight The control of a person over AI output or over the actions of an AI agent: the person approves each output or action before it takes effect, or monitors them and can stop them, as the Risk Tier requires
Evaluation set A set of cases with their expected results, agreed with the Domain, on which a Solution or a model is tested before use and after each significant change An assessment dataset becomes an Evaluation set under the corpus when agreed with the Domain and used at the required checks.
Alert level The value of a monitored measure of a Solution at which a review, an alert, or an incident is raised, stated in its Solution Definition An operational trigger for review or response, distinct from an Investment Guardrail or a WIP limit.
Lab The isolated and logged environment of AICC in which an Experiment runs on read-only extracts of data. It is an environment and not an authority
IT function The function of the Bank that operates technology
Platform team A team of an IT function of the Bank that runs a platform at the scale of the Bank. AICC is not one
Service Management The service management system of the Bank, in which requests and incidents are handled
Strategic Priority A strategic theme of the Statement of Intent, set with the Board, with an Investment Envelope
Strategic Pillar A pillar of the strategy of the Bank to which the adoption of AI contributes, as the Statement of Intent 3.2 lists them; distinct from a Strategic Priority
Investment Envelope The funding allocated for a year to a Strategic Priority A budget line may record the allocation; the Envelope defines funding for the Strategic Priority and is not an individual Initiative approval.
Investment Guardrails The limits that decide what may be committed without the Executive Sponsor These thresholds concern investment commitments. Technical platform controls and operational Alert levels have separate purposes.
Kind of work The purpose of an Initiative: business, enabling, or risk and compliance
Mix of Initiatives The share of the work of each Program Increment that the Executive Sponsor sets for each kind of Initiative, including enabling work, within the Investment Envelopes. The AICC Lead sets the limit on the Active Initiatives within it
Business Model The document that states what AICC is, whom it serves, what it offers in its service areas, how it takes in work in its two modes, how it commits, and how it tracks value and flow
Service area One of the four groupings of the services of AICC by its posture toward the work: Advise and formulate; Build and run; Enablement; Assurance
Service category One of the fifteen kinds of service that a function asks for, each in one service area, as the Business Model states them. A service category is a direction of service and not a commitment A service line or family is equivalent only where it refers to the defined category; a category does not itself create an Engagement commitment.
Mode The way in which work is taken in: run-rate work or an Initiative A Mode determines the intake path. A Lane represents a Class of service on the Program Kanban.
Run-rate work A small, repeatable request of a function, done within one Iteration as a Feature under the Standing Initiative of its service area, with no Initiative Brief and no Service Agreement of its own
Standing Initiative An Initiative of enabling work, one for each service area, with an Initiative Brief approved by the Executive Sponsor, which carries the run-rate work of the service area and does not count against the limit on the Active Initiatives
Enabling work Work of AICC that builds its own capability or serves the Bank as a whole and has no client function. The Executive Sponsor is its client
Engagement An Initiative that has a client function. One Service Agreement is issued for each client function A project label alone does not establish an Engagement; the Initiative must have a client function and the applicable Service Agreement.
Phase A part of an Engagement that its Service Agreement covers: a study, a proof, delivery, or support
Service Agreement The internal working agreement in which AICC records its commitment to an Engagement and the arrangements with the client function, on a best-effort basis within AICC's available capability. It is not a legal document, as Business Model 5.1 states. SOW describes work scope; neither SOW nor contract is automatically equivalent to this working agreement
Assumption A condition or input expected from the client function and recorded in the Service Agreement as a basis for planning the work. If it does not hold, the work is re-planned under that agreement. An Assumption is a planning basis, not a commitment by the client function under the Service Agreement.
Support level What AICC provides after delivery: none, on demand, at agreed response targets, or run by AICC SLA is an agreement on service levels, not a support category
SLA A service-level agreement between a provider and a consumer, including an internal consumer, stating the service levels, how they are measured, responsibilities, and the handling of missed targets. It may form part of a Service Agreement. Use the term when these matters are agreed; the name alone creates no additional guarantee or penalty Support level is a support category; a target alone is not the whole agreement
Outcome Report The report at the end of an Engagement: what was delivered, with the evidence referenced, and who accepted it A final report serves this purpose when it records delivery, evidence and acceptance as required for the Engagement.
Stakeholder For the purposes of a Service Agreement, a person outside AICC who receives notifications about the Engagement. That designation does not assign an AICC Role or create a commitment under the agreement (Business Model 3.1 and 5.4). Outside that Service Agreement context, stakeholder has its general meaning. AICC uses it for the AICC Lead in the Bank's incident process (Solution Lifecycle Model 8.5).
Package A method, kit, engine, catalog of automations, or template set that an Engagement leaves in the Portfolio, described in a Package Definition. Its status is planned, in preparation, available, or withdrawn, and is not a state of an item Reusable asset is broader. A Package has a Package Definition and its own availability status, distinct from a work item's state.
Package Definition The description of a Package in the Portfolio, in the form of the Template AICC-TPL-14: its kind, owner, status, what a function needs to re-deploy it, and the Risk Tier of its uses
Catalog The list in the Portfolio of the Solutions, each with its Solution Definition, and of the Packages, each with its Package Definition
Portfolio The Initiatives of AICC and the Solutions and Packages that they deliver, taken together
Initiative A business program: a long-term business service or product that delivers one or more Solutions, held in the Portfolio Backlog Project is a general work designation; Initiative identifies the portfolio level with the business case and decisions required by the Portfolio Management Model.
Initiative Brief The one-page Record of an Initiative's business case: its hypothesis, scope, benefit, cost and risk.
Business case The justification for an investment or change: the need, options, expected benefits, costs, risks, and basis for deciding whether to proceed. The Initiative Brief records the business case of an Initiative Initiative Brief is the record, not a competing name for the concept
Solution An AI solution or service that an Initiative delivers for a Domain, built or bought. It has an offering type, carries the Risk Tier, and has an entry in the AI Registry A tool, product or use case is a Solution under the corpus only when it meets this definition and the applicable governance requirements.
Solution Definition The description of a Solution in the Portfolio: type, receiver, scope, capabilities, architecture, data classes, and Risk Tier
Service An offering type: a Solution that AICC runs for its consumers until its retirement or its Handover to an IT function of the Bank, as a service to the Domains. A service of AICC, in lower case, is what AICC offers in a service area
Service step One of the steps of the life of a Service that refine its Stages Operate, Evolve, and Retire, as the Solution Lifecycle Model states them. A service step is not a state A step refines the work within a Stage; it does not replace the item's state or authorize an additional state transition.
Four signals The reading of a Service at each review: its service levels, incidents, use, and cost These four review dimensions inform assessment; they do not constitute a single service status or replace the underlying Measures.
Run cost The recurring cost of a Solution in use: its run, licenses, and provider costs, which the Domain pays from its Envelope
Sunset rule The condition, stated in the business case of a Service, on which the Service is retired.
Product An offering type: a Solution built for one consumer, delivered as a version, and supported on demand
Experiment An offering type: a time-boxed trial of a Solution without a consumer, which ends in a decision: accepted with its lessons and closed, a Proposal, or rejected The recorded outcome shall identify the applicable decision and resulting state. Informal descriptions such as promote or shelve do not define a separate outcome.
Receiver The owner who will run or adopt a Solution after its delivery, named in the Solution Definition
Handover The transfer of a Solution to its Receiver, or its delivery to a consumer; it is complete when the Receiver accepts it
Proposal A proposal to the Bank to adopt a Solution at scale, or a proposal of the AI adoption strategy, submitted for decision
Adopted Solution A Solution that others deliver, which AICC oversees and reports on
Capability A capability of a Solution, delivered by the Teams over one or more Program Increments, held in the Program Backlog, and grouped under its Initiative. It maps to an Epic in Jira Epic is the Jira representation of a Capability under the Portfolio Management Model. A differently defined Epic in another framework does not change the AICC work hierarchy.
Feature A deliverable of a Capability that closes within one Program Increment; run-rate work is a Feature directly under a Standing Initiative and is done within one Iteration A story may express a user need; it is a Feature when it meets the scope, timing and acceptance requirements of this level.
Definition of ready The conditions that a Feature meets before it is approved for work, as the Solution Lifecycle Model states them
Definition of done The conditions that a Feature meets before it enters the Done column, including acceptance by the product owner, as the Solution Lifecycle Model states them
Work Item A task of a Team within a Feature A ticket is a system record. It represents a Work Item when it records a Team task within a Feature.
Portfolio Backlog The ranked list of Initiatives
Program Backlog The ranked list of Capabilities and Features. Also called the PI Backlog PI Backlog is an alternative name for this Record. An unqualified backlog may refer to another level and requires context.
Iteration Backlog The Features that the Teams work on in one Iteration, tracked on the Program Kanban with priority lanes A sprint backlog belongs to a Scrum context. The Iteration Backlog follows AICC's calendar-month Iteration and its delivery rules.
Portfolio Kanban The board of the Initiatives by step: Funnel, Reviewing, Analyzing, Portfolio Backlog, MVP, Implementation, and Done, with Deferred, Rejected, and Pivoted off the flow
Portfolio management The management of the portfolio: the Initiatives are tied to the strategy, the priorities are funded by an envelope and guardrails and not Initiative by Initiative, and decisions are taken in small steps on evidence. Its model is the Portfolio Management Model
Funnel The Proposed state of an Initiative: an idea or a need not yet taken in
Minimum viable product (MVP) A probe: the smallest version of the first Solution that is tried, to see whether it works and satisfies the need, and that tests the hypothesis of an Initiative against its leading indicators
Leading indicator A measure that shows early whether the hypothesis of an Initiative holds An early test of the Initiative's hypothesis; it is not automatically an OKR key result or a commitment to a final benefit.
Acceptance criteria The observable conditions used to assess whether a delivered item meets its agreed requirements. The product owner applies them to a Feature or Capability; the AICC Lead applies them at the final acceptance of the Team; and the Business acceptor applies them to the Solution under Solution Lifecycle Model 7.3. They are written as Given a situation, when an action occurs, then an observable result follows.
Value hypothesis The benefit that an Initiative, a Capability, or a Feature is believed to bring, which the work tests
Value stream The end-to-end activities through which a need becomes an outcome of value for a consumer. AICC describes its value stream in the portfolio and service delivery workflow A workflow can describe part or all of a value stream
Program Kanban The board of the Capabilities and Features by state, with the classes of service as lanes and the Limits on Work in Progress
Class of service The urgency of a Feature or a request: Urgent, High priority, or Normal
Lane A row of the Program Kanban that carries one class of service A swimlane may display this Class of service. A Lane does not determine whether work is an Initiative or run-rate work.
Team The people who deliver together: a Solution Engineer with the Domain Expert, the Domain Owner, and the product owner Squad and pod may name a group in other models; those labels do not alter the composition or responsibilities of an AICC Team.
Teams Record The living Record of the Teams and their members
Program Increment One quarter of work, made of three Iterations; named PIQ1 to PIQ4 with the year. Short form: PI A time period for planning and review, distinct from a release train as an organizational grouping in another framework.
Iteration One calendar month of work of a Team, of four or five whole weeks, named I01 to I12, with a goal and an Iteration Backlog. The Calendar Record states the weeks of each Iteration AICC fixes its duration and calendar rules. Use of the term sprint does not change those rules or adopt Scrum by implication.
Innovation and Planning week The last week of the third Iteration of a Program Increment, or an earlier week that the Calendar Record places, for example to keep a year-end week free of events, for the PI Review and Demo, Inspect and Adapt, Innovation, PI Planning, and the quarterly Steering. Short form: IP week
PI Objective The intent and direction of the Teams for a Program Increment, with the business value scored by the Domain Owner. It is not a commitment of scope A general goal or an OKR has its own context. A PI Objective retains the stated business-value assessment and does not guarantee scope.
Dependency A need of one item from another item, Team, function, or person, by a stated Iteration
Calendar The Record of the Program Increments, the Iterations, the weeks, and the blocked and gray days
Cadence The workflow of the general flow of the events by week, without dates; the template of the dated calendar of events
Blocked day, gray day A blocked day is an official or expected non-working day. A gray day is a working day on which people are likely to be out. Events move to the working day before them
AICC portal The portal of the charter and of the pages that explain it (courses, learning paths, the knowledge base, the references, the service catalog), for AICC, the Executive Sponsor, employees, and internal audit, which links to the evidence records of the Registry The portal explains the charter and links to Records; a page does not establish a governing rule or replace an Evidence record.
Operating portal The portal for internal consumers of AICC containing non-sensitive information about the services portfolio and development work. It holds neither governance records nor evidence (Operating Model 7.6).
Corporate share The share of the Bank on which the Registry and the Portfolio are kept, and to which the portals link A corporate folder is part of this resource only where designated for that purpose; storage does not itself grant access.
Dashboard The Record of the state of the Program Increment, the flow, the Dependencies at risk, the risks, and the Measures
Stage A phase of the work inside the discovery or the active state of an item, specific to its level. A gate is the decision at the end of a step of the portfolio Kanban
Environment of use The environment in which a Feature runs for its users: an environment that is not production until the Solution is deployed to its first users, and production afterwards
Check The review of work by a person other than the one who built or wrote it Peer review can meet this definition when performed by someone other than the author. Validation is performed by the designated Control Function Contacts within their remit.
Checker The person who performs a Check; for a Risk Tier 1 Solution, named in the Appointments Record
Validation The review by the Control Function Contacts that a Solution meets the requirements of its Risk Tier
Suspension and stop A suspension pauses a Solution pending review. A stop ends a Solution, and is decided by a Control Function
Governed source A source with a named owner and a review date
Acceptance The decision that a delivered item meets its acceptance criteria, recorded with who and when. It is given at three levels. During development the product owner accepts each Feature and each Capability. Before first deployment to First users and before deployment of each significant change, the AICC Lead gives the final acceptance of the Team. The business acceptance is the judgment of the requester: the Domain Owner, or the Executive Sponsor for an item that spans Domains, is enabling work of AICC, is an Experiment that has no Domain, or has the AICC Lead as Domain Owner, judges the working Solution deployed to its first users, and accepts it, returns it, or rejects it. It is the acceptance of the Solution and, for an Engagement, of its Outcome Report. The release beyond the first users is a separate decision Acceptance assesses the delivered result. Control Sign-Off records a control decision, and Release authorizes use beyond First users; neither replaces the other decisions.
Release The decision, before a Solution is used beyond its first users, to make it available Initial deployment for First users precedes Release. An operational go-live does not by itself establish the release decision required by the corpus.
First users The users that are named in the Solution Definition, and not the whole function. They use the Solution after its first deployment and before its release
Release block The part of the Solution Definition that records the first users, the check or validation, the final acceptance of the Team, the first deployment, the business acceptance, the release decision, and the Acceptance Checklist
Emergency change A change to a Solution in production that cannot wait for the normal process of the change management of the Bank
Limit on Work in Progress The most items allowed in a state, a lane, or a Domain at one time WIP limit is the accepted short form. WIP is the actual amount; capacity concerns available capability or resources
WIP Work in progress: work that has entered a stated system or activity and has not left it, and the amount of such work at a stated time. Its measurement states the counting boundary; Solution Lifecycle Model 10.3 defines the operational measure of AICC WIP limit is the permitted maximum, not the current amount
Risk Tier The class of a Solution by risk: 1 Low, 2 Medium, or 3 High Classifies a Solution and determines applicable controls. Severity classifies an incident or recorded risk, issue or finding; Data class classifies information.
AI Registry The living Record of AICC, kept by the AICC Lead in the Registry, of each Solution, model, and AI agent, with owner, scope, data access, Risk Tier, and reassessment date. The AI Platform feeds it
AI Incident An event in which the use of AI causes or could cause harm, a breach of law or policy, or a loss of control
Severity The class of an AI Incident, as the incident management of the Bank sets it; or of a Risk, Issue, or Finding: Blocker, Major, or Minor An incident's severity follows the Bank's incident process. Blocker, Major and Minor apply to Risks, Issues and Findings; these are not the Solution's Risk Tier.
Exception A limited departure from a policy, decided by the Control Function concerned, or by the AICC Lead for a requirement set by AICC alone An Exception authorizes only the stated departure within the competent decision-maker's remit; it is not a general release from policy or another function's control.
Deficiency An uncorrected failure of a control to operate as required, recorded in the Risks and Issues Record with an owner and a due date until closure. Operating Model 8.5 defines the conditions for the corresponding control status.
Control status One of the five statuses of Operating Model 8.5: Operating (operated and evidenced as required), Open (due, with evidence missing or incomplete), Deficiency (did not operate, failed, or remained Open past its due date), No occurrence yet (its trigger has not happened), or Not yet due (its first date has not come)
Accepted limit A combination of Roles or a gap that the rules of separation forbid, accepted while AICC is small, entered in the Risks and Issues Record with its compensating control Acceptance of a limit does not waive the recorded compensating controls or confer the excluded decision rights.
Risks and Issues Record The living Record of the open risks, issues, Exceptions, deficiencies, and Findings, each with an owner and a due date
AI Risk Appetite Statement The statement in the AICC Charter of the appetite of the Bank for AI risk, decided by the Executive Sponsor
Maturity Level One of the five levels of the Maturity Roadmap of the Statement of Intent
Measure An indicator with a definition, a source, a forum where it is read, a target rule, a baseline, and an owner Metric is the general measurement concept; a KPI is a measure selected as key to an objective
Metric A defined measurement of an activity, process, or result. A metric used as an AICC Measure has the source, review forum, target rule, baseline, and owner required by that definition KPI is a metric selected as key to an objective
KPI A key performance indicator: a Measure selected as particularly important for assessing an objective or result. Its objective and selection are stated; a Measure is not automatically a KPI Metric and Measure are broader concepts
Governance measures The Measures by which AICC measures its own control, as the Operating Model states them
Milestone A dated event in the Roadmap that marks a deliverable or a Maturity Level
Decision A choice based on stated facts, made by the person authorized to decide the matter under the Operating Model. The substance, authority and recorded facts determine the Decision; the word resolution alone does not establish a separate decision right.
Escalation The passing of a Decision, or of an item that its owner cannot close, to a higher level, on the conditions of the Operating Model 5.2
Delegation The written assignment by the Executive Sponsor of decision-making authority to a delegate for a defined scope and period, entered in the Appointments Record. The exclusions in Operating Model 4.7 apply; a delegation longer than two weeks is also entered in the Decision Log.
Decision Log The Record of the Decisions, one line each
Decision Record The evidence record of one Decision that others will need to find later: the facts, the options, the decision, and where it is entered
Appointments Record The living Record in the Registry that maps the Roles to real people, with the log of appointments, the declarations, and the access An assignment map is part of this Record when it identifies the actual Holders; it does not replace the required appointment, declaration and access information.
Control Sign-Off The evidence record of a Control Function Contact's decision within that function's remit: Validation, clearance of a business case, a stop, an Exception or a Provider check.
Business acceptor The requester who gives the business acceptance of a Solution against its acceptance criteria: the Domain Owner, or the Executive Sponsor for an item that spans Domains, is enabling work, is an Experiment with no Domain, or has the AICC Lead as Domain Owner (Solution Lifecycle Model 7.3(c)–(d); Operating Model 4.4(d))
Acceptance Checklist The evidence record of the release of a Solution beyond its first users, listing for each party concerned the items that it confirms within its remit and signs
AI Incident Review The evidence record of the review of one AI Incident
Registry Snapshot The dated evidence record that preserves the Working state at the close of an Iteration or Program Increment, or at cutover. It also records the state, Risk Tier and release of Solution Definitions held in the Portfolio (Operating Model 7.3).
Program Board The board of the dependencies of a Program Increment: for each Capability and Feature, the Iteration in which it is planned and what it needs from other items, Teams, functions, and persons A dependency map serves this purpose when it identifies the relevant items, planned Iterations and required inputs. It is distinct from the Program Kanban's state view.
Roadmap The Record of three horizons: the current PI as intent and direction, the next as planned, the rest indicative, with its Milestones
Template The prescribed form for a Record where a form is required. The Template has its own status and revision; a Record created from it does not retain the Template's metadata block.
Priorities Record The living Record of the Strategic Priorities, with references to the Investment Envelopes, Guardrails, and Measures
Standards Record The living Record of the architecture standards and Platform requirements, and of the external instruments and frameworks, with the map of their terms to the terms of the documents
Control Matrix The living Record in the Registry that keeps the test and the status of each control of the Operating Model 8 by its reference
Registry The folder of the Records of AICC: the process records of the work, the decisions, the proposals, and the governance records
Workflow In the charter, an explanatory description of an AICC loop or flow, including its purpose, sequence and decision points. It states no rule of its own. The general technology term workflow can describe an executable process; the charter Workflow is the explanatory document defined here.
Record Documented information about AICC work, decisions or governance, held as Working state, a Living record or an Evidence record. Its location and retention follow Operating Model 7; it is distinct from a governing charter document. A register is a list or collection; a Record is the documented information itself. Evidence records remain in the Registry; living Solution Definitions are held in the Portfolio.
Living record A Record maintained to reflect current information, including Priorities, Standards, Risks and Issues, the AI Registry and Appointments in the Registry, and Solution Definitions in the Portfolio (Operating Model 7.3). Current information changes as work proceeds; an Evidence record preserves a dated event or state and follows its own correction rules.
Evidence record A closed and dated extract in the Registry of an event: a Decision, an approval, a sign-off, an acceptance, an incident, or an appointment
Working state The live state of the work: the backlogs, boards, Roadmap, Calendar, Program Board, Dashboard, Teams, and Program Increment folder. It moves to Jira and Confluence at the cutover
cutover The day on which the working state of the Program Backlog, the boards, and the Work Items moves from the Registry to Jira, set by the AICC Lead and entered in the Decision Log
Light mode The way AICC runs while the AICC Team has up to three people, with fewer events and a smaller set of states
Loop A cycle that starts with planning and ends with review: the day, the week, the Iteration, and the PI. The control loops of the Operating Model and the portfolio loops of the Portfolio Management Model are loops of Plan, Do, Check, and Act, which run on the events of these cycles
Review week The last week of an Iteration, except in the third Iteration of a Program Increment, where the IP week holds the review
Short forms PI is Program Increment, and IP is the Innovation and Planning week. PIQ1 to PIQ4 name the PIs of a year, I01 to I12 its Iterations, and W1 to W5 the weeks of an Iteration. Iteration is never shortened to IT, because IT means information technology
Event A meeting of a loop, with a stated intent: Daily Stand-up, Weekly Planning, Weekly Review, Backlog Refinement, Iteration Planning, Iteration Review and Demo, Iteration Retrospective, PI Review and Demo, Inspect and Adapt, PI Planning, Innovation, and Steering
Steering Summary The evidence record of one Steering: attendance, advice, Decisions, and actions Minutes serve as the Steering Summary when they record the required attendance, advice, Decisions and actions.
Quarterly Report The report of the AICC Lead on the Portfolio, benefits, risks, and Maturity Levels, which goes to the quarterly Steering and is drafted from the data of the PI Review and Demo
Report to the Board Committee The Quarterly Report as the Executive Sponsor approves and issues it to the Board Committee
quarterly risk check The check of the assurance loop at the quarterly Steering of the open Risks and Issues, the open Exceptions, the Risk Tier reassessments that are due, each Solution of Risk Tier 3, and the reliance on providers and on the Platform Owner
Finding A deviation found by a check of the documents, by an audit, or by a supervisor
Activation The decision that makes a document active Approval is not by itself Activation. The Document Catalog specifies the authorized act, status and recording that bring a document into effect.

¶4.2. The following table defines the states for the levels specified in Solution Lifecycle Model 5.1. That model governs permitted transitions and the light-mode arrangements. A state, a Stage and a control status describe different aspects of the work.

State Meaning Interpretation and distinctions
Proposed Incoming, in a backlog, and not yet looked at Receipt in a backlog does not authorize work.
Discovery Being clarified: researched, scoped, sized, defined, or designed. Work that precedes approval; it learns and does not try the Solution Clarification precedes approval and does not authorize use of unapproved data with AI.
Approved Cleared for work and queued to be pulled, with its Dependencies known Approval to work does not replace approval of AI use for the purpose and Data class or a Release decision.
Active Work in progress Work is underway; its Stage identifies the current activity.
Waiting Stopped on a Dependency outside AICC, which is named. It is a state, shown as a flag on the boards The external Dependency is identified; resumption follows the transition rules. A board flag is a display convention.
Deferred Put on hold for a stated reason and returned to its backlog The item remains available for reconsideration; it has not been rejected on its merits.
Completed The work is finished and not yet reviewed Execution is finished, but acceptance has not yet been given. This state is skipped in light mode.
Review Being assessed against the acceptance criteria. It may return to active The authorized acceptor assesses the criteria; this is distinct from a Control Function Validation.
Accepted The acceptance criteria have been met, with the decision-maker and date recorded. The product owner accepts Features and Capabilities. The Business acceptor accepts Solutions under Solution Lifecycle Model 7.3(c); the Domain Owner, or the Executive Sponsor for enabling work, accepts an Initiative outcome under 5.1. Acceptance of delivery, approval of a Solution Definition and Release are separate decisions.
Closed Complete and finished: accepted, or for a Solution retired, handed off, or ended Closure does not assert successful acceptance when the Solution was retired, handed over or ended.
Pivoted Decomposed or rerouted into a new item, to which it is linked The link to the resulting item preserves the relationship to the original work.
Rejected Decided against on the merits, because the value is not seen: before approval, after the MVP, or in review. It will not be done A decision against the value or outcome, distinct from withdrawal without a decision on the merits.
Cancelled Withdrawn without a decision on the merits, such as an error, a mistake, or a duplicate, in any state from proposed to review except completed, or a Solution that a Control Function stopped The stopped-Solution case follows the Control Function decision; the other cases are withdrawal without a merits decision.
