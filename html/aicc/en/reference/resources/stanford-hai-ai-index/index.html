Stanford Institute for Human-Centered AI: the AI Index

The yearly AI Index is the reference for the data of the field: how much research, which models, at what cost, adopted where, regulated how, and what the public thinks. It is neutral, sourced, and updated each year, and it is the number to cite when a paper of the Bank needs one.

1. Identity

Item Entry
Kind Academic institute; yearly data report
Who The Stanford Institute for Human-Centered AI, with an independent steering committee and contributors from academia and industry
Access Free; the full report, the data, and the charts on its site

2. What it publishes

¶2.1. Chapters on research and development, technical performance, responsible AI, the economy, science and medicine, education, policy and governance, and public opinion; the underlying data for each chart; and a policy summary. The Institute also publishes research and policy briefs on AI governance, foundation models, and transparency.

3. Why it matters to the Bank

¶3.1. A figure about AI in a Quarterly Report, a Proposal, or a strategy page should trace to a source; the Index is the one source that is neutral, current, and comprehensive. Its chapters on responsible AI and on policy track the regulation the Bank watches, and its economy chapter tracks the adoption figures the consulting surveys also report, with the method shown.

4. How to take it, and for what

¶4.1. For any number about the field, start here and cite the Index with its year. For the trend of models, cost, and capability, read the technical performance chapter. For the state of regulation, read the policy chapter beside the Regulators and acts pages. The AICC Lead updates the figures of the Responsible AI course from each new edition.

5. Cautions

¶5.1. The Index reports the field in aggregate, mostly from the United States and Europe; figures for the region are thin. A number is read with its definition, which the Index gives.

Read further

  • Reference

    Bank for International Settlements and its Innovation Hub

    The institution whose research central banks and supervisors read, and whose Innovation Hub builds working prototypes of supervisory and central-bank technology, including AI, with the central banks of its members. Together they are the most credible public source on what AI does in regulated finance and what supervisors make of it.

  • Reference

    International Monetary Fund and the World Bank on fintech and AI

    The institutions that advise the governments and the central banks of the region on digital finance, inclusion, and, increasingly, AI. Their fintech notes, working papers, and country work set the policy context in which the Bank operates, and their inclusion agenda is one the Bank shares.

  • Reference

    World Economic Forum on AI governance and financial services

    The forum where industry, regulators, and civil society state shared positions on AI and on the future of financial services, in reports written to be read by boards. Its AI Governance Alliance publishes frameworks on responsible generative AI; its financial services work publishes on the transformation of banking.

  • Reference

    McKinsey, the McKinsey Global Institute, and QuantumBlack

    The most-cited source on the adoption and the value of AI in business: a yearly state-of-AI survey, the Global Institute's estimates of the economic potential of generative AI by function and industry, and a steady stream of pieces on AI in banking. It sets much of the vocabulary executives use, and it is read as the view of a firm that sells what it describes.
