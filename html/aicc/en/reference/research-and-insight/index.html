Research and insight

OverviewStandards and frameworksRegulators and actsResearch and insightLearning and open resources

These sources shape how banks think about AI: international financial institutions and standard-setters, policy observatories, academic centers, consulting houses, and the fintech press. This page lists the ones worth a bank's attention, explains what each publishes and how to read it, and gives the most influential sources their own pages. This first edition is kept by the AICC Lead; access is free unless marked.

1. How to read this shelf

¶1.1. Four kinds of source sit here, with different ways to read them. The institutions (the BIS, the FSB, the IMF, the World Bank, and the OECD) publish measured, sourced work that supervisors read; their view becomes expectation. The academic observatories, above all Stanford's AI Index, publish data about the field; they are the reference for a figure. The consulting houses publish well-made surveys and frameworks that also sell their services. They are useful for the state of practice; read them as a view, not as evidence. The fintech press is current and shallow; it tells what is happening, not whether it is sound.

2. The institutions and standard-setters

Source Publishes Why it matters to the Bank How to take it Access
Bank for International Settlements, with its Innovation Hub Working papers and bulletins on AI, data, and digital finance; the Innovation Hub's project reports on applied central-bank and supervisory technology The source supervisors read; the Innovation Hub shows what regulated institutions can do with AI in practice For the supervisory view and worked examples of applied AI in finance Free
Financial Stability Board and the Basel Committee Reports on AI in finance, third-party and concentration risk, operational resilience What the Bank's supervisor will expect in time For the risk themes to prepare for Free
International Monetary Fund and the World Bank Fintech notes, working papers, and country work on digital finance, inclusion, and AI; the Bali Fintech Agenda The view of the institutions that advise the region's governments and central banks, with inclusion as a theme the Bank shares For the region's policy context and the inclusion agenda Free
OECD.AI Policy Observatory The live database of national AI policies, incidents, and metrics, and the OECD's AI work The reference map of what governments do, including the region's For the policy landscape and the definitions Free
World Economic Forum Reports and toolkits on AI governance and the future of financial services, from its AI Governance Alliance and its financial services platform Where industry, regulators, and civil society state shared positions; readable by a Board For Board-level framing and cross-industry positions Free

3. The academic and independent centers

Source Publishes Why it matters to the Bank How to take it Access
Stanford Institute for Human-Centered AI, the AI Index The yearly AI Index with data about the field: research, models, cost, adoption, policy, public opinion The reference for a figure about AI; neutral and sourced For any number cited in a Bank paper Free
Alan Turing Institute Research programs on finance and economics, AI ethics, and safe AI, with the UK public sector and regulators Applied, regulator-facing work on fairness, explainability, and AI in finance For methods on fairness and explainability Free
MIT Sloan Management Review and MIT Technology Review Articles on AI in management and the technology itself Well-edited, accessible, often early For orientation on a new topic; some articles require paid access Partly paid

4. The consulting houses

Source Publishes Why it matters to the Bank How to take it Access
McKinsey, the Global Institute, and QuantumBlack The yearly state-of-AI survey, the Global Institute's economic estimates, and banking and AI practice pieces The most-cited surveys of adoption and value; the framing executives use For the state of practice and the market's vocabulary; read as a view Free, registration for some
Boston Consulting Group, Bain, Deloitte, Accenture, PwC, EY, KPMG Surveys, banking outlooks, responsible AI frameworks, and financial services practice pieces The state of practice across banks and the frameworks vendors bring For comparison and the questions a peer bank is asking; each is a view Free, registration for some
Gartner, Forrester Market research, the hype cycle, vendor comparisons The structured market view many procurement teams use For a vendor landscape, when procured; listed for awareness Paid

5. The fintech and banking press

Source Publishes Why it matters to the Bank How to take it Access
Finextra Daily fintech and banking technology news, community articles What is happening in the market this week For awareness; verify before relying Free
The Financial Brand Banking marketing, digital banking, customer experience The customer-facing side of digital banking For ideas on channels and experience Free
FinTech Futures, Fintech Nexus News and analysis on fintech, payments, lending The fintech side of the market, including emerging markets For awareness Free
American Banker, Central Banking Deeper reporting on banks and central banks Serious reporting, often paywalled Listed for awareness Paid

6. For the region

¶6.1. The regulators and bodies of the Kyrgyz Republic, Kazakhstan, and the Russian Federation publish their own research and notices; they appear under Regulators and acts with their pages. The Astana International Financial Centre and the Eurasian Development Bank publish on fintech and digital finance in the region.
