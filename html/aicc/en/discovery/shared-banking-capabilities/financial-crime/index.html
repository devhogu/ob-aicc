Financial crime

Financial crime controls — AML/CFT monitoring, sanctions screening, fraud detection, and STR filing — are mandatory supervisory obligations under the regulator's AML/CFT rules and suspicious-transaction reporting requirements, in line with the FATF Recommendations. Transaction monitoring systems generate alert volumes where 90–98% are false positives, but each alert carries a mandatory review obligation. STR filing with the financial intelligence unit follows prescribed narrative formats with hard deadlines. Sanctions hit rates are low, but the consequence of a miss is severe. The opportunity for GenAI is to absorb the assembly and classification burden that sits before the analyst's judgment step — alert triage, investigation pack assembly, STR narrative drafting, and false-positive pattern analysis — so financial crime professionals spend their capacity on cases that genuinely require human reasoning.

Insights & analytics

Fraud typology distributions, sanctions false-positive rates by screening list and customer segment, and transaction monitoring rule performance are each tracked by separate specialist teams. The cross-typology view — how fraud patterns correlate with AML behavioral indicators, or which rule combinations generate the highest false-positive burden — requires manual synthesis that rarely happens between audit cycles.

Enablement

Sanctions hit review for potential true matches — assessing whether a customer name match against a UN or other applicable sanctions list entry is a genuine sanctions exposure — requires compliance officers to cross-reference all available identity fields, prior screening history, and the sanctions-list entry in detail. The judgment step is concentrated on a small compliance team who also carry STR review and AML governance responsibilities.

Automation

Sanctions hit triage — evaluating name-match alerts against the available identity evidence to produce a high-confidence false-positive or escalation recommendation — follows a structured comparison logic that applies consistently across the alert population. The triage step is the highest-volume, lowest-judgment segment of the sanctions workflow and the clearest automation candidate.

New business opportunities

Banks that systematically reduce false-positive alert rates — through rule tuning informed by pattern analysis rather than intuition — redirect analyst capacity from false-positive processing to genuine investigation. The compounding effect: lower false-positive burden enables the same analyst headcount to handle higher genuine-alert volume, creating financial crime coverage capacity as transaction volumes grow.

Transaction monitoring alert disposition feedback loop

Systematic analysis of alert disposition outcomes — tracking true-positive rate by rule, customer segment, and behavioral profile — to feed the disposition evidence back into the triage prioritization model and the false-positive rule calibration analysis on a monthly refresh cycle. The feedback loop ensures the triage scoring model incorporates the most recent disposition evidence rather than relying on the initial scoring parameters set at deployment.

S

Regulatory Horizon: AI governance

Problem to solve

Triage prioritization models set at deployment use historical base rates; as the customer population, transaction patterns, and monitoring rules evolve, the base rates that drove the initial prioritization logic drift from the current disposition outcomes. Without a systematic feedback loop from actual dispositions to the triage scoring parameters, the prioritization model's accuracy degrades over time, reducing the differential between the true-positive rate in the top-ranked alerts and the queue baseline.

Solution

The AI agent aggregates the monthly disposition outcomes — true-positive rate, STR conversion rate, and false-positive characteristics — by rule, customer segment, and behavioral profile, and produces a calibration report with the specific parameter updates required to maintain triage scoring accuracy. The AML model risk team uses the calibration report to update the triage scoring parameters on the monthly refresh cycle, with the disposition evidence available for the model governance record.

OKR

Triage prioritization model parameters are recalibrated monthly from actual alert disposition outcomes, maintaining scoring accuracy as the customer and transaction population evolves.

Adoption

Disposition feedback loop calibration used in ≥10 monthly triage model refreshes within the first 12 months of go-live.

Acceptance

Triage model accuracy — differential between top-quartile true-positive rate and queue baseline — maintained at ≥2.5x throughout the first 12 months; model risk committee confirms calibration documentation completeness on quarterly review.

Cycle

Calibration report produced within 3 business days of each month-end so parameter updates are deployed before the next month's alert volume accumulates.

Transaction monitoring alert pre-investigation pack

Pre-assembled investigation context for transaction monitoring alert review — aggregating per-customer KYC profile, recent transaction history, prior alert dispositions, behavioral baseline deviations, and cross-customer ring indicators into an investigator-ready summary before the analyst begins disposition analysis. Pre-assembly reduces alert investigation preparation time, enabling the analyst to begin substantive disposition analysis without manual data aggregation across source systems.

M
Problem to solve

Alert volumes at regulated institutions generate 50 to 2,000 cases per day; 90 to 98% are false positives under current monitoring rules, but each carries a mandatory review obligation under AML/CFT requirements, in line with FATF Recommendation 10. Manual context assembly — retrieving KYC, transaction history, prior alerts, and behavioral baseline — consumes 15 to 45 minutes per alert before substantive analysis begins, creating a backlog during high-volume periods and compressing the time available for genuine risk investigation.

Solution

The AI agent reads each new transaction monitoring alert and assembles the investigation context — KYC profile, 90-day transaction history, prior alert and disposition history, behavioral baseline comparison, and related account connections — into a structured investigator pack with source citations. The analyst receives the pre-assembled pack and begins disposition analysis without additional data retrieval, with the context organized to support the prescribed investigation checklist.

OKR

Transaction monitoring alert investigation packs are assembled from source systems before the analyst begins disposition review, reducing preparation time per alert.

Adoption

Pre-assembled packs covering ≥90% of daily alert volume within 9 months of go-live.

Acceptance

Mean alert preparation time reduced by ≥50% vs. pre-deployment manual aggregation baseline; analyst confirmation of pack completeness ≥90% on quality review.

Cycle

Investigation pack assembled within 30 minutes of alert generation so it is available when the analyst queue refreshes at the start of each shift.

Transaction monitoring alert priority triage scoring

Prioritization scoring for the daily transaction monitoring alert queue — ranking alerts by estimated true-positive probability based on the customer's risk classification, prior alert history, behavioral baseline deviation, and typology pattern match — so investigators can concentrate the most experienced analyst capacity on the highest-risk alerts. The prioritization reduces the probability that genuine suspicious activity is reached late in the queue when the filing deadline window is most compressed.

M
Problem to solve

The 90 to 98% false-positive rate in the standard monitoring queue means that genuine suspicious activity is distributed across a large volume of noise; without prioritization, investigators process alerts in queue order, applying the same attention to high-probability false positives as to the subset of alerts most likely to require STR filing. Genuine suspicious activity identified late in the investigation cycle — after the majority of the review window has been consumed by false-positive dispositions — compresses the STR drafting and filing time, increasing the deadline risk for the highest-priority cases.

Solution

The AI agent scores each alert in the daily queue against the customer's risk classification, prior alert history, behavioral baseline deviation magnitude, and typology pattern match, and reorders the investigation queue with the highest-priority alerts at the front. Investigators work the prioritized queue, with the triage rationale for each alert's ranking available in the pre-investigation pack, enabling senior analysts to be assigned to the top-ranked alerts.

OKR

Daily transaction monitoring alert queues are prioritized by estimated true-positive probability so investigators process highest-risk alerts first.

Adoption

Prioritized queue used by the AML investigation team for ≥90% of daily alert volume within 6 months of go-live.

Acceptance

True-positive alert identification rate in the top quartile of the prioritized queue ≥3x the baseline queue rate within 12 months; STR filing deadline compliance rate maintained at ≥99%.

Cycle

Prioritized queue available at the start of each investigation shift so the first alerts worked by each analyst are the highest-risk cases of the day.

AML typology update enrichment

Continuous monitoring of FATF mutual evaluation reports, financial intelligence unit advisories, supervisory AML/CFT circulars, and industry typology publications for emerging transaction and behavior patterns relevant to the Bank's customer and product mix, with the findings summarized and routed to the AML team's investigation guidance library. Updated typology guidance enables investigators to apply current pattern recognition rather than relying on the Bank's last formal typology training cycle.

S
Problem to solve

AML typology guidance in the Bank's investigation manual reflects the patterns known at the last training or manual update cycle; newly published FATF, financial intelligence unit, and supervisory typology updates are not systematically integrated into the working knowledge available to investigators between training cycles. Investigators applying outdated typology patterns are less likely to correctly identify and document the emerging pattern indicators that characterize current STR-filing rationale, creating a gap in the Bank's AML program quality between training events.

Solution

The AI agent monitors FATF, financial intelligence unit, supervisory, and relevant industry typology publication feeds for new or updated pattern descriptions relevant to the Bank's business profile, extracts the key behavioral indicators, and drafts a summary for the AML investigation guidance library. AML management reviews the summary before publishing to the guidance library, ensuring new typology indicators are available to investigators within days of regulatory publication rather than at the next training cycle.

OKR

Emerging AML typology indicators from regulatory and industry publications are extracted and available to investigators in the guidance library within 5 business days of publication.

Adoption

AI-produced typology summaries covering ≥90% of material regulatory typology publications within 6 months of go-live.

Acceptance

≥85% of AML management typology summary reviews result in publication to the guidance library without material revision; investigators confirm guidance usefulness in ≥75% of quarterly survey responses.

Cycle

Typology summary available for management review within 2 business days of source publication so the 5-business-day guidance library update target is achievable.

AML Alert Investigation Pack

The AI agent assembles the investigation pack at alert-open time — KYC profile, full transaction history, prior alert and STR history, related account connections, sanctions screening results, and adverse media — structured into a reviewer-ready dossier before the analyst begins disposition analysis. Assembly time is eliminated from the analyst's case cycle for every alert, regardless of complexity. Analyst effort concentrates on disposition judgment and the STR filing decision rather than on evidence gathering.

M
Problem to solve

AML alert investigation and sanctions hit review each require analysts to assemble context from KYC, transaction, case, and watchlist systems before substantive analysis can begin — pack assembly consumes 30 to 180 minutes per alert depending on case complexity. The assembly burden is uniform across all case types, including straightforward false-positive patterns that require no analytical judgment; analyst capacity is consumed on structurally repetitive evidence gathering rather than on disposition analysis. Alert queue aging under high-volume conditions creates regulatory exposure under AML/CFT requirements, which commonly set disposition timeline standards.

Solution

The AI agent assembles the investigation pack at alert-open time, sourcing KYC records, transaction history, prior alert dispositions, related account connections, sanctions screening results, and adverse media from connected systems. The analyst receives a complete, structured dossier covering all standard evidence components and directs effort to disposition judgment — assessing the typology fit, making the STR filing decision, and documenting the rationale. Alert queue depth and average disposition time are the primary operational metrics; disposition quality is monitored through periodic QA review of closed cases.

OKR

The AML alert investigation pack — KYC profile, transaction history, prior alert and STR history, related account connections, sanctions screening, and adverse media — is assembled at alert-open time for every alert, so analyst effort begins at the disposition judgment step.

Adoption

AI-assembled investigation pack available at case-open for ≥95% of AML alerts within 12 months of go-live.

Acceptance

≥85% of investigation packs rated as complete and accurate by analysts at case close; pack assembly defect rate (missing required evidence component) ≤3% on QA review.

Cycle

Average analyst pack-assembly time per alert eliminated; average case disposition time reduced by ≥30% against pre-deployment baseline across all alert complexity tiers.

AML alert disposition quality analytics

Systematic analysis of completed AML alert dispositions — tracking analyst-level and team-level disposition patterns, documenting quality against the prescribed investigation checklist, and identifying cases where disposition rationale is incomplete or inconsistent with the evidence assembled. The analysis informs analyst calibration and supervision, reducing the risk of supervisory examination findings on investigation documentation quality.

M

Regulatory Horizon: AML/CFT standards

Problem to solve

Under AML/CFT requirements, in line with FATF Recommendation 10, investigation documentation must capture the evidence considered and the basis for the disposition decision; documentation quality is assessed through sampled supervisory review rather than systematically across the full disposition population. Analyst-level documentation gaps and inconsistent disposition rationale patterns that accumulate between supervisory review cycles create examination risk; they are not identified through the current sample-based QA approach.

Solution

The AI agent analyzes completed alert dispositions against the prescribed documentation checklist — evidence assembled, rationale articulation, and disposition consistency with the evidence — by analyst, team, and alert type, and produces a ranked quality gap report with the specific deficiency patterns and their frequency. AML supervisors use the quality gap report to direct analyst coaching and documentation calibration, with the evidence pattern available for supervisory examination pre-preparation.

OKR

AML alert disposition quality gaps are identified across the disposition population each month and available for analyst coaching and supervisory examination preparation.

Adoption

Disposition quality analytics covering ≥80% of monthly alert disposition volume within 9 months of go-live; used in ≥4 AML supervision reviews per year.

Acceptance

Documentation deficiency rate on sampled supervisory review reduced by ≥30% within 18 months; analyst coaching interventions driven by quality analytics rather than exam findings in ≥90% of cases.

Cycle

Quality gap report refreshed monthly from completed disposition data so coaching decisions reflect the most recent 30 days of disposition quality.

False-positive analyst calibration brief

Automated drafting of analyst calibration briefs for transaction monitoring rules with elevated analyst-level false-positive disagreement — identifying the specific alert characteristics where analysts reach different disposition outcomes and summarizing the disposition rationale patterns for calibration review. Calibration briefs enable the AML supervisor to focus the team calibration session on the specific rule and alert-type combinations that are generating inconsistent analyst dispositions.

S
Problem to solve

Analyst-level disagreement on false-positive dispositions — where different analysts reach different outcomes for materially similar alerts — is a source of both over-dismissal risk (genuine activity dismissed) and under-dismissal cost (excessive alert volume retained for investigation). Identifying calibration gaps requires the AML supervisor to review disposition records across the analyst population, a manual process that is not operationally tractable for the full alert disposition history.

Solution

The AI agent analyzes the alert disposition record for each transaction monitoring rule, identifies alert-type and characteristic combinations where analyst disposition outcomes diverge materially, and drafts a calibration brief with the specific alert characteristics, the divergent disposition patterns, and the frequency of disagreement. AML supervisors use the calibration brief to structure the team calibration session, with the disposition evidence available for the calibration rationale discussion.

OKR

Analyst calibration briefs for transaction monitoring rules with elevated disposition disagreement are drafted from disposition data and available for supervisory calibration sessions.

Adoption

Calibration briefs used in ≥4 team calibration sessions per year within 9 months of go-live.

Acceptance

Analyst-level disposition disagreement rate on flagged rule-alert combinations reduced by ≥20% within 18 months of first calibration cycle informed by the briefs.

Cycle

Calibration brief refreshed quarterly from the prior 90 days of disposition data so each calibration session addresses the most recent disagreement patterns.

AML False-Positive Rule Analysis

The AI agent clusters closed-alert dispositions to identify which transaction monitoring rules and customer-profile combinations generate disproportionate false-positive volume, producing targeted parameter adjustment recommendations for model risk committee review. The analysis covers 12 months of closed alerts, providing the statistical population needed to distinguish systematic tuning opportunities from case-specific noise. The model risk and AML teams use the output to prepare tuning proposals for model risk committee submission under AML/CFT supervisory expectations.

M
Problem to solve

Alert false-positive rates of 90 to 98 percent consume analyst capacity, but systematic rule tuning is constrained by senior-analyst availability to conduct the population-level analysis required to support model risk committee submissions. Tuning proposals resting on manual review of 50 to 100 cases do not satisfy the evidentiary standard for model risk committee approval; the full disposition dataset is required but not accessible without structured analytical tooling. Under AML/CFT supervisory expectations, rule changes must be documented, justified, and approved through the model risk governance process — a requirement that delays tuning even when the need is evident from daily alert volumes.

Solution

The AI agent reads 12 months of closed alerts and analyst dispositions, clusters false positives by transaction context, customer profile, and rule parameters, and identifies the specific rule-and-profile combinations driving disproportionate false-positive volume. It produces a ranked tuning opportunity list with the estimated alert volume reduction per adjustment and the supporting disposition evidence for each recommendation. The model risk and AML teams use the output to draft tuning proposals for model risk committee submission; false-positive rate by rule and segment is the primary outcome metric tracked post-implementation.

OKR

AML false-positive rate by transaction monitoring rule and customer-profile segment is systematically analyzed across 12 months of closed alerts, with model-risk-committee-ready tuning proposals produced from the full disposition dataset.

Adoption

AI-produced rule analysis covering ≥12 months of closed alerts used to support ≥2 model risk committee tuning submissions per year.

Acceptance

≥75% of AI-identified tuning opportunities rated as actionable by the model risk committee based on statistical evidence quality; post-implementation false-positive rate reduction ≥15% per approved tuning cycle.

Cycle

Population-level false-positive analysis cycle compressed from 3–6 months of senior-analyst availability to ≤4 weeks from commissioning to model risk committee submission.

Rule tuning impact simulation

Simulation of the alert volume and false-positive rate impact of proposed transaction monitoring rule parameter adjustments — estimating the change in true-positive and false-positive alert counts for each parameter change candidate — before the change is submitted for model risk committee approval. The simulation provides the quantitative impact estimate required for the committee change proposal without requiring a live parameter change in the production monitoring system.

M
Problem to solve

Rule changes require documentation and model risk committee approval under AML/CFT supervisory expectations; the approval pack must include a quantitative estimate of the impact on alert volume and detection coverage. Manual simulation of rule change impact requires the transaction monitoring team to apply proposed parameter changes to the historical alert population, a process that consumes 3 to 5 days of analyst time per rule change candidate.

Solution

The AI agent applies the proposed parameter change to the historical alert population and computes the estimated change in alert volume, false-positive rate, and true-positive preservation for the affected rule, producing a simulation output in the model risk committee's prescribed format. The transaction monitoring team uses the simulation output as the quantitative impact evidence in the committee change proposal, with the true-positive preservation analysis serving as the detection coverage argument.

OKR

Rule parameter change impact simulations are produced from historical alert data and available for model risk committee change proposals without manual analyst simulation.

Adoption

AI-produced simulations used for ≥85% of transaction monitoring rule change proposals within 9 months of go-live.

Acceptance

STR drafting

STR narratives must cover who/what/when/where/why/how under the applicable guidance, cross-reference related prior STRs, and meet a hard filing deadline that runs from the determination of suspicion. Drafting from scratch takes 4–8 hours of AML compliance officer time per STR.

STR quality pre-filing check

Automated pre-filing quality check of STR narratives against the prescribed disclosure checklist — who/what/when/where/why/how completeness, prior STR cross-reference, subject identifier completeness, and transaction detail accuracy — before the STR is submitted to the filing queue. Quality gaps are returned to the AML compliance officer before filing, eliminating the post-filing correction cycle and the associated regulator notification.

S
Problem to solve

STR filings returned by the financial intelligence unit for completeness deficiencies require a corrected refiling within a compressed timeline; corrections after the original filing deadline may constitute a regulatory violation depending on the nature of the deficiency. AML compliance officer review of the full disclosure checklist for each STR is the primary quality gate; under high-volume conditions, checklist adherence drops and deficiency rates increase.

Solution

The AI agent reads the completed STR narrative and checks it against the prescribed disclosure checklist, flagging any missing or incomplete elements with the specific requirement and the applicable guidance reference. The AML compliance officer resolves flagged gaps before submitting to the filing queue, with the quality check record retained in the case file for supervisory review.

STR disclosure completeness is verified by automated pre-filing check before every filing, eliminating STRs returned by the financial intelligence unit for completeness deficiency.

STR narrative drafting

Automated drafting of Suspicious Transaction Report narratives in the format prescribed by the financial intelligence unit, from completed AML investigation findings, covering who, what, when, where, why, and how per the applicable guidance. AML compliance officers review, supplement, and approve the narrative draft before filing, concentrating their effort on the substantive judgment and approval step rather than the initial narrative construction.

M
Problem to solve

STR narratives must cover all prescribed disclosure elements and cross-reference related prior STRs under a hard filing deadline that runs from the determination of suspicion. Drafting from scratch consumes 4 to 8 hours of AML compliance officer time per STR; during periods of elevated STR volume, the drafting burden compresses the review and approval time available before the filing deadline.

Solution

The AI agent reads the completed investigation findings — subject profile, transaction analysis, suspicion basis, and related prior STRs — and drafts the STR narrative in the prescribed format, covering all required disclosure elements and cross-referencing prior STRs for the subject. The AML compliance officer reviews the draft for accuracy and completeness, adds any judgment context not reflected in the investigation record, and approves the narrative before filing.

OKR

STR narratives are drafted from investigation findings in the prescribed regulatory format and available for AML compliance officer review and approval within 5 business days of suspicion determination.

Adoption

AI-drafted STR narratives used for ≥80% of STR filings within 9 months of go-live.

STR population trend analytics

Analysis of the STR filing population — by suspicion type, customer segment, product, and AML compliance officer — to identify trends in the typology distribution, filing volume, and subject profile that indicate changes in the Bank's money laundering risk profile or in the AML program's detection calibration. The analysis supports the AML compliance officer's annual program effectiveness assessment and the board's AML risk appetite review with a structured evidence base.

M
Problem to solve

STR filing trends — changes in typology mix, customer segment concentration, and subject profile distribution — are tracked at aggregate filing volume level; shifts in the typology distribution that indicate emerging risk concentrations or detection calibration gaps are not systematically identified from the STR population. The AML program effectiveness assessment requires demonstration that the filing population reflects the Bank's actual risk exposure; without structured trend analysis, this argument relies on qualitative program review rather than quantitative STR population evidence.

Solution

The AI agent analyzes the STR filing population by typology, customer segment, product, and AML compliance officer, producing a trend report that identifies shifts in the distribution over the prior 12 months and flags typologies or segments with material volume changes. AML compliance management uses the trend report for the annual program effectiveness assessment and the board AML risk appetite review, with the population distribution evidence available for both regulatory and governance documentation.

OKR

STR filing population trends by typology, segment, and product are analyzed on a rolling basis and available for AML program effectiveness assessment and board risk appetite review.

Adoption

STR population trend analytics used in ≥2 annual program effectiveness assessments and ≥1 board risk appetite review within the first 18 months of go-live.

Acceptance
