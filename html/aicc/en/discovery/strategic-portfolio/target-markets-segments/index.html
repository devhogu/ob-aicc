Target markets & segments

Segment strategy defines which customer cohorts the Bank serves, where it concentrates growth capital, and how it calibrates product, pricing, and channel investment per segment. Segment economics — CAC, LTV, wallet share, churn cohort distributions — determine which focal segments sustain profitable scale and which consume capital below the hurdle. The opportunity for GenAI is continuous segment-economics visibility, briefing support for front-line teams, and systematic opportunity detection — replacing quarterly pack assembly with a live read of cohort dynamics across Retail, SME, Corporate, Public Sector and, where the Bank serves them, Private Banking and Wealth Management.

Insights & analytics

Segment economics for Retail, SME, Corporate, Private Banking, Wealth Management, and Public Sector are assembled quarterly or semi-annually from finance, CRM, and risk exports; wallet share is estimated once a year. Segment heads lack a current view of where each segment creates value and where capital is deployed below the hurdle rate.

Enablement

Relationship managers prepare client reviews by hand from multiple systems, and churn lists and credit-appetite guidance reach them in forms that are hard to use at the point of conversation. Advisory breadth depends on each RM's own knowledge, and deals are originated that the credit committee later declines.

Automation

Obligations under national banking programs — subsidized-lending targets, Treasury Single Account servicing, government-payroll participation — are tracked in spreadsheets and reconciled manually before each regulatory reporting cycle. Gaps surface late, with limited time for remediation.

New business opportunities

Prospect and opportunity identification relies on individual RM networks, annual penetration analysis, and manual monitoring of procurement portals. With systematic detection of under-penetrated SME sub-sectors, corporate and private banking opportunities, and public sector tenders, coverage effort goes to the highest-value targets with lead time to act.

Retail

Retail segment strategy defines the Bank's mass-market and emerging affluent propositions — covering acquisition targets by product (current accounts, deposits, consumer loans, mortgage, cards), pricing strategy, channel investment (branch, digital, agent banking), and segment economics tracked on customer lifetime value, cross-sell depth, cost-to-acquire, and NIM per product in the retail book. Retail is typically a bank's largest segment by customer count and its primary source of low-cost funding from transactional deposit balances.

Private banking

Where the Bank has a private banking business, private banking segment strategy defines its proposition and investment for high-net-worth (HNW) and ultra-high-net-worth (UHNW) clients — covering AUM targets, advisory model (discretionary vs. advisory vs. execution-only), product range (investment products, lending against assets, trust and estate services), and the client coverage ratio governing relationship manager (RM) capacity. Private banking economics are tracked on AUM growth, fee yield on AUM, net new money, and client retention rates.

SME

SME segment strategy covers the Bank's proposition for small and medium enterprises — including working capital lending, trade finance, deposit products, and payment services — along with coverage model design (relationship manager capacity, digital self-service scope) and credit approach (standardized scoring vs. judgmental underwriting by size band). SME economics are tracked on portfolio yield, credit loss rate, cross-product penetration, and RAROC against the segment's capital consumption, with SME lending often subject to government guarantee programs that affect the capital efficiency calculation.
