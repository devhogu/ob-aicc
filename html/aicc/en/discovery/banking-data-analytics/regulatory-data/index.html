Regulatory data

Regulatory data is the governed data layer that feeds the bank's supervisory reporting obligations — the datasets assembled, transformed, validated, and submitted to NBKR, NBK/ARDFM, and CBR on monthly, quarterly, and ad hoc cycles. It encompasses the reporting datasets extracted from source systems, the data lineage documentation required by BCBS 239 to evidence aggregation accuracy and completeness, supervisory submission records, and the data residency and localisation controls mandated by Kazakhstan's Law No. 94-V and Russia's Federal Law No. 242-FZ. The regulatory data domain is where data-quality failures and lineage gaps become supervisory findings; its governance disciplines — CDE ownership, documented transformation logic, reproducible aggregation — determine the bank's regulatory risk posture. The GenAI opportunity is to automate regulatory report assembly across multiple form types, map BCBS 239 data lineage from source to report field, and monitor data-residency compliance continuously — sustaining submission quality and reducing the manual assembly burden that currently occupies the regulatory reporting team for 10-15 analyst days per cycle.

Insights & analytics Regulatory reporting dataset completeness — whether the data extracted from source systems covers the full population required for each form — is validated manually during each submission cycle. Incomplete populations due to missing system extracts, extract-timing mismatches, or scope definition errors are identified during the validation step, often too close to the submission deadline for comprehensive remediation.
Enablement Regulatory change management — identifying the data impact of new reporting requirements from NBKR, CBR, or ARDFM — requires the regulatory reporting team to map the new form fields against existing data sources, identify gaps, and define the remediation programme. The mapping exercise is done manually from form specifications; for major reporting changes, the mapping consumes several weeks before implementation work can begin.
Automation Monthly and quarterly regulatory submissions follow prescribed formats applied to structured source data through defined transformation logic. The assembly, validation, and variance commentary steps repeat each cycle with the same rules and the same source systems — the pattern is highly repeatable and a strong candidate for agent-driven execution with regulatory reporting team review and sign-off.
New business opportunities Regulatory reporting teams that automate assembly and validation can redirect analyst capacity from collation to quality assurance, regulatory change management, and proactive supervisor engagement. Banks that demonstrate consistently accurate, timely submissions with documented lineage build supervisory trust that reduces the intensity of examination cycles.

Regulatory reporting datasets

The structured datasets assembled from source systems for each regulatory submission cycle — covering the population, field definitions, transformation logic, and validation rules required for NBKR Form 700 (prudential returns), CBR Form 0409 (credit risk reporting), ARDFM capital adequacy returns, and AML/CFT transaction reporting. The dataset assembly process is the first step of each reporting cycle; its completeness and accuracy determine whether the submission passes regulatory validation checks on first filing or requires restatement.

Regulatory Submission Variance Brief

Agent compares the current cycle's regulatory dataset to the prior cycle's submission and generates a variance brief — identifying material movements by form field, classifying variances by likely cause (portfolio growth, risk grade migration, rate movement, structural change), and flagging unexplained residuals for analyst investigation. The regulatory reporting team uses the brief as the primary quality review input before approving the dataset for submission.

S
Problem to solve

Regulatory reporting quality review requires the team to compare the current cycle's dataset to the prior submission, explain material movements in key fields, and confirm that unexplained residuals do not indicate data errors. The comparison is performed manually for each form; the variance commentary is drafted by the analyst after identifying the movements. For complex forms with many fields, the manual comparison consumes a substantial portion of the available review window, compressing the time available for investigating genuine anomalies.

Solution

Agent reads the current and prior cycle datasets, calculates field-level variances, and classifies each material variance against known drivers — portfolio balance movements, risk grade changes, market rate movements, and FX revaluation — using data available from the credit, market risk, and finance systems. Variances that can be attributed to a documented driver are summarised in the variance brief; residuals that exceed a defined unexplained threshold are flagged for analyst investigation. The brief is structured to align with the form's section structure, enabling the review to proceed field-by-field.

OKR

The regulatory reporting team completes the variance review for each cycle with a structured, pre-populated brief, directing analytical effort to unexplained residuals rather than variance identification.

Adoption

Agent produces variance briefs for ≥ 3 form types for ≥ 12 consecutive submission cycles within the first year; variance driver classification logic validated by the regulatory reporting team within 60 days of go-live.

Acceptance

≥ 80% of attributed variances confirmed as correctly classified by the regulatory reporting team; unexplained residual flags confirmed as requiring investigation in ≥ 85% of cases.

Cycle

Variance review time reduced from 2-3 days of manual comparison to ≤ half a day of brief review and residual investigation per cycle.

Data lineage & BCBS 239 compliance

The documented trace of each CDE from its source system through every transformation step to its final position in the regulatory report — the evidence required by BCBS 239 Principles 2 and 3 (data accuracy and completeness) for supervisory review. For systemically important banks under NBKR and CBR supervision, BCBS 239 lineage documentation is a supervisory examination requirement; gaps in the lineage map are findings that require remediation within defined timelines. Maintaining current lineage as ELT pipelines evolve is a continuous governance discipline, not a periodic documentation exercise.

BCBS 239 Lineage Gap Detection

Agent scans the enterprise data lineage map and identifies CDEs that appear in regulatory reports but lack a complete documented trace from source system through transformation steps to the report field — the gaps that constitute BCBS 239 Principle 2 and 3 findings in supervisory examinations. The data governance team receives a prioritised gap list by regulatory report and CDE, with each gap classified by the remediation effort required and the examination risk if left open.

M
Problem to solve

The scope of the documentation gap grows continuously as ELT pipelines evolve. The data governance team identifies lineage gaps reactively — when an auditor or supervisor requests the lineage trace for a specific CDE — rather than through systematic gap detection. Supervisory examination findings require remediation within defined timelines, creating compressed workloads when gaps are large.

Solution

The monthly gap report is ranked by the regulatory report that consumes each ungapped CDE, the next submission date, and the examination risk tier. The data governance team uses the report to structure the lineage remediation programme.

OKR

The BCBS 239 lineage documentation gap is continuously monitored and remediated, with supervisory examination exposure from undocumented lineage maintained at an acceptable level.

Supervisory data submissions

Each submission carries a defined format, a submission deadline, and a regulatory consequence for late or inaccurate filing. The submission process requires extract, transformation, validation, sign-off, and transmission steps that each consume regulatory reporting team time; ad hoc requests from supervisors require the same data-assembly steps on compressed timelines.

Data residency & localization controls

The governance controls that ensure personal and financial data of Kazakhstan and Russia citizens is stored and processed within domestic jurisdictions, as required by Kazakhstan's Law No. 94-V and Russia's Federal Law No. 242-FZ. Data residency compliance requires maintaining a current data inventory mapped to storage locations, monitoring cross-border data flows for personal data, and maintaining the legal basis register for any cross-border transfers that are permitted under the data protection framework. Cloud adoption and cross-border API integrations create a continuously evolving data flow landscape that periodic mapping exercises cannot fully track.

When a new regulatory reporting requirement is published, the regulatory reporting team must map each new form field to an existing data source, identify fields with no current data coverage, and produce a data impact assessment for the programme team. The mapping requires cross-referencing the form specification against the enterprise data catalog and source system documentation. For major reporting changes, the exercise delays implementation planning by several weeks.

Solution

Agent reads the new reporting form specification, queries the enterprise data catalog for each field requirement, and determines whether an existing CDE covers the data need. For covered fields, it documents the source system, transformation logic, and data quality risk. For uncovered fields, it flags the gap with an assessment of remediation options; the regulatory change programme team uses the agent-generated impact assessment as the basis for the implementation plan.

OKR

The regulatory change programme team receives an agent-generated data impact assessment for each new reporting requirement — mapping new form fields to existing sources, documenting coverage gaps, and assessing remediation options — as the implementation planning baseline.

Regulatory Change Data Gap Assessment

When NBKR or CBR publishes a regulatory reporting change, agent reads the new form specification, maps each new or amended field to existing data sources in the enterprise catalog, and produces a data gap assessment identifying fields with no covering source and fields covered by existing data requiring transformation. The data governance and regulatory reporting teams use the gap assessment to scope the data remediation programme before the submission deadline.

M
Problem to solve

Each regulatory reporting change requires a data impact assessment: which new fields can be sourced from existing data assets, which require new data collection, and which require source system changes. The assessment is produced manually by the regulatory reporting team, combining form specification analysis with knowledge of the current data estate. For complex form revisions — a new NBKR Form 700 section, a CBR Form 0409 amendment — the manual assessment takes two to three weeks and may underestimate gaps due to incomplete data estate knowledge.

Solution

Agent reads the published form specification change, extracts new and amended field definitions, and queries the enterprise data catalog for each field — identifying existing source data assets that cover the definition, transformation requirements, and data quality gaps. Fields with no covering source are identified as requiring new data collection; fields with partial coverage are described with the gap between the source data and the field requirement. The gap assessment is structured as a remediation programme input: field, gap type, covering source or required new source, and estimated remediation effort. The data governance team uses the assessment to scope and timeline the programme.

OKR

The data remediation programme for each regulatory reporting change is scoped from an agent-generated gap assessment, with new data collection requirements and transformation gaps identified within days of the regulation publication.

Adoption

Agent produces gap assessments for ≥ 95% of NBKR and CBR reporting changes within 12 months of go-live; enterprise data catalog coverage sufficient for gap matching within 45 days of go-live.

Acceptance

≥ 80% of gap assessments confirmed as complete by the regulatory reporting team without material supplementary manual analysis; new data collection requirements identified by agent confirmed as genuine gaps in ≥ 85% of cases.

Cycle

Gap assessment delivery time reduced from 2-3 weeks of manual analysis to ≤ 3 business days of agent-generated assessment and team review; remediation programme scoped before the change lead-time window closes.
