Discovery Catalog / Banking Data & Analytics / Customer data

Customer data

Customer data is the Bank's most valuable and most regulated data domain — the integrated record of who the customer is, what they hold, how they behave, and what permissions they have granted. It spans identity and KYC records, the customer 360 profile assembled from CRM and product systems, behavioral and segmentation data derived from transactions and interactions, consent and privacy records governed under applicable law on personal data, and the relationship and interaction history maintained by relationship managers and service teams. Under AML/CFT and customer due diligence requirements, and with reference to FIBO's customer entity model, each layer of customer data carries specific governance obligations. The GenAI opportunity is to assemble the customer 360 view at the point of engagement without manual data retrieval, monitor consent coverage continuously, and surface behavioral signals for credit and product analytics — giving every customer-facing function access to the same complete, current customer picture.

Insights & analytics

Customer identity, product holdings, transaction behavior, and interaction history are each maintained in separate systems with their own reporting cadence. Relationship managers, credit analysts, and product teams assemble their own working view of the customer from manual extracts; the Bank lacks an authoritative, continuously assembled customer 360 profile that any function can access at the point of need.

Enablement

KYC re-verification exercises require the compliance team to retrieve identity documents, current beneficial ownership structures, and prior screening results from separate KYC, CRM, and sanctions-screening systems before the re-verification analysis can begin. For a corporate client with complex ownership, assembling the pre-verification dossier can consume a full analyst day before substantive compliance work starts.

Automation

Customer segmentation model refresh, behavioral cohort updates, and KYC status tracking follow defined rules applied to structured customer data on monthly or quarterly cycles. Each process is repeatable and data-intensive — candidates for continuous AI-driven execution.

New business opportunities

A continuously assembled customer 360 profile — updated with each new transaction, interaction, and product event — gives product and RM teams an analytics substrate for next-best-action recommendations, early attrition signals, and personalized engagement that monthly batch refreshes cannot support.

Customer identity & KYC data

The legal identity record for each customer — covering government-issued identifiers, beneficial ownership structure for legal entities, sanctions screening status, and KYC risk classification under AML/CFT requirements. KYC data is the compliance foundation for every customer relationship; its currency and accuracy determine the Bank's regulatory exposure. KYC refresh cycles — typically annual for high-risk customers, periodic for standard-risk — generate a recurring data-assembly workload for the compliance team.

Beneficial Ownership Structure Analysis

The AI agent analyzes beneficial ownership records for legal entity customers, mapping ownership chains to identify ultimate beneficial owners (UBOs), PEP connections, and high-risk jurisdiction links that may not be visible at the direct-ownership level. The compliance team uses the analysis during KYC onboarding and periodic review to meet the UBO identification requirements of AML/CFT regulation. Complex ownership structures with multi-layer chains, nominee arrangements, or offshore holding entities are flagged for enhanced due diligence.

M

Regulatory Horizon: AML/CFT standards

Problem to solve

UBO identification for legal entity customers with complex ownership structures — multi-layer holding companies, nominee shareholders, cross-border ownership chains — requires compliance analysts to trace ownership records through multiple registry extracts, company filings, and KYC documents. The analysis is time-consuming and inconsistent across analysts; complex structures that exceed analyst capacity within the onboarding window are escalated with incomplete UBO mapping. PEP connections and high-risk jurisdiction links that exist at indirect ownership levels are not reliably identified.

Solution

The AI agent reads the entity's ownership and control documentation from the KYC system, company registry filings, and available third-party entity data, and maps the full ownership chain to the UBO level using graph analysis of the entity relationship structure. The output is a structured UBO analysis: the ownership chain visualization, UBO identities and their risk attributes, and a risk tier recommendation. Structures where UBO identification is incomplete due to data gaps are flagged with the specific missing information required. The compliance analyst reviews and confirms the analysis during KYC onboarding or periodic review.

OKR

Compliance analysts complete UBO identification for legal entity customers with a complete, graph-based ownership chain analysis, reducing the risk of undetected PEP and high-risk jurisdiction exposure.

Consent & privacy records

The formal records of customer permissions for data processing, marketing communications, third-party data sharing, and product-specific terms — governed under applicable law on personal data. Each consent record specifies the processing purpose, the legal basis, and the expiry or withdrawal status. Consent coverage determines the addressable population for data-driven engagement; gaps between the consented and engaged populations create regulatory exposure under data protection enforcement frameworks.

Customer 360 profile

The integrated customer view assembled across all source systems — KYC and identity, product holdings and balances, transaction behavior, interaction history, and service records — that provides any customer-facing function with a complete picture of the customer at the point of engagement. The customer 360 is not a system but an assembly pattern: pulling the current state from each source on demand. Its utility depends on the completeness and currency of the underlying source data and the speed at which the integrated view can be retrieved.

Relationship history is the institutional memory for client coverage; it determines the quality of RM meeting preparation, enables continuity across RM transitions, and provides the evidence base for complaint management and regulatory inquiry responses.

Problem to solve

Credit and product-targeting scores are regenerated on a scheduled cycle — monthly or quarterly — regardless of whether individual customers' behavioral features have changed materially since the last run. Customers whose transaction behavior or product usage has shifted significantly carry a stale score until the next full cycle. For credit limit decisions and real-time product offers, a stale score may misrepresent the customer's current risk or propensity profile. A selective refresh mechanism requires a trigger signal to identify which customers warrant an out-of-cycle score update.

Solution

The AI agent tracks the behavioral feature values — transaction frequency, average balance, payment regularity, product usage intensity — for each customer against the values recorded at the last model score run. When feature movement for a customer exceeds the materiality threshold defined for a given model, the AI agent generates a selective refresh trigger for that customer and model combination. The credit and analytics model execution teams process the selective refresh queue on a weekly basis. The CDO and model risk teams review trigger volumes to calibrate materiality thresholds.

OKR

Credit and product-targeting scores remain current for the highest-movement customers between full model cycles through a structured selective refresh triggered by material feature movement.

Adoption

The AI agent monitors feature movement for 100% of active customers against all in-scope models for ≥ 48 weeks within 12 months; materiality thresholds defined and approved by credit and analytics within 60 days of go-live.

Acceptance

≥ 75% of selective refresh triggers confirmed as yielding a materially different score on re-run; false-positive trigger rate ≤ 15% within 3 months of go-live.

Cycle

Selective refresh queue processed on a weekly cadence; score staleness for the highest-movement decile of customers reduced from full model cycle gap (30–90 days) to ≤ 7 days within 6 months.
