Master & reference data

Master & reference data is the enterprise's authoritative layer of shared entity records — the golden records for customers, counterparties, products, accounts, and the code tables and lookup hierarchies that give those records consistent meaning across systems. Governed through a master data management (MDM) program aligned to DAMA-DMBOK and the FIBO financial industry ontology, this domain defines the canonical version of each entity: who the customer is, what legal entity the counterparty belongs to, which product code maps to which regulatory classification. Supervisory data governance expectations call for critical data elements (CDEs) in master and reference data to carry documented lineage, quality rules, and steward ownership. The GenAI opportunity is to automate golden-record deduplication, continuously monitor CDE quality dimensions, and maintain the data catalog through NLP-driven enrichment — sustaining the accuracy and completeness of the master layer between manual governance cycles.

Insights & analytics

Golden-record completeness and the rate of unresolved duplicate entity records are measured through periodic data-quality campaigns run by the data governance team. Between campaigns, duplicate accumulation and field degradation in customer and counterparty master records are not visible to the CDO; the master layer's reliability is assessed reactively when a downstream system reports inconsistent entity data.

Enablement

Data stewards managing customer, counterparty, and product master records spend a significant portion of their time on manual deduplication — reviewing candidate match pairs generated by probabilistic matching tools, resolving edge cases, and documenting merge decisions. The volume of new records onboarded across channels and the variety of name transliterations between scripts, such as Cyrillic and Latin, amplify the deduplication workload.

Automation

Deduplication candidate generation, CDE quality-rule execution, and reference-table refresh cycles follow defined logic applied to structured master data. Each process is repeatable, rule-driven, and volume-intensive — candidates for AI-driven execution with data steward review at the exception level.

New business opportunities

A continuously accurate customer and counterparty master layer reduces the cost of KYC re-verification, eliminates the revenue risk of duplicate account suppression in marketing campaigns, and improves credit decisioning accuracy by ensuring exposure aggregation across related entities uses a clean entity hierarchy.

Golden record management

The authoritative single record for each customer, counterparty, legal entity, and product — maintained through MDM processes that identify, match, merge, and survivorship-rule records from multiple source systems into the canonical master. The golden record is the reference point for KYC compliance, credit exposure aggregation, and customer analytics. In markets where names are written in more than one script, such as Cyrillic and Latin, name transliterations, the frequency of aliases, and the volume of onboarding channels create a structurally high deduplication workload that periodic manual campaigns cannot fully address.

Survivorship Rule Consistency Audit

The AI agent audits golden records in the MDM hub to identify entity records where the applied survivorship logic has produced an outcome inconsistent with the documented survivorship rules — for example, a source system of lower authority has populated a CDE where a higher-authority source held a non-null value. The data governance team uses the audit report to identify survivorship rule drift, correct affected golden records, and update the MDM hub configuration where rules have been applied incorrectly.

S
Problem to solve

Survivorship rules define which source system's value prevails for each CDE when multiple source systems contribute records to the golden record. In practice, survivorship rule application in the MDM hub may drift from documented policy — due to system configuration changes, new source system additions, or data load sequencing issues. The divergence between documented and applied survivorship rules is not detected systematically; auditors and data consumers discover survivorship inconsistencies when a CDE in the golden record differs from the authoritative source value they expect.

Solution

The AI agent reads golden records from the MDM hub and retrieves the contributing source system values for each CDE, comparing the golden record value against the expected survivorship outcome based on the documented authority hierarchy. Records where the applied survivorship outcome diverges from the documented rule are flagged — with the CDE affected, the golden record value, the expected winning-source value, and the magnitude of the divergence. The data governance team reviews the quarterly audit output, corrects affected golden records, and updates the MDM hub configuration where systematic rule drift is identified.

OKR

Golden records in the MDM hub reflect the documented survivorship rules, with rule drift and configuration errors identified through regular audit and corrected within the MDM governance cycle.

Adoption

The AI agent produces quarterly survivorship rule audit reports covering 100% of active golden records and CDEs for ≥ 4 consecutive quarters within the first year; authority hierarchy mapping approved by data governance within 60 days of go-live.

Acceptance

≥ 80% of survivorship divergence flags confirmed as genuine rule drift or configuration errors by the data governance team; golden record corrections applied within 2 weeks of audit report delivery in ≥ 85% of cases.

Cycle

Survivorship rule audit cycle shifted from ad hoc audit-triggered review to a quarterly structured check; systematic MDM configuration drift identified and corrected within the same quarter of onset.

MDM Golden Record Deduplication

The AI agent deduplicates customer and entity records across source systems using probabilistic NLP matching on name variants, transliterations, and identifier gaps, producing merge candidates for data steward review on a weekly cadence. Where names are written in more than one script, such as Cyrillic and Latin, transliteration variants and alias frequency make periodic deduplication campaigns insufficient. The golden record in the MDM hub is updated following steward approval.

M
Problem to solve

Customer master data accumulates duplicate and inconsistent records across core banking, CRM, and KYC systems as entities are onboarded across channels and products. Data stewards reconcile duplicates during periodic data-quality campaigns, leaving the golden record stale between cycles. Where they occur, transliterations between scripts, alias conventions, and thin tax-identifier coverage compound the deduplication workload beyond what campaign-based review can clear.

Solution

The AI agent reads entity records from all source systems and applies probabilistic matching across name variants, transliterations, tax identifiers, and address fields, producing consolidation candidates with confidence scores. The data steward reviews high-confidence matches and resolves edge cases; the golden record in the MDM hub is updated on a weekly cadence. Lower-confidence candidates are queued for steward judgment rather than auto-merged.

OKR

Customer and entity golden records in the MDM hub are maintained at weekly-cadence accuracy through AI-driven probabilistic deduplication across name variants, transliterations, and identifier gaps, with steward-approved merges and a separate judgment queue for lower-confidence candidates.

Adoption

The AI agent produces deduplication candidates covering all source systems on a weekly cadence for ≥ 48 weeks within the first 12 months; transliteration and alias matching active from go-live for the full customer and entity population.

Acceptance

≥ 80% of high-confidence candidates merged with steward approval on the same weekly cycle; post-merge golden record accuracy validated at ≥ 95% in semi-annual MDM quality assessments.

Cycle

Reference & code tables

Shared lookup hierarchies and code tables — country codes, currency codes, product classification hierarchies, sector codes, regulatory instrument types, and internal organizational hierarchies — that provide consistent meaning to entity attributes across all systems. Reference data is consumed by every reporting and analytics process; when a regulatory taxonomy changes (a new product code, a sector reclassification), the downstream impact must be traced and propagated consistently across all consuming systems before the next reporting cycle.

The data governance team uses the impact map to coordinate the propagation sprint. Each consuming system receives a data transformation note describing the mapping from old to new code.

S

For each consuming system, it drafts the data transformation note covering the mapping from old to new code. The data governance team uses the impact map to plan and sequence the propagation sprint before the next regulatory submission cycle.

OKR

S

For each finding, the AI agent lists the consuming systems and the regulatory reports that will be affected if the gap is not closed before the next submission cycle. The data governance team receives a weekly alert ranked by submission deadline proximity.

OKR

Reference code tables are maintained in alignment with current regulatory taxonomies, with stale and missing values identified and remediated before each submission cycle.

Customer master data

The canonical record set for individual and corporate customers — covering legal identity, tax identifiers, contact attributes, segment classification, and KYC status. Customer master data is the entity anchor for CRM, credit, compliance, and analytics systems; inconsistencies between source-system representations of the same customer propagate into every downstream function that consumes the uncleaned view. AML/CFT and customer due diligence requirements call for the customer master to carry current KYC status and screening history with documented refresh cycles.

Customer Master CDE Quality Monitor

The AI agent monitors completeness, accuracy, and currency of critical data elements in the customer master — legal identity, tax identifiers, contact attributes, segment classification, and KYC status — against defined quality thresholds on a daily basis. Failures are routed to the responsible data steward with a pre-populated remediation ticket. The CDO receives a weekly quality scorecard by CDE and customer segment, aligned to BCBS 239 Principle 3 and DAMA-DMBOK governance standards.

S
Problem to solve

Customer master data quality is assessed during periodic data governance reviews and at the point of regulatory reporting, when quality failures surface as submission restatements or supervisory findings. Between reviews, new customer onboardings and updates may introduce missing tax identifiers, stale contact records, or inconsistent KYC status flags that propagate into CRM, credit, compliance, and analytics systems. AML/CFT and customer due diligence requirements call for the customer master to carry current KYC status with documented refresh cycles; gaps are a regulatory risk.

Solution

The AI agent reads the customer master daily and applies quality rules for each CDE: completeness check for mandatory fields, format validity for tax identifiers and contact data, currency check comparing KYC status review date against the required refresh cycle, and consistency check across source-system representations of the same customer. Failures are categorized by CDE and customer segment and routed as remediation tickets to the responsible data steward. The CDO receives a weekly quality scorecard showing trend lines per CDE and segment.

OKR

Customer master CDEs are maintained at defined quality thresholds through daily automated monitoring and steward-directed remediation, supporting regulatory compliance and downstream data quality.

Customer Master Onboarding Quality Check

The AI agent validates each new customer record at the point of onboarding before it is committed to the customer master — checking mandatory field completeness, identifier format validity, deduplication against existing records, and KYC status consistency — and flags incomplete or potentially duplicate records for remediation before they enter the master. The onboarding officer receives an inline quality check result with specific failure reasons, enabling same-session correction for the majority of failures.

M
Problem to solve

Customer master data quality failures originate disproportionately at the point of onboarding, when data entry errors, missing identifiers, and duplicate records are introduced under time pressure. Post-onboarding data quality campaigns remediate these failures retrospectively but at a higher cost than preventing them at entry. Deduplication against the existing customer master is a manual check performed inconsistently; duplicate records are introduced when the onboarding officer does not identify the existing record.

Solution

The AI agent intercepts each new customer record submission, applies a quality check covering mandatory field completeness, tax identifier and contact format validity, and fuzzy matching against the existing customer master for potential duplicates. Records failing the quality check receive a structured inline result — field-level failure descriptions and, for potential duplicates, the closest matching existing records with confidence scores. The onboarding officer reviews the result and resolves failures before submission proceeds. High-confidence duplicates require explicit confirmation before a new record is created.

OKR

Customer master data quality failures originating at onboarding are identified and resolved at the point of entry, reducing the volume of post-onboarding remediation work.

Adoption

The AI agent's quality check is applied to ≥ 95% of new customer record submissions across all onboarding channels within 12 months of go-live; deduplication matching active from go-live.

Acceptance

Customer Master Source Reconciliation

The AI agent reconciles customer master records across core banking, CRM, and KYC systems on a weekly basis, identifying customers where the same entity is represented with conflicting attribute values in different source systems — mismatched names, different tax identifiers, inconsistent segment classifications, or divergent KYC status flags. The data governance team uses the reconciliation report to direct golden-record updates and source-system alignment initiatives, with conflicts ranked by downstream impact severity.

M
Problem to solve

Customer data is maintained in parallel in core banking, CRM, and the KYC system; updates applied in one system are not propagated consistently to others. The same customer may carry a current KYC status in the KYC system but a stale flag in core banking, a segment classification updated in CRM but not reflected in the analytics warehouse, or a legal name corrected in the KYC system but unchanged in core banking. Cross-system conflicts propagate into every downstream function that joins customer data from multiple sources; the scope and distribution of conflicts is not systematically known.

Solution

The AI agent reads customer records from core banking, CRM, and the KYC system, matches records to the customer golden record, and identifies attribute-level conflicts for each matched entity. Conflicts are categorized by CDE and ranked by downstream impact — KYC status conflicts affecting AML and regulatory reporting at the top, contact attribute conflicts at lower priority. The weekly reconciliation report shows conflict count and type by source-system pair and provides a resolution recommendation — which source holds the authoritative value for each conflicting CDE based on the documented source-of-truth hierarchy. The data governance team uses the report to direct golden-record updates and source synchronization.

OKR

The data governance team holds a weekly, structured view of cross-system customer master conflicts, directing golden-record updates to the CDEs with the highest downstream impact.

Adoption

The AI agent produces weekly source reconciliation reports covering 100% of the customer master population for ≥ 48 weeks within 12 months; all three source systems integrated from go-live.

Acceptance

≥ 85% of conflicts confirmed as requiring golden-record update by the data governance team; source-of-truth recommendations accepted without amendment in ≥ 80% of cases within 3 months.

Cycle

Cross-system conflict identification cycle shifted from ad hoc discovery to a weekly structured reconciliation; golden-record update backlog cleared within 4 weeks of go-live; ongoing conflicts cleared within 2 weeks of identification.

In line with BCBS 239 Principle 3 (accuracy and integrity) and DAMA-DMBOK governance standards, CDEs carry formally approved quality rules with documented steward ownership. The governance framework determines whether quality-monitoring signals translate into timely remediation or accumulate as unactioned alerts.

S

It tracks each ticket status and generates an SLA breach escalation to the domain data owner when the deadline is exceeded. The weekly governance council summary is produced from the open-ticket state, formatted for the council agenda.

OKR

MDM Quality Rule Review Trigger

The AI agent monitors CDE quality rule performance — false-positive rates, failure volume trends, and rule-coverage gaps identified from undetected data issues — and generates a review trigger when a rule shows performance deterioration or when a new pattern of data failures lacks a covering rule. The data steward and governance team use the trigger to initiate quality rule amendments or additions within the defined review cycle under DAMA-DMBOK governance standards.

S
Problem to solve

Data quality rules are defined at governance program inception and amended reactively when rules generate excessive false positives or fail to detect known data issues. The performance of existing rules — whether false-positive rates are acceptable and whether the rule set covers the actual failure modes observed in the data — is not monitored systematically. Rules that generate excessive noise are disabled informally by stewards rather than formally reviewed; gaps in rule coverage that allow data failures to reach downstream systems are identified only when the failure surfaces in a report or audit.

Solution

The AI agent reads quality rule execution results over a rolling window and calculates per-rule performance indicators: false-positive rate (failures not confirmed as genuine by stewards), failure volume trend, and coverage gap indicators (data failures reaching downstream systems that triggered no quality rule). Rules where false-positive rate exceeds the governance threshold, or where failure volume has increased materially, are flagged for review. Undetected failure patterns are presented as candidate new rules for steward consideration. The governance team uses the trigger list to structure the quarterly quality rule review agenda.

OKR

The quality rule set is maintained at current effectiveness through continuous performance monitoring and structured review triggers, with coverage gaps and high-noise rules identified between scheduled governance cycles.

Adoption

The AI agent monitors quality rule performance for 100% of active CDE rules for ≥ 48 weeks within 12 months; performance indicator thresholds approved by data governance within 45 days of go-live.

Acceptance

≥ 80% of review triggers confirmed as requiring rule amendment or addition by the governance team; quarterly quality rule review agenda informed by the AI agent's triggers in ≥ 4 consecutive quarters.

Cycle

Rule performance deterioration identified within 4 weeks of onset; quality rule review cycle kept current without waiting for the annual governance program review.

S

The credit data team and finance team receive the integrity report with specific failure types and suggested resolutions.

The AI agent validates pricing parameters in the product master — interest rate margins, fee structures, and pricing tier definitions — against the current approved pricing schedule and identifies products where the master record carries parameters that diverge from the approved schedule. The product management and finance teams use the validation report to correct product master pricing before the parameters are consumed by the pricing engine and profitability attribution system.

S
Problem to solve

Product master pricing parameters are updated when product pricing is changed; the update process requires a change request to the product master team, which applies the change during a scheduled maintenance window. Delays between approval of a pricing change and its application to the product master create a window where the pricing engine consumes outdated parameters. For products with margin-sensitive pricing — term deposits, corporate loans — even a brief divergence between approved and applied parameters affects customer billing accuracy and profitability attribution.

Solution

The AI agent reads pricing parameters from the product master and compares them against the current approved pricing schedule held in the product pricing approval system. Products where the master record diverges from the approved schedule — the margin, fee, or tier definition differs — are flagged with the specific divergence and the effective date of the approved change. The product management team receives the weekly validation report with priority ranked by the billing and profitability impact of the divergence. The product master team applies corrections within the defined SLA.

OKR

Product master pricing parameters reflect the current approved pricing schedule, with divergences identified and corrected before they affect customer billing and profitability attribution.

Adoption

The AI agent validates pricing parameters for 100% of active products against the approved schedule on a weekly basis for ≥ 48 weeks within 12 months; approved pricing schedule source integrated within 45 days of go-live.

Acceptance

≥ 90% of pricing divergence flags confirmed as genuine by the product management team; corrections applied within the defined SLA in ≥ 85% of flagged cases within 3 months of go-live.

Cycle

Pricing parameter validation cycle shifted from monthly profitability reconciliation discovery to a weekly proactive check; billing errors attributable to stale pricing parameters reduced by ≥ 60% within 6 months.

Data Catalog Usage Analytics

The AI agent analyzes data catalog access logs to identify which assets are actively consumed by analytics and reporting teams, which cataloged assets have no active consumers, and which assets are searched for but not found — indicating undocumented fields with active demand. The CDO uses the usage report to direct steward effort toward high-demand assets and to reprioritize catalog enrichment backlog items.

S
Problem to solve

The enterprise data catalog is maintained by data stewards prioritizing assets based on a subjective assessment of importance and urgency. Assets with high actual consumption remain underdocumented when stewards are unaware of consumer demand; assets with low consumption absorb steward effort that could be redirected. The catalog has no feedback loop connecting consumer behavior — what is searched, what is accessed, what is not found — to the stewardship prioritization process.

Solution

The AI agent reads catalog access logs, search query logs, and API usage telemetry, and produces a monthly usage report: the most-accessed assets by domain, assets with zero consumption in the trailing period, and search queries that returned no results (indicating undocumented demand). It ranks catalog enrichment backlog items by the frequency of associated search demand. The CDO and data governance council use the report to reprioritize steward effort and to identify high-demand undocumented assets for priority cataloging.

OKR

Steward effort is directed by evidence of consumer demand — high-consumption assets are documented first; undocumented assets with active search demand are identified and added to the enrichment queue.

Adoption

The AI agent produces monthly usage reports for ≥ 12 consecutive months within the first year; access log and search query telemetry integration completed within 60 days of go-live.

Acceptance

≥ 80% of high-demand undocumented assets flagged by the AI agent added to the stewardship enrichment queue within the following sprint cycle; CDO confirms stewardship reprioritization decisions informed by usage data in ≥ 10 of 12 monthly cycles.

Cycle

Catalog enrichment prioritization shifted from subjective steward judgment to demand-evidence-based ranking; high-demand undocumented assets cataloged within 4 weeks of identification in ≥ 80% of cases.

Catalog Lineage Completeness Check

The AI agent scans the enterprise data catalog for assets where the lineage link — source system to downstream report or analytics layer — is absent, incomplete, or stale relative to the current ELT pipeline configuration. The AI agent produces a monthly lineage gap report prioritized by regulatory reporting impact, supporting lineage documentation in line with the BCBS 239 principles and the CDO's governance program. Data stewards receive pre-populated lineage draft entries for review and approval.

S
Problem to solve

BCBS 239 Principles 2 and 3 call for documented lineage from source data to regulatory report fields. The enterprise data catalog carries lineage links for a portion of CDEs; assets added through new data pipelines are cataloged without lineage entries until a steward manually maps and documents the transformation path. The lineage gap grows as ELT pipelines are deployed faster than stewards can document them. Supervisory examination requests for lineage evidence expose the gap.

Solution

The AI agent reads the ELT pipeline configuration and compares the source-to-target mappings in active pipelines against the lineage entries in the data catalog. Assets with no corresponding catalog lineage entry and assets whose catalog lineage does not reflect the current pipeline configuration are identified. For each gap, the AI agent generates a draft lineage entry — source system, transformation steps, and target asset — from the pipeline metadata, for data steward review and approval. The monthly gap report is ranked by the number of regulatory reports that consume each affected asset.

OKR

Data catalog lineage coverage is maintained at current accuracy through monthly automated gap detection and steward-reviewed draft entries, supporting BCBS 239 supervisory examination readiness.

Adoption

The AI agent produces monthly lineage gap reports covering 100% of active ELT pipelines for ≥ 12 consecutive months within the first year; draft lineage entry generation active from go-live.

Acceptance

≥ 80% of draft lineage entries approved by data stewards without material amendment; BCBS 239 lineage coverage for regulatory reporting CDEs confirmed at ≥ 90% within 6 months of go-live.

Cycle

Lineage gap identification cycle shifted from episodic audit-triggered review to a monthly automated scan; lineage documentation turnaround from pipeline deployment reduced from weeks to ≤ 2 sprint cycles.
