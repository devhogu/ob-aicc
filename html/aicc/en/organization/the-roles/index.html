The Roles

OverviewPlace in the BankRolesWho does whatAppointmentsGrowth

AICC uses seven Roles. A Role is named for its responsibility and not for a person; one person may hold several, and a Role may have several Holders, within the rules of separation. This part states each Role in one line and the rules that keep them apart; each Role has its own page with its profile, its decisions, and its responsibilities.

1. The seven Roles

Role Does Decides
Executive Sponsor Holds the mandate and the funding; appoints the AICC Lead and names the AI Steering Committee; approves the Quarterly Report and decides whether to bring it to the Board Committee or the Board The Strategic Priorities, Envelopes, Guardrails, and the mix of Initiatives; the AI Risk Appetite Statement; a business case above a guardrail, across Domains, or for enabling work, and the decision after its MVP; the release of a Risk Tier 3 Solution; a risk beyond appetite
AICC Lead Leads AICC as its lead engineer and architect; accountable for the Operating Model and for every document and Record; the product owner while the Team has up to three people; prepares the Quarterly Report Taking an item into discovery, deferring or rejecting it; pulling an Initiative and the limit on the Active Initiatives; the rank of the backlogs; the Risk Tier; the final acceptance of the Team; whether a change needs a new check
Solution Engineer Owns a Solution end to end: designs, builds, deploys, and runs it with the Domains; keeps the work visible; coaches Domain Experts; checks the work of others How a Solution is designed and built; the approval of Features at Iteration Planning; the order of the work within the ranking
Domain Owner Owns the results of AI adoption in the Domain; as the requester, states the value and judges the working Solution; names the Domain Expert Whether the Domain takes part; the business case within the Domain and below a guardrail and the decision after its MVP; the use of a Solution for a data class; the acceptance, the release of Risk Tier 1 and 2, and the retirement of a Solution
Domain Expert Explains the routine work; works with the Solution Engineer; tries the Solution in real work; then scales adoption and trains colleagues Nothing on funding, acceptance, or control
Control Function Contact Advises on requirements; may raise the Risk Tier within its remit, and the Contact of model risk alone may lower it; clears a business case that expects Risk Tier 2 or 3; validates; may suspend or stop Within its remit, finally: clearance, validation, Exceptions, stop
Platform Owner Provides and operates the AI Platform, outside AICC, to the requirements in the Standards Record; keeps the evidence, logs, and traces on which the validation relies The design of the AI Platform within those requirements

¶1.1. The team also agrees who takes the Hats that the work needs, such as the keeper of the Program Backlog, the facilitator of the events, or the coach. A Hat is not a Role: it changes when the team decides and needs no appointment.

2. The rules of separation

¶2.1. No person validates or checks work that the person built. The Domain Owner of a Solution accepts it as the requester and releases it for Risk Tier 1 and 2, and does not validate it. A Control Function Contact is not a member of AICC and does not build Solutions that the Contact reviews. The AICC Lead may build a Solution, and then does not check, validate, release, or give the business acceptance of it; the Executive Sponsor approves its Solution Definition, assigns its Risk Tier, and approves its use for a data class, and, where the AICC Lead is also its Domain Owner, gives its business acceptance and releases it. Until AICC has a second Solution Engineer, the check by a person other than the builder is done by an engineer of the IT function or the Domain whom the AICC Lead names. Internal audit gives assurance only and does not validate, release, or stop.

¶2.2. While the Team is small, one person holds several Roles within these rules. Two combinations are accepted as limits, each listed in the Risks and Issues with its compensating controls: the AICC Lead issues the Service Agreement, delivers, and writes the Outcome Report; and the AICC Lead gives the acceptance of the Features and the final acceptance of the Team for a Solution the AICC Lead built. The compensating controls are the test by another person, the check or validation by another, the acceptance of the Domain Owner, the release decision, and the monthly sample of the Decisions of the AICC Lead by the Executive Sponsor.

3. Rule source

Operating Model 4.1 to 4.5; the Organization guide 3; the Role pages of this section.
