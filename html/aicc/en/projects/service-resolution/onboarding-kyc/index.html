Candidate Project Charter — Onboarding/KYC Progress Support

Status: candidate for selection and completion

Parent proposal: Customer Intelligence–Enabled Service Resolution

Technical profile: Onboarding/KYC Progress Support

Parent proposal version/reference at approval: [document version or commit]

Decision requested

Approve a controlled employee-assist pilot for one customer segment, onboarding path, and support queue. Authorize the named business team and capacity to establish the baseline, complete control decisions, operate the pilot, and return with evidence for scale, refinement, or closure.

Project intent

Help customers progress stalled onboarding or KYC cases through one accurate explanation of current service state, outstanding customer information, responsible queue, and approved next step, without exposing or interpreting restricted financial-crime information.

Common delivery commitments

This charter incorporates the parent proposal's delivery workflow, process integration, governance and RACI, control coverage, operational workflow, dependencies, and continuing-ownership requirements. The journey-specific boundaries and acceptance conditions below apply where they are more restrictive. A material change to customer cohort, onboarding path, data purpose, information boundary, user, channel, decision authority, or automation level requires reassessment and approval.

Problem and selected scope

Included: one customer segment and onboarding path; customer-service metadata consisting of case state, completion status of required checks, document type and receipt/validation status, approved customer-facing reason code, responsible queue, and prior approved communications.

Information boundary: the pilot may explain only an established service state and the next customer or servicing action permitted by the Journey Process Owner. It does not infer or expose the reason for a regulated assessment.

Excluded: identity-document contents or images; analyst free text; customer risk scores; screening matches or hits; suspicious-activity reports or financial-crime intelligence; KYC/AML decisioning; customer risk-rating changes; onboarding approval or rejection; bypass of required checks; and customer communication without employee approval.

Evidence and control-limit schedule required before live use

The completed charter records eligibility and exclusions, metric formulas and denominators, baseline/comparison design, observation period, state and exception coverage, live-workflow sample, and outcome sample or directional-evidence rationale. Each control limit records its metric, source, owner, baseline-informed tolerance, review frequency, and suspension threshold. Outcome reporting must also test for materially different treatment across relevant customer cohorts where permitted and meaningful.

Acceptance rule

The Sponsor may approve scale when the primary customer outcome and journey-progression or operational-benefit target meet their confirmed levels, all mandatory quality and control-limit criteria pass, the Onboarding/KYC Process Owner accepts the case-state and next-step interpretation, Financial Crime accepts the information boundary, and the Measurement Owner confirms that the evidence supports that decision.

Directional evidence may authorize only a defined extension or further measurement. Refine applies when quality and controls pass but benefit evidence is inconclusive. Stop applies when a required control could be bypassed, restricted information could be disclosed, service state cannot be represented reliably, or customers could receive inconsistent treatment.

Risk, compliance, conduct, and privacy position

  • Employee-assistive use remains within the existing onboarding-support process; the employee verifies the evidence and owns the response.
  • Service state, permitted metadata, and next steps remain grounded in authoritative records and approved customer-facing procedures.
  • The approved purpose, minimum necessary fields, restricted-field exclusions, and role access are recorded before use.
  • Customer communication, transparency, accessibility, and vulnerable-customer handling follow approved onboarding rules.
  • Recommendations, source evidence, employee decisions, corrections, and outcomes are traceable and retained under an approved rule.
  • Incidents, suspension authority, restart approval, and material-change triggers are agreed before live use.
  • KYC/AML assessment, customer risk rating, approval, rejection, and regulatory decisions remain in existing governed processes.
  • Inconsistent state, potential discrimination, vulnerability, or financial-crime concern routes to a specialist without exposing restricted rationale.
