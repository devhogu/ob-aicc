Customer Intelligence–Enabled Service Resolution — Platform Capability and Readiness Map

Purpose

Define the shared IT capabilities required to implement the Technical Pilot Blueprint and its journey technical profiles, without assuming the bank's current products, maturity, deployment model, or interface availability.

Vendor and low-level runtime selection follows bank capability discovery. The project first determines whether each required capability can be reused, extended, supplied as a controlled pilot component, or cannot safely support the proposed scope.

How the bank assesses each capability

Every capability receives one recorded disposition:

  • Reuse: an existing supported bank service satisfies the pilot need.
  • Extend: an existing service can satisfy it through a limited enhancement.
  • Pilot-local: a temporary implementation is permitted behind the intended enterprise contract.
  • Narrow/block: the journey or live mode is reduced because a safe dependency cannot be supplied.

Decisions required before solution commitment

The bank does not need every target platform capability before the pilot. It must make the following decisions explicitly:

Decision Required record
Selected journey and mode historical, silent evaluation, employee-assistive live, customer communication, or later limited-authority action
Capability disposition reuse, extend, pilot-local, or narrow/block for every capability above
Model deployment position approved managed route, existing internal endpoint, or separately justified on-prem serving
Workflow and AI-assistant implementation bank-supported durable workflow option, lightweight orchestration approach and framework-independent contracts
Integration approach per source API, façade, event, governed extract or unavailable; owner and supported purpose
Data and knowledge boundary allow-listed fields and sources, explicit exclusions, classification, retention and access
Tool and action authority reads, writes, employee confirmation, limits, prohibited actions and manual fallback
Environment and service position environments, network zone, identity, approximate workload, service expectation, support and suspension
Evidence position historical coverage, locked acceptance set, live sample, outcome comparison and required trace
Continuing ownership journey service owner and owner of each shared or temporary component

Product and framework choices are documented as architecture decisions after discovery. They must satisfy the contracts in the common blueprint and remain replaceable where the market or bank strategy is unsettled.

Minimum technical readiness gates

Architecture readiness

  • Selected journey profile is completed and consistent with its business charter.
  • Capability dispositions, enterprise interfaces, temporary components and owners are recorded.
  • Journey-specific logic is separated from shared platform capabilities.

Data and integration readiness

  • Authoritative sources, identifiers, status semantics and supported integration paths are confirmed.
  • Context and knowledge allow-lists, exclusions, freshness and quality behavior are tested.
  • Historical evaluation data and outcome measurement can be produced for the selected scope.

Control readiness

  • Identity, purpose, field access, model route, retrieval, output and tool decisions are enforced and evidenced.
  • Journey-specific prohibited data, decisions and actions are technically blocked.
  • Threat, privacy, conduct, incident, suspension and material-change decisions are recorded.

Operational readiness

  • Environments, deployment, secrets, telemetry, dependency monitoring, fallback and support are exercised.
  • The existing manual service remains available when AI, knowledge or source dependencies fail.
  • Workload and model observations are sufficient for controlled-live capacity, without implying premature scale hardware.

Quality readiness

  • Common states and material exceptions pass the approved historical and silent evaluation.
  • Critical errors defined by the selected profile do not pass the release gate.
  • Employee workflow, correction, escalation and outcome capture operate end to end.

CTO decision on reusable platform capabilities

Promotion requires evidence of a real second consumer, stable interface, named product owner, independent service expectation, support, cost and lifecycle. The CTO receives:

  • current bank capabilities successfully reused;
  • gaps filled temporarily by the pilot;
  • components that remain journey-specific;
  • shared capabilities ready for productization;
  • quality, security, resilience, cost and portability evidence;
  • target owners and an ordered investment recommendation.

This makes the pilot a deliberate first platform consumer and evidence generator. It does not assume that every temporary component deserves to become part of the long-term platform.
