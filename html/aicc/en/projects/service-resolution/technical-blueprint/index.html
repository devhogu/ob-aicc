Customer Intelligence–Enabled Service Resolution — Technical Pilot Blueprint

Technical proposition

The bank implements one controlled service-resolution journey across authoritative banking systems, purpose-bound customer context, governed GenAI interpretation, employee-approved action, and measured customer outcome.

The business proposal and delivery model fixes the customer outcome, ownership and operating boundary. A common technical architecture supplies customer context, approved knowledge, AI-assisted workflow, policy enforcement, evidence and the route to shared platform capabilities. The journey technical profiles apply the specific system, information and action boundaries for Payment Issue Resolution, Card Dispute Progress Support, and Onboarding/KYC Progress Support. The platform capability and readiness map determines which bank capabilities can be reused, extended, supplied locally or must narrow the pilot.

The recommended first implementation is a Payment Issue Resolution Workspace supported by a Limited-Authority Service Resolution Assistant. Payment issue resolution provides a relatively clear authoritative state, material service volume, measurable repeat contact, and a controlled decision boundary. The same architecture can later be configured for disputes, onboarding/KYC progress, and other service journeys with their respective information and action limits.

Executive visual — from banking facts to reusable platform

The pilot creates customer value on the solid line. The dotted lines show which assets can become shared platform capabilities after reuse is demonstrated.

What the pilot must demonstrate

The technical pilot must demonstrate that the bank can:

  1. assemble an authorized, source-linked view of one customer-service problem from several banking systems;
  2. use GenAI to interpret fragmented interaction history and approved knowledge more effectively than the existing workflow and a data-only alternative;
  3. constrain the AI assistant to a defined workflow, approved tools, and explicit employee decision points;
  4. deliver a customer-visible service benefit through the existing servicing process;
  5. replay the complete path from source records through model interpretation, employee action, and customer outcome;
  6. retain reusable customer-context, knowledge, evaluation, tool, control, and outcome components for later journeys.

Where GenAI adds value

GenAI is applied to work that is difficult to express as stable deterministic rules:

  • interpret fragmented contact notes, chats, cases, and complaints;
  • reconstruct a concise customer-issue timeline;
  • distinguish the current need from earlier related requests;
  • identify and explain a likely unresolved-service blocker;
  • retrieve and apply the relevant approved procedure;
  • propose a permitted next service action;
  • create a clear, source-grounded explanation for employee review.

GenAI does not establish customer identity, payment status, eligibility, deadlines, permissions, or transaction outcome. Those remain facts and decisions supplied by authoritative banking systems and approved policy.

Target pilot architecture

The architecture separates five responsibilities:

  1. Banking systems provide authoritative facts and execute governed banking processes.
  2. The Customer Context Service creates the minimum authorized view required for this journey.
  3. The Service Resolution Assistant interprets the context and knowledge within a controlled, limited-authority workflow.
  4. The Service Resolution Workspace keeps the employee accountable for communication and action.
  5. The evidence and outcome path demonstrates benefit and contributes reusable Customer Intelligence.

Enterprise placement and pilot isolation

The pilot is isolated operationally but integrated architecturally. It receives a dedicated workload identity, data purpose, deployment boundary, source allow-list, knowledge corpus, tool allow-list, evaluation set, telemetry stream, and suspension control. It integrates through bank-owned contracts that can survive replacement of the pilot implementation.

This avoids two opposite risks: coupling an experiment directly to core systems, and producing a disconnected demonstration that cannot enter normal service.

The enterprise boundary is:

Service Resolution journey application
  consumes limited-authority AI-assistant and shared AI platform capabilities
  consumes purpose-bound Customer Intelligence context
  invokes registered banking APIs and workflows

AI and agentic platforms
  provide model, retrieval, orchestration, policy, tool and evidence primitives
  do not own journey logic, customer facts or regulated banking decisions

Banking, data and identity platforms
  remain systems of record and enterprise trust roots

The pilot may implement a missing capability locally behind the target interface. A local component becomes a shared platform capability only when it has another committed consumer, a named product owner, independent service expectations, funding, support, and a lifecycle. This enables validation without prematurely creating a large platform estate.

Integration principles

  • Use versioned service and event contracts so model, agent framework, UI, and source adapters can be replaced independently.
  • Keep journey-specific process interpretation in the service resolution solution; extract only genuinely reusable capabilities into platforms.
  • Access core systems through existing APIs or controlled façades. Use event streams or CDC for history and outcome propagation where supported.
  • Use governed snapshots for historical development and evaluation, not as an unmonitored live source of truth.
  • Avoid direct model access to core databases, production credentials, message buses, or unrestricted enterprise search.
  • Preserve authoritative source references through context assembly, model interpretation, employee action, and outcome reporting.

Banking systems and information required

Every journey uses the following banking capability set. The actual systems, fields, interfaces, and restrictions are completed in the selected journey technical profile.

Banking capability Information used Authority retained by the banking capability
Customer master or CRM customer identity, servicing relationship, segment and relevant preferences customer identity and relationship record
Selected journey system journey identifier, business object, timestamps, authoritative status and approved state/reason codes payment, dispute, onboarding or other journey state
Case or workflow system investigation state, responsible queue, outstanding step, actions and resolution operational case state and process execution
Contact platforms previous contacts, contact reasons, messages or permitted transcripts source interaction record
Complaints system complaint, escalation and resolution state formal complaint record and obligations

Only fields required for the selected journey and service resolution workflow are provided. The model receives purpose-bound context through controlled services rather than unrestricted access to core systems.

Integration approach

The pilot uses two information paths:

  • Historical evaluation path: governed extracts from the relevant systems are normalized into reviewed historical cases for development, evaluation, and baseline measurement.
  • Live servicing path: controlled read APIs or adapters retrieve current authoritative state when an eligible case is opened. Event feeds may supply recent contacts and outcome changes where they already exist.

Every value provided to the workspace carries a source reference and freshness timestamp. Missing, stale, conflicting, or incorrectly linked information is surfaced as an exception and follows the existing manual investigation route.

Purpose-bound Customer Context Service

The Customer Context Service is the first concrete Customer Intelligence component. It:

  • links the customer, selected journey event or application, open case, and related contacts using stable bank identifiers;
  • normalizes records into a common service-event timeline;
  • distinguishes authoritative facts, calculated values, and GenAI inferences;
  • applies purpose, role, field, and retention restrictions;
  • provides source, timestamp, freshness, and ownership metadata;
  • produces a reproducible context snapshot for every model interpretation and employee action;
  • publishes the recorded action and outcome back into the customer-service event history.

For the pilot, this is a journey-specific context service rather than an enterprise customer 360. Its identity, event, provenance, and outcome contracts should be designed for reuse by later customer-intelligence journeys.

Minimum event structure

Each normalized service event should contain:

event identifier
customer and journey identifiers
source system and source record
event type and business timestamp
authoritative status or approved reason code
responsible process owner or queue
purpose and access classification
freshness and ingestion timestamp

Inferences are stored separately with their evidence, model and prompt version, confidence, purpose, and expiry. They do not become authoritative customer facts.

Limited-Authority Service Resolution Assistant

The Service Resolution Assistant is implemented as a controlled workflow rather than an open-ended autonomous loop.

The workflow controller enforces:

  • a fixed sequence and maximum number of steps;
  • an allow-listed set of read and write tools;
  • structured request and response schemas;
  • per-case time, model-call, and cost limits;
  • source and citation validation;
  • explicit separation of facts and inferences;
  • abstention when evidence is insufficient or conflicting;
  • a manual fallback at every material failure point;
  • employee approval before customer communication or operational action.

The pilot uses request-scoped workflow state. Durable customer memory belongs to the governed Customer Intelligence layer rather than free-form agent memory.

Software and agentic realization

The architectural requirement is a durable, observable state machine with typed tools and policy checkpoints. The implementation may use an established workflow engine combined with an agent framework, or an agent framework that supplies equivalent durable execution. Framework choice is subordinate to the following contracts:

  • explicit workflow states and transitions rather than an unrestricted reasoning loop;
  • structured model outputs validated against versioned schemas;
  • resumable execution around employee approval and external-system latency;
  • deterministic retry, timeout, idempotency and compensation behavior;
  • model, prompt, retrieval, tool and policy versions attached to the case trace;
  • replay of historical trajectories against a changed component;
  • framework-independent tool and context interfaces.

The recommended software split is:

Concern Architectural choice for the pilot
durable execution a bank-supported workflow/state-machine runtime owns pauses, employee approval, retry, timeout and recovery
LLM orchestration a lightweight orchestration component operates inside defined workflow states and communicates only through the model gateway and registered tools
customer context a separate typed service supplies authorized facts and snapshots; it is not agent memory
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) a separate retrieval service indexes approved journey knowledge, applies access/effective-date filters and returns citations
tools versioned bank API contracts are canonical; an MCP adapter is optional at the agent boundary
authorization the bank policy service and tool broker decide access and action authority; the agent framework does not
evaluation a framework-independent harness replays the same cases across model, prompt, RAG and orchestrator changes
observability enterprise telemetry receives a common execution-trace schema independent of the chosen orchestration library

The main functional services compose as follows:

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is used for approved procedures and communication guidance. Customer facts are obtained from the Customer Context Service rather than a vector index. Retrieval must inherit document access, effective dates, ownership and withdrawal state, and the generated output must cite the retrieved source.

MCP may be used as a tool-description and invocation adapter when it fits the bank's engineering standards. It is not the authorization boundary. The bank's tool broker still owns workload identity, delegated employee/customer context, policy, credentials, confirmation, rate limits and audit. Direct MCP connections from a model or desktop to core banking systems are outside the target architecture.

Multi-agent collaboration and A2A are unnecessary for the first service resolution journey. Separate specialist agents should be introduced only when an independently owned capability has a distinct context, tool authority, lifecycle and evaluation requirement. The initial workflow is clearer and safer as one orchestrator calling deterministic services and limited model functions.

Registered tools and authority

The initial tool catalogue can contain:

Tool Authority Pilot use
get_customer_service_context read retrieve the authorized customer and journey context
get_journey_status read obtain the current authoritative state permitted by the selected journey profile
get_related_contacts read retrieve permitted related contacts and prior communication
get_open_case read obtain case stage, owner and outstanding service step
get_applicable_procedure read retrieve effective approved procedure and communication guidance
get_permitted_actions read return actions approved for the verified process state
record_employee_feedback constrained write record acceptance, correction, rejection or escalation reason
create_specialist_referral constrained write with employee approval open or update an existing governed referral workflow
draft_case_note draft only produce a structured note for employee review
record_service_outcome constrained write record the resulting service state and measurement event

The pilot does not expose tools that move or reverse money, change an authoritative journey state, make a regulated decision, determine reimbursement, alter fraud/AML/sanctions state, or send an unreviewed customer message. Each journey profile adds its more restrictive exclusions.

For every tool call, the tool broker verifies workload and employee identity, customer context, declared purpose, scope, input constraints, required confirmation, and audit obligations. Write operations use the existing system's validation, idempotency, authorization, and recordkeeping controls.

Service Resolution Workspace

The employee experience should be a side panel within, or closely coupled to, the existing service desktop. It presents:

  1. Customer request — the need currently being handled.
  2. What happened — a concise evidence-linked timeline.
  3. Verified current state — from the authoritative journey system.
  4. Likely blocker — visibly marked as an inference with its evidence.
  5. Applicable procedure — effective approved source and relevant section.
  6. Permitted next step — selected from the Journey Process Owner's action catalogue.
  7. Suggested customer explanation — grounded draft for employee approval.
  8. Source evidence — deep links to the underlying records.
  9. Employee decision — accept, correct, reject, investigate, or escalate.

The workspace should avoid an unconstrained general-chat experience. The task-oriented view makes the source evidence, action boundary, corrections, and customer outcome observable.

Existing enterprise model providers, search, IAM, logging, deployment, security-monitoring, and data-platform capabilities should be reused where fit. The bank must own the customer-context contract, banking adapters, action catalogue, policy rules, evaluation cases, knowledge authority, tool contracts, and outcome definitions.

The gateway contract should permit either an approved managed model or an in-bank model-serving endpoint. A broader on-prem LLM infrastructure programme is therefore an explicit dependency choice rather than a prerequisite embedded into this pilot.

Deployment and infrastructure position

Agents do not inherently require GPU infrastructure. GPU demand comes from embedding, reranking, and foundation-model inference choices. The workspace, orchestrator, tool broker, context service, policy enforcement, evaluation control plane, and most integration services run on normal application compute.

Three deployment positions are viable:

Position Pilot infrastructure implication Long-term implication
Approved managed model through the bank gateway no dedicated inference GPU; deploy application and data services in the bank environment and enforce approved network/egress routes fastest validation while retaining provider portability through the gateway
Existing internal model-serving platform consume its authenticated endpoint and declared service levels validates whether the current platform meets quality, latency, capacity and control needs
New on-prem model serving provide a separate L1 inference service with GPU scheduling, model registry, scaling and support justified only where data, residency, economics, latency or strategic control require it

For a new on-prem path, hardware cannot be responsibly selected from the word “agent.” Capacity depends on model size and quantization, context length, tokens generated, concurrent cases, latency objective, availability design, embedding/reranking load, and growth reserve. A single inference node may support laboratory evaluation. Controlled live service normally requires failure isolation and redundant serving capacity. High-bandwidth GPU interconnect is needed only when the selected model or throughput profile spans several GPUs; it is not a general requirement of agent orchestration.

The pilot therefore supplies Infra and SRE with measured request rate, token profile, concurrency, latency, storage, retention, and availability observations before a scale hardware commitment.

Operational and SRE expectations

  • separate development, evaluation, and controlled-live environments;
  • infrastructure as code, secrets management and workload identity;
  • dependency health for banking APIs, gateway, model, retrieval, policy and tool broker;
  • end-to-end correlation from employee request to source reads, model calls, tool actions and outcome;
  • service-level indicators for availability, latency, error, fallback, queue age and cost;
  • graceful degradation to context-only or existing manual service when AI dependencies fail;
  • progressive release, rollback, model-route failover and tested suspension controls;
  • capacity, cost and incident ownership for every shared component.

Engineering, CI/CD and test strategy

Application code, infrastructure, prompts, schemas, policies, retrieval configuration, knowledge versions and evaluation datasets are versioned release inputs. The delivery pipeline should run the appropriate evidence for each change:

Test layer Required acceptance evidence
Software unit and component deterministic rules, schemas, redaction, policy and workflow transitions behave correctly
API and contract source, context, tool and outcome contracts remain compatible and enforce authorization
Data quality identifiers, status mappings, completeness, freshness, exclusions and quarantine rules pass
Retrieval correct effective source is returned; stale, conflicting, restricted and irrelevant sources are handled
Model evaluation factual support, case interpretation, next-step quality, abstention and communication quality meet the charter threshold
Agent execution path allowed paths, maximum steps, approvals, retries, timeouts, duplicate prevention and tool denial behave correctly
Security and adversarial prompt injection, data exfiltration, cross-customer access, malicious documents and tool manipulation are resisted
Resilience and performance dependency failure, degraded mode, recovery, capacity, latency and suspension operate as designed
Business acceptance service SMEs validate representative states and exceptions; employees can correct and use the result

Release progression is blocked by failed mandatory evaluation or policy tests. Controlled-live deployment uses a small eligible cohort, observable feature flags, and component-level rollback. A changed model, prompt, retrieval corpus, status mapping, tool, policy or workflow runs through its defined regression scope before promotion.

Data and evaluation assets

The Data and Test Strategy teams provide three deliberately separated assets:

  1. Development cases for exploration, taxonomy and workflow refinement.
  2. Locked acceptance cases unseen during development and owned by the Measurement Owner and Journey Process Owner.
  3. Live monitoring samples drawn from eligible production cases under the approved purpose and review process.

Historical cases should be pseudonymized or consistently tokenized across source systems so that customer, journey event or application, contact and case relationships remain realistic while direct identifiers are removed. Dates and values may be shifted or transformed only where process order, state transitions, thresholds and exception behavior remain valid. Synthetic cases can extend rare and adversarial coverage but do not replace real representative cases for benefit or operational acceptance.

Each accepted case records the source state, expected interpretation, permitted action, relevant procedure, material exclusions and SME rationale. Access to any re-identification mapping remains outside the model and ordinary development environment.

Each contributing team should be asked for a named owner, agreed interface or deliverable, supported environment, service expectation, constraint, and fallback. The project should avoid asking platform teams for an undefined “AI environment” or unrestricted source access.

AI Policy and Security Enforcement

The AI policy and enforcement layer is distributed through explicit policy decision and enforcement points:

Security and compliance depend on the following capabilities rather than on prompt instructions alone:

  • workload identity and employee/customer delegation propagated end to end;
  • least-privilege source and tool access with short-lived credentials;
  • field-level minimization before model invocation;
  • encrypted transport and storage with approved residency and retention;
  • provider terms and configuration preventing bank data from being used for model training;
  • protection against instructions embedded in contacts, documents or retrieved knowledge;
  • separation of retrieved content from system instructions and tool authority;
  • output schema, source, sensitive-data and prohibited-action validation;
  • immutable or protected decision and action evidence with privacy-aware content logging;
  • cross-customer isolation tests, access review, incident route and service suspension controls.

The policy service returns an enforceable decision and reason. The context service, AI gateway, retrieval service, orchestrator, tool broker and communication path enforce that decision and emit evidence. This is the pilot realization of the bank's wider AI policy layer.

How the pilot isolates GenAI benefit

A simple before-and-after comparison would combine several effects and would not show whether GenAI itself created value. The pilot should compare three operating modes:

Mode Employee receives Question answered
Existing process current systems and procedures what is the business baseline?
Context-only workspace linked facts, deterministic timeline and source links without generated interpretation how much benefit comes from data consolidation?
GenAI-assisted workspace context plus summary, blocker interpretation, retrieved procedure, proposed next step and explanation what incremental benefit comes from GenAI?

The comparison begins with blinded historical evaluation, proceeds through silent evaluation alongside the existing process without influencing customers, and then uses controlled live populations where operationally feasible.

Measures are separated by component:

  • time required to understand the case;
  • correctness of current-state interpretation;
  • correctness of the selected next step;
  • unsupported or materially misleading statements;
  • employee corrections and rejected recommendations;
  • handling and investigation effort;
  • repeat contact and case progression;
  • customer complaints and adverse outcomes;
  • technical availability, latency, failures and fallback;
  • cost per processed case and per successfully resolved case.

If the context-only workspace produces most of the improvement, the result supports investment in customer-context integration while limiting additional GenAI investment. GenAI is justified by measurable incremental improvement in interpretation, knowledge use, explanation quality, employee effort, or adaptability to additional journeys.

Technical validation progression

The solution increases exposure only after the preceding behavior is demonstrated:

  1. Historical case reconstruction and blinded SME evaluation.
  2. Customer Context Service and deterministic timeline.
  3. Governed knowledge retrieval.
  4. Structured GenAI interpretation and proposed action.
  5. Evidence validation, policy tests, and regression gates.
  6. Employee workspace in silent evaluation alongside the existing process.
  7. Controlled live employee assistance.
  8. Employee-approved customer communication through an existing channel.
  9. Authenticated read-only customer self-service after service-state and explanation quality are established.
  10. Limited-authority customer actions only after separate tool, policy, authentication, and customer-confirmation approval.

This progression separates permission to mobilize and build from permission to use live customer information, influence communication, or execute an action.

Technical validation criteria

The completed pilot should demonstrate:

  • correct customer, journey object or event, and case linkage or explicit quarantine;
  • authoritative status never replaced by model inference;
  • facts, calculations, and inferences remain visibly distinct;
  • every material displayed fact and proposed action links to its source and version;
  • no prohibited tool or action can be invoked through the AI-assisted workflow;
  • low-confidence, stale, conflicting, and unavailable-source conditions use the manual fallback;
  • historical and live evaluations can be rerun against a new model, prompt, knowledge, or policy version;
  • every live case can be replayed from source context through employee decision and outcome;
  • model, retrieval, orchestration, and tool failures can be isolated and suspended;
  • the GenAI-assisted mode provides measurable incremental value over the context-only mode;
  • cost and operational performance are compatible with the intended service workflow;
  • components produced by the pilot have named continuing technical and business owners.

Journey-specific numerical thresholds remain in the approved project charter and its evidence-and-control-limit schedule.

The pilot is designed to become the first consumer and proving ground of a shared platform, while keeping the journey solution replaceable. Scale occurs by extracting stable, reused capabilities rather than expanding the pilot application into an enterprise monolith.

What the future platform covers

The resulting platform gives delivery teams a supported way to:

  • register and classify an AI-enabled journey;
  • obtain an approved model route;
  • connect governed knowledge and purpose-bound context;
  • build and evaluate a limited-authority assistant workflow;
  • invoke banking tools with scoped delegated authority;
  • inherit security, policy, evidence, deployment and monitoring controls;
  • measure quality, cost and customer outcome;
  • change models or frameworks without rewriting the banking integration.

It does not replace core banking systems, enterprise IAM, API management, the data platform, business workflow systems, regulated decision engines, or journey ownership. It composes those capabilities into a safe AI delivery path.

CTO scale decision

At the end of the pilot, the CTO should receive a capability assessment rather than only a use-case demonstration:

  • which existing enterprise capabilities were successfully reused;
  • which local pilot components should remain journey-specific;
  • which components have a committed second consumer and should become shared platform capabilities;
  • quality, latency, resilience, security and unit-cost evidence for each layer;
  • vendor and framework dependencies, portability and exit constraints;
  • source-system and API gaps that limit additional journeys;
  • the proposed platform product owners, service expectations, operating cost and investment sequence.

This makes the scale decision explicit: reuse the first end-to-end journey, adopt validated shared capabilities, replace temporary components behind stable contracts, and retire pilot-only infrastructure when its function is available on the supported implementation path.

Reusable outcome and strategic fit

The pilot produces more than a single assistant:

  • the first purpose-bound customer-context interface;
  • a common customer-service event model;
  • a governed journey-knowledge product;
  • bank-owned historical evaluation and regression cases;
  • a controlled model and prompt route;
  • a limited-authority assistant-orchestration design;
  • a delegated banking-tool authorization design;
  • an end-to-end evidence trace;
  • an outcome-event loop feeding Customer Intelligence.

The resulting separation is deliberate:

  • Banking systems remain authoritative for customers, accounts, payments, cases, complaints, and regulated decisions.
  • Customer Intelligence provides purpose-bound context, service events, derived understanding, and outcome history.
  • The AI platform provides governed model access, knowledge retrieval, evaluation, AI safety controls, registries, and observability.
  • The AI workflow and tool-use layer provides controlled workflow state, tool use, delegated authority, checkpoints, and execution evidence.
  • The journey application owns the employee experience, business logic, and customer-service outcome.

This end-to-end pilot gives IT a concrete solution to estimate and challenge while establishing reusable foundations for broader customer intelligence and future authenticated self-service.
