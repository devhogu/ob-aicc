Business case

Customer Intelligence–Enabled Service Resolution

Use-case intent

Help a service team resolve repeat or stalled customer requests by reconstructing what happened, identifying the likely blocker, and presenting the appropriate next step.

The use case creates a first practical customer-intelligence asset from fragmented service history. It provides an immediate customer benefit through faster and more consistent resolution.

The corresponding banking-systems, GenAI, agentic, and platform implementation is defined in the Technical Pilot Blueprint.

Problem

A customer contacts the bank several times about the same issue. Calls, chats, cases, complaints, and status changes sit in separate records. The employee must reconstruct the history manually and may miss the actual blocker, repeat an earlier request, or transfer the customer again.

Pilot scope

  • One journey with meaningful repeat-contact or unresolved-case volume.
  • One service team.
  • Recent contact, case, complaint, and status history for that journey.
  • An internal employee view.
  • Employee approval before any customer communication or operational action.

Journey selection and expected benefit

The project selects one journey from a short business-owned candidate list. The entries below are starting points for discussion with Customer Service and the relevant process owner.

Candidate journey Pilot service team Customer problem Primary outcome to test Underlying process owner
Payment issue Payments servicing/contact team customer repeats contact because a failed, pending, or reversed payment and its ownership are unclear fewer repeat contacts and faster confirmed resolution Payments Operations
Card dispute progress Cards or disputes servicing team customer cannot understand progress, evidence needs, or next step fewer status contacts and lower unresolved-case age Disputes Operations
Onboarding/KYC progress Onboarding support team application stalls across document, verification, and review steps fewer repeated information requests and faster progression Customer Onboarding/KYC
Loan application status Lending application support team customer receives fragmented status and is transferred between teams fewer handoffs and clearer committed next step Lending Operations
Complaint resolution and remediation Complaints or specialist resolution team prior contacts have not produced a complete response or durable resolution fewer reopened complaints and stronger resolution quality Complaints owner with affected product operations

Selection should favor high repeat-contact volume, clear process ownership, accessible records, an established resolution process, and a customer outcome that can be measured from existing management information.

Selectable project charters

Three candidate charters apply the common proposal to journey-specific workflows, benefits, risks, and acceptance criteria:

  • Payment Issue Resolution
  • Card Dispute Progress Support
  • Onboarding/KYC Progress Support

Common charter selection and evidence rules apply to all three charters.

Benefit baseline

Before approval, the Benefit Owner and Measurement Owner establish a baseline for the selected journey. The Benefit Owner is accountable for realizing the customer and operational outcome. The Measurement Owner independently defines, calculates, and reports the evidence used to judge it.

Baseline dimension Measures to establish
Customer demand total contacts, repeat-contact rate, contacts per case, reopened cases
Customer resolution first-contact resolution, time to resolution, unresolved-case age, abandoned cases
Customer experience complaints, dissatisfaction, escalations, available satisfaction measures
Operational effort investigation/handling time, handoffs, transfers, manual system checks, rework
Process failure missing information, incorrect routing, unclear ownership, failed step, status inconsistency
Economics cost per contact or case where reliable data exists

The charter selects one primary customer outcome, one operational benefit, and the customer-protection and conduct limits that must remain stable. It records the baseline source, calculation, owner, and target. A useful default is repeat-contact reduction as the primary outcome and investigation effort as the operational benefit.

Business-domain placement

The project sits in the bank's customer-servicing chain:

Customer Service owns the customer interaction, service level, communication, and resolution experience. The selected product-operations function owns the underlying journey and the process steps required to resolve the issue.

Examples of the required pairing are:

Selected journey Customer-facing owner Underlying process owner
payment issue or investigation Head of Customer Service Payments Operations process owner
card servicing or dispute progress Head of Customer Service Cards Operations or Disputes process owner
onboarding/KYC progress Head of Customer Service Customer Onboarding/KYC process owner
loan application status Head of Customer Service Lending Operations process owner
complaint resolution and remediation Head of Customer Service Complaints process owner and affected product-operations owner

The Head of Customer Service is the natural business sponsor. The selected journey process owner is accountable for the correctness of diagnosis, actions, routing, and resolution. Data and delivery specialists build the supporting capability under those business owners.

What is built

For each selected customer case, the service employee receives:

  • the customer's current request;
  • a concise timeline of previous contacts and actions;
  • current case and service status;
  • completed, pending, and failed steps;
  • the likely reason the issue remains unresolved;
  • the approved procedure and proposed next step;
  • links to the underlying records and knowledge sources.

Process integration map

The capability augments the existing servicing workflow at specific points:

Existing process step Existing work Capability introduced by this project Accountable process role

The project's process boundary starts when a relevant contact or stalled case is identified. It ends when the customer receives a resolution or explicit next step and the result is recorded. Upstream process redesign discovered through the pilot becomes a separate improvement backlog owned by the journey process owner.

Journey Process Owner definition work

The Journey Process Owner makes the common proposal specific enough to operate. They must:

  • define the journey start, completion, and unresolved states;
  • define when separate contacts belong to the same customer need;
  • map the current process, decision points, queues, ownership changes, and customer communications;
  • identify the common blockers and evidence used to recognize them;
  • approve the actions available to the service employee for each relevant state;
  • define exceptions, specialist referrals, complaints, vulnerability, fraud, and other escalation conditions;
  • define what constitutes resolution, a committed next step, an unsuccessful resolution attempt, and a reopened case;
  • approve the structured status, blocker, action, and outcome language used by the project;
  • review historical cases and confirm the correct interpretation and next action;
  • own the upstream process-improvement backlog revealed by recurring failure patterns.

The current and target process maps, action catalogue, exception map, and outcome definitions are the Process Owner's formal inputs to the project.

Execution rule

Each step must leave an owned reusable asset. The historical case set becomes evaluation data. The taxonomy becomes a common service language. The linked timeline becomes a customer-context component. The employee view becomes a reusable interaction design. The outcome record becomes the learning loop.

Project Manager initiation responsibilities

The Project Manager converts this use-case definition into the bank's project-initiation form. At minimum, they:

  • record the selected journey, team, cohort, scope, exclusions, sponsor, Process Owner, and workstream owners;
  • establish the benefit baseline, planning targets, metric definitions, evidence-sizing approach, measurement sources, Benefit Owner, and Measurement Owner;
  • ensure each customer-protection and control limit has a source, owner, tolerance, review frequency, and suspension threshold before live use;
  • confirm named participants and capacity for service, process, data, quality, control, and delivery work;
  • turn the delivery action workflow into an integrated plan and dependency map;
  • maintain the project backlog, RAID log, decision log, change control, and approval evidence;
  • establish the governance forum, reporting path, escalation route, and decision cadence;
  • coordinate historical-case readiness, employee readiness, control review, and live-use readiness;
  • ensure training, operating instructions, fallback, support, and outcome monitoring are ready before live use;
  • prepare the evidence and recommendation for scale, redesign, reuse, or closure;
  • confirm continuing ownership for every capability and process change produced by the project.

The Project Manager owns coordination and evidence completeness. The Sponsor owns the investment decision, the Journey Process Owner owns process correctness, and the Service Owner owns the customer outcome.

Project controls

  • One approved pilot charter defines the journey, cohort, customer outcome, scope boundary, owners, baseline, and acceptance criteria.
  • One integrated backlog connects process, data, technology, control, adoption, and measurement work.
  • A dependency and decision log records source access, policy interpretation, process exceptions, integration, and readiness decisions.
  • Historical-case validation precedes employee use. Employee use with full review precedes customer-visible intervention.
  • Live quality and customer-outcome review can suspend the affected feature immediately.
  • Scale requires continuing business, process, data, technology, support, and control ownership.

Operational workflow for each customer case

Actions available to the employee

The selected journey defines a short approved action list. Typical actions are:

  • explain current status and the reason for delay;
  • complete an outstanding internal service step;
  • request specific missing information once;
  • correct routing or ownership;
  • create a callback or specialist referral with full context;
  • provide a clear committed next step and expected service state;
  • escalate a complaint, vulnerability, fraud, or other specialist condition.

The use case recommends from this approved list. It does not invent process steps.

Initial risk position

The pilot is an employee-assistance capability within an existing customer-service process:

  • The employee remains accountable for interpretation, communication, and action.
  • Every customer communication and operational action receives employee approval.
  • The capability uses approved service records and authoritative journey knowledge for the selected purpose.
  • Recommendations are limited to actions approved by the Journey Process Owner.
  • Low-confidence or conflicting evidence routes the case to the existing investigation process.
  • Inferred blockers remain reviewable working hypotheses. They do not become permanent customer facts.
  • Credit, eligibility, pricing, fraud, AML, sanctions, suitability, or other regulated decisions remain outside scope.
  • The existing customer complaint, correction, escalation, and human-service routes remain available.

This position is confirmed against the bank's AI risk classification and local regulatory obligations before live employee use.

The relevant control functions review their areas before controlled live use. Their role is to confirm the operating limits and evidence requirements. The Journey Process Owner and Service Owner remain accountable for the business process and customer outcome.

Pilot acceptance

  • The selected primary customer outcome and operational benefit meet the targets recorded in the project-initiation record, or provide sufficient directional evidence for the Sponsor's stated next decision.
  • Historical validation satisfies the approved coverage plan for common states, blockers, and material exceptions; 200–500 manually reviewed cases is the normal planning range for one defined journey scope.
  • Live workflow evidence covers a representative employee group and enough eligible cases to expose usability, correction, training, and operational failure modes; at least 100 cases is the normal planning minimum.
  • Customer-outcome evidence meets the post-baseline sample and comparison design required for the claimed decision. High-volume journeys may need approximately 1,000–3,000 eligible cases in each pilot and comparison population; lower-volume journeys record why directional evidence is sufficient for a limited next step.
  • At least 80% of reviewed summaries and proposed next steps are accepted as useful by service experts.
  • Every displayed fact and status can be traced to a source record.
  • Unsupported or low-confidence conclusions are withheld or clearly marked for investigation.
  • Investigation or handling effort improves against the agreed baseline.
  • Repeat contact, unnecessary handoff, unresolved-case age, or rework improves without deterioration in complaints or customer outcomes.
  • Every employee correction, accepted action, escalation, and result is recorded.
  • No critical privacy, access, security, or customer-treatment incident occurs.

The charter records eligibility and exclusions, metric formulas and denominators, comparison method, observation period, state and exception coverage, and treatment of reopened, transferred, abandoned, and incomplete cases. Statistical significance is claimed only when the Measurement Owner confirms that volume and cohort construction support it.

Scope boundary

The pilot does not require a general customer 360, customer chatbot, autonomous customer action, transaction intelligence, or enterprise-wide knowledge base. It uses the minimum sources and procedures needed for one service journey.

The business problem, process, ownership, benefit, risk position, and initiation requirements are agreed before detailed solution architecture, platform selection, delivery service levels, and formal IT commitment.

Reuse after validation

The same delivery model can be extended to another service journey by replacing its taxonomy, source mappings, approved actions, and evaluation cases. The reusable core is:

  • customer interaction timeline;
  • service intent and unresolved-state model;
  • evidence-linked employee view;
  • governed service knowledge retrieval;
  • correction and outcome feedback;
  • controlled analysis and audit path.
