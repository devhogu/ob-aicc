Our values and principles define what the Hub stands for, how it makes choices, and how it sees work through to the result. Every project that goes through the Hub and every solution it offers follows them.

<section class="vp-mission" aria-labelledby="g-mission"><h2 id="g-mission">Mission</h2><p>To make AI a reliable everyday tool at work: to confirm its value together with the departments that work with customers, to pool our experience of using it, and to keep people accountable for every use of AI.</p></section>

<section class="vp-group" aria-labelledby="g-values"><h2 id="g-values">Values</h2><p class="vp-applies">Three values stand behind every decision: integrity, prudence, and respect for people. If speed or gain comes into conflict with them, the values come first.</p><ol class="vp-list">
<li><span><strong>Integrity</strong> — laws and rules are followed without exception.</span></li>
<li><span><strong>Prudence</strong> — the pace of adoption matches the risk tier.</span></li>
<li><span><strong>Respect for people</strong> — AI helps employees and customers, and employees learn to work with it and take part in adopting it.</span></li>
</ol></section>

<section class="vp-group" aria-labelledby="g-adoption"><h2 id="g-adoption">Adoption principles</h2><p class="vp-applies">AI is adopted where it pays off. These principles help decide where to start using AI, where to expand it, and where to wind it down, so that we invest in what works and learn from what didn't.</p><ol class="vp-list">
<li><span><strong>Value.</strong> AI is adopted where it brings measurable benefit to customers or employees; where there is no benefit, it is dropped.</span></li>
<li><span><strong>Proof through results.</strong> Every solution first goes through a pilot and is assessed against measures recorded in advance; its use can be expanded only on the basis of facts.</span></li>
<li><span><strong>Grounding in verified knowledge.</strong> AI's answers rely on up-to-date sources whose origin can be traced, and they include links to those sources.</span></li>
<li><span><strong>Reuse.</strong> Shared components, knowledge, and ready-made assets matter more than individual solutions.</span></li>
<li><span><strong>People.</strong> AI extends what employees can do. Employees are trained before they start using it, and the department's own specialists take part in adopting AI in their area.</span></li>
<li><span><strong>Common standards.</strong> Principles and minimum standards are the same for everyone; each department applies them in line with its own rules and responsibilities.</span></li>
</ol></section>

<section class="vp-group" aria-labelledby="g-application"><h2 id="g-application">Principles of use</h2><p class="vp-applies">These principles apply to any use of AI, no matter who built the solution. This is our promise to customers, employees, regulators, and auditors.</p><ol class="vp-list">
<li><span><strong>Accountability.</strong> A specific person, the business owner, is accountable for each solution, its results, and its risks. Accountability can't be shifted onto AI, and a purchased solution is held to the same standard as one built in-house.</span></li>
<li><span><strong>Fairness.</strong> AI treats customers and employees impartially and acts in the customer's interest; before release it is tested for bias, and in operation it is monitored, to the extent the risk tier sets.</span></li>
<li><span><strong>Transparency and explainability.</strong> AI's results and the decisions made with its help can be explained to customers, employees, regulators, and auditors. Anyone who deals with AI knows that they are dealing with AI.</span></li>
<li><span><strong>Privacy and data protection.</strong> AI uses only the data it needs, stores it according to the classification rules, and protects it.</span></li>
<li><span><strong>Security and reliability.</strong> AI is tested, evaluated, monitored in operation, and protected against attacks specific to AI. Important services and their suppliers have a fallback option.</span></li>
<li><span><strong>A human makes the decision.</strong> Employees oversee AI in proportion to the risk and have the right to stop it or reverse its actions. A customer has the right to reach a human and ask for a decision made with AI's help to be reviewed.</span></li>
<li><span><strong>Compliance and proportionate checks.</strong> Compliance with the law, regulatory requirements, and internal rules is confirmed before release. The depth of checks is proportionate to the risk tier, and they can't be bypassed.</span></li>
</ol></section>

<section class="vp-group" aria-labelledby="g-work"><h2 id="g-work">Working principles</h2><p class="vp-applies">This is how work through the Hub is organized: reliance on facts, fast reversible decisions, a minimum of procedure, and judgment by results.</p><ol class="vp-list">
<li><span><strong>Decide where the facts are known.</strong> The person closest to the work decides: on the spot if the rule is known in advance, or at the forum for their level.</span></li>
<li><span><strong>Keep decisions reversible.</strong> Reversible decisions are made quickly; irreversible ones follow conditions recorded in advance.</span></li>
<li><span><strong>Product pace, financial-institution reliability.</strong> Work at the pace of product development without lowering the level of checks expected in a financial institution.</span></li>
<li><span><strong>Only what's needed.</strong> Keep the steps and records that someone actually uses, and drop the rest.</span></li>
<li><span><strong>Impact over volume.</strong> The result achieved matters more than the work done, and collaboration matters more than negotiating terms. The plan may change; the goal stays.</span></li>
</ol></section>

<section class="vp-group" aria-labelledby="g-delivery"><h2 id="g-delivery">Build and rollout principles</h2><p class="vp-applies">This is how solutions are built: a working product over documentation, collaboration with the department over negotiating terms, readiness for change over the original plan, and facts over opinions.</p><ol class="vp-list">
<li><span><strong>Work in plain sight.</strong> All work is shown on cards and boards, WIP limits cap unfinished work, and new work is taken on when room frees up.</span></li>
<li><span><strong>Small steps.</strong> A hypothesis is tested on a minimum viable product (MVP), the result is measured against measures recorded in advance, and only then is the solution scaled.</span></li>
<li><span><strong>Value first.</strong> The order of work is set by value and urgency, taking effort into account.</span></li>
<li><span><strong>One rhythm.</strong> Work is planned and run in iterations and program increments with fixed dates; the plan sets the direction but doesn't fix the scope.</span></li>
<li><span><strong>Built-in quality.</strong> Checks are part of the work itself, not a separate stage after it.</span></li>
<li><span><strong>Decisions rest with those who know.</strong> The team decides how to do the work; the product manager accepts each Feature; the business owner accepts the working solution.</span></li>
<li><span><strong>Dependencies in plain sight.</strong> They are managed the same way as the work itself.</span></li>
<li><span><strong>Continuous improvement.</strong> Every iteration and every program increment ends with a retrospective and improvements.</span></li>
</ol></section>
