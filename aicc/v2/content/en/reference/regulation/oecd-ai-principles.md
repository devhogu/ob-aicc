## Briefly {#short}

The OECD AI Principles are the OECD Council's Recommendation on Artificial Intelligence, the first intergovernmental standard in this field. The OECD Council adopted it on 22 May 2019, refined its definition of an AI system in November 2023, and updated the text on 3 May 2024 to take account of [[generative-ai|generative AI]]. It is a recommendation, not a law: it is not legally binding, but the countries that adhere to it make a political commitment to follow it.

## What it means in practice {#practice}

For the organization, this is the most concise statement of what regulators and investors will expect from the use of AI. Many national AI strategies and laws build on these principles, so if our own rules are consistent with them, it is easier for us to speak the same language as regulators, partners, and investors.

For an employee, the five principles come down to a few questions worth asking before you hand work to AI:

- Who does the result help, and could it harm anyone, a customer for example?
- Are people's rights and privacy respected? If you are about to put customer data into an AI tool, first check that this is allowed, and use only the tools approved in your organization.
- Can I explain how the result was produced and tell people that AI was involved?
- Have I checked the result before using it?
- Who is responsible for the result? A person is, not the model.

## Key ideas {#ideas}

- **Five principles.** Inclusive growth, sustainable development, and well-being; human rights and democratic values, including fairness and privacy; transparency and [[explainability]]; robustness, security, and safety; accountability.
- **Trustworthy AI.** The aim of the principles is AI that is useful and can be trusted: it respects people's rights, is understandable and safe, and someone is accountable for how it works.
- **AI system.** The principles define it as a machine-based system that infers from the input it receives how to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions. The definition is broad: it covers both a credit scoring model and a chat assistant.
- **AI actors.** The principles address everyone involved in the lifecycle of an AI system: not only developers but also organizations that use other people's models. That includes a bank.
- **Five recommendations to governments.** Invest in research; foster an open environment for AI; build regulation that is consistent across countries; prepare people for changes in the labor market; cooperate internationally.

## Good to know {#details}

- The Recommendation was adopted on 22 May 2019, its definition of an AI system was updated on 8 November 2023, and the text was revised on 3 May 2024.
- In June 2019, at the Osaka Summit, G20 leaders welcomed the G20 AI Principles, drawn from this Recommendation.
- According to the OECD register of legal instruments, as of October 2026 the Recommendation has been adhered to by the 38 OECD member countries, 12 other countries, and the European Union. The Kyrgyz Republic and Kazakhstan are not on that list.
- The OECD Digital Policy Committee monitors implementation: it reports regularly to the OECD Council, and the first such report was prepared in 2024.
- There are no penalties: this is a voluntary benchmark. The official text is in English and French; the OECD publishes translations into several languages, but Russian is not among them.
