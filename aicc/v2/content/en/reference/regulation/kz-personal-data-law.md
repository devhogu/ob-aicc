## Briefly {#short}

The Law of the Republic of Kazakhstan “On Personal Data and Their Protection” (<span lang="ru">Закон «О персональных данных и их защите»</span>), No. 94-V, was adopted on 21 May 2013 and has been amended more than once since. It sets out on what grounds personal data may be collected and processed, how to obtain a person’s consent, what rights people have, when data may be transferred abroad, and where it must be stored. The law is binding on everyone who processes personal data in Kazakhstan.

## What it means in practice {#practice}

In Kyrgyzstan we have our own personal data rules, above all the Digital Code. The Kazakh law becomes important when the work involves partners or suppliers that process the data of Kazakhstan residents: for example, a shared service for customers in both countries, a Kazakh contractor, or a cloud platform in Kazakhstan.

In that case, it is worth finding out three things from the partner in advance: on what grounds and with what consent it processes the data, where the data is stored, and whether it may transfer the data to another country, including to an AI provider. If a partner offers an AI solution, that too is processing of personal data, and the law’s requirements apply to it in full.

An employee only needs to remember a simple rule: customers’ personal data, including that of Kazakhstan residents, must not be pasted into an AI tool unless it is an approved work service for such data. For more, see the guide [What you can and cannot share with AI](page:kb/guides/what-to-share).

## Key ideas {#ideas}

- **Personal data.** Any information about a specific person, or about someone who can be identified from it, including biometric data, on any medium.
- **Consent.** Usually the person’s consent is needed, in writing or in electronic form that can be confirmed. It states who processes the data, which data and for how long, and whether it will be passed to third parties or abroad.
- **Storage in the country.** Databases with personal data must be located in Kazakhstan. This matters when choosing cloud and AI services that store data abroad.
- **Transfer abroad.** Data may be transferred freely to countries that protect it; to other countries only in special cases, for example with the person’s consent.
- **A person responsible for data.** An organization appoints someone who oversees compliance with the law internally and responds to people’s requests about their data.
- **Reporting a breach.** An incident of unlawful access to personal data must be reported to the authorized body within one business day of its discovery.

## Good to know {#details}

- Adopted on 21 May 2013 (No. 94-V); for the current version with all amendments, see the Adilet portal.
- Policy in this area is run by the ministry responsible for digital development; the prosecutor’s office also supervises compliance.
- The law applies to everyone who collects and processes personal data in Kazakhstan: government bodies, companies, and other organizations.
- The law has no separate concept of “sensitive data”; some such information is protected by sector laws, for example on medical confidentiality.
- The law is amended regularly. Before a project involving the data of Kazakhstan residents, check the current version together with the legal team.
