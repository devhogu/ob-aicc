## Briefly {#short}

The main law in this area is Law of the Kyrgyz Republic No. 87 of 6 August 2018, now titled “On Countering the Financing of Criminal Activity and the Legalization (Laundering) of Criminal Proceeds” (<span lang="ru">О противодействии финансированию преступной деятельности и легализации (отмыванию) преступных доходов</span>). It is binding on banks and other financial institutions: they get to know their customers, monitor transactions, screen them against sanctions lists, and report suspicious transactions to the financial intelligence unit. That body collects and analyzes these reports and passes material on to law enforcement.

## What it means in practice {#practice}

**AI helps but does not remove the duties.** AI can spot unusual transactions, match names against lists, gather information about a customer, and draft a conclusion. But every check the law requires stays: [[kyc|know your customer (KYC)]], risk assessment, sanctions list screening, and suspicious transaction reports. The decision that a transaction is suspicious, and the responsibility for it, stay with people and the bank's internal control program.

**The decision trail.** The law requires keeping customer due diligence records, transaction records, and conclusions from transaction analysis for at least five years. If AI suggested a conclusion, it must be clear what data it was based on and who checked and made the decision ([[human-in-the-loop]]).

**Don't tip off the customer.** Employees may not tell the customer or third parties that a report on their transaction has been or will be sent to the financial intelligence unit. This applies to AI too: a chatbot or an automated letter must not reveal this by accident.

**For employees.** Material on suspicious transactions, reports to the financial intelligence unit, and sanctions checks are highly sensitive. Work with them only in approved systems and don't paste them into general-purpose AI tools (see [What you can and cannot share with AI](page:kb/guides/what-to-share)).

## Key ideas {#ideas}

- **Risk-based approach.** The bank assesses and documents its risks, groups customers by risk level, and applies enhanced measures to risky ones and simplified measures to low-risk ones.
- **Customer due diligence.** Establish and verify the identity of the customer and the beneficial owner, understand the purpose of the relationship, and keep checking that transactions match what is known about the customer.
- **Suspicious transaction report.** Sent to the financial intelligence unit when there is a suspicion that funds come from crime or are linked to financing terrorism, extremism, or weapons of mass destruction, whatever the amount.
- **Targeted financial sanctions.** People and organizations on the sanctions list may not be given funds or services, and their transactions and funds must be frozen immediately.
- **Internal control program.** The bank's internal rules for meeting all these requirements; AI tools used in this work fit into it rather than bypass it.

## Good to know {#details}

- Law No. 87 was adopted on 6 August 2018. In January 2025 its title and scope were changed: it now covers the financing of criminal activity in general, including terrorism, extremism, organized groups, and the proliferation of weapons of mass destruction.
- The financial intelligence unit is the authorized state body that collects and analyzes suspicious transaction reports, passes material to law enforcement, and applies targeted financial sanctions. Today this is the State Financial Intelligence Service under the Ministry of Finance.
- A suspicious transaction report is sent within five hours after the transaction has been formally recognized as suspicious.
- Customer due diligence and transaction records are kept for at least five years.
- The Cabinet of Ministers determines the supervisory bodies for financial institutions; the National Bank sets requirements within its competence.
- How the country meets the international standards of the FATF (Financial Action Task Force) is assessed by the Eurasian Group on Combating Money Laundering and Financing of Terrorism (EAG); the mutual evaluation report on the Kyrgyz Republic was approved in May 2018.
