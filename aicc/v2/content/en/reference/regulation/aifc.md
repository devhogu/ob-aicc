## Briefly {#short}

The Astana International Financial Centre (AIFC) is a territory in Astana with a special legal regime in the financial sphere. It was established by a Constitutional Statute of the Republic of Kazakhstan of 7 December 2015. The center has its own rules for financial services, data, and technology, based on the principles of the law of England and Wales, its own regulator, the Astana Financial Services Authority (AFSA), and its own court. The rules are binding on the center’s participants; for us they are a benchmark.

## What it means in practice {#practice}

For an organization in Kyrgyzstan, the AIFC is interesting for two reasons. First, it is an example of how a regulator in the region lets new financial services, including AI-based ones, be tested in a controlled environment before they go to market. We look at our own pilots the same way: first a test under limited conditions, then expansion.

Second, it is a possible venue for partners. If a partner or supplier is registered in the AIFC and offers us an AI solution, it helps to understand that it works under the center’s rules, including its own data protection rules, and that disputes with it may be heard by the AIFC Court.

For an employee, the takeaway is simple: even in a sandbox, the regulator expects AI risks to be under control and a person to check the result.

## Key ideas {#ideas}

- **A special legal regime.** Within the center, its own acts apply, based on the principles of the law of England and Wales and on international standards.
- **Its own regulator.** The Astana Financial Services Authority (AFSA) authorizes the center’s participants and supervises them.
- **Regulatory sandbox.** The FinTech Lab is an AFSA environment where companies develop and test new financial and [[regtech|regtech]] solutions under simplified rules before they receive full authorization. AI solutions can be tested there too.
- **Its own data rules.** The center has separate data protection rules that apply to its participants, including in AI solutions.
- **The AIFC Court.** It is legally separate and independent from Kazakhstan’s judicial system. A contract can be concluded in any language, but if it comes before the AIFC Court, it must be provided in English.

## Good to know {#details}

- The legal basis is the Constitutional Statute “On the Astana International Financial Centre” of 7 December 2015.
- The regulator is the Astana Financial Services Authority (AFSA).
- We found no separate binding rules on AI at the center. In 2025, AFSA surveyed participants: of the 42 who responded, 45% use AI, mostly in customer communication and marketing. AFSA plans a new survey in 2026 and will decide based on its results whether the rules need to change.
- The center’s acts and main materials are published in English; the center’s website also has Russian and Kazakh versions.
