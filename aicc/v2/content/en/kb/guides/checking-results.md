This guide is for everyone who passes on the results of working with AI: to colleagues, a manager or a customer. After it, you will be able to check an assistant's answer against a short list and find what in it needs checking against the source.

An assistant writes smoothly and confidently, even when it is wrong. So check everything that leaves your hands. How thoroughly depends on what happens if a mistake slips through.

## Checklist {#checklist}

- **Facts and figures.** Every figure, date, name and link has been checked against the source. A made-up link or contract clause is a common [[hallucination|hallucination]].
- **Completeness.** The answer covers the whole task, and nothing important was lost in shortening: deadlines, amounts, exceptions.
- **Meaning.** The retelling has not changed the meaning of the original text; the conclusions follow from the data.
- **Tone and audience.** The text suits whoever will read it: a customer, a manager, a regulator.
- **Anything extra.** The text has no data that shouldn't be there: personal, internal or someone else's.

## Example: a summary of a new regulation {#example}

The assistant has written a half-page summary of a new regulation from the regulator. Before you send it to your manager:

1. Check every date against the original (for example, when the regulation takes effect) and every clause number.
2. Make sure the summary hasn't lost any exceptions or transition periods.
3. Find phrases like “banks must” and make sure that requirement is really in the text and wasn't invented.

## When to check more thoroughly {#depth}

<div class="rai-cards rai-cards--3">
<article><h3>For yourself</h3><p>A draft, an idea, a summary for your own work: reading it and judging it sensibly is enough.</p></article>
<article><h3>For colleagues and your manager</h3><p>Check the figures and facts against the source, and make sure the meaning hasn't been distorted.</p></article>
<article><h3>For customers and outsiders</h3><p>A full check against the list; if in doubt, a second person. Decisions about a customer are made by a person.</p></article>
</div>

## Techniques {#tips}

- Ask the assistant to show which part of the source each statement relies on: “for each point, give a quote from the document”.
- Ask it to find the weak spots in its own answer, then check them yourself.
- If mistakes keep coming back, fix the prompt: that way you correct them once, not in every answer.

## Try it now {#try}

Take an answer from the assistant, for example the email summary from the guide [Getting started](page:kb/guides/getting-started). Find one statement in it with a figure, date or name and check it against the original text. If you don't have the original at hand, ask the assistant to show the quote it relied on, and find it yourself.

Don't take an answer on trust just because it is well written: [[overreliance|over-reliance]] on AI is one of the main risks of working with it.
