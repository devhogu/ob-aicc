Pricing & profitability

Pricing and profitability management determines whether the Bank's commercial activity generates returns above the risk-adjusted cost of capital — linking product pricing through funds transfer pricing (FTP) frameworks to customer and segment profitability measurement. In line with Basel III Pillar 2 and the regulator's ICAAP requirements, RAROC-based profitability measurement informs capital allocation decisions. Interest Rate Risk in the Banking Book (IRRBB) under BCBS 368 adds a further dimension: FTP rates must reflect the full cost of hedging interest rate risk embedded in each product. The opportunity for GenAI is to compress the analytical cycle from pricing model to profitability attribution — synthesizing experiment results, surfacing below-hurdle segments, and automating the production of profitability reports that currently require multi-day assembly across risk, finance, and product teams.

Insights & analytics FTP rate accuracy, product pricing model performance, and pricing experiment outcomes are each tracked by separate teams — Treasury for FTP, Product for pricing, Finance for profitability — without a consolidated view of how FTP assumptions flow through to product margin and customer-level RAROC. Mispriced products consume risk budget below the hurdle rate for months before the gap appears in quarterly profitability reporting.
Enablement Pricing model recalibration — driven by rate environment changes, competitor moves, or IRRBB framework updates — requires Treasury, Risk, and Product teams to coordinate across FTP curve reconstruction, margin sensitivity modeling, and customer-segment impact assessment. The coordination bottleneck and multi-week production cycle delays pricing responses to market movements.
Automation Pricing experiment design, result synthesis, and the management narrative summarizing what a rate or term change achieved are structured analytical exercises with repeating formats. The experiment-to-insight cycle — which currently takes weeks of analyst time — involves data extraction, statistical testing, segment decomposition, and narrative drafting that fit well within an AI-assisted workflow.
New business opportunities Banks that run pricing experiments continuously — testing rate levels, fee structures, and bundled-product offers across microsegments — and synthesize results faster than competitors build a compounding pricing intelligence advantage. Systematic experiment-to-action cycles that take days rather than months create a structural ability to optimize pricing faster than peers operating on annual review cycles.

Pricing experiment analytics

Synthesis of A/B pricing experiment results across products and segments — measuring conversion impact, margin effect, competitor response, and customer-segment differential — to inform pricing decisions. Pricing experiments across rate levels, fee structures, and bundled-product offers generate structured data that requires statistical testing, segment decomposition, and management narrative before the result can drive a pricing decision. Current experiment-to-decision cycles take four to six weeks from data availability to management sign-off.

Pricing experiment design review

Pre-launch review of proposed pricing experiment designs — evaluating sample size adequacy, segment stratification, control group specification, and measurement period against the proposed learning objective — with a structured design assessment and any recommended adjustments. Design review before launch reduces the rate of underpowered experiments that produce inconclusive results, enabling the pricing team to commit experiment capacity to designs with sufficient statistical power.

S
Problem to solve

Pricing experiments that are underpowered — insufficient sample size, inadequate control group, or measurement periods that do not capture the full seasonal effect — produce inconclusive results that cannot support a pricing decision, wasting the experiment window and the pricing team's bandwidth. Experiment design review is informal; the statistical adequacy assessment relies on the analyst's individual knowledge rather than a systematic pre-launch check against the experiment's learning objective.

Solution

The AI agent reads the proposed experiment design — learning objective, pricing treatment, segment, sample size, control group specification, and measurement period — and produces a design assessment covering statistical power, confound risk, and any recommended parameter adjustments before the experiment launches. The pricing team reviews the design assessment, implements the recommended adjustments, and launches the revised experiment with sufficient statistical power for the learning objective.

OKR

Pricing experiment designs are reviewed for statistical adequacy before launch so experiments are powered to produce actionable results.

Adoption

Pre-launch design review used for ≥90% of new pricing experiments within 6 months of go-live.

Acceptance

Inconclusive experiment rate — experiments producing results below statistical significance — reduced by ≥30% within 18 months of adoption; pricing team confirms design review materially improved experiment design in ≥80% of review sessions.

Cycle

Pricing competitive response signal

Continuous monitoring of competitor product pricing — deposit rates, lending rates, and fee structures — to identify pricing moves that affect the Bank's conversion and retention rate on actively running experiments and the Bank's competitive positioning on in-market products. Competitor pricing changes that materially affect experiment conditions are flagged to the pricing team, enabling a decision on whether the experiment should be paused, extended, or its interpretation adjusted.

M
Problem to solve

Pricing experiments are designed against a competitive context that is current at launch; competitor pricing changes during the experiment window affect conversion rates in both the treatment and control groups, confounding the experiment's ability to isolate the treatment effect. Without systematic competitor pricing monitoring, experiment results affected by in-period competitor moves are not distinguished from results driven by the pricing treatment itself, leading to potentially incorrect pricing recommendations.

Solution

The AI agent monitors competitor product pricing feeds for the Bank's key product markets — deposit and lending rate publications, fee structure disclosures, and promotional rate announcements — and flags changes that affect the competitive context of a running experiment. The pricing team receives the competitor move alert with the estimated impact on experiment conditions and an assessment of whether the change warrants a pause, extension, or interpretive adjustment.

OKR

Competitor pricing moves that materially affect running experiment conditions are identified and flagged to the pricing team within 1 business day of the change.

Adoption

Competitive pricing monitoring covering the Bank's primary product markets within 6 months of go-live; alerts used in ≥80% of experiment review sessions.

Acceptance

≥75% of competitor move alerts confirmed as materially affecting experiment conditions by the pricing team; experiment interpretation adjusted for competitor-confounded experiments in ≥90% of flagged cases.

Cycle

Competitor pricing change alerts issued within 1 business day of the change so the pricing team can adjust experiment management before further data accumulates under altered competitive conditions.

Segment profitability deep-dive

Ad-hoc profitability attribution analysis for a specific customer segment, product, or geographic unit — decomposing NIM, fee income, credit loss allocation, FTP impact, and operating cost to explain under- or over-performance against the RAROC hurdle. These analyses are commissioned by business unit heads when performance deviates from plan and require financial analysts to rebuild the attribution from raw data with no reusable framework, creating unplanned analytical load.

Segment cost-to-serve benchmarking

Analysis of operating cost allocation by customer segment and product — benchmarking cost-to-serve against the Bank's internal segment profitability targets and available peer data — to identify the segments where operating cost is the primary drag on RAROC and the cost components driving the deviation. The analysis supports the business unit's cost efficiency program prioritization with segment-level cost-to-serve evidence.

S
Problem to solve

RAROC underperformance can be driven by NIM compression, credit loss, or operating cost; segment profitability attribution identifies cost as a driver but does not decompose the operating cost to the specific service component or channel allocation that is above the efficiency benchmark. Without segment-level cost-to-serve decomposition, cost efficiency program effort is allocated without evidence of which service components and channel allocations produce the largest cost-to-RAROC drag in the underperforming segments.

Solution

The AI agent reads the operating cost allocation for each segment by service component — servicing, origination, collections, and channel cost — and benchmarks cost-to-serve against the Bank's internal targets and available external peer data, producing a ranked cost deviation report with the specific components above the benchmark. The business unit head uses the cost deviation report to direct cost efficiency program effort toward the highest-impact service components in the underperforming segments.

OKR

Segment operating cost-to-serve is benchmarked against internal targets and peer data on a quarterly basis and available for cost efficiency program prioritization.

Adoption

Cost-to-serve benchmarking used in ≥4 business unit cost efficiency program reviews within the first 18 months of go-live.

Acceptance

Cycle

Segment profitability deviation signal

Continuous monitoring of segment profitability metrics against plan and the RAROC hurdle — detecting intra-quarter deviations by product segment, geographic unit, and customer tier that indicate a material performance trajectory change before the quarterly reporting cycle. Early deviation signals enable business unit heads to commission an attribution analysis and initiate corrective action within the quarter rather than receiving the deviation as a board-reported result.

M
Problem to solve

Segment profitability deviations are identified at the quarterly reporting cycle; deviations that develop over weeks of intra-quarter trading are not visible to the business unit head until the quarter is already closed. Corrective actions initiated on a closed-quarter deviation can only affect the following quarter; deviations identified intra-quarter allow intervention within the period, reducing the magnitude of the final reported underperformance.

Solution

The AI agent monitors segment profitability metrics — NIM, credit loss run-rate, fee income, and cost — on a weekly basis against the plan trajectory and the RAROC hurdle, and routes a deviation signal to the business unit head when a segment's intra-quarter trajectory projects a material end-of-quarter variance. The business unit head reviews the deviation signal and commissions a full attribution analysis where the projected variance is above the materiality threshold, enabling intra-quarter corrective action.

OKR

Intra-quarter segment profitability deviations that project a material end-of-quarter variance are identified and routed to business unit heads on a weekly monitoring cycle.

FTP & RAROC pricing signal

Continuous comparison of front-book product pricing against RAROC hurdle rates — identifying products and segments where current origination pricing falls below the board-set risk-adjusted return threshold. FTP rates embody the cost of funding and interest rate risk hedging; the RAROC hurdle incorporates expected credit loss, capital charge, and operating cost allocation. Products priced above FTP cost but below the RAROC hurdle consume risk budget at a negative risk-adjusted return.

RAROC hurdle breach alert

Automated daily comparison of front-book origination pricing against the board-set RAROC hurdle rate by product and segment, with breach alerts routed to the product pricing team when a product's current origination pricing falls below the hurdle. Alerts include the specific product, segment, pricing band, and the estimated RAROC implied by the current origination rate, enabling pricing intervention before a material volume of sub-hurdle business is written.

M
Problem to solve

Products priced above FTP cost but below the RAROC hurdle consume risk budget at a negative risk-adjusted return; under the ICAAP and risk appetite frameworks aligned with Basel III, persistent below-hurdle origination must be identified and remediated as a capital efficiency issue. FTP and RAROC attribution reports are produced quarterly; by the time below-hurdle origination is identified at the quarterly reporting cycle, a quarter's volume of sub-hurdle business has been written.

Solution

The AI agent reads the daily origination pricing data and the current FTP and RAROC parameters, computes the implied RAROC for each product-segment pricing band, and routes a breach alert to the product pricing team for any band where origination pricing falls below the board-set hurdle. The product pricing team reviews the alert, determines whether the below-hurdle pricing reflects a deliberate strategic decision or an unintended pricing drift, and initiates a pricing adjustment or obtains board exception approval as appropriate.

OKR

Front-book origination pricing that falls below the board-set RAROC hurdle is identified on a daily basis and routed to the product pricing team before material sub-hurdle volume accumulates.

Adoption

Daily RAROC breach alert covering ≥90% of front-book product-segment origination volume within 6 months of go-live.

Acceptance

Cycle

The trend analysis informs the strategic planning cycle's assumptions and the board's understanding of structural profitability trajectory.

M
