# Functional vocabulary reconciliation

Reviewed: 4 October 2026. Scope: corresponding English and Russian Vocabulary and Style editions, revision 3.2; their current catalog descriptions and portal projection.

## Review basis

The paired semantic review covered all 175 existing defined concepts, 13 states and 12 stage groups. It compared scope, responsible actors, permissions and duties, timing, conditions, exceptions and record identity. One concept already established by Operating Model 7.3, Living record / Актуализируемая запись, was added explicitly. Final inventory: 176 concepts, 13 states and 12 stage groups. The inventory below records the paired names and changed cells; it is review evidence, not an automated claim of semantic equivalence.

The shared terminology reference retains its 71 entries in one accepted set. Fixed form, permitted language variant and context-specific form are usage conventions, not acceptance grades. No gray/white classification or measured 70/80-percent consensus is asserted. The governing vocabulary establishes functional meaning; the shared reference supplies professional terminology and naming conventions.

## Reconciliation and governing basis

| Subject | Correction | Governing basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interpretive function | Purpose, scope and third-column heading now describe interpretation and distinctions. Bare lists of related expressions now state their relationship to defined concepts. Corresponding language editions preserve scope, conditions and exceptions without language precedence. | Vocabulary and Style 2; Document Catalog 4–5; shared terminology 1.3–1.5 |
| Acceptance | Acceptance criteria and Accepted now include the proper business acceptor and Executive Sponsor cases. Acceptance, Control Sign-Off, Solution Definition approval and Release remain separate decisions. | Solution Lifecycle Model 3.3, 5.1, 7.3–7.4 |
| Delegation | English explicitly identifies decision-making authority. Both editions retain written scope, period, exclusions and the additional Decision Log entry for more than two weeks. | Operating Model 4.7, 5.4, 5.7 |
| Records | Record encompasses working state, living records and evidence. Living Solution Definitions remain in the Portfolio; evidence remains in the Registry. Snapshot covers Iteration/PI close and cutover, including Portfolio solution state, tier and release. | Operating Model 7.1–7.4 |
| Business case | Initiative Brief, Sunset rule and Control Sign-Off no longer narrow the Russian meaning to financial justification. Business-case stage meaning covers need, options, benefits, costs and risks. The existing Russian stage designation is retained to match the lifecycle model, with its scope expressly clarified. | Portfolio Management Model 5–7; Business Model 5; Solution Lifecycle Model 5 |
| Agreement and assumptions | Internal best-effort commitment is bounded by available capability. Client assumptions are planning inputs, not commitments imposed on the client. Stakeholder is scoped to the agreement; wider incident-process usage is distinguished. | Business Model 3.1, 5.1–5.4; Solution Lifecycle Model 8.5 |
| Control and evidence | Deficiency denotes uncorrected failure to operate as required; its control status follows the existing classification. Accepted limits preserve compensating controls and excluded rights. | Operating Model 4, 8.5 |
| Portal and workflow | Operating portal contains non-sensitive information and no governance/evidence records. Charter Workflow is an explanatory document; the general technology concept is broader. | Operating Model 7.6; Document Catalog 5; Vocabulary and Style 2.1 |
| Yearly Steering | Russian input wording covers the year's review findings rather than only the defined deviation category. | Operating Model 6.5 |
| States and stages | Distinctions clarify receipt versus approval, approval versus AI-use permission, waiting versus deferral, completion versus acceptance, and closure versus successful acceptance. Existing transitions, levels and light mode are preserved. | Solution Lifecycle Model 5.1, 6.6, 7.3, 8.1; AI Policy 2.1–2.2 |

Language-specific spelling and title-capitalization rules remain appropriate to their respective languages. The changes do not constitute a corpus-wide migration of existing defined names or historical Record titles.
